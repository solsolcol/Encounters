## 2. Why the colouring was sloppy — the cause, not the look

The source model has **no textures and one material** (checked:
`images 0, textures 0, materials 1`). Every colour on it is ours. The v16.8
bake classed every TRIANGLE on its own, from its height, slope and mesh
density — so one column came out as SIX classes (base, roof, soffit,
white, gold, red: piece 0 is `{base 20, roof 96, soffit 126, white 111,
gold 61, floor 30}`) and was painted six ways; the hall's wainscot ended in
a saw-tooth wherever a wall triangle happened to be classed 'base'; the
window frames, the brackets and the beam ends were plain white because
they were classed 'white'. No amount of tuning the per-class patterns
could fix that: the labels were wrong.

## 3. What changed: colour by PART

`masters/v16.9/temple/relabel.mjs` splits the baked mesh into its 2,572
connected PIECES (welded by position, the same union-find as the
segmentation report) and names each one for what it is — measured, with a
guard that throws if a re-bake renumbers the pieces:

- **column** (the twelve): limewashed shaft; the capital (the top 3.25
  model units, where the radius flares 1.46 → 1.85) gilded with petal
  lines; a lacquer ring; a red band with the gold stencil; a gold rule; a
  gold foot over a lacquer ring.
- **beam** (the brackets out of the flank columns, the gable tie beams):
  red lacquer, the gold stencil on the faces, gilt arrises.
- **screen** (the two gable ends): the scalloped valance under the eaves
  red with a fine gold stencil; the gable board red lacquer with the gold
  lattice; gilt edges; plain lacquer behind.
- **barge** / **mosaic** (the bargeboards and their inner layer): gilt
  faces, lacquer behind; the inner layer green and blue glass mosaic with
  gold chips.
- **roof**: orange terracotta tiles, two rows of green glaze along the
  eaves, a gold ridge; teak boards underneath; the tiers' fascias lacquer
  with a gold line.
- **wall**: outside limewash with a gold plinth rule, GILT window frames
  (gold / red / gold), a pointed lacquer-and-gold CROWN over every window,
  the reveals red lacquer with a gold lip, the mullions lacquer; the front
  and back doors framed the same way. Inside: the lacquered wainscot with a
  gold stencil and a gold rule (decided per PIXEL by height now — no saw
  tooth), limewash, gilt frames round every window and door, the
  red-and-gold frieze under the ceiling; the ceiling plain pale teak
  (Chad's v16.4 ruling), a shade lighter.
- **frame** (the back door's surround, the west windows' own frames):
  the same gilt frame pattern, so east and west windows match.
- **rail**: limewashed balusters, a gilt capping.
- **base**: stone courses, a lacquer band with gold rules under the
  floor's edge; the teak floor and runner on top inside, stone slabs out.
- **carve** (everything else, 434k triangles of the carving): gilt where
  it stands proud, deep lacquer in its hollows with a chip of coloured
  glass here and there.

COLOR_0 now carries (AO, distance from the piece's top, distance from its
bottom) / 25 — which is how a column knows its capital from its shaft
without a guess by height. Gold is metal-ish and glossy per pixel, lacquer
glossy, limewash matte (roughness and metalness set in the shader), and the
baked occlusion is dosed per part (a wall greys under a full dose).

`tools/preptemple.mjs` is unchanged but for the flat-shaded class name
(`carve`, ships with no NORMAL, as the gold did). Still **every triangle**:
473,896, 2.96 MB (was 3.3 — COLOR_0 is three channels now).
