## The kit (`assets/thaikit.glb`, 2.27 MB)

`tools/prepthai.mjs` picks 42 objects out of the two packs **by their place
in each file** (the catalogue index — the objects under the single-child
Sketchfab wrappers, in file order) and bakes each into one named node,
`thai_<name>`, its origin on its base centre, in the packs' own metres. The
mesh hangs off a child node so `meshopt()`'s dequantizing transform and the
chapter's own position never share a node.

- 126 objects across the two files; 42 taken (30 from pack 1, 12 from
  pack 2): the seated gold Buddha, a standing gold figure, candles, an
  incense pot, orchids, lilies, papyrus, a pedestal tray, marigold strings,
  cushions (stacked, triangle, flat), rugs, a spirit house, roosters,
  banana / traveller's palm / elephant ear / fern / monstera / hedge / grass,
  clay pots and a glazed water jar, a footed lotus bowl, a ceiling fan, a
  desk fan, an oil lamp, a tea set, a bell, low and side tables.
- 101,797 triangles → **74,307** (the orchids, lilies, palm and fans
  simplified per object; everything small left alone). The topiary was tried
  and left out: 27,840 triangles of unwelded leaf cards the simplifier could
  not touch, for a garden shape no Thai wat grows.
- Base colour only; alpha kept where the MATERIAL is MASK/BLEND (v6.16) as
  palette PNG, everything else JPEG; sheets ≤ 256 px (512 for the Buddha,
  the rugs and the spirit house); metalness ≤ 0.3 (v8.9).
- Two things measured, not assumed: simplify BEFORE measuring the base
  (it moves the extremes — the standing figure's base read 2.3 mm off), and
  the ceiling fan is centred on its ROD, not its box — a five-blade fan's
  box is 0.118 m off its hub, and it spins.

## Where it went (e3c1)

`thai(name, x, y, z, o)` stands a clone and hides `o.hide` — the primitive
it replaces — **only when it has landed** (v4.7: a failed download costs a
nicer prop, never the chapter). Blockers are the primitives' and never move;
a new piece where the player walks brings its own (`o.block`, a trunk for a
plant, the jar's own size for a jar). `o.tint` gives a piece its own
material (the kit's are shared by every clone).

- **The sala altar**: the Buddha (×3.5, warmed to the altar's gold — the
  pack's gilt read as pale stone in the dawn light), the two side images,
  purple orchids in white vases for the lotus vases, the candles on turned
  stands (our flames kept), the incense pot, two pedestal trays on the
  lowest tier, marigold strings on the screen's edges.
- **The sala**: both ceiling fans (turning), a stack of floor cushions and
  a triangle cushion against the east rail.
- **The private room**: the shrine's three Buddhas (sized so the flame
  clears the shelf above), gold standing images at the top shelf's ends,
  candles, the incense pot; the ceiling fan; the standing fan became the
  pack's DESK fan on the cabinet, turning (`standHead` is whatever turns),
  with an oil lamp beside it and a tall vase of papyrus in the corner it
  left; a brass tea set for the jug and cups; a cushion on the mat.
- **The courtyard**: the spirit house (×1.2, same facing) with a row of
  rooster figurines at its foot; the three lotus jars as the pack's footed
  lotus bowls; the gallery's six Buddhas; banana, traveller's palm,

  gate and at the kuti; two roosters loose in the courtyard.

## The staircase naga (`assets/naga.glb`)

Chad's own naga (743,400 triangles, 32 MB → **118,944, 2.8 MB**, the same
tool). It is a balustrade serpent lying FLAT, head rearing at +z, and a
stair's cheek wall slopes — so the prep BENDS it (`BEND=zTop,zFoot,drop`):
a SHEAR, not a tilt, lifting the body by the wall's full rise behind the
top of the flight and by a straight ramp down the flight, and leaving
everything past the foot alone. A shear keeps every vertical line vertical,
so the head still rears straight up; the normals follow the shear
(n' = M⁻ᵀn). The numbers are the chapter's: at ×1.38 the nose is at z 1.07,
the neck on the pedestal (z 0.62…1.12), the ramp −0.573 exactly the cheek
wall's, the tail on the wall's top at the sala end. One model, both sides.
The primitive serpent (tube, spine, hood of five heads) is drawn until it
lands; its blocker stays.
