# v5.26 · HIS VOICE — the boy gets his own bus

Chad, after choosing to keep River: *"he's way too soft in volume. can you
universally increase his volume in all cutscenes, options, playable scenes,
scorecards, outcomes, everywhere basically that has his voice"*.

## Why he was soft, which is not what I expected

The takes measure FINE. Over all 79 of River's files:

| | mean | peak |
|---|---|---|
| James / River, 79 takes | **-20.8 dBFS** | -4.3 dBFS |
| mother, auntie, tang-ki, 18 takes | -19.6 dBFS | -3.9 dBFS |

He is 1.2 dB under the rest of the cast. Audible, but nowhere near "way
too soft". The number that explains the complaint is the CREST: mean -20.8
against peaks reaching **-1.9 dB** on the loudest take. That is a 19 dB
gap, and it is a direct consequence of the shipping rule — every take is
**peak-matched** to the file it replaced (the v4.8 rule, kept at v5.15 and
v5.18). Peak-matching aligns the single loudest instant and says nothing
about the average, and River is a soft, breathy read: his average sits a
long way beneath his peaks.

Then compare him to what he talks over. Chapter 2's fan bed is -24.3 dBFS
mean, the flat's `amb` is -27.6. A voice at -20.8 is only **3.5 dB** over
the fan. That is the whole complaint: not that he is quiet in absolute
terms, but that he has almost no margin over the room he is standing in.

## Why raising every file was the wrong fix

The loudest take (`vscare1`) peaks at -1.9 dBFS. A uniform lift — in the
engine or by re-encoding all 158 files — can therefore buy **1.9 dB**
before that take clips. 1.9 dB is not what "way too soft" means, and
re-encoding would also have burned the one property the mp3s are kept for:
they are the proven, peak-matched fallback.

The gap between "measures fine" and "sounds soft" is a DYNAMICS problem,
so it wants a dynamics tool.

## What was built

A bus for his voice only: a boost, then a compressor that holds the peaks
the boost creates.

```
his take -> gain (VOICE_BOOST = 2.0) -> DynamicsCompressor -> packGain -> masterGain
                                        threshold -18, knee 12, ratio 4
                                        attack 4 ms, release 250 ms
```

It hangs **before `packGain`, deliberately**. Mute (`packMuteSync`), the
volume slider (`masterGain`) and the cutscene ducking all already act on
packGain, so putting him through it rather than around it means not one of
those had to be touched, re-reasoned or re-tested. Routing him to
`masterOut()` instead would have been the obvious move and would have
silently broken the mute button.

Five call sites now ask `outFor(name)` where they used to name `packGain`,
which is every way his voice can reach a speaker:

| site | what it covers |
|---|---|
| `snd()` | every cutscene line (they arrive via `sfx` -> `sting` -> `snd`) |
| `say()` | in-world narration during play |
| `speak()` | the lines the outcome and rank cards wait on |
| `scaredGasp()` | his four scare reactions |
| `queueVoice()` | chapter 1's opening line, its own asset — it was on `sfxGain`, outside the pack entirely |

That is Chad's "everywhere" enumerated: play, cutscenes, films, outcome
cards, rank screens.

## Measured, not assumed

`dbgvoicebus.mjs` decodes his real takes with ffmpeg and renders them
through the REAL `DynamicsCompressor` in an `OfflineAudioContext` — Web
Audio's compressor has its own curve, so modelling it in ffmpeg would have
been a guess about the one component doing the work. Four configurations
were compared on twelve takes:

| variant | boost / threshold / ratio | avg lift | min lift | worst peak | clips |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A (shipped)** | **2.0 / -18 / 4** | **+4.8 dB** | **0.0 dB** | -0.28 dBFS | 0 |
| B | 2.2 / -22 / 5 | +4.1 dB | -1.5 dB | -0.61 dBFS | 0 |
| C | 1.8 / -20 / 4 | +4.1 dB | -0.8 dB | -0.98 dBFS | 0 |
| D | 2.4 / -24 / 6 | +3.6 dB | -2.4 dB | -0.97 dBFS | 0 |

A wins on every axis, and the `min lift` column is why: B, C and D all make
`vscare1` QUIETER. Pulling down a scare reaction to lift a whisper is the
opposite of the ask. A is the only one that lifts everything or leaves it
alone.

The shape of the lift is the point:

```
v4wake3am  (the 3 a.m. whisper)   was -25.8  ->  +7.7 dB
vrelief    (the wordless exhale)  was -25.6  ->  +6.5 dB
v5learnD                          was -22.2  ->  +5.8 dB
vA                                was -18.2  ->  +4.5 dB
vscare1    (the loudest take)     was -11.8  ->  +0.0 dB
```

The quietest lines gain the most and the loudest gains nothing. It is not
a volume knob — it evens him out, which is what a soft, wide-dynamic read
needed.

## The trap: his files are not identifiable by name

`JAMES_TAKES` in main.js spells out all 79 sample names. It cannot be a
prefix rule: his files are `v*`, and so are the **mother's** (`v2ma`,
`v4ma1`) and the **auntie's** (`v3aunt1`). `/^v/` would have quietly put
two other actors on his bus.

A hand-copied list is exactly the kind of thing that rots, so
`chaptertest` now asserts the set is **exactly** `who === 'james'` out of
`src/voicelines.js`, in both directions. A new line for him that never
reaches the set does not error at runtime — it just plays at the old
volume, which nobody would hear until a whole chapter was built. Now it
fails the build. (Same family as the cue check: *a mistyped cue is silent
with no error, and that is not a bug a screenshot can catch.*)

## What was deliberately NOT changed

**The other three speakers.** The mother, the auntie and the tang-ki still
run flat into `packGain`. Chad asked for the boy, and there is a real
argument it should stay that way: he is the voice INSIDE the player's head
and they are people across a room, so him sitting forward of them is a mix
decision rather than an oversight. It does leave him ~5 dB over the cast.
If that reads as the others being too quiet, the fix is one line — route
them through the same bus at a lower boost.

**Every audio file.** Not one byte of `assets/audio/` or
`assets/audio-opus/` changed. The whole thing is `VOICE_BOOST` and four
compressor numbers, so it is tunable in one edit and revertible in one
line — where a re-encode of 158 files would have been neither.

**No re-timing.** A bus changes level, not duration, so every cutscene
timeline, every measured `secs` and the whole v5.15 overlap scan stand.
