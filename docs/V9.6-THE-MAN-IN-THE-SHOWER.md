# v9.6 · THE MAN IN THE SHOWER

Chad's six notes after v9.5 went live. Five are corrections to things v9.4 and
v9.5 shipped; one is a new beat, and it is the one that matters — it puts the
chapter's premise somewhere the player can walk into it hours early.

`src/main.js` gains one `STING_SAMPLE` row and one name in `TEEN_TAKES` and
nothing else, so episode 1 is unchanged by construction.

## 1 · THE TWO ITEM NAMES

*"rope should be called toggle rope. Dressing should be called FAD kit."*

`item3` and `item4` in the chapter's `words`. `ITEM_GLYPH` — the internal key
that picks which shape `itemIcon` draws — is untouched: it has been documented
as "which glyph, whatever the sheet calls it" since v9.4, so a rename is a
sheet change and not a drawing change.

## 2 · A SHORTER WAIT

`FREE_SECS` 45 → **30**, `FREE_WARN` 18 → 12. Measured on the shipped build:
the timer reads **0:30** when the evening opens. (The first read said 0:00 —
that is the OBJECTIVE COMPLETE banner's own frame, the v9.4 artefact; sampling
three times a second apart shows 0:00 → 0:30 → 0:28.)

## 3 · THE NOTICE BOARD IS GONE

*"it doesnt really add anything spiritual."* It was the one interaction in the
bunk that was pure barracks furniture — next week's live firing — and this
episode's standing rule since v9.5 is that every beat earns its place by
pointing at the haunting.

The board still hangs on the wall; you just cannot ask it anything. `k1board`
and `n1board` stay in the pack and in the registry: nothing else names them,
and deleting a recorded take to remove a prompt is a trade with no upside.

**One bug fell out of removing it.** The bunkmate's own hotspot was gated on
`seen.has('board')`, so with the board gone he would have been unreachable for
the whole chapter — the hotspot would have been listed, enabled never, and
nothing would have said so. Measured after: the hotspots are `shower`, `buddy`,
`bunkmate`, and the last is offered on its own.

## 4 · THE MAN IN THE SHOWER

*"If the player enters the toilet during the free interaction time, make it
obvious that the semi-transparent FBO soldier is standing at the shower area
that is the same one that will be turned on later at 3am. The soldier fades
away after being seen. Player has a voiceline saying 'wait, is there someone in
there?'"*

Which is the chapter's premise put where the player can walk into it: the
shower that turns itself on at three in the morning has someone standing in it
at ten at night, and the game never mentions it again.

Same asset and same treatment as the tenth man on the line (`fbosling`,
`ghostify`). Two moments, on purpose:

- **ARMED** within 2.6 m of the toilet doorway — so he is already standing
  there before the player is far enough in to see down the cubicle lane, and
  nothing pops into existence in front of an eye already pointed at it;
- **SEEN** once the player is inside (past `BLOCK.z0 + 0.7`), which starts
  `n1ghost` and then fades him out over 1.8 s.

Measured on the shipped build: alpha 0 → 1 at the doorway (stage 1), the line
and the fade on entry (stage 2), and gone with the group hidden (stage 3).
`reset()` puts it back to 0 — the v8.1 law.

**WHERE HE STANDS WAS FOUND BY PHOTOGRAPH, NOT BY ARITHMETIC.** The first pass
put him at `WATER_AT.z - 0.32`, directly under his own shower head, and from
the doorway he was a grey smudge: the three partitions are 1.1 m DEEP centred
at `BLOCK.z1 - 0.55`, so they span z 6.40–7.50 and he was standing in the
middle of that, hidden at the grazing angle every approach uses. He stands at
**z 6.05** now — 0.35 m clear of their front edge, still on his own shower's x,
in the open.

**And "make it obvious" turned out to be a LIGHTING problem.** The block runs
one tube at 1.6, and the standard ghost treatment (0.35 grey over a near-black
glow) rendered him as a stain on white tile. `ghostify` now takes an optional
look and this one passes `{ grey: 0.62, glow: 0x2c3a54 }` — paler, and faintly
self-lit in cold blue-grey, which also reads as the wrong kind of thing rather
than as a soldier standing in the dark. The defaults are v4.9's inline numbers
exactly, so the corridor figure and the tenth man on the line are untouched.

### the take

`n1ghost`, Aaron, eleven_v3, `[uneasy] Wait... is there someone in there?` —
his words with one added ellipsis (the v5.28 rule). Two takes, both clean; the
pick is `_a` at 3.16 s for the **0.26 s hesitation** it puts mid-question
("is there someone... in there?"), against `_b`'s 0.08 s, which says it
straight through. A man stopping in a doorway does not say it straight through.

**One law paid for here: the two encoders move in opposite directions.**
libmp3lame LOSES about half a dB and libopus GAINS about seven tenths, so one
peak-matched WAV cannot serve both. Matched at −3.8 for the mp3, the ogg came
out 0.9 dB hotter than anything else in the pack — inaudible in testing and
shipped to every browser that decodes Opus, which is most of them. Each encoder
is fed its own source now: mp3 from a −3.3 wav (→ −3.8, matching `n1shower`,
`n1lights` and `n1wake` exactly), ogg from a −3.8 wav (→ −3.1).

## 5 · THE SLEEPERS, LOWER AGAIN

`SLEEP_SINK` 0.035 (v9.3) → 0.060 (v9.4) → **0.085**. Measured on the shipped
build at lights out, all eight: **8.5–8.6 cm** into the mattress, statues and
rigs alike.

## 6 · THE NEIGHBOUR ANSWERS

*"during the cutscene of talking to him, he should at first be in the more idle
animated sleeping animation, and only change to the groaning or moving one when
he starts speaking."*

So the groan stops being his RESTING state and becomes his ANSWER. `SLEEP_TAKE`
gives him `Sleep_Normally` like everyone else; scene C hands him
`Groan_Holding_Stomach_in_Sleep` on the same frame his line starts (7.2 s), as
a hard cut, because a sleeping man shifting has no blend to make.

A man groaning through the whole night is noise; a man who starts the moment
you whisper at him is the beat.

`sleepTake()` is how a scene says so and `sleepTakeReset()` puts every man back
on his own — called by `restore()` when a cutscene ends and by `reset()` on a
replay, because **a take a SCENE set must be cleared by whatever ends that
scene** (the v8.1 law in its animation form). Measured through the real scene:
`Sleep_Normally` at 3.0 s → `Groan_Holding_Stomach_in_Sleep` at 7.6 s →
`Sleep_Normally` after it ends.

## 7 · ONE THING THAT RODE ALONG

`dispose()` stopped the mixers of the sergeant, the buddy, the bunkmate, the
encik and `ghostFig` — and not `ghostLine`, which v9.5 added and which has
therefore been leaving a running mixer behind since that release. Both ghosts
are in the list now. `leaktest` was green either way (a mixer is not GPU
memory), which is exactly why it survived a release.
