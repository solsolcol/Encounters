# v21.16 · No block figures, anywhere

Chad, 21 Oct 2026, while v21.15 was live:

> "You should never ever use those generated fake block figures in any part of the
> game. They ruin the entire look of the game. All your generated block figures
> must all be replaced by real 3d model characters."

It is a standing rule now (CLAUDE.md, under the minigame rule). This file is the
record of applying it to the whole game: what was found, what each thing became,
and how it was checked.

## How the game was searched

Three read-only sweeps ran side by side, one per episode (episode 1 and the
engine; episode 2; episode 3), each told to find every person or figure built
from boxes, spheres, cylinders, cones or capsules, or painted onto a canvas, and
to say WHEN a player can see it: always, only until a model lands, only if a
download fails, only in a film, or never. Every finding below was then read in
the code before anything was touched.

The answer was better than feared and worse than it should have been:

- **Seen in normal play: two places.** The passengers in episode 3 chapter 3's
  buses and coach (a cylinder, a ball and a cap each — the film's morning bus,
  ending C's night bus, both coaches), and his own crossed legs in episode 3
  chapter 2's opening film (six capsules on the flat's floor).
- **Seen only on a slow or failed download: everywhere else.** Every chapter
  built a placeholder for each character — a capsule, or (episode 1) a figure of
  boxes and balls — and hid it when the model landed. The loading curtain covers
  the wait (since v14.15), so a player saw one only when a file never came, and
  then it stood there for the whole chapter. Some placeholders were statues: the
  Buddhas, the gate's giants and the altar's deities in episode 3, the paper
  effigy in episode 1 chapter 3.

## Kept, and why

- **e2c4's Figure 11 target boards.** A range target IS a printed silhouette; it
  is a prop, not a person (the rule says so).
- **e3c2's shadow on the mats.** His own kneeling shadow, painted, with the crown
  growing on it — an effect Chad kept by name at v19.4 ("the dark shadow on the
  mats stays"). It stands for a shadow, not for a person. Put to him in the report.
- **Dead code that draws a figure and is never called or never shown** (e3c1's
  `makeAbbot` and `mkRuesi`, e3c2's `glimpse`): nothing on screen.

## Checked

All on the shipped build, deploy `6ad9350ab68becd73c8a86c1`, byte-verified (all 236
served files identical to the snapshot every check below ran on).

- **Every chapter builds with its stand-ins hidden**: `leaktest` builds all thirteen
  chapters (`everyChapterBuilds`), and a chapter rebuilt eight times gives back every
  geometry and texture it took — no error.
- **The buses**: a census of every seat list in the film's pocket — the film's bus
  5 seats / 5 people, ending C's night bus 7 / 7, the coach 4 / 4, the second coach
  4 / 4 — and photographs from the kerb, the windows and up close, desktop and phone,
  morning and night: real seated people behind every window, no cylinder or ball
  anywhere.
- **His legs** (episode 3 chapter 2's film, shot 3): photographed at 21.5, 24, 28,
  34.8 and 35.3 s on a phone and a desktop — two whole knees in white trousers under
  the resting hand, the chant phone beyond them, the legs out of frame while his eyes
  follow the hand up (28 s), and no torn edge in any frame. The cut is checked by
  `tools/probes/legscore.mjs`: zero back-face pixels through the shot's lens at both
  ends of its push-in and both pitches, on both crops.
- **The film's first cut was wrong, and the record says so**: a horizontal cut "just
  over the hips" (the first try) took the tops off his knees — a cross-legged man
  holds them higher than his waist — and showed the hollow inside of his trousers as
  torn paper (600–2,200 back-face pixels at every placement that showed a leg). The
  cut that ships keeps what each vertex is SKINNED to (docs/LEARNINGS.md).
- **Episode 3 chapter 3 played through on a phone by real taps** after the change
  (the M5 play-through in docs/V21.12-PLAN.md): every line in order, none over another,
  no page error — the stand-ins it no longer draws were never in the way of anything.
