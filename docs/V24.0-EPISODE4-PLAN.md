## 2. The episode's one axis: THE RECEIVER

S2·03 says it outright: when the same message keeps arriving, *"the honest question is not whether the messengers are
reliable. It is whether you are."* The episode turns him from someone looking OUTSIDE for the right master into
someone working on himself:

1. **WHO** — the wrong master. He learns to check whom he trusts.
2. **WHAT** — the right teacher. He learns what his sensitivity is for, and the first skill: open and close.
3. **CLARITY** — the night he cannot close. Control needs a clear mind; the fifth precept.
4. **CALL** — two masters, two tongues, one message: the receiver must improve.
5. **ROBE** — thirty days of discipline, and a disturbance answered with merit instead of fear.

The skill learned in chapter 2 is the episode's thread: practised in 2, lost in 3, asked of him again in 5.

## 3. The five chapters

The house shape for each: one small dense place, an opening film, play with the kit, one decision of four options
that are four different KINDS of answer, each ending a whole story, and the case file's lesson first.

### Chapter 1 · Not Every Master Should Be Trusted  (S1·11 — the trial's five chapters in one)
- **Film.** His own amulet shop in Singapore — a student's first stock. Then Thailand: a master someone recommended.
- **Play, in three short places.** (1) **The first master's room** — he talks fortune and business, offers piece
  after piece; at the edge of sight, a presence behind him and one covered object. **OBSERVE**: what is seen is noted
  as what was SEEN, never as what it MEANS (the trial's 5.1). (2) **Home in Singapore, in a fog** — the wallet, a
  search with the picture dimmed and dragging (the cold's seams). (3) **Lersi Chin's shrine** (the lersi masks already
  ship) — he tells it as it was; Lersi Chin reads it through his own practice and **clears it**, the head bowed while he
  chants and blows; then his warning: do not go back to prove anything (the trial's 5.2–5.3).
- **Decision — the trial's 5.3, verbatim:** A **return to test your suspicion** (worst) · B **observe from a distance
  without entering** · C **contact the first master and confront him** (bad) · D **follow the warning and walk away**
  (best). Four acts, four endings; the first master is never shown to be anything, and the endings come from what the
  player does (the no-villain rule — the trial wrote it that way).
- **Closes on** *"…none of them could answer the question I actually carried."*

### Chapter 2 · I Finally Found My Answers  (S1·12 — the Wai Kru)
- **Film.** The questions, one by one, over the episodes already played (the hands, the dance, the roar, the
  children); a Wai Kru in Thailand at dawn — white cloth, many masters, many disciples.
- **Play.** Among all of them, the man in white. **Finding him** is the first beat — a look through a crowd that
  rewards stillness, not searching. Then he tells Pho everything, and Pho answers. **NEW — OPEN AND CLOSE**, the
  first teaching: a breath-paced control the episode keeps — open to let what is around him in, close before it
  overwhelms him, on Pho's count; graded gently, a teaching, not a test.
- **Decision (new; DECIDE the words):** what he asks of the teacher — power (worst), a sign that proves him (bad),
  to be told his future (bad), or *how to control it and use it for others* (best — "not for myself").

### Chapter 3 · The Night I Lost Control  (S2·02 — the party)
- **Film.** A friend's birthday, a crowded flat or a karaoke room, glasses filled again and again.
- **Play.** The familiar pull comes, and the skill from chapter 2 is there to close it — but the drink is in the way:
  the CLOSE drifts and lags, and the manifestations take him in turn — Lersi, Hanuman, Tiger, Naga — while he is aware
  inside the whole time (the chapter's own `hands`, `tremble` and `handPose` seams; the four as light, never as
  bodies, as episode 3 drew them). His friends see a man they cannot reach.
- **Decision (new; DECIDE):** the morning after — laugh it off (bad), drink less "next time" (bad), avoid parties (good
  in part), or *stop drinking — clarity is part of the practice* (best, the fifth precept).

### Chapter 4 · The Message That Led Me To Monkhood  (S2·03 — two tongues)
- **Film.** 2016 — his arms in full sleeve Sak Yant (**DECIDE:** the viewmodel's arms inked from here on), the drive
  to the Ah Pek's home with Grandmaster Pho.
- **Play.** The Ah Pek speaks in Hokkien; Pho speaks through Thep; neither follows the other. **NEW — TWO TONGUES:**
  he is the only one who hears both — the two messages arrive as captions in their own languages over what they mean,
  and he matches what one says to what the other says, until the same answer stands on both sides: ordain first.
- **Decision (new; DECIDE):** believe it because they agree (bad — agreement is not the reason), doubt them both
  (worst), ordain to unlock more power (bad — S2·04's lesson), or *accept that the receiver is the problem, and ordain
  to become a clearer one* (best).

### Chapter 5 · My First Monkhood  (S2·04 and S2·05 — the robe and the kettle)
- **Film.** The ordination: the head shaved, the robes, the precepts taken. **DECIDE** what is shown; it must be
  right, and is researched before it is written (§4).
- **Play — a day, then a night.** Dawn alms round barefoot with the bowl, chanting, sweeping, meditation — the
  monastic day as the chapter's clock (episode 2 chapter 1's shape), with RESTRAINT asked of him (what a monk does not
  take, touch or keep) rather than tasks to win. Then December in the mountain temple, too cold for his kuti, a
  concrete hall, another monk asleep beside him — and the **kettle**: click, it boils, click, off, click again.
- **Decision (from S2·05's lesson; DECIDE the words):** argue with it (bad), lie there afraid (bad), wake the monk and
  insist on an answer (bad), or *sit up, meditate and dedicate the merit to whatever is there* (best — it stops).
- **Closes on** *"It was only the beginning of the path that would eventually lead to becoming Master Z."*
