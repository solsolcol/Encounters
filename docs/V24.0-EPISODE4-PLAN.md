## 2. The episode's one axis: THE RECEIVER

S2·03 says it outright: when the same message keeps arriving, *"the honest question is not whether the messengers are
reliable. It is whether you are."* The episode turns him from someone looking outside for the right master into
someone working on himself, and every chapter ends where the next one begins:

1. **LOOK** — the master he did not know. He learns to keep what he saw as what he saw.
2. **CHECK** — the fog, the second opinion, the clearing and the warning. He learns to check whom he goes to.
3. **CONTROL** — the teacher who can answer. The first skill: open and close.
4. **CLARITY** — the night he cannot close. Control needs a clear mind.
5. **CALL** — two masters, two tongues, one message. The receiver is the problem; he will ordain.

That is the progression Chad asked for: the first two chapters are about choosing a master, the last three about
being a better student. The skill learned in chapter 3 is the episode's thread — practised in 3, lost in 4, held in 5.

## 3. The five chapters

The house shape for each: one small dense place, an opening film, play with the kit, one decision of four options
that are four different KINDS of answer, each ending a whole story (the v21.30 rule), and the case file's lesson first.

### Chapter 1 · The Master I Did Not Know  (S1·11, the first half · the trial's 5.1)
- **Film.** His own amulet shop in Singapore, a student's first stock, in his voice ("…though I was still very much a
  student of this world."). Then Thailand: a master someone recommended, whom he did not know well.
- **Play — the master's house.** A waiting room, then the room where he receives people.
  1. **The consultation.** The master reads the fortune of the business, courteous and unhurried.
  2. **The items.** Piece after piece laid out in front of him; he takes little, as the case file says — one piece,
     his pick (the kit's choose window). Nothing later hangs on which.
  3. **OBSERVE (new).** While the master works, the room shows things: a presence standing behind him at the edge of
     sight, one covered object the light does not seem to reach, smoke leaning the wrong way. Each is looked at (the
     press-aim look-spots the kit already has) and WRITTEN DOWN, and the note offers two ways to write it — what was
     there ("A figure stood behind him.") or what it might mean ("He keeps a spirit.") — and the player keeps one. The
     notebook rides the save into chapter 2. This is the trial's "Observe before concluding" made into something to do.
- **Decision — the trial's 5.1, verbatim:** A **Observe quietly before reacting** · B **Ask directly about what you
  notice** · C **Assume the master is dangerous** (worst) · D **Leave without getting more involved** (best). Watch,
  ask, judge, leave: four kinds.
- **Endings.** Four whole stories, and every one ends on the flight home and the case file's own line: *"When I got
  back to Singapore, I stopped feeling like myself."* No answer stops the fog, deliberately: the mistake was made
  before the door — going to a master nobody could vouch for — which is S1·11's lesson and the reason chapter 2 exists.
- **The no-villain rule** (v21.30), kept the way the case file and the trial keep it: the first master is never shown
  doing harm, never hostile, mocking, greedy or cruel. What makes the room uneasy is only what Master Z perceives, and
  later what Lersi Chin reads "according to his practice"; the case file's "whether you interpret that spiritually or
  otherwise" is said. The endings come from what the player does. (And the first master must NOT wear the model of
  any real teacher already in the game — Ajahn Krukai is a real, named master.)

### Chapter 2 · Not Every Master Should Be Trusted  (S1·11, the second half · the trial's 5.2–5.5)
- **Film.** The days after, in Singapore: the shop counter, a customer's question he cannot follow, the till counted
  twice, his phone in his hand and no memory of why.
- **Play, in two places.**
  1. **His flat, in a fog.** The picture dull and dragging, sounds far off (episode 3 chapter 4's cold — its blur, its
     smear, its drained colour — given a grey look instead of the Yin shop's blue: an option on a shipped seam). The
     wallet is not where he left it, and he cannot remember what he did with it. Then the trial's **5.4** as play: a
     movement in the dark corner of the room — fear wants it to be Thailand; the light switched on finds a shirt on a
     hanger in the fan's air. "Check the new situation on its own evidence."
  2. **Lersi Chin's shrine** (his model and his voice; the lersi masks and Chad's Lersi pieces already ship). He tells
     it as it was — the notebook from chapter 1, read back. A note kept as a meaning is answered in the trial's own
     words (5.2: "Seeing something is not the same as understanding it"). Lersi Chin examines it according to his
     practice and says what he believes: something negative was placed on him. **The clearing:** head bowed before
     him, his katha captioned (the caption seam), the blowing, the water — and the fog lifts, the colour, the sound
     and the focus coming back over it. Then his warning: do not go back to prove anything.
- **Decision — the trial's 5.3, verbatim:** A **Return to test your suspicion** (worst) · B **Observe from a distance
  without entering** · C **Contact the first master and confront him** (bad) · D **Follow the warning and walk away**
  (best).
- **Endings.** Four whole stories; the trial's 5.5 is what each one carries forward, and all four close on the case
  file's own words: *"After that I became far stricter about who I go to … And yet still, none of them could answer the
  question I actually carried — why I had these abilities at all, and what they were truly for."*
- **The lesson card** is S1·11's own lesson, first: ask the ordinary questions — who are they, their character, their
  intentions, who can genuinely vouch for them.

### Chapter 3 · I Finally Found My Answers  (S1·12 — the Wai Kru)
- **Film.** The four questions, in his voice, over the episodes already played — the note on the void deck, the
  shower at three, the hands that moved, the dance, the roar, the children on his altar: *"Why was I so sensitive?
  Could any of it be used to help others? How could I cultivate enough to control it — and eventually use it at
  will?"* Then a Wai Kru in Thailand: many masters, many disciples in white.
- **Play.**
  1. **The Wai Kru** — the offerings to the teachers and the chant, as the research says they are (§4).
  2. **The man in white.** Among all of them, one man — offered only once the player has stopped looking around and
     stood still. A beat of stillness, not a search.
  3. **He tells Pho everything** — the sensitivity, the senses, the trances, the confusion — and Pho answers each of
     the four questions in turn (the objective's count).
  4. **OPEN AND CLOSE (new) — the first teaching.** On Pho's count: open, and the world comes in (the sound swells,
     the light widens, the pull begins); close, before it overwhelms, back to stillness. Breath-paced and graded
     gently — a teaching, not a test — with its own picture (the minigame rule).
- **Decision (new; DECIDE the words) — what he asks of his teacher now:** A **power, to be stronger than whatever
  comes** (worst) · B **to be told his future** (bad) · C **first, who Pho is and who vouches for him** (good — chapter
  2's lesson kept; S1·12 itself says "with everything I said about vetting a teacher still applying") · D **how to
  control it, and use it for others** (best — "Not for myself.").
- **Closes on** *"For the first time in my life I had found the teacher who could guide me to the real answers."*

### Chapter 4 · The Night I Lost Control  (S2·02 — the party)
- **Film.** A friend's birthday — a flat or a karaoke room; glasses filled again and again.
- **Play.** The party, and every glass makes chapter 3's CLOSE lag and drift. The familiar pull comes; he tries to
  close and cannot. Then the four take him in turn — Lersi, Hanuman, Tiger, Naga — round and round on the chapter's
  clock, his hands and body moving on their own (episode 3's `hands`, `tremble` and `handPose` seams), aware inside the
  whole time; his friends, worried, unable to reach him; and at the edges of the room the presences the four turn to
  block — "guarding my body, not harming it". The four are figures of light from Chad's own models where they exist
  (the Hanuman, the naga and the lersi already ship); the tiger needs one (the no-block-figures rule).
- **Decision (new; DECIDE the words) — the morning after:** A **laugh it off: it was only the drink** (worst) ·
  B **drink less next time** (bad) · C **ask Grandmaster Pho what happened** (good — the case file's own "later,
  through my spiritual learning, I understood") · D **stop drinking: a clear mind is part of the practice** (best — the
  fifth precept, which the case file names itself).
- **The lesson card** ends with the case file's own sentence about speaking to someone if drinking cannot easily be
  stopped (**DECIDE**).

### Chapter 5 · The Message That Led Me To Monkhood  (S2·03 — two tongues; the episode's end)
- **Film.** 2016. His arms in full sleeve Sak Yant (**DECIDE**: the hands' viewmodel inked from here on). The drive to
  the Ah Pek's home with Grandmaster Pho.
- **Play.**
  1. **The receiver at his peak.** His sensitivity is the strongest it has been, and while two men speak to the unseen
     he holds himself closed and clear — chapter 3's skill, kept.
  2. **TWO TONGUES.** The Ah Pek speaks Hokkien; Pho speaks through Thep; neither follows the other, and he is the
     only one who hears both. Their words come as captions in their own tongues over what they mean, and he sets what
     one says beside what the other says until the same answer stands on both sides: *ordain first*. Probably the
     kit's `match` kind (v9.4) with sentences in place of pictures — a new seam only if the words need more room than a
     tile.
  3. The Ah Pek also speaks with his guardian deity, and says it plainly: *"if you truly want to bring your
     cultivation to the next level, the best path is to go into monkhood."*
- **Decision (new; DECIDE the words):** A **believe it because they agree** (bad — the case file: "not that they were
  right because they agreed") · B **doubt them both** (worst) · C **ordain to unlock more power** (bad — Episode 5's
  first lesson: monkhood is not a shortcut) · D **the receiver is the problem: ordain to become a clearer one** (best).
- **Closes the episode on** *"That day I decided I would ordain. It was only the beginning of the path that would
  eventually lead to becoming Master Z."* — and the episode-complete card closes case 4 and opens Episode 5.
