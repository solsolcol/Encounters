## 2. The episode's line: from the wrong master to the work

Every chapter ends where the next begins, and each one changes what he can do:

1. **LOOK** — the master he did not know. He learns to keep what he saw as what he saw.
2. **CHECK** — the fog, a second opinion, the clearing, the warning. He learns to check whom he goes to — and still
   no one can answer *why* he is this way.
3. **FIND** — the teacher who can. The first teaching: control, open and close.
4. **EXAMINE** — his teacher's first case with him in the room. He learns to look at a person's history before any
   conclusion — including the conclusion that something is spiritual at all.
5. **SENSE** — the second case. His own sense opens (a smell nobody else can smell), and his Lersi comes in to protect
   him while his teacher clears a house. He learns that experiencing something is not the same as understanding it.

The skill learned in chapter 3 is the thread: he holds it while assisting in chapter 4, and in chapter 5 meets a sense
it does not cover. The episode closes on S1·14's own words: *"Cultivation, control, experience and discernment — that
is the real path, and there is no shortcut through any of it."*

## 3. The five chapters

The house shape for each: one small dense place, an opening film, play with the kit, one decision of four options
that are four different KINDS of answer, each ending a whole story (the v21.30 rule), and the case file's lesson first.

### Chapter 1 · The Master I Did Not Know  (S1·11, the first half · the trial's 5.1)
- **Film.** His own amulet shop in Singapore, a student's first stock, in his voice ("…though I was still very much a
  student of this world."). Then Thailand: a master someone recommended, whom he did not know well, for guidance about
  the fortune and future of his business.
- **Play — the master's house.** A waiting room, then the room where he receives people.
  1. **The consultation** — the master reads the business's fortune, courteous and unhurried.
  2. **The items** — piece after piece laid out; he takes little, as the case file says (one piece, his pick, by the
     kit's choose window; nothing later hangs on which).
  3. **OBSERVE (new)** — while the master works, the room shows things: a presence standing behind him at the edge of
     sight, one covered object the light does not seem to reach, smoke leaning the wrong way. Each is looked at (the
     kit's press-aim look-spots) and WRITTEN DOWN, and the note offers two ways to write it — what was there ("A figure
     stood behind him.") or what it might mean ("He keeps a spirit.") — and the player keeps one. The notebook rides
     the save into chapter 2.
- **Decision — the trial's 5.1, verbatim:** A **Observe quietly before reacting** · B **Ask directly about what you
  notice** · C **Assume the master is dangerous** (worst) · D **Leave without getting more involved** (best).
- **Endings.** Four whole stories, all ending on the flight home and the case file's line: *"When I got back to
  Singapore, I stopped feeling like myself."* No answer stops the fog, deliberately: the mistake was made before the
  door — going to a master nobody could vouch for — which is S1·11's lesson and why chapter 2 exists.
- **The no-villain rule**, kept as the case file and the trial keep it: the first master is never shown doing harm,
  never hostile, mocking, greedy or cruel; what makes the room uneasy is only what Master Z perceives, and later what
  Lersi Chin reads "according to his practice"; the case file's "whether you interpret that spiritually or otherwise"
  is said. He wears no real teacher's model (Ajahn Krukai is a real, named master).

### Chapter 2 · Not Every Master Should Be Trusted  (S1·11, the second half · the trial's 5.2–5.5)
- **Film.** The days after, in Singapore: the shop counter, a customer's question he cannot follow, the till counted
  twice, his phone in his hand and no memory of why.
- **Play.**
  1. **His flat, in a fog** — the picture dull and dragging, sounds far off (episode 3 chapter 4's cold — its blur, its
     smear, its drained colour — with a grey look instead of the Yin shop's blue: an option on a shipped seam). The
     wallet is not where he left it, and he cannot remember what he did with it. Then the trial's 5.4 as play: a
     movement in a dark corner — fear wants it to be Thailand; the light switched on finds a shirt on a hanger in the
     fan's air. "Check the new situation on its own evidence."
  2. **Lersi Chin's shrine** (his model and his voice). He tells it as it was — the notebook read back; a note kept as
     a meaning is answered in the trial's own words ("Seeing something is not the same as understanding it"). Lersi
     Chin examines it according to his practice and says what he believes: something negative was placed on him.
     **The clearing**: head bowed before him, his katha captioned, the blowing, the water — and the fog lifts, colour,
     sound and focus coming back over it. Then his warning: do not go back to prove anything.
- **Decision — the trial's 5.3, verbatim:** A **Return to test your suspicion** (worst) · B **Observe from a distance
  without entering** · C **Contact the first master and confront him** (bad) · D **Follow the warning and walk away**
  (best).
- **Endings** carry the trial's 5.5 and close on the case file's own words: *"After that I became far stricter about
  who I go to … And yet still, none of them could answer the question I actually carried — why I had these abilities at
  all, and what they were truly for."*

### Chapter 3 · I Finally Found My Answers  (S1·12)
- **Film.** The questions, in his voice, over the episodes already played — the note on the void deck, the shower at
  three, the hands that moved, the dance, the children on his altar: *"Why was I so sensitive? Could any of it be used
  to help others? How could I cultivate enough to control it — and eventually use it at will?"* Then a Wai Kru in
  Thailand: many masters, many disciples in white.
- **Play.**
  1. **The Wai Kru** — the offerings to the teachers and the chant, as the research says they are (§4).
  2. **The man in white** — among all of them, one man, offered only once the player stops looking around and stands
     still: a beat of stillness, not a search. "Something inside me knew I had to speak to him one day."
  3. **The day the chance comes** — he tells Pho everything (the sensitivity, the senses, the trances, the confusion),
     and Pho answers each of the four questions in turn (the objective's count).
  4. **OPEN AND CLOSE (new) — the first teaching.** On Pho's count: open, and the world comes in (the sound swells,
     the light widens, the pull begins); close, before it overwhelms, back to stillness. Breath-paced and graded gently
     — a teaching, not a test — with its own picture (the minigame rule).
- **Decision (new; Chad approves the words) — what he asks of his teacher:** A **power, to be stronger than whatever
  comes** (worst) · B **to be told his future** (bad) · C **first, who Pho is and who vouches for him** (good — chapter
  2's lesson kept; S1·12 itself says "with everything I said about vetting a teacher still applying") · D **how to
  control it, and use it for others** (best — "Not for myself.").
- **Closes on** *"For the first time in my life I had found the teacher who could guide me to the real answers."*

### Chapter 4 · The Woman Who Rose From Her Wheelchair  (S1·13 — case one)
- **Film.** An elderly Indian lady brought to see Grandmaster Pho in her wheelchair: years unable to walk properly, many
  doctors with no clear explanation, many practitioners and nothing improved. He is in the room as his teacher's
  student.
- **Play.**
  1. **EXAMINE — her history first.** Before anything is done, he learns it from her family, piece by piece — the
     doctors and how long, the practitioners she has seen, and that she once served as a medium of Mae Kali (people to
     talk to; no new seam). The case file's first lesson as something to do: look at a person's history, previous
     practices and circumstances before any conclusion.
  2. **The blessing** — Pho perceives a spirit sitting on her back, tied to past karma and matters left unresolved, and
     begins the blessing to release it. He assists (the holy water in his hands, the kit's hold) and keeps himself
     CLOSED as the room's energy rises — chapter 3's skill, held.
  3. **Her trance** — her whole demeanour changes into a deep trance associated with Mae Kali; the spirit lets out a
     loud cry and is forced from her back. She opens her eyes — and stands.
- **Decision (new; Chad approves):** her family asks him what it means. A **it was spiritual all along — the doctors
  were wrong** (worst) · B **promise them it will last, and that it can do the same for others** (bad — "what I
  witnessed, not a promise") · C **say nothing, and leave every question to Pho** (fair) · D **keep seeing her doctors;
  this is what we witnessed, not a promise** (best — the case file's own order: doctors first, and keep seeing them).
- **The lesson card** begins where the case file does: "Not every unexplained physical problem is spiritual, and medical
  checkups matter."
- **Care:** Mae Kali is a goddess her devotees revere; the spirit that oppressed this woman is not her, and the trance
  is shown with the respect the house rule gives every tradition. Researched before a word is written (§4).

### Chapter 5 · The House With Nine Prai  (S1·14 — case two)
- **Film.** A male devotee who has been offending people outside, and at home disturbance after disturbance; the
  investigation; the lift.
- **Play.**
  1. **SENSE (new) — the smell.** The lift doors open and he catches an extremely heavy, foul stench, nowhere near the
     unit yet; the two followers with them smell nothing. He follows it down the corridor to the door — a sense only he
     has, drawn as a gauge that rises as he nears, with its own picture. Pho: the spiritual sense of his nose is already
     open.
  2. **Nine** — inside, Pho detects nine spirits and gives his reading: those who bore the devotee a grudge spilled
     corpse oil of nine Prai in the house (said as Pho's belief, as the case file says it).
  3. **The cleansing, from inside** — as it begins, his own Lersi comes in to protect him and he goes into trance,
     aware the whole time, his mouth chanting words he did not know; play is staying present inside it (episode 3's
     STAY PRESENT kind) as Pho moves through every room drawing from the air like a vacuum, blowing what he gathers into
     a black bag and tying it, and the follower holding the Naga ritual stick feels it trembling, as if spitting
     fireballs.
  4. **After** — the air lighter, the bag tied tightly and disposed of according to the practice, the devotee's thanks,
     the case closed.
- **Decision (new; Chad approves):** what he makes of the smell. A **it proves nine Prai were there — tell everyone**
  (bad — experiencing something unusual does not prove its cause) · B **go looking for that smell elsewhere, to test
  it** (worst — curiosity is not protection) · C **it was nothing, forget it** (bad) · D **keep it as his own
  experience, and keep training under his teacher** (best — "experiencing something is not the same as understanding
  it").
- **Closes the episode on** *"Cultivation, control, experience and discernment — that is the real path, and there is no
  shortcut through any of it."* — and the episode-complete card closes case 4.
