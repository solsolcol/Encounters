## 2 · What was read on 11 Nov 2026 (new; not yet refuted)

**[W1] Wikipedia, "Wai khru".** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wai_khru (English; a tertiary source, to be traced to
its own references at CP1).
- A ritual in which students pay respect to their teachers, to express gratitude and formalise the relationship.
- In the performing arts, held on a Thursday (the day of Brihaspati, the teacher of the gods).
- Buddhist rites the evening before or that morning; monks may be invited.
- "The presiding teacher, traditionally dressed in white", leads the homage (said of the performing-arts rite).
- Lustral water and an anointing (choem); usually followed by the covering (khrop khru).
- The teacher's offering bowl (khan kamnon): flowers, joss sticks, candles, a white cloth, a small sum of money.

**[W2] THE STANDARD, 7 Mar 2026, the Wai Khru at Wat Bang Phra.** https://thestandard.co/sak-yant-wai-kru-wat-bang-phra/
(Thai; the same event as V25.0's [25]).
- A yearly rite in memory of Luang Phor Poen; participants keep rules such as the five precepts.
- The "khong khuen" (ของขึ้น) of some who attend is reported "according to believers' belief".

**[W3] Wikipedia, "Baci".** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baci (English; tertiary).
- The su khwan rite of Laos and Isan: the khwan called back to the body.
- A tray (pha khwan) with a banana-leaf cone at its centre; white cotton threads.
- A thread tied at the wrist, the hand held up chest-high; worn at least three days, untied not cut.

**[W4] อ้อมใจ วงษ์มณฑา, ประสิทธิ์ รัตนมณี, อารีย์ ธรรมโคร่ง (Prince of Songkla University, Pattani), "การอนุรักษ์ภูมิปัญญา
การสักยันต์ ด้านพิธีกรรมและความเชื่อ กรณีศึกษาเครือข่ายสำนักสักยันต์อาจารย์พันธ์ พุฒยอด อำเภอโคกโพธิ์ จังหวัดปัตตานี",
Journal of Social Science and Cultural, vol. 9 no. 8 (August 2025), pp. 357 on.**
https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JSC/article/download/287040/190799/1267938 (Thai; a research article; its
subject is ONE lineage in the far south, at Wat Chang Hai, not Isan). What it reports of that lineage's Wai Khru:
- The Wai Khru is the rite in which disciples gather to honour their teachers, once a year, "a day of showing
  gratitude" to the past teachers.
- Prepared for it: betel and areca, marigolds, savoury and sweet food (beef is avoided).
- The officiant lights incense and candles and recites; holy water is sprinkled over the disciples for good fortune,
  which closes the rite.
- Disciples in their thousands, men and women, and from China, Malaysia and **Singapore**.
- The rite begins at an auspicious time (about 08:30 in 2025). The temple's monks, led by the abbot, take their place
  in the ceremonial pavilion (ปะรำพิธี) and meditate in remembrance of the teachers.
- Disciples bring **trays of flowers** (พานดอกไม้) to honour the teachers.
- A wide ground **ringed by the sacred thread** (วงสายสิญจน์), people sitting packed inside it.
- Every year some disciples show khong khuen during the rite: trembling, jerking, crying out, heavy sweating, rising
  to dance. **Reported as belief:** it is said to come to those with strong faith, a mind fixed on their teachers and
  "a mind easily moved" (จิตใจอ่อนไหวง่าย), when they close their eyes and think of the teachers; "a soft mind shows it
  easily, a firm mind not at all". Helpers bring a person out of it by tapping the ears lightly or stroking the nose,
  and by telling them to **breathe deeply and keep their eyes open**.
- After the rite the offerings (the bai sri's, fruit, savoury and sweet food) are asked for and eaten, for good
  fortune.
- The covering with the teacher's head was first a performing-arts rite; the Sak Yant schools adopted it, and it is
  now the main rite of their Wai Khru. (The plan stages no covering. Chad, 11 Nov: "No need to put lersi masks on
  the people, instead it should be empowered through the bai sri as shown in the photos".)
