# v27.0 · Episode 4 chapter 4 · R-V: the refuting reader's verdicts on R-A (Mae Kali) and R-B (the blessing)

**17 Nov 2026.** The second reader of docs/research/V27.0-KALI.md (R-A) and docs/research/V27.0-BLESSING.md (R-B), as
the tracker's M1 asks (docs/V27.0-E4C4-BUILD.md, R-V). Every claim below was checked against its source, opened again
today (or against the text saved from the note's own download), and where possible against a second source the notes
did not use. **Where this file and either note differ, this file wins** (the plan's §2 and both notes' headers say so;
the notes name per-note verdict files, `-KALI-VERDICTS.md` and `-BLESSING-VERDICTS.md`: this one file is both).

**The rules of the pass.** A claim that could not be confirmed counts as refuted: the game may not state it. A claim
not tested is listed in §5, and nothing may rest on it alone. Supernatural claims are judged only as reports of belief.
No private person is named: the Vellore devotee of R-A's [20], the Malaysian informant of its [34] and the informants of
the Thai dissertation below stay unnamed; scholars and public figures are named as authors.

**Verdicts.** **CONFIRMED** the source says it, in the words quoted. **CORRECTED** it stands, with a detail fixed or
added. **QUALIFIED** the source says less than the note (or the plan) takes from it. **CONTESTED** sources disagree.
**REFUTED** the source says otherwise, or nothing could be found to confirm it. Source numbers: **K[n]** is R-A's list,
**B[n]** R-B's, **N[n]** the new sources of §6.

## 0 · What differs from the notes (the short list)

1. **The Serangoon Road temple's dates are CONTESTED, not FACT** (R-A §0.1, §1.1; R-B §1.7). NHB's Roots says a shrine
   was "believed" to stand "as early as 1855" and Bengali labourers built a temple in 1881 [K1]; URA's conservation
   record says "The temple was founded in 1835 by Tamil pioneer workers who built a small clay shrine" [N1]; the temple's
   own history gives no year and says of the Bengali builders "there are no temple records which confirm this report"
   [N2]. The game states no founding year.
2. **The recommended print shows more than the note says** (R-A §5.2 A, §5.3). Looked at today: besides Kali on Shiva,
   a second, dark-skinned figure lies slain in blood at lower left with a sword and a shield (by the iconography a
   defeated demon; the record names no one), blood is pooled on the ground, it runs from the severed head into the bowl
   and drips from the bowl, and she shows fangs. Used whole (the house rule), all of that is on her altar. The licence,
   bytes and sha1 are exactly as the note says. Put the picture itself to Chad before it is hung (the plan's D10).
3. **The "verses that usher beings home" usher the DEVAS home** (R-B §0 4, §1.3, §4; plan §2.8). *A Chanting Guide*
   titles them "Verses Ushering the Devas Back Home"; their first stanza wishes all beings free of suffering, danger and
   sorrow, but "May they go to a heavenly destination" (*Gacchantu devatāgatā*) is said of the devas who came, and the
   Bhikkhu Manual chants them because "the devas have been invited at the beginning". They do not wish a heavenly
   destination for the released spirit, and they are not a verse that sends a spirit away.
4. **New: the word *anumodana* is inside the chosen verses.** The dedication (§0016) says "Anumodantu te sayaṁ" and
   "Anumodana-hetunā"; the ushering verses (§0063) say "Sabbe devānumodantu". There it means "may they rejoice", said of
   those receiving merit, and laypeople chant both; but Chad's rule (v24.25) gives *anumodana* to monks only, and a lay
   master voicing it would be heard against that rule. His call. A spoken English dedication (the plan's own default)
   avoids it.
5. **"Laypeople make and pour holy water as well as monks" is CONTESTED, not FACT** (R-B §0 2, §1.2). The monks who
   wrote R-B's own [5] say, beside the bowl-carrier: "if no monk taking responsible of sprinkle holy water, lay people may
   not be accepted because monk and holy water are come together"; Textor (via B[6]) says it is "not their exclusive
   right"; lay healers do it in the north and Isan [B19][B18]. The game's ground is the casebook (Pho and Master Z use
   it), called their practice.
6. **A medium who neglects her deity: R-B's "NOT FOUND" is partly refuted** (R-B §3.3; R-A §4). A Thai source exists:
   Thai spirit mediums believe "As a result of negligence, the god will punish the spirit medium with illness or
   misfortune", and two informants' accounts tie refusing the role to death or to a soul that cannot be reborn [N3]. It
   is Thai (the narrator's frame), not Tamil, and it says nothing of another spirit filling a "space"; it makes the
   plan's care with Pho's words more necessary, not less. R-A's "no reliable source" for Tamil Singapore and Malaysia
   stands; the study that would answer it is Babb 1974 [N8], cited everywhere and readable nowhere here.
7. **The karmic creditor "sitting on someone's back" is REFUTED as a sourced claim** (R-B §0 7, §3.1). Its only source is
   an uncited Wikipedia line; Thai searches for เจ้ากรรมนายเวร with เกาะหลัง, and for ผีเกาะหลัง, found nothing. The term
   itself is sound [B24], but nothing ties it to a back. The spirit on her back rests on the case file alone.
8. **Option C's "practice behind it" is QUALIFIED** (R-B §7 may 8, §8; plan §2.12). The Thai helpers' เตือนสติ [B22] is
   said to the person in *khong khuen* ("ให้ผู้ที่มีอาการของขึ้น..."), and the Isan rite's consoling [B45] is "Consoling
   the patient". No source shows a helper calming a frightened relative. C rests on the casebook's ethic and plain care.
9. **Smaller corrections.** The Phi Fa paper is not "northern (Lanna) belief" (K[33]: a general study of a rite it ties to
   Isan, published by a Lanna campus). "Wat Khaek ... increasingly perceived as offensive" is Wikipedia's alone, not
   re-opened, and English-language Thai press uses the name in its own headline [K29]. K[34]'s "28 Feb 2025" is its
   informant's interview, not a publication date. K[29] announces the procession; it does not report it as held. The
   temple's "Mother Goddess" reading is a modern essay it reproduces ("the chaotic sea of quantum-foam"), so it is the
   temple's chosen reading, not evidence of a traditional Tamil one. R-A's own K[19] has a medium carrying the
   *karakam* pot (a man, in Sri Lanka), so a medium carrying it is documented elsewhere; still no woman, and not in
   Singapore. "My part was small; I only held the holy water" is S1·16's (episode 5's case): borrow the role here, do not
   voice the sentence.
10. **Strengthened.** The wanderers' "share of merit" has a better Thai source than R-B's lifestyle page: the southern
    Sat Duean Sip festival, where the ancestors' spirits come up "เพื่อมารับส่วนบุญ" and food is set at the temple for
    spirits "ที่ไม่มีลูกหลานมาทำบุญให้" [N4]. The urumi's tie to trance has a second, independent Malaysian source [N11].

Everything else tested below was CONFIRMED.
