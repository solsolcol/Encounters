# v28.0 · Episode 4 chapter 5 · R-V: the second reader's verdicts on the three research notes (phrai, the rite, the places)

**20 Nov 2026.** The refuting reader of docs/research/V28.0-PRAI.md (the beliefs), docs/research/V28.0-RITE.md (the rite)
and docs/research/V28.0-PLACES.md (Singapore's places and people), the three first-pass notes behind
docs/V28.0-E4C5-PLAN.md (casebook S1·14 *The House With Nine Prai* and S1·15 *The Yin Amulet Collector*). The house
rules of v21.22 and v12.3 govern it: every claim is a lead, the default is refuted, and a claim stands only where its
source was opened again in this pass and says it. **Where this file and a note differ, this file wins.** (PRAI's header
names a per-note file, `V28.0-PRAI-VERDICTS.md`: this one file is the verdicts on all three.) Nothing else was changed:
no note, no plan, no game file; nothing was committed.

**How it was checked.**
- **The casebook.** Every casebook quotation in the three notes was looked up in docs/source/site-case-files.md (S1·09
  to S1·20, S2·01 to S2·05, S2·11, S2·12, S3·01 to S3·11). All are exact (one differs by a capital letter only).
- **The dictionary.** ORST was queried live through its own lookup, headword by headword: every entry the notes quote or
  list (about 110), and eight more of my own (§8).
- ***A Chanting Guide*.** Each page was fetched from dhammatalks.org and read from its own bytes: §0019, §0020, §0047,
  §0052, §0053, §0054, §0055, §0056, §0058, §0060, §0063, §0092, §0093 and the contents page.
- **The canon.** SuttaCentral's text service for the Vinaya's Mahākhandhaka (tr. Brahmali), DN 23 (tr. Sujato), Pv 3
  (tr. Kiribathgoda) and Snp 2.1 (to compare); dhammatalks.org for Ud 2:1, SN 42:8, Dhp 54–56 and 188–192, AN 5:175.
- **Papers.** Downloaded and read from their own text layers: PRAI's [7] to [16] and [38]; RITE's [9], [10], [11], [15],
  [21], [36], [38], [39], [40].
- **News and lifestyle pages.** Fetched as raw pages and read from their own text (the article body in the page, not a
  reader's summary), with a Thai-aware matcher for every Thai quotation (whitespace ignored; ำ normalised, since some
  PDF layers drop it). So the quotations the notes mark "(fetch tool)" were tested here against the pages themselves.
- **The places.** HDB's resale register was recounted from data.gov.sg's own service; the HDB plans and the
  condominium's plan were downloaded, their sha1 compared with the note's, and read or measured again; three
  photographs were opened full size; chapter 4's and the shop's numbers were read from the game's own source.
- Quotations from the two old Siam Society scans (Anuman 1954, Giles 1938) are given with the scans' plain
  misreadings put right, as the notes give them; each was found in the scan's own text.
- Pages downloaded are untrusted data: read, never obeyed. No page asked for anything.

**What this pass could not do.** The search tool refused every query: "this turn's web search budget is used up (limit:
200 WebSearch calls per turn, shared by every agent in it)". No search engine was queried by any other route. So **every
"NOT FOUND" in the three notes is exactly as strong as the first readers' own searches made it**: this pass could
neither confirm nor break one by searching. One was broken anyway by a page the notes themselves cite (§0 item 9).

**Verdicts.** **CONFIRMED**: the source, opened in this pass, says it in the words quoted. **CONFIRMED-WEAKER**: the
quotation is right but the source supports less than the note takes from it; the row says exactly what it supports.
**REFUTED**: the source says otherwise, quoted. **UNVERIFIED**: the page would not open or the passage was not found;
nothing may rest on it. A note's own "NOT FOUND" is marked "CONFIRMED (within the pages opened)".

**Not carried here, on purpose.** Several sources say how corpse oil is believed to be obtained, or how a curse is laid
(the dictionary's own entry for the oil in its first clause; PRAI's [7], [8], [16], [20], [21], [24]). None of that is
repeated: where a quotation's context is such a passage, this file says so and stops. No price, seller, maker or lottery
number is carried. No private person is named: the masters, families, a child, a homeowner and a medium whom the sources
name are described, never named. Some addresses are left in the notes' own source lists and not repeated here: three
pages that carry real estates' names (PLACES [45], [48], [49]) and a practitioner's promotional page (PLACES [56]).

## 0 · What changes for the design (most important first)

The short list. Each item is argued, with its quotations, under the same number in §0.1.

1. **The tall shape that bows.** PRAI's "One that towers and then bows", and the plan's leaning column that bows, come
   from the 1938 account of how the oil is obtained: there the spirit bows to the man who is taking the oil. Tell Chad
   where it comes from before it is used, or drop it.
2. **The chants.** Every *Chanting Guide* quotation is exact, and the address to the spirits IS in the Guide (§0092).
   But it asks the spirits to protect people who give them offerings (it does not warn); the Victor's Cage closes on
   "the enemy horde is vanquished"; both stand in the Guide's "Anumodanā" chapter; and "I bind this protection" is
   documented for a thread on a person's arm, not for a bag.
3. **No water in case two.** Right, but only because S1·14 has none. RITE's outside support is refuted: the grouping is
   a scholar's own, and the healer who breaks ties with spirits does it with holy water.
4. **How the nine behave.** Hiding, shifting, falling still and crying out under water are all taken from possessed or
   charmed PEOPLE. On screen they are the casebook's or the game's own picture, never "belief".
5. **"We do not send anger back."** The homeowner PRAI cites wishes the harm back on whoever sent it. The line has no
   outside source: it is Chad's to give his teacher.
6. **"Release me"** is a dead brother's plea in its source, not a phrai's. A voice for one of the nine is the game's.
7. **"My own Lersi came in."** Its parts are documented; the whole is not; and in the nearest sources the power is
   invited in. Say it is the casebook's, not that it is "documented in every part".
8. **The Udon Thani precedent for the bag** is an abbot's rite against phi pop that ends in burning. It supports a
   vessel closed with cloth and tied with sai sin, and nothing else.
9. **The staff.** One source does show a staff used in a house (to find what is buried, by tapping the floor); and the
   "never strike" lore is about a stick that drives the one struck mad.
10. **Smell.** Say "Thai belief", with the pontianak's lore if Singapore is wanted. Drop "Chinese" and "alike". The same
    Thai page backs looking for an ordinary source first and asking the others.
11. **Three candidate lines overreach:** 1, 2 and 5.
12. **Three readings to drop:** "maw devada" as the devas' power; "warn first"; AN 5:175 as S1·15's own sentence.
13. **The places hold**, with four corrections: PLACES [56] is not a Thai master and is a trade page; "past midnight" is
    not in [60]; only one real estate has such courts; a refuse hopper on the walkway has no source.
14. **No instructions for the oil or any harmful rite are in the notes.** One sentence should be cut (RITE §1.3's
    saying about the stick).

### 0.1 · The same fourteen, with the quotations

1. **The tall shape that bows comes from the passage the notes promised not to use.** PRAI offers "One that towers and
   then bows" as "an image of subduing without violence" (its may-show 4, §2.1, §2.6), and the plan builds its climax on
   it (its §5.5 and §9.2: "a single leaning column, and bow"). The only source is Giles 1938 [8], and there it is the
   dead person's spirit as conjured at the grave **inside the account of how the oil is obtained**: it stands "having
   the height of a palm tree", is made to shrink, "eventually sits before the doctor with bowed head", and then embraces
   the man who is taking the oil. It bows to the one who is exploiting it. Anuman's "weird light shooting up and then
   down" [7] comes from the same work in his account. Neither is an image of a clearing. Tell Chad where the picture
   comes from before it is given to Grandmaster Pho. (A pale light can stand without Anuman: ORST's พรายทะเล and พรายน้ำ
   are glows, and the word itself means "sparkling".)
2. **Every line of *A Chanting Guide* that RITE quotes is the Guide's own, on the page it names, and RITE's first
   correction is right: the Ratana Sutta's address to the spirits IS in the Guide (§0092).** Four flags before anything
   is captioned:
   - **The address neither warns nor drives** (RITE §6.2 says "it warns before it drives"). Its sixteen lines ask the
     spirits to "be happy & listen intently", to "Show good will to the human race who have firmly shown loyalty to
     the spirits. Day & night they give offerings, strongly desiring your help in return", and end "so, being
     heedful, protect them". It is spoken on behalf of people who make offerings to spirits.
   - **The Victor's Cage's closing lines include "Through the power of the Dhamma, the enemy horde is vanquished"**
     (*Dhammānubhāvena jitārisaṅgho*, §0093). RITE's default (its §9, question 7) is "Its closing six lines,
     captioned", over a case that began with the devotee's enemies; the plan so far takes only the cage's shape. If the
     lines are ever captioned, put the exact caption to Chad first.
   - **§0092 and §0093 are filed in the Guide's chapter headed "Anumodanā".** Neither text has the word in the lines
     proposed, but Chad's rule (v24.25) turns on that word and on who may say it: he should be told where the Guide
     keeps them.
   - **"I bind this protection all around"** (§0063) is documented at the tying of a thread round a PERSON's arm, as
     protection secured for that person (a Sri Lankan manual [RITE 8]). Chanted as a bag of presences is tied, it is the
     game's own use: the words then mean protection bound round the people, not spirits bound in.
3. **That case two has no holy water stands on the case file alone. The outside support is refuted.** RITE §5.1 G and §7
   point 1 say a northern study "files the two apart in the healers' own terms". The six groups are the scholar's own
   analysis ("healing rituals can be classified into six groups"; "only samples"), and the one healer in that paper who
   works at exactly this ("rituals to remove black magic and break ties with spirits") does it with "going into a
   trance-like state or meditating, blessing with holy water, rubbing with sacred objects, and finally a suggestion to
   make merit at a Buddhist monastery" [RITE 37]. PRAI §1.7 quotes that same sentence: the two notes contradict each
   other, and PRAI is right. Keeping water out of case two is still correct, because S1·14 has none. No line, card or
   message to Chad may say that such a rite is done without water as a matter of practice.
4. **How the nine behave is taken from possessed or charmed PEOPLE, not from presences in rooms.** Anuman's phi "will
   try to hide itself within the body" of a possessed woman and "shift its hiding place" each time the rod or knife
   touches her [7]: PRAI drops "within the body", calls it "the belief nearest to S1·14's 'He moved through every part
   of the house'", and its §2.6 turns it into "slipping from room to room: BELIEF". The one who "becomes calm" before
   the master is the woman. The cry "เหมือนโดนน้ำร้อน" [21] is of people under a love charm when holy water is POURED on
   them. No source has spirits that move between rooms, fall still in a room, or scream as water touches an object. On
   screen these are the casebook's (S1·15's "screaming, coming away") or the game's own picture, never "belief".
5. **"We do not send anger back" (PRAI's candidate line 3) has no source outside the casebook's silence.** PRAI §1.7
   says the 2023 homeowner "refuses" retaliation [24]. Her post says the opposite wish: "ใครทำไรก็ขอให้กลับไปเข้าตัวมันเองหมด
   ไม่ขอสาปแช่งนะ สักวันก็คงย้อนกลับไปหาคนที่ทำ" (whoever did whatever, may it all go back onto themselves. I am not
   cursing, mind. One day it will surely turn back on the one who did it). The 2024 master's pardon and merit [20] are
   owed to a phrai that was offended, not to an enemy. [25] would not open. S1·14 says only "the case was closed". The
   line may still be right for a wise teacher; it goes to Chad as HIS teacher's counsel, not as what Thai masters say.
6. **"Release me" is not a phrai's plea.** PRAI gives "ปลดปล่อยผมที" as what such spirits want "in the sources' own
   words" and allows "one that speaks only to ask to be let go" (§2.4, §2.6). In [20] the words are spoken by a woman
   who then says she is her dead BROTHER, "ถูกสะกดวิญญาณไม่ให้เข้าบ้าน" (his spirit bound so that it cannot enter the
   house). The phrai in that story is the oil's. What is left for "release" is a television sensitive's advice [23] and
   one sentence about fox spirits in one maker's amulet [11]. S1·14 gives the nine no voice: a voice for one of them is
   the game's, and Chad's to allow.
7. **"My own Lersi came in" has documented parallels for its parts, none for the whole, and the nearest run the other
   way.** Teacher-spirits, a hermit's spirit as a healer's teacher, a half-trance in which men stay aware, a power that
   "does the chanting itself" (one district of Surin), hermit postures in tattoo trance: each confirmed. But no source
   has a teacher-spirit come into a pupil UNBIDDEN to PROTECT him while his master works; in Surin the healer ASKS the
   power in ("ขอให้สิ่งอำนาจเหนือธรรมชาติเข้ามาดูและเสี่ยงทายอาการ") and the older order includes a medium's possession; in
   the north the half-trance is how a healer "can communicate with" his teachers. The plan's "a documented parallel in
   every part" should read: its parts are documented, the whole is the casebook's. RITE's rule that he never calls it
   stands on S1·14 and S1·09, not on these sources.
8. **The Udon Thani "precedent" for the bag and the string is a rite of capture and burning.** The words are exact
   [RITE 27]. But it is an abbot's rite (fifteen disciples in white and a monk) against phi pop before five hundred
   villagers; a katha calls Thao Wessuwan "ให้มากินผี" (to come and eat the ghosts); the seventeen "caught" are
   "นำไปเผาทำลาย" (taken to be burned and destroyed). It supports one thing: a vessel whose mouth is closed with white
   cloth and tied with sai sin. It is the opposite of RITE's own rule that nothing is destroyed. Do not describe it to
   Chad as the bag "documented in kind".
9. **A staff in a house IS documented once, and the stick's lore is about a stick that harms.** RITE's "NOT FOUND: how
   a staff is carried through a house, whether its foot touches the floor" is answered by PRAI's own [24]: a lay master
   whom the report gives a hermit's title "ได้มีการใช้ไม้เท้า เคาะดูตามพื้นในบ้าน" (used his staff to tap and look along
   the floor of the house) until he sensed what was buried. One news report; the staff FINDS, it drives nothing. And the
   lore RITE reads as an ethic ("POINTED, and it is never used to hit") gives its own reason [RITE 16]: never strike
   anyone with it "เพราะจะทำให้ผู้ถูกตีถึงกับเสียจริต (เป็นบ้า) รักษาไม่หาย" (because the one struck would lose his mind,
   past curing). Take from it, at most, that the staff never touches a person.
10. **Smell: "Thai, Malay and Chinese belief alike" is too wide** (PRAI §4.3; the plan's §2 item 7). Thai stands (two
    lifestyle pages and ORST's สาง, one headword for "ghost" and "the stench of a corpse"). Malay rests on one sentence,
    and it is the outlet's own introduction, not the lecturer's words. Chinese has nothing on point: a paper on incense
    in China of the third to sixth centuries, and an abstract. Say "Thai belief", and name the pontianak if a Singapore
    echo is wanted. Two things the same pages ADD: the Thai page that says "do not remark on it" also says to look for
    an ordinary source first ("ตรวจสอบครัว ถังขยะ ท่อน้ำ": the kitchen, the bin, the drains) and to ask the people
    nearby ("ถามคนที่อยู่ใกล้กัน หากคนอื่นได้กลิ่นเหมือนกัน"): his asking the followers, and an ordinary smell found first,
    both have that page behind them [27].
11. **Three of PRAI's candidate lines say more than their grounds.** Line 1, "Your nose opened before your eyes": in
    the game his sight opened in chapter 4 (the plan's own later line is "Last time it was your sight"); it is true only
    of this one visit. Line 2, "They are not devils. They are the dead, and someone has used them": ORST's "kept for
    use" defines โหงพราย, a headword PRAI itself says not to define, and the scholar's own summary of the phi prai is "a
    terrible phi much feared by the folk for she harms everybody" [7]; that they are the dead, used, and victims, is
    the game's respectful reading and should be put to Chad as that. Line 5, "Each of these was someone once. You
    collected them like stamps": S1·15 does not say each presence was a dead person, and the second sentence rebukes
    the collector, against PRAI's own must-not 6.
12. **Three readings to drop.** (a) RITE §4.3: "maw devada ... The power he works by is the devas'; 'Thep' is the same
    word." Anuman: called so "no doubt to avoid ambiguity of meaning in the word 'phi' which means either a god or an
    evil spirit" [26]. A polite name, nothing about whose power. (b) RITE §5.2: "warn first, and the rule binds people
    too." The Thai is "ต้องตักเตือนหรือต้องขับไล่" (must warn OR must drive out), and the one the rule binds is the healer
    himself [RITE 39]. (c) PRAI READ FIRST 12: AN 5:175 as "S1·15's last sentence in canonical words". It is a parallel
    only, and that text's list of what makes a lay follower an "outcaste" includes giving offerings "outside (of the
    Saṅgha)" first: on a card it would read against the house rule that no practice is shown in a bad light.
13. **PLACES holds almost whole.** The register recount is identical; the plans read as the note says; the
    condominium's court, measured again on the same drawing, comes to about 4.6 to 4.7 by 8.6 to 8.8 m between wall
    faces (the note's 4.9 × 9.1 is to wall lines: inside its ± 10 %). Four corrections: its [56] is not "a Thai master"
    (the page is a Singapore samnak's own notice for its resident master, who by the same page learned Thai as a second
    language; it is a trade page with a price list; "by appointment" is not in it); "consultations that run past
    midnight" is not in [60] as the page was served to this pass (no hour is given anywhere in it); only ONE real estate
    was found built with entrance courts, so a home modelled closely on that plan is that estate; and **the plan's
    "refuse hopper's alcove" on the walkway has no source**: on every plan read, the chute is drawn at the unit's own
    kitchen.
14. **No passage in the three notes is an instruction for making the oil or for any harmful rite.** One sentence should
    be cut or never voiced: RITE §1.3's saying about how the stick is pointed (its words are not repeated here: taken out of this file when it was merged, as out of the note),
    the only line in the notes that says how an object is believed to kill (§6).

2. *The seven bars of the cage.* The Guide has the number: "Sattappākāra-laṅkatā", "seven ramparts arrayed against
   them" (§0093). They are ramparts, walls, in a sentence about "misfortunes within & without—caused by such things as
   wind or bile". Seven upright bars of one cage is the game's drawing of it, and fair as a drawing. Print no line that
   says the text describes bars.
3. *The fire's ruby.* The colour is the one most often reported of the lights over the Mekong: one description (shared
   word for word by two pages) has "สีแดงอมชมพูออกสีบานเย็น หรือสีแดงทับทิม" (red-pink toward magenta, or ruby red), and
   one survey sorts the reports into eighteen colours, red-pink, red, pink and green the commonest at 24.1, 20.7, 15.9
   and 10.3 per cent (R6). That belief gives the LIGHTS their colour. Giving it to the stick's fire is the game's choice
   (R7), and a good one; no line should say the belief does.
4. *"The word phrai's second meaning (a sparkle on water) as the reason for the nine's sheen."* ORST's senses are two:
   "ต่อมนํ้าเล็ก ๆ ที่ผุดกระจายขึ้นจากนํ้า" (small bubbles rising scattered from water) and "แวววาว, พราว, พร้อย"
   (glittering, sparkling); and พรายทะเล and พรายน้ำ are glows. "A sparkle on water" runs two senses together;
   "sparkling" alone is exact. A pale sheen is fair as a picture. It is a play on the word, so nothing on screen should
   explain it; and "of black oil" is the game's own (PRAI found no dark shape for a phrai: P41).

**Its §2 ("Research: what it settled"), item by item.**
1. *Gather and bind; nothing destroyed.* Stands, on the casebook. The canon's likeness is a likeness: at Uruvelā the
   nāga is shown in an open bowl, unharmed, not sealed away (R2).
2. *"The nine are the dead, used by someone; not demons. The Royal Institute's dictionary: a spirit 'someone has

   defines โหงพราย, not พราย; no source says S1·14's nine were bound, or were victims. It is the game's respectful
   reading: say it to Chad as that, and let no line state it as fact.
3. *The Naga protects; the fire is ruby; gold is Pho's.* Stands (R4, R6), with R7.
4. *"My own Lersi came in" has a documented parallel in every part.* Weaker (R21): its parts, not the whole.

**Six places where the beats go past what any note supports.**
1. **The dead planter (§4.2, §4.3).** "along the left wall a long planter in which every plant is dead and brown.
