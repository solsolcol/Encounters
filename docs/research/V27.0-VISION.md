# v27.0 · R-D · Spirit Vision: how games do it, and how this engine does it

For episode 4 chapter 4 (docs/V27.0-E4C4-PLAN.md §0: Chad's description is the specification). This note is the craft:
what the familiar effect is, the techniques behind each part, what was chosen for three r185 on a phone, and what was
measured on the first light.

## 1 · The effect he names ("common in video games")

| Game | What it does | What we take |
|---|---|---|
| Death Stranding (2019), the Odradek scan | a ring of light runs out over the ground from the player; terrain is outlined as it passes; hazards marked | the WAVE in world space from his eyes; lines traced at its front |
| No Man's Sky, the scanner | a sphere expanding from the player; everything it crosses outlined; things of interest tagged | the outline at the front, the distance it reaches |
| Batman: Arkham, Detective Mode | the world re-coloured (blue, x-ray); people as glowing skeletons, seen through walls | a different look for the whole world; entities as glowing figures, faint through walls |
| The Witcher 3, Witcher Senses | the screen drained, a ripple out from Geralt when it opens; clues glow red | the ripple on opening; colour that MEANS something |
| Hitman, Instinct; Dishonored, Dark Vision | the world muted; targets and threats as silhouettes through walls | entities as silhouettes; the cost of looking |

Chad's own additions, which no single game has together: the colours of the world INVERTED (a negative), a slight
blur as the camera moves, a slight vignette, its own sound, ambience and music, the heart rising, and the wave RETURNING
to him when it closes.

## 2 · The techniques (each part, and the choice made)

1. **The wave** is a radius R(t) in metres from his eyes. Every pixel's distance comes from the DEPTH buffer: the depth
   becomes a view position through the inverse projection, then a world position through the camera's matrix, and the
   distance to the eye is compared with R. Behind the front the pixel has the vision's look; ahead of it the world's.
   The sky (depth at the far plane) is given a fixed distance (70 m), so the windows turn last.
   R runs out on the curve 0.30u + 0.70u³ over 1.3 s (slow through the room, so he SEES it cross the room, fast to the
   far windows) and home on the same curve backwards over 1.0 s (fast far away, slowing as it reaches him).
2. **The lines** are the depth's own edges: its slope (a silhouette: one surface behind another) and its bend (a
   corner, a crease: the Laplacian of linear depth), both relative to the depth there, plus a little of the colour's
   own edges (a pattern on cloth, the frame of a picture). At the front they burn (2.8 times their strength in the front's
   colour); behind it they stay faint (a sixth), so the room keeps its drawing while the vision is open.
3. **The inversion** is done after the tone mapping, on the colours as they are shown (1 − c), which is what "inverted"
   means to an eye. The world is drawn into a half-float target (light before tone mapping), and the pass tone-maps and
   encodes it with three's own functions (`toneMapping()`, `linearToOutputTexel()`, given to a ShaderMaterial drawn to
   the screen), so what the wave has not reached looks as it does without the vision.
4. **The blur** is the camera's own motion: each pixel's world point is carried through LAST frame's view-projection to
   where it was on the screen; the difference is the pixel's motion, scaled (0.45: "slight"), clamped (3.5 % of the
   screen) and sampled seven times along. Only the vision's side of the wave is blurred; his hands, which move with
   the lens, are not.
5. **The vignette** darkens the corners by up to 42 % of the vision's openness (k, the first six metres of R).
6. **The spirits** are meshes on their own layer (9), never drawn by the world's pass. A spirit's material is three's
   normal material underneath (so a skinned body bends as it does) with its output replaced: a rim of light where the
   surface turns away (1 − |n·z|)^2.4, a faint fill, a small flicker, revealed where the wave has passed (its own world
   position against R), and faint (0.3) where the world's depth stands in front of it, so a spirit behind a wall is
   still seen, as a shape through the wall. They are drawn additively into a half-size target whose smaller levels
   (mipmaps) give the glow: the pass adds the spirit, then the same at levels 2.5 and 4 for the halo.
7. **His hands** are drawn into their own target (colour and coverage), so they turn with the world as the wave
   leaves him (at 0.45 m) and are laid over everything.
8. **The rest of the engine** is untouched when the vision is shut: the world is drawn straight to the screen as
   before, the moment the wave is home.

## 3 · Costs (on three r185, WebGL2)

- Targets at the drawing buffer's size: the world (RGBA16F, 4× multisample, a 24-bit depth texture resolved by
  three's blit), his hands (RGBA16F, 4×), the spirits (RGBA16F, half size, mipmapped). At a 390 × 844 phone drawn at
  two pixels a point that is 1.3 megapixels: about 10.5 MB per full target before multisampling.
- The pass reads about twenty texels a pixel (the depth five times, the colour up to twelve, the spirits three, his
  hands one). On an Adreno 750 (his phone) this is a small part of a frame; on a weak phone the blur's seven taps are
  the first thing to trim.
- Half floats need EXT_color_buffer_float (or _half_float) to be drawn into; without it the targets fall back to bytes
  (light banded in the dark before the tone mapping). `__enc.vision()` reports which.

  runs), its wash cut from 0.18 to 0.05; a spirit's fill 0.22 → 0.10, its halo halved; the vignette 0.30 → 0.42.

## 5 · Sources

1. three.js r185 source, node_modules/three: WebGLTextures.updateMultisampleRenderTarget (depth resolved by blit when
   `resolveDepthBuffer`), WebGLProgram (toneMapping() and linearToOutputTexel() given to a ShaderMaterial drawn to the
   screen), ShaderChunk tonemapping_pars_fragment (ACES Filmic) and meshnormal.glsl.js (the normal material's output).
2. The engine's own passes it sits beside: main.js `smearPass` (a full-screen pass over the drawn frame), `kitPulseSet`
   (the heart), `loopVol` (beds).
3. Depth-based edge detection and world-position reconstruction from depth are standard real-time techniques (e.g.
   the three.js examples' SSAO and outline passes reconstruct view position from depth the same way).
