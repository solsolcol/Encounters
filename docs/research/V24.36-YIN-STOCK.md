# v24.36 · The samnak's cabinets: the yin pieces, from real photographs

Chad, 2 Nov 2026 (docs/V24.32-E4C1-NOTES.md, ask 6): "in the ajahn samnak, the amulet display shelf can have real
amulets instead of generated ones, but it should look more like the yin, barang, ghost based kind of amulets. But base
the model generation off real yin amulets when you generate more models."

Each piece below was found as photographs of REAL pieces first, the reference picture on white was made FROM those
photographs (Higgsfield, GPT Image 2.5, 2k; masters/v24.36/refs/), looked at against them, and only then modelled (Meshy
image-to-3D and its remesh; masters/v24.36/make.sh). Supernatural claims are popular belief, said as such; nothing of
the trade (no prices, no promises). The dealers' photographs are reference only, never shipped. They live in the
session's scratch folder and are not kept in the repo. Their pages are listed here.

## Kuman Thong (กุมารทอง), e4kuman
- **What it is, in popular belief:** a figure that houses a child's spirit, kept at home and cared for like a child
  of the family.
- **Sources (English):** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuman_thong ("a household deity of Thai folk religion");
  https://thailandfoundation.or.th/?p=2409.
- **Sources (Thai):** https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/กุมารทอง ("เด็กไว้จุก นุ่งโจงกระเบนอย่างโบราณ"). Its old recipe
  ends "ลงรักปิดทอง", lacquered and gilded.
- **The form modelled:** the classic seated boy. He sits cross-legged on a low oval red-lacquered base, palms joined
  at the chest, with a topknot and beaded chains. The gold is worn through to the dark lacquer on the high points.
- **Photographs:**
  - ENNXO listing 1208788: https://www.ennxo.com/product/1208788 (front and sides).
  - Museum of International Folk Art FA.2015.56.1, c. 1935, Nakhon Pathom, chalkware, 4.2 cm:
    https://collection.moifa.org/objects/82673/kumanthong-the-lucky-golden-boy
- **The reference picture:** refs/kuman.jpg, made from the ENNXO photographs, which show the generic classic form.
  The modelled piece carries no maker's inscription.

## Nam Man Phrai (น้ำมันพราย), e4phraioil
- **What it is, in popular belief:** an oil carrying the power of a phrai, a spirit of the dead. Older English writing
  calls it a love philtre.
- **Sources (English):** F. H. Giles, "About a Love Philtre, Known to the Siamese as Nam Man Prai – Spirit Oil",
  Journal of the Siam Society 30(1), 1938, pp. 25–28:
  https://thesiamsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/JSS_030_1d_Giles_AboutALovePhiltre.pdf. Giles describes the
  vessel as "closed and sealed with a piece of cloth on which talismanic signs have been inscribed".
- **Sources (Thai):** https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/น้ำมันพราย, which cites the Royal Institute's dictionary.
- **The form modelled:** a small straight-sided clear phial, about 5 cm, of golden-yellow oil. Inside are a cord-bound
  takrut and a small carved figure. The cap is covered with saffron cloth tied with red cord, the hand-finished closure
  real pieces show. No wax seal: no real photograph shows one.
- **Photographs (Thailand Amulets / thailandamulet.net / buddhamagic.net):**
  - https://thailand-amulets.ecwid.com/Nam-Man-Prai-Fang-Takrut-Pra-Jao-Yim-Pasom-Pong-Bpathamang-Chiang-Tung-Corpse-Cord-Wrapped-Ajarn-Gai-Paya-Yant-p244989749
  - https://thailand-amulets.ecwid.com/Kumarn-Tong-Mai-Mayom-Dtaay-Prai-Chae-Nam-Man-Prai-Aathan-Waet-Takrut-Hua-Jai-Choke-Lap-Pokasap-Pra-Ajarn-Supasit-2555-BE-p495006456
  - https://www.buddhamagic.net/nam-man-prai/how-is-nam-man-prai-made/

## Phra Ngang Ta Daeng (พระงั่งตาแดง), e4ngang
- **Spelling:** every source spells it พระงั่ง (mai ek).
- **What it is, in popular belief:** a figure charm of Khmer origin, a "low" charm (ของต่ำ) that its keeper must feed.
- **Sources (English):**
  - https://thailex.info/THAILEX/THAILEXENG/LEXICON/Lexicon%20-%20Ngang%20Tah%20Daeng%20Keht%20Khot.htm
  - Penn Museum 85-24-3: https://www.penn.museum/collections/object/44521
- **Sources (Thai):**
  - Ubon Ratchathani University, Journal of Liberal Arts 12(1), 2016, pp. 315–316:
    https://so03.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/jla_ubu/article/view/87638
  - https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/งั่ง
- **The form modelled:** the classic old Ngang Ta Daeng, cast copper, about 8 cm. He sits in meditation with his
  hands in his lap and his torso bare. He has a grimacing grin, big ears, faceted red glass eyes in raised rims, and a
  tall ringed topknot with its tip bent. He sits on a tall block base cut with triangles.
- **Left out on purpose:** the modern variants (gold plating, skulls, four faces, oil cases) and the explicit nude
  forms, which are separate charms.
- **Photographs:**
  - Penn Museum 85-24-3, front and side with scale (non-commercial educational use).
  - Modern Majik, a Thai Ngang collector: https://modernmajik.com/พระงั่งตาแดง/ (reference only).
- **The reference picture:** refs/ngang.jpg.

## Mae Hong Phrai (แม่โหงพราย), e4phraitab
- **What it is, in popular belief:** the ghost of a woman who died a violent death, usually together with her unborn
  child, called into the amulet by a master and shown holding that child.
- **Why this form:** among one Thai dealer's 116 female-phrai pieces, the โหงพราย names were the largest single group.
- **Sources (English):**
  - https://beadsofparadisenyc.com/products/new-amulet-65
  - https://www.buddhamagic.net/phi-thai-ghosts/phi-tai-thang-klom/
- **Sources (Thai):**
  - The Royal Institute's dictionary, https://dictionary.orst.go.th/ (พราย: "ผีจำพวกหนึ่ง (มักกล่าวกันว่าเป็นผีผู้หญิงที่ตายทั้งกลม)").
  - https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/ผีตายทั้งกลม
- **The form modelled:** the oval brown-ochre powder tablet of a woman cradling a baby (Pra Ajarn Oe's). It is a real
  variant, though black is the commoner powder. A row of them hangs on each bay's velvet.
- **Photograph:** Thailand Amulets, Mae Hoeng Prai Takian Tong, Pra Ajarn Oe:
  https://thailand-amulets.ecwid.com/Mae-Hoeng-Prai-Takian-Tong-Ongk-Kroo-Pim-A-Nuea-Pong-Prai-Luan-3K-Takrut-1-Solid-Gold-Takrut-4-Ploi-Sek-&-Vishnu-Chakra-Pra-Ajarn-Oe-p510786918

## Nang Tani (นางตานี), e4tani
- **What it is, in popular belief:** the female spirit of the wild banana tree.
- **The form modelled:** a small painted figure of a young woman. She wears a green sabai and a green pha sin, has a
  flower behind her ear, holds bananas and stands on a stump base.
- **Photographs:** a Thai seller's (watermarked หงส์ ๙ เซียน), painted figures and powder tablets of her.
- **What the record lacks:** the research for this piece was stopped early, at Chad's word ("know when to stop"), so
  the seller's page URLs and a Thai/English source pair were not written down.

## Two more tablets, for the shelves' variety (deploy 3), e4praicase and e4tanitab
Chad, after the first deploy: "Cant you improve the diversity of the amulets in the shelf? Why so many rounded
amulets?" Two more tablet kinds were modelled the same way, so no row on a bay's velvet repeats one shape.
- **e4praicase:** a glossy black, oil-soaked phrai powder tablet with a worn relief of a crouching figure, in a clear
  bell-shaped case with a rim and a ring. The black powder is the commoner Mae Hong Phrai variant (above). The reference
  picture (refs/praicase.jpg) was made from one photograph in the Mae Hong Phrai researcher's set. The picture service
  refused that set's other two photographs as unsafe, so this cased tablet was modelled from the one it accepted.
  The page that photograph came from was not written down when the research was stopped.
- **e4tanitab:** a pale sage-green powder tablet of Nang Tani standing in a long wrap dress, hands together, worn
  relief. The reference picture (refs/tanitab.jpg) was made from two of the Thai seller's photographs of Nang Tani
  powder tablets (the seller under Nang Tani, above; the same gap in the record).
