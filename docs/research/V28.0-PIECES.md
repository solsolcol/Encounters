# v28.0 · Episode 4 chapter 5 · the collector's three pieces, from photographs of real ones

Plan 4.7 and 9.4: of the collector's "about twenty", THREE are looked at in Spirit Vision, each with a presence on it
(the case file: "some angry, some hungry, some simply looking for energy"). The plan names no kind for any of them, so
three were chosen here. Each was found first as photographs of REAL pieces of its kind; the reference picture on white
(GPT Image 2.6 through Higgsfield, 2k) was made with those photographs as its references and checked against them;
then Meshy image-to-3D at the recipe's best (masters/v28.0/props/RECORD.md has the jobs, the sizes and the faults).
Supernatural claims below are POPULAR BELIEF, said as such. Nothing of the trade is carried (no price, no maker, no
temple, no lottery, no wish). The sellers' photographs were reference only: they lived in the session's scratch folder
and are not in the repository.

## Why these three

- **Not the game's own kinds again.** Already in the game: kuman thong, nam man phrai, Phra Ngang Ta Daeng, the Mae
  Hong Phrai tablet (brown and black), Nang Tani (figure and tablet) (episode 4 chapter 1, docs/research/V24.36-YIN-STOCK.md;
  these seven are the plan's declared reuse on the collector's cloth) and the young woman's locket of episode 3 chapter 4
  (`yinamulet`, Chad's). So: no child figure on a lacquer base, no woman, no oval tablet, no cased tablet.
- **"Yin" is the buyers' word, not a Thai category** (docs/research/V28.0-PRAI.md §3.1: the Chinese-language market's
  陰牌 "compresses and flattens a wide array of Thai magical rituals into a single category, with some never having
  anything to do with Buddhist amulets to begin with"). The three are folk charms of saiyasat (ไสยศาสตร์), not Buddhist
  images, which is exactly what such a collector's case holds under that word. None of the three is a sacred image of
  the Buddha, a monk or a deity.
- **Three different silhouettes on one cloth:** an animal (cast metal), a bottle (glass, oil, two small carved
  figures), an effigy (black lacquer over bound reed).
- **Which presence sits on which piece is THE GAME'S OWN PICTURE**, never a claim of belief (docs/research/V28.0-VERDICTS.md
  item 12: how presences behave is the game's picture drawn from the casebook's word "presences"). The pairing is chosen
  only so that each picture reads on its piece: the ANGRY knot on the charging bull, the HUNGRY reaching shape on the
  bottle of two small figures, the pale one LOOKING FOR ENERGY on the effigy. No line, caption or item text may say a
  kind "is" angry, hungry or empty, or that it causes anything. The collector's health is "see a doctor" in every ending
  (plan 4.7), and nothing here changes that.

## 1 · Wua Thanu (วัวธนู), the "arrow bull" · key w5wuathanu · angry

- **What it is, in popular belief:** a charm in the form of a bull or ox, believed sent out "like an arrow from the
  bow" to fight harm and to ward off sorcery and spirits; also kept to guard a house and its herd. A Lanna charm; one of
  the five "most popular" Lanna charms in the source below.
- **Thai source (scholarly):** วิลักษณ์ ศรีป่าซาง, "คง เข้ม ข่าม ขลังเครื่องรางล้านนา" (Lanna Amulet), วารสารมนุษยศาสตร์และ
  สังคมศาสตร์ มทร.ล้านนา 3(1), ม.ค.–มิ.ย. 2558, pp. 13–27:
  https://so05.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/balajhss/article/view/75181 (PDF: .../article/download/75181/60597, read whole).
  Its words: "คำว่า วัวธนู หมายถึง รูปลักษณ์วัวอาคม แล่นไปคล้ายธนูออกจากแหล่ง เพื่อกิจอย่างน้อย 2 ประการ คือ ต่อสู้กับภัยร้ายนานา
  และป้องกันสิ่งชั่วร้ายคุณไสย" (a wua thanu is the form of a bull made potent by spells, which runs out like an arrow from
  its source for at least two tasks: to fight harms of every kind, and to ward off evil things and sorcery); the old
  pieces are of two materials, "เนื้อผงคลุกรัก บางชิ้นลงชาดทอง" (powder mixed with lacquer, some with cinnabar and gold) and
  "ครั่งผสมมวลสารหุ้มโครงลวด" (lac with sacred matter over a wire frame); "หลายขนาด เล็กเท่าปลายนิ้วก้อยสามารถพกติดตัวได้ ตัวเขื่อง
  สำหรับตั้งบูชา" (many sizes: small as a little fingertip, to carry; larger, to set up and venerate); "มีบ้างที่ตาประดับ
  อัญมณี" (some with gem eyes). Two dealers there say hill peoples across the border use such bulls to keep fields
  fertile, keep a herd together and protect a house. ORST has no entry for วัวธนู (queried 21 Nov 2026).
- **English source:** Beads of Paradise, "Kwai Wua Thanu Magic Buffalo Copper Amulet" (a dealer; reference only):
  https://beadsofparadisenyc.com/products/kwai-wua-thanu-magic-buffalo-copper-amulet ("Magic Bulls or Buffalos that have
  the ability to watch over one's house or dwelling ... protective of territory"; "Approx. 1 1/4 x 1 x 2 1/8 in";
  "Cast and hand burnished copper"). Also listed: Museum of International Folk Art, "wuathanu bull arrow amulet"
  (object 82676; its page did not load on 21 Nov 2026, so nothing is taken from it).
- **What makes the kind recognisable:** a small, simply modelled humped bull (the zebu's hump over the shoulders is the
  strongest mark), standing four-square on short tapering legs, the head held LOW with short horns forward, a short tail
  hanging; hand-made and slightly uneven; cast copper or bronze burnished to a warm brown, or black lacquer-powder with
  red and gold. No base.
- **Photographs studied:** the dealer's three (side, front three-quarter, rear three-quarter: a copper bull 5.4 cm long,
  head lowered, hump high); ENNXO listing 1164143, "วัวธนู เครื่องรางโลหะหล่อโบราณ" (https://www.ennxo.com/product/1164143:
  an old cast bull, head lowered, held in a hand, so not sent to the picture service).
- **The form modelled:** the cast copper bull, about 5.4 cm long (the dealer's measure), head lowered as if to charge.
  The lacquer-and-gold form the paper describes was not modelled: no photograph of one was reached.

## 2 · Rak Yom (รักยม), the pair in oil · key w5rakyom · hungry

- **What it is, in popular belief:** a pair of tiny child figures carved from the wood of two trees, kept together;
  believed to bring goodwill and favour. Sellers add that they are kept "like kids", given food and sweets as a pair.
- **Thai source:** the Royal Institute's dictionary (ORST, queried 21 Nov 2026): "รักยม น. ของขลังอย่างหนึ่ง เป็นรูปตุ๊กตาเด็กเล็ก
  ๒ ตัว ทำด้วยไม้รักดอกและไม้มะยม เชื่อว่าทำให้เกิดเมตตามหานิยม" (a kind of charm: two small child dolls made of rak-dok and
  mayom wood, believed to bring goodwill and popularity). https://dictionary.orst.go.th/
- **English sources:** Sinnott 2014, as cited in docs/research/V23.0-KUMANTHONG.md ("two small wooden figures in oil, a
  love charm"); Beads of Paradise, "Rak Yom Twin Kuman Thong Brothers Amulet" (a dealer; reference only):
  https://beadsofparadisenyc.com/products/rak-yom-twin-kuman-thong-brothers-amulet ("Rak Yom are a Kuman Thong Twin
  Brothers, one named Rak and the other, Yom"; "the owner must offer the Two Spirits sets of food or snacks because they
  come as Twins": a SELLER'S claim, the only source here for feeding; it is not stated anywhere on screen).
- **What makes the kind recognisable:** TWO very small, crudely carved figures, side by side, a rounded head with a
  knob of a topknot over a stubby body, one dark (black) and one red, standing in a small stoppered glass bottle of oil.
  (A second form, two figures cast or pressed joined side by side, is in the dealer's photographs.)
- **Photographs studied:** Wikimedia Commons, "File:รักยม.jpg" ("ขวดรักยม", a bottle of rak yom; CC BY 4.0; uploader
  unknown; 2017): https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%A1.jpg (a squat
  apothecary bottle with a yellow stopper, half full of amber oil, one black and one red figure); the dealer's three
  (the joined pair, front, side and back). The reference picture follows the Commons photograph closely (a reference
  only: it ships nowhere, and the model is not that piece).

## 3 · Hun Phayon (หุ่นพยนต์), the bound effigy · key w5hunphayon · looking for energy

- **What it is, in popular belief:** an effigy that a master binds together from materials and enchants so that it is
  "like a living figure"; kept as a guardian of a house and its owner.
- **Thai source:** ORST (queried 21 Nov 2026): "หุ่นพยนต์ [-พะยน] น. รูปที่ผู้ทรงวิทยาคมเอาวัตถุมาผูกขึ้นแล้วเสกเป่าให้เป็นเหมือน
  รูปที่มีชีวิต" (a figure that one learned in magic binds up out of materials and then enchants, by spells and breath,
  to be like a figure with life); and พยนต์: "สิ่งที่ผู้ทรงวิทยาคมปลุกเสกให้มีชีวิตขึ้น เช่น หุ่นพยนต์ (ป. วยนฺต; ส. วฺยนฺต)". (Thai
  Wikipedia's "หุ่นพยนต์" page is about European funeral effigies and was not used.)
- **English source:** Beads of Paradise, "Hoon Phayon Warrior Bodyguard Thai Lacquer Amulet with Sacred Yants" (a dealer;
  reference only): https://beadsofparadisenyc.com/products/amulet-181 ("Hand cut and assembled reed/ bamboo figure
  ritually wrapped with cotton cord ... painted and adorned with Sacred Yants"; "4 x 3 x 1 in"; "can be made from many
  materials such as soil, metal, bamboo, clay and fabric"; "a guardian of the house"). English Wikipedia's "Hoon payont"
  did not resolve on 21 Nov 2026 (its search result only); nothing is taken from it.
- **What makes the kind recognisable:** a crude doll-shaped figure, round head, arms out stiffly, two short legs,
  BOUND (the ridges of the binding show), here coated in glossy black lacquer and marked in white with circles and yant
  script. Other kinds are of straw, leaves or vine.
- **Photographs studied:** the dealer's four (a pair, each figure front and back), on white.
- **The form modelled:** one black lacquered bound figure, about 10 cm tall (the dealer's 4 in). Its white marks in the
  reference picture follow the photograph's yant-like strokes (the prompt asked for abstract marks; the picture made
  letter-like swirls): they are not read, mark no maker, temple or name, and are a few millimetres high on the model.
