## READ FIRST — what the second reader changed

1. **He is an ORDAINED ascetic, not a "lay" one.** ORST: ฤๅษี is "นักบวชพวกหนึ่ง", and นักบวช is one who has undergone
   ordination; the draft's own main paper [2] p. 690: ruesi and monks are both ordained renunciants and differ only in
   their precepts. Say: an ordained ascetic of a ruesi line, outside the Buddhist Sangha — not a bhikkhu.
2. **"Not a monk" holds by DEPICTION, not by the word.** Some monks also take ruesi ordination ([2]; a famous monk is
   known as หลวงพ่อฤๅษีลิงดำ). Lersi Chin is not a monk because Chad's model shows him so (tiger-print cloth, topknot,
   no saffron), so the house rule stands: he says **thank you**, never anumodana.
3. **The chant: the Sakkatvā verses are in A Chanting Guide** (Section 0062, "Abhaya Paritta: The Danger-free
   Protection", in its "Blessings" part), word for word as the draft gives them, with the "your" (te) form and the
   translation. Credit the book as **The Dhammayut Order in the USA** (it never names its translator; CC BY-NC 4.0).
   The standard Thai text is printed by Wimutti and Kalyanamitra (inside the monks' Twelve Tamnan); never caption from
   the blog's non-standard spellings. **The te form is the monks' blessing over a layperson** — close to the anumodana
   rule — so its use by Lersi Chin is a choice for Chad (the plan's Q7): the te form, the self-blessing (me) form, or
   Namo tassa and an uncaptioned katha.
4. **The clearing is "his practice", not "the Lersi rite".** The steps (homage, holy water drunk, bathed or sprinkled
   with mayom, thread tied, advice to make merit) come from different healers in different regions; the one documented
   single sequence is [4]'s. Blowing on the crown (เป่ากระหม่อม) is NOT sourced as a clearing step (ORST gives it for
   being liked and favoured): left out.
5. **Doctors first.** Every source's sign of sorcery is an illness doctors could not explain, and villagers "normally
   begin with a visit to a modern clinic" [4]; Master Z's own S1·07: "see a doctor as well. Spiritual guidance sits
   beside proper care, never in place of it." The examination asks whether he has seen one.
6. **The warning is the casebook's own.** No outside source documents a Thai rule against going back to a master;
   Master Z says it six times (S1·04, S1·05, S1·09, S1·20, S2·06, S3·11 — "You do not need to invite something in just
   to prove that something is there"). Lersi Chin warns; he never denounces the first master (a devotee's own taboo:
   never insult a teacher, your own or another's).
7. **Staging.** One bow (a wai, or a single prostration) to a teacher; three prostrations are for the Buddha. The rite
   is done in the open, others present, the client clothed, touch kept to head, hands and wrist. No Por Kae head or
   mask is ever set low, damaged or treated as evil. His serious take, never the angry one ("he becomes serious").
8. **His Thai name is unknown** — "Chin" can be ชิน, จีน, จิน or ฉิน — so no Thai spelling is printed until Chad or
   Master Z gives it.
9. **A real ruesi with Singapore followers exists** (AFP, Khon Kaen, 2017: white robes, arm tattoos, protective string,
   followers in Hong Kong, Taiwan, China, Malaysia and Singapore; "Now hermits have to live in towns so they can help
   people easily"). Prices in that piece are left out.
10. Smaller corrections (each in the verdicts, §A12 and §C): the MThai "teacher even of the gods" line is about
    Jupiter's deity, not Por Kae; Ta Wua is named by [2] among the best-known ruesi; [23]'s rite was bathing and
    pouring, not sprinkling; [4] sets the Buddha, monk and hermit side by side, not ranked; the "Chonburi 2024" item has
    no source and is dropped.

# Lersi (ฤๅษี) masters, their places and their clearings — research draft

Research for episode 4 chapter 2 (S1·11's second half: the fog, Lersi Chin's clearing, the warning — the trial's 5.2–5.3).
Written 3 Nov 2026. A draft for a second reader to try to refute. Nothing in the game or the plans was changed.

**The brief.** Who a Lersi master is in Thailand today; whether the `lersichin` model's dress matches reality; what his
place holds; how a Thai practitioner examines someone and clears them; what he would chant (with a published
translation); whether "Lersi Chin" is a findable person; Thai practitioners and Singapore; and whether "do not go back"
is a known piece of advice.

**How it was done.**
- Searched in English and Thai (ฤๅษี / ฤาษี, พ่อแก่, ครอบครู, ถอนคุณไสย, ปัดรังควาน, รดน้ำมนต์, เป่ากระหม่อม, สะเดาะเคราะห์,
  ใบมะยม, คาถาบูชาพ่อแก่, ถ้ำฤๅษี, ฤๅษีดัดตน, and others).
- The backbone is the Royal Institute dictionary (ORST [1]) and peer-reviewed papers: six ThaiJO articles in Thai [2][3][6][7][8][38],
  two Siam Society papers [4][11], *Inter-Asia Cultural Studies* [5], *JSEAS* [9] and *Rian Thai* [12]. After those come
  Thai news, then lifestyle and horoscope sites, which are marked as such. ThaiJO PDFs were read in full from their text
  layers, not from summaries.
- Every source listed was opened unless it says **not opened**. Pages that refused or sat behind a check are listed in
  §9. They support nothing here.
- Nothing of the trade is reported: no prices, fees, lottery numbers or wish-granting. Where a source gave an amount, it
  is left out.
- **Thai quotation note:** the PDF text layers of [2] and [6] drop the letter ฤ/ฤๅ and some tone marks. Quotations from
  them are given with those restored, and a restored letter is shown in [square brackets].

**Tags.** **FACT**: documented. **PRACTICE**: documented as how it is done; no efficacy implied. **BELIEF**: popular
belief, true as a report of belief. **UNCERTAIN**: weak, single or conflicting sources. **NOT FOUND**: searched for and
not found.

## 0 · The short answers (for the chapter's design)

- **A Lersi master today** is a lay ascetic in a hermit line, not a monk. He keeps precepts and his own rules. He is a
  healer, a "refuge through spells" and a counsellor, and his altar puts the Buddha above the hermits [2][4][19]. He
  holds a yearly Wai Khru and initiates disciples by placing the hermit's head-mask on them (ครอบครู) [1][2]. Ruesi
  ordination exists as a rite [22]. Lersi Chin is therefore not a monk, and he says *thank you*, not anumodana (CLAUDE.md
  rule; a Singapore abbot: "Arjans are not part of the Buddhist Sangha" [41]).
- **The model's dress.**
  - Consistent: tiger-print cloth over one shoulder, a rosary, a topknot, barefoot.
  - Plausible but thinly sourced: tattoos.
  - Not found in Thai sources: beads wound up the hair, wrist beads, and rudraksha as the bead.
  - The **red forehead mark** reads as Hindu (a red tilak or pottu). The Thai forehead mark is เจิม, dots of scented powder,
    and there is no source for a red mark as a Thai ruesi custom (§2).
  - The model is Chad's and is used as delivered. This is noted only so the scene's words do not explain the mark as
    Thai custom.
- **Examining someone** is documented as homage to the teachers, then questions about the symptoms, the horoscope, and
  meditation, semi-trance or "inner sight" (นั่งทางใน) [4][2][7]. The signs taken as คุณไสย (sorcery) are "illness with no
  cause found", e.g. **เหม่อลอย** (dazed, absent) [7]. That matches the case file's fog. **Eggs, candle-reading and
  water-gazing: NOT FOUND** in a reliable source.
- **A clearing** is documented as homage to the master's teachers, Namo ×3 and a katha, then holy water (drunk, poured
  or sprinkled), blowing a katha onto the person, rubbing with sacred objects and tying cotton thread. It ends with
  advice to **make merit at a monastery** [4][5][6][7][2]. Mayom leaves (seven, bundled) are a known sprinkler [37].
  Efficacy is **BELIEF** throughout.
- **The chant.** Recommended: **Namo tassa ×3, then the *Sakkatvā* verses** ("Having revered the jewel of the Buddha, the
  highest, most excellent medicine … may all obstacles vanish. May your sufferings grow totally calm").
  - Thai lay healers are documented cutting "คาถาสักกัตวา" from the parittas to heal with [6].
  - Thanissaro's *A Chanting Guide* prints it with a translation, and in the second-person form (*te*, "your") said over
    someone else [29].
  - Alternatives are in §5. The game has already used Namo tassa and Itipiso (e3c1, e3c3); Sakkatvā would be new.
- **"Lersi Chin": NOT FOUND** in any public source, Thai or English. The case file is the only source. Same-name hits are
  other people and must not be merged (§6).
- **Singapore.** Thai arjans are reported to visit Singapore regularly "to perform blessings and spiritual tattooing"
  [41]. Singapore
  disciples attended a ruesi Wai Khru in Thailand [22]. Video-call consultations are now common in the trade [42], and the
  casebook itself has one (S1·17). **The case file does not say where Lersi Chin saw him.**
- **"Do not go back to prove it": NOT FOUND** as a documented general rule. The closest documented things are these:
  ลองดี/ลองของ (to test or challenge someone's power, "mostly disdainful or challenging", ORST [1]); healers ending with
  merit-making rather than confrontation [4]; and Dhammapada 5's "Hostilities aren't stilled through hostility" [31].
  The warning itself rests on the trial's own 5.2D/5.3 [47].

## 1 · Who a "Lersi (ruesi) master" is in Thailand today

### 1.1 The word and the figure
- **FACT.** ORST, ฤๅษี: "น. ฤษี, นักบวชพวกหนึ่ง มีมาก่อนพุทธกาล สละบ้านเรือนออกไปบำเพ็ญพรตแสวงหาความสงบ." — "a kind of
  ascetic, existing before the Buddha's time, who leaves home to practise austerities in search of peace." ORST, ฤษี:
  the same, adding "(ส. ฤษี ว่า ผู้เห็น, ผู้แต่งพระเวท; ป. อิสิ)" — "Sanskrit: the seer, the composer of the Veda;
  Pali *isi*." [1]
- **FACT.** Spelling: ORST's headwords are **ฤๅษี** and **ฤษี**. ฤาษี, common in the press, is not an ORST headword [1].
  On-screen Thai should prefer ฤๅษี.
- **FACT.** ORST, ดาบส: "ผู้บำเพ็ญตบะคือการเผากิเลส, ฤษี" — "one who practises *tapas*, the burning-off of defilements; a
  ruesi" [1].
- **FACT.** A ruesi's place has its own word. ORST, อาศรม: "ที่อยู่ของนักพรต" — "an ascetic's dwelling". สำนัก (samnak)
  is "a place of study and training" [1]. Living ruesi name their places อาศรม: อาศรมฤาษีมุนีเทพ [22], อาศรมฤษีจี้กง [23].
- **FACT.** "Hermits have been part of Buddhist practice in Thailand for centuries." This is McDaniel 2013, quoted in
  Cohen 2019, fn 18 [9][10]. Lanna chronicles name hermits as founders of towns, among them Haripunchai (Lamphun) [9][15].

### 1.2 Por Kae (พ่อแก่), the "Old Father"
- **FACT.**
  - In the Thai arts, Por Kae is the ruesi as supreme teacher: "พ่อแก่, พระฤาษี หรือบางครั้งก็เรียกกันว่า ครูฤาษี ถือเป็น
    บรมครูแห่งศาสตร์ของการแสดง" — "Por Kae, Phra Ruesi, sometimes called Khru Ruesi, is held to be the supreme teacher of
    the performing arts" [20].
  - The same page calls him "อาจารย์แห่งสรรพวิชาความรู้ทั้งมวลรวมถึงเป็นอาจารย์ของเหล่าเทพเทวดา" — "teacher of all knowledge,
    even of the gods" (**BELIEF**) [20].
- **FACT.** ORST, ครอบ ๒: "(๑) ประสิทธิ์ประสาทวิชาให้, ถ่ายทอดความรู้ให้ (๒) ทำพิธีรับรองความรู้ เช่น ครูครอบศิษย์ (อย่างครูครอบ
  ศิษย์ด้วยหัวโขน)." — "to confer knowledge; to perform the rite that certifies it, as a teacher 'covers' a pupil (as a
  teacher covers a pupil with the khon head)" [1].
- **FACT.**
  - The royal Wai Khru for khon and lakhon teachers has three parts: a Buddhist ceremony, the homage itself (ไหว้ครู),
    and the "covering" (ครอบ).
  - The officiant dances partly as Phra Bharata Rishi (พระภรตฤษี), the sage behind the rite.
  - Only a senior teacher of the hero (พระ) roles may officiate. [21]
- **UNCERTAIN: which ruesi "Por Kae" is.**
  - A horoscope site names Narada: "ครูของฤาษีทั้งหลายคือ พระฤาษีนารอด หรือ ปู่ฤาษีนารอด" — "the teacher of all ruesi is
    Phra Ruesi Narod (Narada), Grandfather Ruesi Narod" [19].
  - The arts' rite centres on Bharata [21].
  - A seller's blog gives several, each with its art (Narot for music, Ta Fai for herbs) [46].
  - Traditions differ. The game should not fix one identity on Por Kae unless Chad does.

### 1.3 Lersi practitioners today (the closest academic study: five samnaks in Maha Sarakham, 2025)
- **FACT** [2]:
  - The abstract: "[ฤา]ษีเป็นนักบวชที่ถือศีลมีข้อวัตรปฏิบัติข้อห้ามตามวิชาอาคมที่เรียนมา ส่วนหนึ่งพระภิกษุบวช[ฤา]ษีควบคู่กัน" —
    "ruesi are ascetics who keep precepts and observe rules and prohibitions according to the occult knowledge they
    learned; some Buddhist monks also take ruesi ordination alongside."
  - Their roles: "1. เป็นผู้รักษาอาการป่วยทั้งทางกายและจิต 2. เป็นที่พึ่งทางใจของผู้คนด้วยเวทมนต์ และ 3. เป็นนักพัฒนาสังคม" —
    "healers of physical and mental illness; a refuge for people's minds through spells; social developers".
- **FACT.** They act as folk healers for people who come "ให้ทำการรักษาหรือปรึกษาปัญหาทางเศรษฐกิจและปัญหาสังคม โดยท่านใช้
  วิธีการรักษาแบบธรรมชาติ ไสยศาสตร์ และพุทธศาสตร์รวมกัน" — "for treatment or to consult on economic and social problems,
  using natural, occult and Buddhist methods together" [2]. A troubled shop owner coming to a ruesi for counsel fits this.
- **FACT.** The samnak heads hold "พิธีไหว้ครู ขอขมาครู และพิธีครอบครูด้วยเศียรปู่[ฤา]ษีให้แก่ลูกศิษย์ทุกปี" — "a Wai Khru, a
  rite asking the teachers' forgiveness, and a 'covering' with the head of Grandfather Ruesi for their disciples, every
  year" [2].
- **FACT.** They make and consecrate (ปลุกเสก) amulets and sacred items in their samnak's name [2]. No trade detail is
  given here.
- **FACT.** Ruesi once lived in forest or cave. These have adapted by "การเป็นอยู่และตั้งสำนักอยู่ในชุมชน" — "living and
  keeping their samnak in the community" [2].
- **FACT.** The study records a gathering of ruesi "จากทั่วทุกภาคของประเทศไทยและจากต่างประเทศ" ("from every region of
  Thailand and from abroad") at Maha Sarakham on 18 Aug 2019 [2].
- **FACT.** On 30 Apr 2014, at อาศรมฤาษีมุนีเทพ in Suphanburi, Posttoday reported [22]:
  - the "first Ruesi Wai Khru" in Thailand, with more than 400 ruesi and disciples from Thailand, India, Laos, Cambodia,
    **Singapore**, Hong Kong, Taiwan and China;
  - the installation of ฤาษีมุนีเทพ (อาจารย์ทรัพย์) as successor of the ruesi ordination line;
  - a "พิธีบวชฤาษีตามแบบโบราณ" (traditional ruesi ordination).
- **FACT.** Living practitioners are titled "ฤๅษี + name": ฤาษีมุนีเทพ [22], ฤาษีจี้กง (Ajarn Chu) [23]. "Lersi Chin"
  (ฤๅษีชิน) follows a real naming pattern.
- **PRACTICE.** A 2021 Daily News visit (a sceptical "real or fake?" feature) found Ajarn Chu of อาศรมฤษีจี้กง, Nakhon
  Ratchasima, giving a young woman a holy-water rite (รดน้ำมนต์), with villagers coming for it all day [23].

### 1.4 Are they Buddhist?
- **FACT.**
  - At all five Isan samnaks the Buddha image is the principal image on the same altar, placed higher: "ที่ถือหลักทาง
    พระพุทธศาสนาเป็นหลัก ลัทธิ[ฤา]ษีเป็นรอง" — "Buddhism first, the ruesi cult second".
  - Their rites are mixed. "มีการไหว้พระและการเจริญพระพุทธมนต์โดยพระสงฆ์ก่อนที่จะทำพิธี" — monks chant first, then the ruesi
    rite.
  - The samnak heads "ต้องยึดหลักคำสอนของพระพุทธศาสนาตามคติท้องถิ่น" — "must hold to the Buddha's teaching, as locally
    understood". [2]
- **FACT.** A northern Thai healer whose teacher is a (Burmese) hermit keeps "a Buddha image and a picture of a holy monk
  … on the same shrine as an image of the hermit", and heals in front of all three [4].
- **BELIEF / PRACTICE.** "ห้ามวางพ่อแก่ในที่สูงกว่าหรือเสมอกับพระพุทธรูป" — "never place Por Kae higher than, or level with,
  a Buddha image" [19].
- **FACT.** A lay *arjan* is not a monk. The abbot of a Thai temple in Singapore: "Arjans are not part of the Buddhist
  Sangha" [41].

### 1.5 Mediumship of Por Kae, or the master in his own person
- **FACT.** Among the Isan ruesi-healers' roles: "หมอธรรมก็เป็นร่างทรง โดยใช้วิธีเข้าทรงพ่อแก่" — "the healer is also a medium,
  through possession by Por Kae" [2]. ORST, เข้าทรง: "อาการที่เจ้าเข้าสิงในตัวคนทรง" — "a spirit-lord entering the medium's
  body" [1].
- **FACT.** Urban mediums in Bangkok and Nonthaburi channel Hindu gods, national heroes, and lower deities "ได้แก่ ฤาษี
  กุมารทอง เจ้าแม่ต่าง ๆ" ("such as ruesi, kumanthong, various goddesses"). Clients come to have problems solved and
  "ปัดเป่า" (misfortune brushed away) [8].
- **FACT.** In a sample of 15 folk healers in Kamphaeng Phet, only one used possession, and with a different spirit.
  The rest used questioning, examination, horoscope and "พลังสมาธิ" (meditative power) [7].
- **FACT.** Northern Thai male healers reach their teachers "through meditation or a semi-trance like condition (khao
  chan)". Full trance is "exclusively in the female sphere" [4].
- **NOT FOUND:** any figure for how common Por Kae mediumship is.
- *For the scene:* the case file says only that Lersi Chin "examined the situation according to his practice" [47]. No
  trance is stated. The casebook elsewhere has Master Z's own Lersi "come in" (S1·14; S2·02: "the posture and hand
  movements of a Lersi") [47]. That is the casebook's belief, not this chapter's.

The evidence behind each row:

- **Tiger skin.**
  - **FACT (iconography).** The reusi "is depicted wearing tiger fur and often with a long beard" [17].
  - **BELIEF (lore).** "พระฤาษีที่บวชตามป่าตามเขา ก็จะใช้หนังเสือมาเป็นเครื่องนุ่งห่ม" — "hermits ordained in forests and hills use
    tiger skin as clothing". Tigers are believed "ตบะเก่งกล้า มีฤทธิ์เดชมาก สามารถปราบผีหรือสิ่งเลวร้ายของชีวิตได้" — "of strong
    tapas and great power, able to subdue ghosts and life's evils" [18].
- **What practitioners wear today.**
  - **FACT.** Each Isan samnak "นิยมแต่งกายด้วยผ้า ที่ทำจากผ้าฝ้ายย้อมด้วยสีที่แตกต่างกันไป เช่น สีพระราชนิยม สีกรักแดง กรักน้ำตาล
    สีกรมท่า ผ้าฝ้ายขิดลายขอ ผ้าฝ้ายลายเสือ และมีอุปกรณ์อื่น ๆ … คือมีห้อยลูกประคำ ถือไม้เท้า ผ้าเบี่ยงบ่าหรือผ้าคลุมไหล่".
  - In English: "dresses in cotton dyed in various colours — the royal colour, red-brown, brown, navy, khit-patterned
    cotton, **tiger-patterned cotton** — with a hanging rosary, a staff, and a cloth slung over one shoulder or covering
    the shoulders" [2].
  - **FACT.** During the Jatukam Ramathep craze, "มีหลายบุคคลนุ่งผ้าลายเสือเข้าพิธีปลุกเสกวัตถุมงคล" — "many people wore
    tiger-patterned cloth to sit in amulet consecrations" [2].
- **The classical image (Wat Pho, Rama III).**

- **The rosary.**

    like a dish-cover with a long tapering spire; from Pali/Sanskrit *jaṭā*, 'hair twisted and piled up high'". ORST, มวย ๒:
    hair coiled into a knot [1].
  - The Wat Pho hermits "มีการรวบเส้นผมเกล้าเป็นมวยแล้วสวมหมวกนักบวช ทรงดอกลำโพง" — "gather the hair up into a topknot and put
    on the ascetic's hat shaped like a trumpet-flower" (also called "หมวกผ้าขาวปก") [3].
  - Beads wound up the hair: **NOT FOUND**.
- **The forehead mark.**
  - **FACT.** ORST, เจิม ๑: "เอาแป้งหอมแต้มเป็นจุด ๆ ที่หน้าผากหรือสิ่งที่ต้องการให้มีสิริมงคล" — "to dab scented powder in dots
    on the forehead, or on a thing, for auspiciousness" [1]. ORST gives no colour.
  - **FACT.** Thai worshippers at Bangkok's Sri Maha Mariamman (a Tamil Hindu temple) leave "with red pottu (tilak) on their
    foreheads" [28].
  - **FACT (iconography).** Ruesi Ta Fai is shown with "a third eye on his forehead", linked by some to Shiva [17].
  - **UNCERTAIN.** No source shows a red forehead mark as a Thai ruesi custom. It reads as Hindu (Shaiva) influence. This
    is plausible for a Hindu-leaning practitioner, but unconfirmed.
- **The beard.** Long (white) in iconography [17]; the Rama III statues are "ไม่มีหนวด", youthful [3]. A goatee is
  **UNCERTAIN**: neither confirmed nor contradicted.
- **The tattoos.**
  - **FACT.** "ยันต์พ่อแก่ฤาษี" is named among Luang Phor Poen's Sak Yant designs [24].
  - Sak Yant wearers attend a Wai Khru before and after, and vow the five precepts [26]. Participants in Luang Phor

  - Thai arjans tattoo in Singapore [41].
  - One documented ruesi (Ajarn Chu) has full-body tattoos, though he says he does not tattoo others [23].
  - **UNCERTAIN** as a general ruesi trait.
- **Barefoot.** **FACT.** "the footwear should always be removed" in homes; "it is required to remove the shoes before
  entering the chapel where Buddha images are housed" [27].

## 4 · "Examining a situation" and "a clearing"

### 4.1 The Thai words (ORST [1] unless noted)
- **FACT.** คุณไสย: "อาถรรพณ์ คือ พิธีทำร้ายต่ออมิตร โดยเสกสิ่งใดสิ่งหนึ่งเข้าในตัวหรือฝังรูปฝังรอย เรียกกันว่า กระทำคุณไสย,
  ผู้ถูกกระทำ เรียกว่า ถูกคุณไสย" — "sorcery: a rite to harm an enemy by magically sending something into the body, or by
  burying an image or trace; the one it is done to is ถูกคุณไสย, 'hit by sorcery'." This is the case file's "something
  negative had been placed on me" in Thai terms (**BELIEF**).
- **FACT.** Sending harm: เสก includes "ร่ายมนตร์เพื่อให้เกิดพลังส่งสิ่งใดสิ่งหนึ่งไปทำร้ายศัตรู เช่น เสกหนังเข้าท้อง" — "to cast a
  spell that sends something to harm an enemy, e.g. a piece of hide into the belly".
- **FACT.** Clearing a spirit or misfortune:
  - ปัดรังควาน: "ทำพิธีขับไล่ผี, โดยปริยายหมายถึง ขับไล่อัปรีย์จัญไรให้พ้นไป" — "a rite to drive out spirits; figuratively, to
    drive misfortune away".
  - ไล่ผี: "ทำพิธีร่ายมนตร์ขับผีที่เชื่อว่าสิงอยู่ในคนหรือสถานที่" — "to drive out by spell a spirit believed to possess a
    person or place".
  - สะเดาะ ๒: "ทำให้หมดสิ้นไปหรือเบาบางลง เช่น ทำบุญสะเดาะเคราะห์" — "to make it end or lessen, as in making merit to ease
    ill-fortune" (whence สะเดาะเคราะห์).
- **FACT.** The holy-water words:
  - น้ำมนต์: "น้ำที่เข้าพิธีทางพระพุทธศาสนาเพื่อให้อาบ กิน หรือประพรม … ถือกันว่าเป็นมงคล" — "water that has been through a
    Buddhist rite, for bathing, drinking or sprinkling; held to be auspicious".
  - ประพรม: "ประโปรยด้วยสิ่งที่มีลักษณะเป็นน้ำ เช่น ประพรมน้ำมนต์" — to sprinkle.
  - สิญจน์ (2): "รดน้ำมนต์" — to pour holy water.
  - สายสิญจน์: "ด้ายดิบสีขาวที่นำมาจับทบเป็น ๓ เส้น หรือ ๙ เส้น ใช้ในพิธีทางศาสนา" — "white raw cotton thread doubled into 3 or 9
    strands, used in religious rites".
- **FACT.** เป่ากระหม่อม: "ร่ายมนตร์คาถาแล้วเป่าลงกลางศีรษะเพื่อให้เกิดเมตตามหานิยมเป็นต้น" — "to recite a mantra, then blow
  onto the middle of the head, for being liked and favoured, *and so on*" (the "and so on" is the dictionary's; the
  purpose is not limited).
- **FACT.** "ถอนคุณไสย / แก้ของ / ถอนของ" are not ORST headwords. Scholars write *thon tu / thon khut* ("removing black
  magic / calamity") [4] and *rot nam mon* [5].

### 4.2 How a practitioner examines
- **PRACTICE.** In Kamphaeng Phet, every healer asks the patient to tell the symptoms ("การซักประวัติ"), and looks and
  touches where needed. Some check the horoscope "เพื่อดูเคราะห์ของคนไข้" (to see whether ill-fortune causes the illness).
  Some use "พลังสมาธิ หรือเข้าทรง" (meditative power or possession) to divine [7].
- **PRACTICE.** "Most folk healers begin with diagnosis rituals", meaning homage to the masters and fortune-telling,
  which "serves as a spiritual etiology before the healers will know what healing methods should be taken" [4].
  - A healer who specialises in removing black magic concluded "through diagnosis" that a client's trouble was "an attack
    of sorcery or black magic (tu or khun khon)".
  - His removal "starting with going into a trance-like state or meditating" [4].
- **PRACTICE.** In Isan, one class of healer gives "คำตอบเกี่ยวกับสาเหตุของความเจ็บป่วยด้วยการนั่งทางใน และให้การรักษาหลังจากรู้
  สาเหตุ" — "answers on the cause of the illness by sitting in inner sight (นั่งทางใน), and treats once the cause is known"
  [2].
  - ORST, ทางใน: "การหยั่งรู้ด้วยพลังจิต, โดยปริยายหมายถึงการนึกเดาเอาเอง" — "insight by mental power; figuratively, guessing
    for oneself" [1].
  - That fits the case file's "whether you interpret that spiritually or otherwise" and the trial's "seeing something is
    not the same as understanding it".
- **PRACTICE / BELIEF.** The signs read as sorcery: "การรักษาคุณไสย หมอจะสังเกตอาการของผู้ป่วยที่โดนคุณไสย (มีการเจ็บป่วยหา
  สาเหตุไม่ได้) เช่น เหม่อลอย พูดพร่ำคนเดียว … ซึ่งได้รับรักษาด้วยการแพทย์ต่าง ๆ แล้วไม่หาย" [7]. In English: "to treat
  sorcery, the healer observes the symptoms — illness with no cause found, such as being dazed and absent, talking to
  oneself — that medicine has not cured".
  - A horoscope site lists the same: "เหม่อลอย ไม่มีสมาธิ ขี้หลงขี้ลืม" (dazed, unfocused, forgetful), and "เมื่อไปหาหมอแล้วไม่
    สามารถหาสาเหตุของอาการเหล่านี้ได้" (no cause found by doctors) (**BELIEF**, lifestyle) [36].
  - This is the case file's fog and lost wallet.
- **NOT FOUND:** eggs rolled on the body, candle-reading or gazing into a bowl of water as documented diagnostic methods.
  Only unverifiable forum and news mentions turned up.

### 4.3 The clearing — a documented sequence (each step from its own source)
1. **Homage to the master's teachers.**
   - **PRACTICE.** Before any rite, healers ask clients to pay homage (*yok khan tang*) to the masters, "compelling the
     clients to accept … their ritual and moral authority" [4].
   - "ทุกท่าน ก่อนทำการรักษาผู้ป่วย จะต้องทำพิธีกรรมไหว้ครู" — every healer performs a Wai Khru before treating, "เพื่อเคารพครู
     บาอาจารย์และสร้างขวัญกำลังใจให้กับผู้ป่วย" (to honour the teachers and give the patient heart) [7].
   - Kanchanaburi healers set a teacher-tray, then "พนมมือบอกกล่าวครู" (join palms and tell the teacher) [6].
2. **Namo ×3, then a katha.** **PRACTICE.** Treatment begins "ด้วยการตั้งนะโม 3 จบ ท่องคาถา แล้วลงมือรักษา" — "with Namo
   three times, the katha recited, then the treatment" [6].
3. **Holy water.**
   - **PRACTICE.** For sorcery, "หมอจะทำน้ำมนต์ และบริกรรมคาถา ให้คนไข้ดื่ม" — "the healer makes holy water, reciting a katha,
     and has the patient drink it" [7].
   - *Rot nam mon* is "bathing or spraying sacralized holy water to ward off bad luck and provide protection … from bad
     spirits" [5].
   - A ruesi pouring and sprinkling holy water on a client [23].
4. **Blowing.**
   - **PRACTICE.** *Pao katha*: "blowing incantations (in which the practitioner blows on the person being treated)" [4].
   - The crown is the target in เป่ากระหม่อม [1].
   - The head is "the highest and, thus, most sacred point of the body" [27]. Only a respected practitioner blows there.
5. **Rubbing with sacred objects; tying thread.**
   - **PRACTICE.** The black-magic removal goes on to "blessing with holy water, rubbing with sacred objects" [4].
   - Isan healers "ผูกฝ้ายและรดน้ำมนต์ให้" (tie cotton thread and pour holy water) for those believed harmed by spirits [2].
6. **Merit.** **PRACTICE.** The same removal ends with "finally a suggestion to make merit at a Buddhist monastery" [4].
   Another healer "asked them to make merit (tham bun) to Buddhist monasteries" [4].
- **The materials.**
  - **PRACTICE / BELIEF.** Mayom, "ใช้ในพิธีปัดรังควาน และใช้สำหรับพระสงฆ์ประพรมน้ำมนต์แทนหญ้าคาโดยนิยมใช้ใบมะยม (ทั้งก้าน) 7
    ใบ มัดรวมกัน" — "used in rites driving out misfortune, and by monks to sprinkle holy water instead of cogon grass: seven
    mayom leaves, stems on, bound together" [37].
  - **PRACTICE / BELIEF.** Som poi (Lanna): "เชื่อในอำนาจของส้มป่อยที่มีสรรพคุณช่วยปลดปล่อยสิ่งเลวร้าย แก้คุณไสย" — "believed to
    release evil things and undo sorcery" [38].
  - Lime (มะนาว) in a clearing: **NOT FOUND**.
- **The client's posture.**
  - **FACT.** Before "revered parents, teacher or mentor", the *mop krap*: one "kneels before the revered person with
    raised hands … in the shape of a 'budding lotus'", lowers them to the floor "and bows with the forehead resting on the
    'budding lotus' hands at the thumbs" [11].
  - **FACT.** It is rude "to point one's feet, especially the sole, at other people, or revered objects like Buddha
    images" [27].
  - Kneeling, head bowed and palms joined is the documented attitude before a teacher. The exact posture under holy water
    in a ruesi's clearing: **UNCERTAIN** (no reliable description found).
- **On efficacy.** All of it is **BELIEF**. Both studies frame the healing as moral and emotional support: "a need for
  strengthening morality" [4]; medium-based treatment "เน้นการรักษาที่ให้กำลังใจผู้ป่วย … ลดความเครียด และความวิตกกังวล"
  ("gives the patient heart, lessening stress and anxiety") [7].

### 4.4 What the client is told afterwards
- **PRACTICE.** Make merit at a monastery [4].
- **PRACTICE.** Taboos (คะลำ) after a herbal or holy bath, Isan: "ไม่ให้ทานลาบเลือด ไม่เป็นชู้กับภรรยาชาวบ้าน และถือคะลำมังสาหรือ
  เนื้อสัตว์ 30 อย่าง" — "no raw-blood larb, no adultery with another's wife, and abstain from thirty kinds of meat" [2].
  Relatives "ช่วยจดจำข้อห้ามหรือข้อปฏิบัติหลังการรักษา" (help remember the taboos and the after-care) [6].
- **BELIEF (lifestyle site).** Keep the five or eight precepts, give sangkhathan, bring holy water home from a temple
  (some to bathe, some to drink), and chant the Jinapañjara (ชินบัญชร) [36].
- **BELIEF (lifestyle site).** For Por Kae devotees: "ห้ามลอดราวตากผ้า" (never pass under a clothesline) and keep the third
  precept [19].
- **BELIEF (forum; weak).** A teacher forbade looking back after the removed thing was thrown into water, or returning to
  that spot within three days [39].

## 5 · What is chanted

### 5.1 The opening
- **PRACTICE.** Namo tassa ×3 opens the folk healer's treatment [6]. Isan healers begin "นะโม ตัสสะ พุทธัง สะระณัง คัจฉามิ"
  [2]. Por Kae worship begins "ตั้งนะโม 3 จบ" [20][19].
- **FACT (text and translation).** "Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammā-sambuddhassa. (three times) — Homage to the Blessed
  One, the Worthy One, the Rightly Self-awakened One." [29 §0064]

### 5.3 Canonical and paritta texts in lay practice
- **PRACTICE.** Kanchanaburi folk healers' katha are often "ตัดมาจากพระปริตรและบทสวดในพิธีสงฆ์" (cut from the parittas and
  monks' chants), e.g. "คาถาสักกัตวา", usually by their opening words, "เช่น 'สักกัตวา' หรือ 'นักขัตยักขะภูตานัง...'".
  - The reason given: "ความเชื่อที่ว่าบทสวดเหล่านี้เป็นคาถาศักดิ์สิทธิ์" (these chants are believed sacred).
  - Villagers "นิมนต์" (invite, as one invites a monk) their healers, who heal to "เอาบุญ" (make merit) [6].
- **FACT.** The Bhanayak (ภาณยักษ์, the Āṭānāṭiya Paritta) is, "ตามที่ปรากฏในพระไตรปิฎก", a protection: "มีจุดประสงค์หลักคือ
  การคุ้มครองพุทธบริษัทให้ได้ปฏิบัติธรรมอย่างสงบสุข" ("its chief purpose is to protect the Buddhist community so they can
  practise in peace").
  - The same Thai academics warn that its rite is now often turned to occult ends and away from the Dhamma [34].
- **FACT.** The Ratana Sutta (Sn 2:1, identical with Khp 6) is addressed to spirits: "Whatever spirits have gathered here …
  Show goodwill to the human race" [30]. The Chanting Guide's six verses end "Nibbanti dhīrā yathā'yam-padīpo … enlightened —
  go out like this flame" [29 §0052].
- **UNCERTAIN.** The Jinapañjara is a Thai protective chant.
  - It is presumed composed by a Lanna elder and "นัยว่าเป็นนิพนธ์ของ … สมเด็จพระพุฒาจารย์ (โต พรหมรังสี)" (reportedly
    Somdet To's). The page says it "can protect against accidents, dangers, and all enemies" (Thai Wikipedia, uncited) [35].
  - It is recommended against คุณไสย on a horoscope site [36].
  - It is said by the protected person in the first person ("Thus am I utterly well-sheltered, well-protected") [29 §0093].

### 5.5 The one chant for the clearing — recommendation: the *Sakkatvā* verses
Why this one:
- (a) It is documented in Thai lay healing as a katha cut from the parittas [6]. A Thai blog calls it "โอสถะปริตร"
  (medicine-protection) and says it was once recited over medicine (**BELIEF**) [32].
- (b) It has a reliable published translation [29 §0062].
- (c) The Chanting Guide's form ends each stanza *te*, "your": the form one says over another person. The Thai blog's form
  ends เม, "my": the form said for oneself [32].
- (d) The game has not used it. Its picture of the Triple Gem as "the highest, most excellent medicine" fits a fog lifting
  without promising anything.

The Pali and translation, verbatim from *A Chanting Guide* [29 §0062]:

> Sakkatvā buddha-ratanaṁ / Osathaṁ uttamaṁ varaṁ / Hitaṁ deva-manussānaṁ / Buddha-tejena sotthinā /
> Nassant'upaddavā sabbe / Dukkhā vūpasamentu te.
> — Having revered the jewel of the Buddha, / the highest, most excellent medicine, / the welfare of human & heavenly
> beings: / Through the Buddha's majesty & safety, / may all obstacles vanish. / May your sufferings grow totally calm.
>
> Sakkatvā dhamma-ratanaṁ / Osathaṁ uttamaṁ varaṁ / Pariḷāhūpasamanaṁ / Dhamma-tejena sotthinā /
> Nassant'upaddavā sabbe / Bhayā vūpasamentu te.
> — Having revered the jewel of the Dhamma, / the highest, most excellent medicine, / the stiller of feverish passion: /
> Through the Dhamma's majesty & safety, / may all obstacles vanish. / May your fears grow totally calm.
>
> Sakkatvā saṅgha-ratanaṁ / Osathaṁ uttamaṁ varaṁ / Āhuneyyaṁ pāhuneyyaṁ / Saṅgha-tejena sotthinā /
> Nassant'upaddavā sabbe / Rogā vūpasamentu te.
> — Having revered the jewel of the Saṅgha, / the highest, most excellent medicine, / worthy of gifts, worthy of
> hospitality: / Through the Saṅgha's majesty & safety, / may all obstacles vanish. / May your diseases grow totally
> calm.

The Thai as a Thai blog prints it, in the self-blessing form ending เม, with the blog's spelling รัตตะนัง [32]:

> สักกัตวา พุทธะรัตตะนัง โอสะถัง อุตตะมัง วะรัง หิตัง เทวะมะนุสสานัง พุทธะเตเชนะ โสตถินา นัสสันตุ ปัททะวา สัพเพ ทุกขา
> วูปะสะเมนตุ เม
> (ธัมมะรัตตะนัง … ปะริฬาหูปะสะมะนัง … ภะยา วูปะสะเมนตุ เม; สังฆะรัตตะนัง … อาหุเนยยัง ปาหุเนยยัง … โรคา วูปะสะเมนตุ เม)

- **UNCERTAIN.** Its place in the corpus: the Chanting Guide groups it under the Abhaya Paritta [29]; the Thai blog says it
  comes "from the full Āṭānāṭiya" [32]. Neither names a canonical sutta. Call it a traditional paritta verse, not
  "the Buddha's words".
- **NOT FOUND:** a Thai-script source for the เต ("your") form. Master Z or a Thai chanting book could confirm it.
- **Alternatives, both in the Chanting Guide** [29]:
  - The Abhaya Paritta's first stanzas: "Whatever unlucky portents & ill omens … evil planets, upsetting nightmares: By
    the Buddha's power may they be destroyed." These are about removing ill omens.
  - The last Ratana verse (canonical; the sutta is addressed to spirits [30]).

## 8 · A warning not to return

- **NOT FOUND** as a documented, general Thai rule ("after a clearing, do not go back to the person or place suspected, or
  confront them"). Searches in Thai and English turned up forums and lifestyle pages only.
- **FACT (the nearest Thai concept).**
  - ORST, ลองของ: "ทดลองดูว่าเครื่องรางของขลังนั้นจะขลังจริงหรือไม่, โดยปริยายหมายความว่า ลองดี" — "to test whether a charm is
    really potent; figuratively, ลองดี".

    really as good as claimed (mostly disdainful or challenging)" [1].
  - Going back "to prove whether his suspicion is correct" is, in Thai terms, ลองดี: a challenge, not an inquiry.
- **PRACTICE (what healers are documented to advise instead).** The black-magic removal studied by Ganjanapan ends with "a
  suggestion to make merit at a Buddhist monastery". Confrontation is not part of it [4]. In that case the client "believed
  that the wife of the factory owner had used black magic against her". The study records no confrontation [4].
- **BELIEF (forum; weak).** Do not look back at, or return within three days to, the place where the removed thing was
  thrown [39]. This is about the disposal spot, not the sender.
- **A canonical text that names the structure** (offered as a lead only; the lesson line and any term are Chad's, per
  CLAUDE.md): Dhammapada 3–5, Thanissaro [31] —
  > "He insulted me, hit me, beat me, robbed me" — for those who brood on this, hostility isn't stilled. … Hostilities
  > aren't stilled through hostility, regardless. Hostilities are stilled through non-hostility: this, an unending truth.
- The game's own source for the warning is the trial: 5.2D "Lersi Chin advises him not to return merely to test, provoke
  or prove a theory", and 5.3 [47].
