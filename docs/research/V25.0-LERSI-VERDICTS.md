## A. What changes (the short list)

1. **REFUTED: "a *lay* ascetic".**
   - ORST defines ฤๅษี as "นักบวชพวกหนึ่ง". ORST นักบวช is "ผู้ที่ได้เข้าพิธีบวชตามลัทธิศาสนาต่าง ๆ เช่น ภิกษุ สามเณร บาทหลวง":
     one who has undergone ordination in some religion.
   - The draft's own backbone [2] (p. 690) says "ฤาษีกับพระสงฆ์มีความเป็นนักบวชหรือผู้ถือบวชเหมือนกัน ที่แตกต่างกันก็ตรงที่ว่า
     ทั้งสองมีศีลต่างกันเท่านั้น": ruesi and monks are alike in being ordained renunciants, and differ only in their precepts.
   - **Correct wording:** an ordained ascetic (นักบวช) of a ruesi line, outside the Buddhist Sangha; not a bhikkhu.
2. **WEAKER: "Lersi Chin is therefore not a monk".** The title does not settle it.
   - [2]'s abstract: "ส่วนหนึ่งพระภิกษุบวชฤาษีควบคู่กัน" (some monks also take ruesi ordination).
   - One of [2]'s five samnak heads is "ผู้เป็นทั้งพระสงฆ์และฤาษี" (p. 692).
   - A famous monk with a royal ecclesiastical title, Phra Ratchaphromyan, is "known as หลวงพ่อฤๅษีลิงดำ" (Naewna, 19 Dec
     2020).
   - Lersi Chin is a non-monk because of how Chad's model shows him (tiger-print cloth, topknot), not because of the
     word "Lersi". So *thank you*, not anumodana, holds by depiction. Say so to Chad that way.
3. **REFUTED: MThai [20] calls Por Kae "teacher of all knowledge, even of the gods".**
   - On that page the phrase "อาจารย์แห่งสรรพวิชาความรู้ทั้งมวลรวมถึงเป็นอาจารย์ของเหล่าเทพเทวดา" describes **Phra Phruehatsabodi**
     (Jupiter). That deity is made from nineteen ruesi, which is why Thursday is the teachers' day.
   - What the page says of the Phra Ruesi is "ผู้ประทานสรรพวิชาความรู้ทั้งมวลแก่มนุษยชาติ" (giver of all knowledge to humankind).
4. **REFUTED: "Ta Wua: only in a seller's blog".** [2] (p. 684) names Ta Wua twice:
   - "[ฤ]าษีที่คนไทยรู้จักมากที่สุด ก็คือ [ฤ]าษีนารอด พระ[ฤ]าษีตาวัว และพระ[ฤ]าษีตาไฟ" (the ruesi best known to Thais).
   - "[ฤ]าษีผู้เป็นใหญ่ 3 ตน คือ [ฤ]าษีพิลาไลย [ฤ]าษีตาไฟ และ[ฤ]าษีตาวัว", citing มนัส โอภากุล 2533.
   - The draft's "'most common': NOT FOUND as data" is therefore WEAKER: [2] states it, though without data.
5. **REFUTED: "NOT FOUND: a Thai-script source for the เต ('your') form" of Sakkatvā.**
   - Wimutti prints the standard Thai text "…ทุกขา วูปะสะเมนตุ เต" with a Thai translation: "ขอทุกข์ทั้งหลายของท่านจงสงบไปโดยดี".
   - Kalyanamitra prints it inside **"เจริญพระพุทธมนต์ บทสวดสิบสองตำนาน: อะภะยะปะริตตัง"**, right after the three ยันทุนนิมิตตัง
     verses. That is exactly where Thanissaro's Chanting Guide places it.
   - So "its place in the corpus: UNCERTAIN" becomes resolvable. It is the closing part of the Abhaya Paritta in the
     monks' Twelve Tamnan. The blog's "from the full Āṭānāṭiya" is the outlier. It is still not canonical sutta text.
   - The blog's spellings (รัตตะนัง, "นัสสันตุ ปัททะวา") are non-standard. The standard forms are **ระตะนัง** and
     **นัสสันตุปัททะวา**. Never caption from the blog.
6. **WEAKER: "Thai lay healers are documented cutting คาถาสักกัตวา".** [6] documents **one** healer:
   - He is นายดุ่ม สืบใหม่ (84), Ban Nong Khao, Kanchanaburi. He treats dog bites and the childhood ailment *sang*
     (หมอเหยียบหมา, หมอพ่นซาง).

   - In the Royal Thai General System of Transcription (RTGS), จ, ฉ and ช all become *ch*, and both อิ and อี become *i*.

## B. The seven points the brief asked about

**(1) What a Lersi is, and whether he is a monk.**
- A ruesi is an ordained ascetic (นักบวช) outside the Sangha: ORST ฤๅษี / นักบวช; [2] p. 690.
- There is a ruesi ordination rite, "พิธีบวชฤาษีตามแบบฉบับเดิม" [22].
- Roles: healer, refuge through spells, social developer, counsellor [2].
- He is by default not a bhikkhu, but monks can also hold ruesi ordination [2], and a monk can be nicknamed ฤๅษี
  (Naewna).
- An anthropologist quoted by AFP frames clients as seeking power "from alternative people other than monks"
  (The Star / AFP, 2017).
- Verdict: the draft's "not a monk" is CONFIRMED as the default. "Lay" is REFUTED. "Therefore" is WEAKER (A1, A2).

**(2) Dress.**
- Tiger-print cloth over one shoulder: CONFIRMED.
  - Tiger-pattern cotton, a rosary, a staff and a one-shoulder cloth [2] p. 691.
  - Tiger fur in iconography [17], and as lore [18].
- Real tiger skin: avoid. Section 17 of the Wildlife Act B.E. 2562 [40] (ONEP, which cites มาตรา ๑๗).
- Dress varies by samnak (red-brown, brown, navy) [2]. AFP's Khon Kaen hermit wears **white robes** (2017).
- Rosary: CONFIRMED. ORST ประคำ; [3]: "มีเพียงสายประคำเท่านั้น", "สายประคำสั้น"; [2].
  - Nuance in [3]: four Wat Pho statues wear none, and one wears a cord and earrings.
- Rudraksha, wrist beads, beads wound in the hair: NOT FOUND. My own Thai searches found nothing either.
- Topknot: CONFIRMED (ORST ชฎา, มวย ๒). The classical Wat Pho figures wear the trumpet-flower ascetic's hat over it [3].
- Forehead mark: the draft's UNCERTAIN stands.
  - Thai เจิม is scented powder (ORST เจิม ๑; ORST กระแจะ ๑: "ผงเครื่องหอม…สำหรับทา เจิม"). No colour is given.
  - A red pottu/tilak is documented at Bangkok's Tamil temple [28].
  - Nuance the draft lacks: Thai ritual art does carry red marks between the brows. The Phra Lak Na Thong mask has
    "ตรงกลางหว่างคิ้วมีรอยเจิมสีแดง" (SAC 2022), and Ta Fai has his third eye [17].
  - So the mark "reads Hindu" to most viewers but is not exclusively Hindu. Still, never explain it as a Thai ruesi
    custom.
- Tattoos: the draft's "plausible" is strengthened. AFP's hermit is shown "decorating his arms in hand-etched tattoos",
  and [23]'s Ajarn Chu is tattooed all over.
- Barefoot: CONFIRMED [27].

**(3) Examining, and the signs read as คุณไสย.** CONFIRMED.
- [7] (an informant): "การรักษาคุณไสย หมอจะสังเกตอาการของผู้ป่วยที่โดนคุณไสย (มีการเจ็บป่วยหาสาเหตุไม่ได้) เช่น เหม่อลอย
  พูดพร่ำคนเดียว เป็นต้น ซึ่งได้รับรักษาด้วยการแพทย์ต่าง ๆ แล้วไม่หาย) จากนั้นหมอจะทำน้ำมนต์ และบริกรรมคาถา ให้คนไข้ดื่ม".
- [7] also: every healer takes the history (ซักประวัติ), some check the horoscope, and some use "พลังสมาธิ หรือเข้าทรง".
- [4]: diagnosis rituals first (wai khru, du mua).
- [2]: one class of Isan healer (หมอธรรม, หมอผีฟ้า) gives the cause "ด้วยการนั่งทางใน". [2] is quoting Phennapha &
  Phattraporn (2000) on Isan healers in general.
- [36] (lifestyle): "เหม่อลอย ไม่มีสมาธิ ขี้หลงขี้ลืม", when "ไปหาหมอแล้วไม่สามารถหาสาเหตุ…ได้".
- Note: every source examined is a **folk healer** (mo muang, หมอพื้นบ้าน, หมอธรรม). Only [2] is about ruesi acting as
  หมอธรรม. Both [7] and [36] presuppose that **doctors were seen first**; see D1.
- Eggs: only a forum describes one, as a *removal* rite [39]. The draft's NOT FOUND (reliable sources) stands.

**(4) The clearing.**
- Holy water: CONFIRMED.
  - Drunk [7]; bathed or sprayed [5] pp. 212–213; poured [23].
  - ORST น้ำมนต์: water "ที่เข้าพิธีทางพระพุทธศาสนา…เรียกเต็มว่า น้ำพระพุทธมนต์".
- Mayom: CONFIRMED [37]: "ใช้ในพิธีปัดรังควาน และใช้สำหรับพระสงฆ์ประพรมน้ำมนต์แทนหญ้าคา…ใบมะยม (ทั้งก้าน) 7 ใบ มัดรวมกัน".
  Note it is described as the **monks'** sprinkler. [36] (lifestyle) also has mayom stems soaked in holy water to
  sprinkle a house.
- Crown-blow: WEAKER (A11).
- Sacred thread: CONFIRMED.
  - Isan หมอธรรม "ผูกฝ้ายและรดน้ำมนต์ให้" for those harmed by spirits [2] p. 688.
  - AFP: "wraps protective string around" a client.
  - ORST สายสิญจน์ is the white thread of 3 or 9 strands that monks hold while chanting.
- The sequence: WEAKER as "a documented sequence". It is stitched from different healers in different regions. The
  only one-person documented sequence is [4]'s Po Liang Kaeo Ma, whose khru is a Burmese hermit: "starting with going
  into a trance-like state or meditating, blessing with holy water, rubbing with sacred objects, and finally a
  suggestion to make merit at a Buddhist monastery". Stage it as "his practice", which is the case file's own phrase.

**(5) The chant.**
- The Chanting Guide **does** contain the Sakkatvā verses with a translation. Section0062, "Abhaya Paritta: The
  Danger-free Protection", in the book's part titled **"Blessings"**, after a "* * *" separator. Verbatim:

  > Sakkatvā buddha-ratanaṁ / Osathaṁ uttamaṁ varaṁ / Hitaṁ deva-manussānaṁ / Buddha-tejena sotthinā /
  > Nassant'upaddavā sabbe / Dukkhā vūpasamentu te.
  > Having revered the jewel of the Buddha, / the highest, most excellent medicine, / the welfare of human & heavenly
  > beings: / Through the Buddha's majesty & safety, / may all obstacles vanish. / May your sufferings grow totally calm.
  >
  > Sakkatvā dhamma-ratanaṁ / Osathaṁ uttamaṁ varaṁ / Pariḷāhūpasamanaṁ / Dhamma-tejena sotthinā /
  > Nassant'upaddavā sabbe / Bhayā vūpasamentu te.
  > Having revered the jewel of the Dhamma, / the highest, most excellent medicine, / the stiller of feverish passion: /
  > Through the Dhamma's majesty & safety, / may all obstacles vanish. / May your fears grow totally calm.
  >
  > Sakkatvā saṅgha-ratanaṁ / Osathaṁ uttamaṁ varaṁ / Āhuneyyaṁ pāhuneyyaṁ / Saṅgha-tejena sotthinā /
  > Nassant'upaddavā sabbe / Rogā vūpasamentu te.
  > Having revered the jewel of the Saṅgha, / the highest, most excellent medicine, / worthy of gifts, worthy of
  > hospitality: / Through the Saṅgha's majesty & safety, / may all obstacles vanish. / May your diseases grow totally calm.

  This matches the draft character for character.
- Namo tassa: CONFIRMED (Section0064, "Taking the Five Precepts").
- New for Chad (the anumodana rule):
  - In both the Chanting Guide ("Blessings") and Thai practice (เจริญพระพุทธมนต์, สิบสองตำนาน), the *te* form is the
    **monks' blessing over a layperson**.
  - A lay healer using it as a katha is documented once [6], so a non-monk chanting it is defensible.
  - It sits close to "only a monk says anumodana". Put the choice to Chad: the *te* form, the self-blessing เม form, or
    Namo tassa plus an uncaptioned katha.

**(6) ลองดี, and advice not to go back or confront.**

- [39] is about the disposal spot, as the draft says: CONFIRMED.
- The in-corpus anchor the draft missed is in D2.

**(7) "Lersi Chin" has no public information.** CONFIRMED.

## D. Important omissions for a scene of a Lersi master examining a foggy, forgetful young man and clearing him

   mind, see a doctor as well. Spiritual guidance sits beside proper care, never in place of it."
   - The sources define the sign of sorcery as illness doctors could not explain: [7] "ได้รับรักษาด้วยการแพทย์ต่าง ๆ แล้วไม่หาย";
     [36] "ไปหาหมอแล้วไม่สามารถหาสาเหตุ…ได้".
   - [4] p. 67: villagers "normally begin with a visit to a modern clinic".

2. **The casebook's own "do not go back to prove it".** In Master Z's voice (docs/source/site-case-files.md):
   - S1·04: "don't engage, don't provoke, don't feed your own fear … Understanding when not to interfere is just as
     important as knowing what to do."
   - S1·05: "Not everything needs to be challenged or investigated."

   - S2·06: "A good reader does not need to prove how much he can see."
