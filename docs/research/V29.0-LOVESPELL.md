# v29.0 · Episode 5 chapter 1's research (26 Nov 2026): love charms, their breaking, protection, the chants

Every claim below is popular belief, never fact, and is said in the game only in a character's mouth or as Master Z's own
account. Nothing of the trade (no price, no seller, no instruction for making or sending anything) is used. The first
reader's searches (English and Thai) are in §1; the second reader, who tried to refute each claim, is in §2, and **the
second reader's verdict wins where they differ**.

## 1 · The claims and their sources (first reader)
- C1 A love charm is เสน่ห์ (sanae); a maker is popularly a หมอเสน่ห์, "charm doctor"; charms are believed to need things
  like the person's photograph, name, birth date, or something of theirs. Chiang Mai CityLife, "The Love Doctor" (2010,
  issue 08): https://www.chiangmaicitylife.com/citylife-issue/2010/2010-issue-08
- C2 Thai sorcery (คุณไสย) is said to need the target's birth date (and time), sometimes something of theirs; Sanook frames
  it as superstition: https://www.sanook.com/horoscope/165597/
- C3 Protection: amulets, takrut, ผ้ายันต์, protective mantras; เบี้ยแก้ "both guards against and undoes" evil and
  คุณไสย: https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/life/1685643 ; https://www.wongnai.com/articles/thai-protection-amulet ;
  https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=75484
- C4 Holy water (น้ำมนต์) is made with chanting, and is traditionally traced to the commentary's story of Ānanda
  sprinkling water from the Buddha's bowl round Vesālī in the plague (the Ratana Sutta's setting); monks sprinkle it
  with a brush, and wax from a candle may be dripped into the water: https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/Jiabu/article/download/217153/150640 ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratana_Sutta ; https://thailex.info/THAILEX/THAILEXENG/LEXICON/nahm%20mon%20(น้ำมนต์).htm ;
  lustral water in house blessings, illness: Cambridge JSEAS, "Cries over spilled holy water".
- C5 White sacred thread (สายสิญจน์, sai sin) is strung round a house while monks chant, against evil spirits (a
  housewarming): https://thailandfoundation.or.th/thai-housewarming-ceremony/ ; tied on the wrist (well known);
  strung round new houses' ceilings to bless and cleanse: https://folklore.usc.edu/folk-object-buddhist-strings/
- C6 The Jinapañjara Gāthā (ชินบัญชร), "The Victor's Cage" in Thanissaro Bhikkhu's *A Chanting Guide*:
  https://www.dhammatalks.org/books/ChantingGuide/Section0093.html ; https://www.encyclopediaofbuddhism.org/wiki/Jinapanjara_Gatha
- C7 Love-spell services are advertised in Singapore today (Malay Mail, 28 Dec 2024; The Star and SCMP, 26 Dec 2024):
  https://www.malaymail.com/news/singapore/2024/12/28/can-a-bloodworm-spell-fix-your-love-life-this-singapore-spiritual-service-says-yes/161334
- C8 That a broken charm is "sent back", or that its maker looks for whoever undid it: the first reader found nothing.
- C9 That 3 a.m. is an hour of such attacks: the first reader found nothing.

## 2 · The second reader's verdicts (26 Nov 2026; these win)
- C1 PARTLY: เสน่ห์ is standard; "charm doctor" is CityLife's gloss (the full article not opened); photo, name, birth date
  attested in general, clothing weaker ("something of theirs"). Say: "a practitioner popularly called mor sanae, 'charm
  doctor'; such charms are believed to need things like the person's photograph, name and birth date, or something of
  theirs".
- C2 SUPPORTED (seen through a search summary): "birth date and time, and sometimes something of theirs".
- C3 SUPPORTED for เบี้ยแก้ ("กันทั้งแก้"); the general list is common knowledge, no single source lists all four.
- C4 SUPPORTED, with "traditionally traced to"; the story is in the commentary, not the sutta itself; Thai practice
  differs and adds verses; the brush and the candle wax: Thailex.
- C5 PARTLY: the thread round a house during chanting, against evil spirits, SUPPORTED; the wrist, well known; **a ring of
  sanctified sand or rice round a house: NOT SUPPORTED. Dropped.**
- C6 PARTLY: "one of Thailand's most popular protective chants" (not "the most"); its common Thai version credited to
  Somdet Phra Phutthachan (To Phrommarangsi) of Wat Rakhang, who is said to have revised an older text (also known in
  Myanmar and Sri Lanka). "The Victor's Cage" CONFIRMED.
- C7 SUPPORTED. The seller is never named, no price quoted.
- C8 **NOT SUPPORTED as worded.** Attested is ของย้อน, that sorcery can REBOUND on whoever made or commissioned it (one
  Thai page: done by a qualified ajarn "so the thing does not rebound on oneself"). Nothing found of a maker sending
  something to find out who undid it and targeting the helper; ลองของ means testing someone's power, not retaliation.
  **So the chapter never presents helper-targeting as Thai belief: it is Master Z's own account of his own night (S1·16:
  "He told me the other practitioner was sending back yant to find out who had helped her clear it"), said only as what
  happened to him and what his teacher told him that night.**
- C9 **NOT SUPPORTED.** "3 a.m." as the spirits' hour is film marketing (ตีสาม 3D, 2012) and Western popular culture.
  **The chapter's three o'clock is the case file's own hour and nothing more: no line calls it a special hour.**

## 3 · The chants, as captioned (Thanissaro Bhikkhu, *A Chanting Guide*, verbatim)
The Jinapañjara's first four verses (https://www.dhammatalks.org/books/ChantingGuide/Section0093.html), as the page has
them (the page's own apostrophes and its "Sārīputta" in the English kept):
1. *Jay'āsan'āgatā Buddhā / Jetvā Māraṁ savāhanaṁ / Catu-saccāsabhaṁ rasaṁ / Ye piviṅsu narāsabhā* — "The Buddhas,
   noble men who drank the nectar of the four noble truths, having come to the victory seat, having defeated Māra
   together with his mount:"
2. *Taṇhaṅkar'ādayo Buddhā / Aṭṭha-vīsati nāyakā / Sabbe patiṭṭhitā mayhaṁ / Matthake te munissarā.* — "These Buddhas—28
   leaders, sovereign sages beginning with Taṇhaṅkara—are all established on the crown of my head."
3. *Sīse patiṭṭhito mayhaṁ / Buddho dhammo dvilocane / Saṅgho patiṭṭhito mayhaṁ / Ure sabba-guṇākaro.* — "The Buddha is
   established in my head, the Dhamma in my two eyes, the Saṅgha—the mine of all virtues—is established in my chest."
4. *Hadaye me Anuruddho / Sāriputto ca dakkhiṇe / Koṇḍañño piṭṭhi-bhāgasmiṁ / Moggallāno ca vāmake.* — "Anuruddha is in my
   heart, and Sārīputta on my right. Koṇḍañña is behind me, and Moggallāna on my left."
The Ratana Sutta's verses for the breaking are taken from the same guide when the lines are written (its section is
named there); both chants go to Chad before they are voiced.
