## 0 · The answer on one screen

1. **Switching.** One button, always in the same place, free at any moment of the fight. A lockout of about 0.6 s
   after each switch so a double tap cannot bounce. The one switched TO arrives with a small gift (the teacher: one
   fireball at the nearest marked spirit; the student: a second inside his ward). The one left behind keeps doing a
   lesser version of his last thing, cannot be lost, and never uses the pair's big acts. Whatever was already cast
   finishes as cast. No cost and no cooldown beyond the lockout: the one game found that began with a limit on
   changing characters took it out again as "punishing the players" (§1.2).
2. **The student's three** (§2.4): SPIRIT VISION (he finds and marks; only marked spirits take the fire fully), THE
   WARD (his Lersi's protection: spirits that come at it are held at its edge, and each one held brings the golden
   shockwave sooner), THE CHANT (begun with one tap, it goes on after the switch: spirits slow, the fire burns truer).
   Each is useless alone and each makes the teacher's next act count for more: that is what makes two bodies one team.
3. **The camera** (§4): one raised room camera, three-quarter view, about 35 to 40 degrees down from just under the
   ceiling, on the room's long axis, the near wall and ceiling cut away; it holds BOTH bodies, so a switch moves no
   camera. Not over the shoulder: at this lens an upright phone sees a strip 1.3 m wide two metres ahead, and a
   camera that follows a body into the middle of a room shows about a seventh of its floor, where the room camera
   shows four fifths (mine, §4.3).
4. **The controls** (§3): the game's own floating stick under the left thumb; under the right, one large button for
   the character's main act and two smaller ones on an arc; the switch button above them. No look drag is needed, and
   nothing is aimed by hand: a tap fires at the best target, a tap on a spirit picks it. Every act is one tap or one
   hold; nothing is mashed and nothing is timed.
5. **Forgiving** (§6): the fight cannot be lost on reflexes. A mistake costs a little that is seen and said (and is
   on the card), never a restart of the fight; at most two spirits press at once, and only from inside the frame; a
   player who does nothing is prompted, then helped, then carried (the ladder of §5.3), as chapter 4 already does when
   it opens his sight for him.
6. **The five bugs most likely to bite** (§9): (a) a touch owned by the wrong thing across a switch, a cancelled
   touch or two presses in one frame; (b) cooldowns and waves on the wrong clock under a pause, a hidden tab or a
   slow frame; (c) a switch in the middle of a cast, or with a fireball or the ring in flight; (d) the partner stuck
   on furniture or standing where the player stands, and the camera behind a wall; (e) the fight ending, or a save and
   a Continue, with an effect, a loop or the third-person state still alive.

## 1 · Switching characters in shipped games

### 1.1 Game by game

Each entry: how the switch is triggered · what happens to the one left behind · whether the switch itself does
something · what limits it · what makes the two feel like a team.

**Final Fantasy VII Remake.**
- Trigger: the d-pad, "at any time during battle" [FF7R-WP][FF7R-FEX].
- Left behind: the AI plays him on. His ATB (the gauge abilities are paid from) refills at a reduced rate
  [FF7R-TAC] (extract); a guide calls the default AI "kind of bad at" building it and tells players to switch often
  [FF7R-INV] (extract). In "Classic" mode it is the other way round: "your characters are fighting automatically" and
  the player's job "will only be to handle ATB options" [FF7R-CLASSIC].
- The switch itself: nothing of its own in anything I read (no attack, no moment of safety).
- Limit: none found.
- Team: each has his own gauge, so one builds and another spends; an ally's ability can be ordered WITHOUT switching
  ("Use L2 or R2 to issue commands to allies"), and choosing a command slows time [FF7R-FEX][FF7R-CLASSIC]. Players
  report that enemies press whoever is controlled, so a switch also moves the pressure [FF7R-STEAM] (extract; an
  anecdote, not a rule I could confirm). Whether the reduced AI is deliberate, to make the player switch, I could not
  confirm from any developer.

**Final Fantasy VII Rebirth** adds two joint moves [REBIRTH]. *Synergy Skills*: hold guard and press a button, "at any
time"; they "require at least two party members present"; several are the partner's protection ("Your partner defends
you while you move around"; "Takes up a strong defensive stance with your partner"; a joint counter to a ranged
attack). *Synergy Abilities*: earned by using abilities, picked from the command menu; "This cost increases each time
a Synergy Ability is used in a single battle"; each pays a joint attack plus a boon (a longer stagger, a raised limit
level, MP free for a time).

**Final Fantasy XV.** At release the three friends were AI only: a bar fills and "Noctis can command his companions to
perform special moves called Techniques"; "Link-strikes" fire as joint attacks; with "Wait Mode", "if all player input
stops the battle pauses" [FFXV-WP]. Switching was patched in later ("A post-release update enables control of all four
characters during battle", unlocked on the Ascension grid [FFXV-WP][FFXV-GEM] (extract)); each friend then plays in
his own style [FFXV-TR] (extract).

**Genshin Impact.**
- Trigger: one of four is on the field; "Players can quickly switch between the four characters during combat"
  [GI-WP].
- Left behind: he leaves the field (there is no body to manage), but what he placed stays: skills and bursts "remain
  active even after having swapped off" [GI-OFF] (extract).
- The switch itself: nothing of its own. A swap CANCELS the outgoing attack ("Most Normal Attacks can be animation
  canceled at any time with Skill, Burst, dash, jump, and swap") and nothing can be queued for the incoming one: "Any
  action after swapping to a character also can't be buffered" [KQM-FRAMES].
- Limit: a short lockout after each swap. Guides disagree on its length: "a 0.10-second cooldown" [GI-GW], one second
  [GI-TGG] (extract). Not checked at an official page.
- Team: elements. One applies, the next reacts [GI-WP]. And a rule the engine needs an answer to: a thing left on the
  field either keeps the numbers it was cast with ("Talents that snapshot use the character's stats when cast") or
  follows its owner's live ("Dynamic talents continuously update") [KQM-SNAP].

**Honkai: Star Rail** is not real-time: "turn-based combat", a team of up to four [HSR-WP]. It has no switch. What it
has for a team: one pool of Skill Points shared by the whole team, at most five [HSR-PGG] (extract), and follow-up
attacks that fire out of turn when their condition is met [HSR-FUA] (extract). That an Ultimate can cut into any turn
is from memory: confirmed by nothing I could open.

**Zenless Zone Zero.** The closest model for a switch that is itself a move.
- Trigger: "The player can freely change the controlled Agent at any time in combat" [ZZZ-WP].
- Left behind: "other Agents may perform attacks or enhancements autonomously" [ZZZ-WP].
- The switch itself IS the defence. A gold flash on an incoming attack: press Switch and the incoming Agent parries
  it (Defensive Assist: "parry the enemy's attack and increase their Daze greatly") or dodges it (Evasive Assist,
  enemies slowed); a Basic Attack then "will trigger an Assist Follow-Up", during which he cannot be hurt. A red flash
  cannot be assisted [ZZZ-HOYO].
- Limit: Assist Points, at most 6, spent by Perfect Assists; "Use Chain Attacks and Ultimates to regenerate Assist
  Points" [ZZZ-HOYO]. With none, the incoming Agent only dodges [ZZZ-G8] (extract).
- Team: attacks fill an enemy's Daze; stunned, it "receives increased damage" [ZZZ-WP], and a heavy hit on it opens a
  Chain Attack, each Agent in turn: once on a normal enemy, twice on an elite, three times on a boss [ZZZ-HOYO].

**Wuthering Waves.** "Intro skills are triggered when the characters enter combat and Outro skills are triggered when
the characters leave combat. Both skills are triggered when the characters are switched." [WW-WP]. The limit is a
gauge, Concerto Energy: attacking and dodging fill it, and a switch with it full spends it and fires both
[WW-G8][WW-GW] (extracts). Every paid switch is therefore a small joint move: the leaver's parting act and the
comer's entrance. (That many parting acts leave a boost for the one coming in is from memory.)

**Xenoblade Chronicles 3.** "players have the ability to swap between all six main party characters during combat"
[XC3-WP] (hold ZL and press L or R [XC3-TR] (extract)). The others "auto-attack" in range. The team is ROLES:
Defenders "draw enemy aggro towards them", Attackers gain from where they stand round the enemy, Healers support; a
shared gauge opens a Chain Attack; certain pairs "fuse into Ouroboros, larger forms that are invincible" [XC3-WP].

**Tales of Arise.** One character is played and the rest are AI, but a supporter can be CALLED IN for one Boost Attack
without giving up control, and each Boost Attack answers ONE enemy behaviour: armour, fliers ("temporarily restricts
their flight abilities and downs them"), dodgers, casters; the defender's is a ward: "Kisara sticks her giant shield
into the earth to attack the enemy and protect allies", and if it hits "it'll down them and weaken any subsequent
charge attacks". On a weakened enemy an icon appears for a joint finisher [TOA-BN]. Each has his own gauge; his icon
"will flash a golden color" when it is ready [TOA-TW] (extract).

**Tales of Xillia.** Two characters are linked: "the supporting partner provides unique abilities to the character",
and fills a gauge for joint "linked artes" [TOX-WP].

**Kingdom Hearts II and III.** No switch in the base games; the partners are AI whose behaviour "the player is allowed
to customize ... to a certain extent" [KH2-WP]. The team is in three devices: Reaction Commands, "special
enemy-specific attacks that are triggered when the player presses the triangle button at the correct time"; Drive
Forms, in which "Sora bonds with party members to become more powerful" (the partner becomes the power) [KH2-WP]; and
team-up attacks "which combine Sora and various party members into one attack" [KH3-WP].

**Nier: Automata.** No switch at will (the story changes who is played). The companion follows one of six behaviour
presets and "cannot die" [NIER-AI] (extract). Two things worth taking: the VIEW changes by section, "from the normal
third-person perspective to a top-down or side-scrolling view", and Easy mode has chips "which automate functions such
as attacking, dodging and pod fire" [NIER-WP].

**The LEGO games.** In the first: "By walking up to another friendly character, the player can switch control over to
that character" [LEGO-WP]; later ones cycle with the shoulder buttons [LEGO-GS] (extract). The switch exists for
ABILITIES: it "is necessary in order to use another character's abilities to complete certain puzzles". And nobody is
lost: when hearts run out "their character is broken apart and they lose studs (as opposed to lives)"; the game "does
not feature a game-over scenario"; it is "geared towards family play" [LEGO-WP]. He comes back where he fell
[LEGO-GP] (extract).

**Trine.** Three heroes, one on screen in single player, switched at any time; each has "their own health and energy
meter"; "When a character dies, the player must choose another living character"; only when all three are dead does
the level go back to a checkpoint [TRINE-WP]. Its makers first put "a time limit for character change" on it: "This
design was now seen as punishing the players and was completely removed". And they let any hero solve most things:
"Almost all situations in Trine can be solved with any character and clever use of the environment" [TRINE-PM].

**It Takes Two / A Way Out.** No switching: two players. They are here for ROLES. "The game frequently gives Cody and
May different abilities that are tied to the theme of the current level"; the tools "must be combined to move
forward"; a fallen player "respawn[s] quickly" [ITT-WP]. One fires sap and the other lights it; its designer on a new
kit each level: "it's basically like teaching a player a new game in an hour", and of the fix for a level that did not
work: "Design by subtraction" [ITT-TALK] (extract). A Way Out: "each situation can be approached differently, with
both characters taking different roles" [AWO-WP].

**God of War (2018).** No switch: one button commands the son. "they can look at an enemy and press the button, and
Atreus will shoot an arrow at the enemy"; "he distracts the weaker ones as Kratos fights the stronger ones"; "he
cannot be knocked out or killed" [GOW-WP]. Its makers: "From the beginning, I wanted to make sure we weren't making an
escort-mission game"; his strong effects (a stun, a knock-down) were moved "on to a command button", and what he does
alone is small: "all he's doing is enhancing what you are already doing" [GOW-GI]. A peer's description of the
result: "fully autonomous and never passive" [GOW-PSB]. In the sequel he is held back for the story's sake: "so he
couldn't seem too powerful and break that narrative immersion" [GOW-EVO]. The GDC talk on his making exists but only
its listing could be read [GOW-GDC].

**Marvel vs. Capcom 3 and Infinite.** 3: teams of three; players "can choose to switch between them at any point
during the match", and can also "call in one of their off-screen characters to perform a single special move" (an
assist) without switching [MVC3-WP]; a tagged-out character recovers the red part of his health while he waits
[MVC3-RED] (extract). Infinite: two against two; "Players can immediately switch between their team members at any
point, even while mid-air or during attack animations"; "Counter Switch" tags the partner in while one is being hit;
and for newcomers an auto-combo lets them "repeatedly press the light punch button" [MVCI-WP].

**Resident Evil: Revelations 2.** The closest model for a fighter and a helper who cannot fight.
- Trigger: a button; "When played in single player mode, the player must switch characters to solve puzzles and do
  certain actions" [REV2-WP].
- Left behind: AI, "competent enough", though the reviewer's partner once got stuck in the environment and a few
  times failed to dodge [REV2-GR]; the fighter does not shoot while the helper is played [REV2-TW] (extract).
- Team: "only two of the four playable characters use guns"; the other two "find hidden items with a flashlight or
  special senses" [REV2-WP]. One helper can see enemies through walls, so the player has to keep going back to her;
  the other's torch blinds enemies; both spot items their partners would miss [REV2-GR]. A critic's reading: the
  helper's sense is what makes the fighter's stealth possible at all [REV2-PM].

**Luigi's Mansion 3.** The closest model for two bodies in small rooms. Click the right stick to bring out the second
body, click again to switch [LM3-NIN]. The one left behind is inert where he stands (guides say he sleeps or goes
limp: extract, the page not pinned). The second body "can use the exact same moves", can "pass through narrow gaps,
such as iron bars or air vents", has 25 HP that regrows, and will "melt away if he steps in a puddle of water"
[LM3-NIN][LM3-MW]. The camera is "a fixed third-person perspective" [LM3-WP]: a switch moves no camera.

Two smaller notes. *Persona 5 Strikers*: while the skill menu is open "the battle will pause to allow tactical
positioning" [P5S-WP]. *Final Fantasy XVI*: the dog "responds to Clive's commands", and its easiest mode "offers
accessories to simplify combat" [FF16-WP]: help worn as an item, not picked from a menu.

### 1.2 What these say

1. **The switch is free where switching is the game.** Trine removed its limit as "punishing the players"
   [TRINE-PM]; Genshin keeps only a brief lockout [GI-GW]; Zenless Zone Zero and Wuthering Waves put their price on
   the BONUS of a well-timed switch, never on the switch [ZZZ-HOYO][WW-G8] (extract); Infinite lets it happen "even
   while mid-air or during attack animations" [MVCI-WP].
2. **The games that feel most like a team make the switch itself a move**: an entrance, a parting act, a parry
   [ZZZ-HOYO][WW-WP][MVCI-WP]. Where the switch does nothing (FF VII Remake, Genshin), the team is carried by
   something else: a gauge, or a state left on the enemy.
3. **The one left behind is never a liability and never the star.** He leaves the field (Genshin), cannot die
   (God of War, Nier), is inert (Luigi's Mansion 3), or fights on at a lower rate (FF VII Remake). Nobody asks the
   player to mind two health bars at once. §7 has the rules.
4. **What ties two into a team is a thing they share**: a gauge one fills and the other spends (FF VII, Rebirth,
   Xillia, Zenless), a state on the enemy that one sets and the other uses (elements, Daze, sap), or knowledge only
   one has (Revelations 2). Two characters who each have a full kit of their own are two separate fights.
5. **Asymmetry gives the reason to switch.** Revelations 2 (one sees, one shoots), Luigi's Mansion 3 (one passes
   bars, one survives water), Tales of Arise (each support move answers one enemy behaviour). The player switches
   because the world asks for the other one, not because a meter says so.
6. **A command without a switch is the lighter form of tag** (FF VII Remake's L2/R2, God of War's one button,
   Marvel vs. Capcom's assists, Arise's Boost Attacks, FF XV's Techniques). For a player who never gets used to
   switching, it is the safety net.
7. **Time bends for the choice** where the choice is hard: FF VII Remake slows it, Persona 5 Strikers and FF XV's
   Wait Mode stop it [FF7R-CLASSIC][P5S-WP][FFXV-WP].

### 1.3 The switching rule this points to

- **Trigger:** one button, in one place for the whole fight, showing the face of the one NOT played. On a desktop,
  one key (and the same button under the mouse).
- **Limit:** a lockout of about 0.6 s, long enough to stop a bounce and to let the HUD change hands, too short to be
  felt. No cost, no gauge, no cooldown: the plan's players are not gamers, and the one shipped game that began with a
  limit on changing characters removed it [TRINE-PM].
- **The switch does something small and always good** (pattern 2): the teacher arrives with one free fireball at the
  nearest marked spirit; the student arrives with about a second inside his ward. It is the safety a non-gamer needs
  while he finds himself on the screen, and it makes the first switch feel like a move.
- **Left behind** (pattern 3 and §7): he keeps a lesser version of his last act going and cannot be lost.
- **What is in flight stays as cast**, on the numbers it was cast with (the "snapshot" answer [KQM-SNAP]): simple to
  explain, and a switch can then never take anything back.
- **Nothing is carried across in the fingers**: a press made before the switch is never spent by the one who arrives
  ("Any action after swapping to a character also can't be buffered" [KQM-FRAMES]).
- **No slow motion on the switch.** With one room camera holding both bodies (§4) there is nothing to re-find, and a
  second clock is a family of bugs of its own (§9, B). Slow motion is kept for the last hit (§6.3).

## 2 · Support and defence kits that are fun to play

### 2.1 Why support turns into a chore, and the known ways out

- The complaint is old and specific: healing is watching bars. "sitting at the back of raids playing whack-a-mole on
  a row of character portraits" [HEAL-TERA] (extract); the healer "becomes something that players need to play (in
  order to win), rather than something they want to play" [HEAL-TUTS] (extract).
- **Way out 1: tie the two players' eyes together.** Valve on its Medic: "Our main goal in designing the Medi Gun was
  to build a strong relationship between the Medic and his healing target"; "We wanted there to be skill involved in
  being a good healing target"; the design "ensures that the Medic and his target are extremely conscious of each
  other" [TF2-WELL].
- **Way out 2: support builds toward a moment.** The same gun fills a charge by healing (2.5 % a second: full in 40
  seconds), which is then spent on 8 seconds in which both cannot be hurt; when it is ready "the meter on the HUD
  begins flashing" and he says so [TF2-UBER]. The dull work is the fuse of the best moment in the match.
- **Way out 3: defence that feeds attack.** Overwatch's Zarya: "Create a damage barrier around an ally"; "Damage
  blocked by barriers increases Particle Cannon damage" [OW-ZARYA]. A barrier placed well is not time lost: it is
  where her power comes from.
- **Way out 4: the support acts ON THE ENEMY, in the world, not on a bar.** A mark ("Launch this orb at an enemy to
  increase the damage they take" [OW-ZEN]), a hold, a reveal. The player looks at the fight, not at the HUD.
- **Way out 5: no healer at all.** One studio: making players "simply kill enemies" to heal "worked best versus other
  healing methods" [HEAL-OUT] (extract).

| Prime and detonate | Mass Effect 3: "The Source Power determines the type of Power Combo that will be set up" and "the detonator triggers the combo, causing a massive explosion" [ME3]; Guild Wars 2: one skill lays a field, another finishes in it [GW2][GW2-SC] (extracts); It Takes Two's sap and match [ITT-TALK] (extract) | Two different acts, in order, by two different characters |

| Heal | The Medi Gun [TF2-WELL] | Avoid as a separate act: with two characters who cannot be lost (§6), there is nothing to heal |

Half-Life 2: Episode One deserves its own line, because it is the same pair as this chapter: a player who cannot yet
fight and a partner who can. In its dark levels "the player had to use the flashlight to direct Alyx's fire", and
players kept doing it even after they had weapons; the dark also "slows the player down, which most of the time keeps
Alyx at your side" [EP1]. The player's only verb was to SHOW; the partner's was to shoot.

And the critics' warning on the two-step: Alan Wake's light-then-gun is praised for its weight (enemies "react to gun
fire and being hit by light") and faulted because every enemy takes the same two steps: "the tactics used for each one
don't differ", so fights become "running in a circle trying to knock out their shields" [AW-GD]. Nine spirits that all
answer to the same two presses would be the same fault: some should ask for a different first step (§2.4).

| Answer one enemy behaviour | The enemy is open only after it | Arise's Boost Attacks [TOA-BN] |

### 2.4 A kit of three for the student

The plan's own proposal (its R3 row: the ward, the chant, Spirit Vision) holds up against the references; what the
research adds is what each must DO to the teacher's next act. The mechanics below are a recommendation (mine), for the
design to accept or change.

**1. SPIRIT VISION: he finds and marks.** (Reveal + mark; Revelations 2, Episode One, Alan Wake, Orb of Discord.)
- What it does: opened, it shows the spirits that are hidden, to the player and so to the teacher; each one his
  sight has rested on stays MARKED for some seconds after he shuts it. The teacher's fire goes to marked spirits
  first and only on a marked spirit does it burn fully.
- Its limit is already built and already taught: the strain ring round the eye button (chapter 4, R95 to R99). While
  the ring empties he cannot look, so the natural thing to do is to switch.
- Why it makes them a team: only the student can find them and only the teacher can burn them. It is the lesson
  itself: he SEES, and seeing is not mastery; what he sees he must hand to his teacher.
- In third person (mine): chapter 4's wave must start from his BODY in the room, not from the lens.

**2. THE WARD: his Lersi's protection.** (Barrier + gather + charge; Zarya, Kisara's shield, the Medic's charge.)
- What it does: a circle of light round him (laid round his teacher when he stands beside him). A spirit that comes
  at it is HELD at its edge for a few seconds; nothing inside is touched. A spirit clinging to the teacher is thrown
  off when the ward closes round him (the only "cleanse", and it is seen in the world).
- The pay-off: every spirit the ward holds brings the golden shockwave sooner (the plan gives it a long cooldown: the
  student's protection is what shortens it), and the ring, released through spirits held at a ward, takes them all.
- Why it makes them a team: his defence is where his teacher's greatest act comes from. "He is there to support his
  teacher" stops being a line and becomes the fastest way through the fight.
- The case file: "my own Lersi came in to protect me".

**3. THE CHANT: begun with one tap, it goes on without him.** (Leave-behind + slow; Genshin's off-field skills,
Wuthering Waves' parting act.)
- What it does: one tap and he chants for a set time, and goes on chanting after the player has switched to the
  teacher. While it lasts the spirits move slower and the fire burns truer (it finds its target more surely and
  spreads to a spirit beside it). A blow that reaches him outside his ward breaks it off: so the ward comes first.
- Why it makes them a team: it is an act whose whole benefit belongs to the OTHER character, and it is what the
  student is seen doing when the player is not him (§7: never idle).
- The case file: "I stayed in that state throughout, my mouth chanting something I did not know at the time."
- To settle before it is voiced: whether the chant has words. A chant with words is captioned (v21.31); a murmur
  meant to be wordless is put through a transcriber (v25.4); and he "did not know" what he chanted.

**The order the three teach** is one sentence: *ward, chant, look, switch, fire*; and then the ring through whatever
the ward is holding. Each press is one tap; nothing is timed.

**For the teacher**, from the same references:
- FIRE is his main act: a tap sends one fireball at the best target (§3.2); held, it repeats at its own pace.
- THE GOLDEN SHOCKWAVE is the pair's big act and is never used by the AI (§7).
- The case file gives a third, and the references say it is worth having: he "began drawing from the air, like a
  vacuum ... then blowing them into a black bag and tying it". That is a CAPTURE, the finishing half of the oldest
  two-step in ghost games (Luigi's Mansion: stun, "snag", "weaken their health down to zero in order to capture them"
  [LM3-WP]; Arise's finisher icon over a weakened enemy [TOA-BN]). A contextual button that appears only when a
  spirit is spent, and takes it into the bag with one HOLD, gives each of the nine a visible end, gives the fight its
  counter (nine, eight, seven), and keeps the fire from simply deleting things.

**Variety without a fourth button** (the Alan Wake warning): a few of the nine can ask for a different FIRST step.
One that cannot be seen at all without his sight. One that the fire only scatters until the ward has held it. One
that goes for the student and breaks his chant unless he is warded. The second step is always the teacher's.

### 2.5 What to leave out

- A heal, a revive, or a health bar for the partner: nobody is lost (§6), so it would be a bar to watch.
- A cleanse as its own button, or any act answered to an icon and not to something seen in the room.
- A taunt as its own button: the ward already draws them (the spirits go to the one who is open to them).
- Anything held down WHILE moving, or two presses at once: "Usually only two inputs at a time are possible"
  [MS-TOUCH]; "Ensure that multiple simultaneous actions (eg. click/drag or swipe) are not required" [GAG].
- A kit of more than three: with the stick and the switch it is already five things under two thumbs.

## 3 · Third person on a phone, upright, by touch

### 3.1 The evidence on hands and targets

- **How phones are held.** Of 1,333 people observed, 780 were touching the screen: one-handed 49 %, cradled (two
  hands holding, one touching) 36 %, two-handed 15 %; two-handed use was 90 % portrait. "Users change the way they're
  holding their phone very often—sometimes every few seconds." The reach charts are "only approximate and vary for
  different individuals" [HOOBER]. (2013, before today's larger phones.)
- **How big a target must be.**
  - Apple, for games: "Make sure frequently used controls are a minimum size of 44x44 pt, and less important
    controls, such as menus, are a minimum size of 28x28 pt" [HIG].
  - Android: "a width and height of at least 48 dp", which it gives as "about 9 mm"; "The recommended target size for
    touchscreen objects is 7–10 mm"; targets kept at least 8 dp apart [ANDROID-48].
  - WCAG: 44 by 44 CSS pixels at its highest level [WCAG-255]; 24 by 24 at the middle one [WCAG-258] (extract).
  - The study behind the thumb numbers: targets of 9.2 mm for single taps and 9.6 mm for runs of taps "should be
    sufficiently large for one-handed thumb use" [PARHI] (extract).
  - On a 6.1-inch phone of 390 × 844 points, one point is 0.167 mm (mine). So 44 pt is 7.3 mm, 48 pt is 8.0 mm, and
    the study's 9.6 mm is 58 pt. The game's eye button is 46 px (7.7 mm), its RELOAD 54 px (9.0 mm), its FIRE 76 px
    on a phone (12.7 mm; 82 px on wider screens), in a row above the bottom safe area (shell.html, mine).
- **Where they go.** Apple: "Place frequently used buttons near a player's thumb, avoiding the circular regions where
  players expect movement and camera input to happen. Place secondary controls, like menus, at the top of the
  screen"; keep clear of "the Home indicator or Dynamic Island" [HIG]. Microsoft, from over 200 console games given
  touch controls: "Typically, the player's thumbs are over the left and right wheels for most of gameplay. Theese
  locations are where the primary actions for your game should go"; "the regions of comfortable movement are often
  almost radial from where the device is being gripped"; the bottom centre "is the least frequently used area for
  controls" [MS-TOUCH].
- **How many at once.** "Touch interfaces, in contrast with gamepads, are much more constrained. Usually only two
  inputs at a time are possible by using the left and right thumb while holding a device" [MS-TOUCH].
- **What a finger does not get.** "Always include visible and tactile press states ... a glow, that they can see even
  when their finger is covering the control. Combine this press state with sound and haptics" [HIG]. Touch gives no
  click: "you need to pick up some of the slack with additional audio and visual flags on touchscreen" [TOUCH-GD];
  "precision has to be enormously forgiving" [MIKEY].

### 3.2 The schemes

**The virtual stick.** Apple: "players expect to control movement using the left side of their screen, and control
camera direction using the right side"; "opt to show a virtual thumbstick wherever the player lands their thumb
instead of a static thumbstick position"; "For camera control, opt to use direct touch to pan the camera instead of a
virtual thumbstick" [HIG]. This is what the game already does in first person: a stick born under the first touch in
the left 45 % of the screen, a 52 px throw, a look drag anywhere else (main.js `onTouchStart`, mine). The dissent is
worth knowing: a designer of touch-first games: "no floating d-pads ... They don't really work, and even games that
execute them well feel awkward and stilted" [TOUCH-GD]; a platformer's makers cut their pad to left and right only,
because every extra direction asks for feedback glass cannot give [MIKEY]. Both point the same way for this fight:
movement must never need to be exact.

**The look drag.** Needed only if the camera is the player's. Always relying on the player to control the camera is
one of the fifty camera mistakes: most players struggle to turn a camera and move a character at once, so the camera
should anticipate what they need [CAM50] (a translated write-up: the words are not the speaker's). The same talk's
other half: "It's important to always honor the player's intent. If the player wants to turn the camera one way, the
camera must turn that direction, otherwise the player will get very frustrated" [CAM-VICE] (extract). Together: a
camera that needs no touching, and that still answers a drag a little (these players have dragged to look for
nineteen chapters).

**Ability buttons with cooldowns.**
- The picture on the button is the act: "Use symbols that communicate the actions they perform ... Avoid using
  abstract shapes or controller-based naming like A, X, or R1" [HIG].
- Fewer is better: "Eliminate all unnecessary buttons"; rank every action as primary, secondary or tertiary by how
  often it is used and place it so [MS-TOUCH]. "Combine functionality into a single control ... Leverage gestures
  such as double tap and touch and hold to provide different variations of the same action" [HIG].
- Show only what is live: "You can hide controls when an action isn't available or relevant" [HIG]; Minecraft
  Dungeons shows "only basic controls such as movement and melees" at first and adds a control "when acquiring ranged
  weapon for the first time" [MS-TOUCH]. (The game's own rule says the same: an interaction with nothing to offer is
  not offered at all, v12.5.)
- The cooldown is drawn on the button itself: a dark wipe round it and, for a long one, the seconds left; this is
  common practice and not one source's rule. The game already draws one: the strain ring on the eye button (R96).

**Auto-aim and tap-to-target.** The mobile standard is that the game aims.
- Archero: "Archero's gameplay consists of two states: Move and Shoot"; shooting "is automated and targets the
  closest enemy"; its "one-touch joystick" "spawns wherever the player touches the screen and is perfect for mobile".
  The split "gives the player the right amount of control and agency"; a rival that also moves the hero for you is
  "more automated (aka not as engrossing)" [ARCHERO].
- Brawl Stars: a tap fires at the nearest target and a drag aims by hand; auto-aim is "extremely effective at close
  range" [BRAWL] (extract; the tap detail from a fan wiki I could not pin).
- Dead Cells on phones added "Auto-Hit", which attacks nearby enemies for the player [DEADCELLS] (extract). Call of
  Duty: Mobile's Simple Mode fires by itself when the crosshair is on a target [CODM] (extract).
- Soft lock (the character turns to the target by himself): "Soft-Lock allows players to move in any direction
  unrestricted, but always attacks accurately"; its weakness: "you can't tell what you're going to attack before you
  hit the button" [LOCKON] (extract). Two cures found: choose by where the player is heading as well as by distance,
  and do not flicker between two targets that are close together [TARGETSEL] (extract); and SHOW the target before
  the press (a ring under it).
- Shots that bend a little toward what was meant ("bullet magnetism") are standard on consoles; one studio writes
  that a game without it often feels buggy [WITCHFIRE][HALO-AA] (extracts).
- A tap on the thing itself: the engine already presses a hotspot when a tap lands on it (`hits(x, y)`, v14.7, mine).

**One-handed.** The portrait hits are one-thumb games: Archero [ARCHERO]; Tomb Raider Reloaded, "designed to be played
with one hand, holding my phone vertically" [TRR] (extract); Mighty DOOM, whose hero "shoots automatically" [MDOOM]
(extract). All three give the thumb ONE job (move) and take the rest away. Sky offers "One-Handed Mode and two
Two-Handed Mode settings" for a full third-person game [SKY], and a reviewer found the one-handed mode "ridiculously
unreliable in even the most basic situations" [SKY-DT] (extract). A stick plus four buttons is a two-thumb layout; it
cannot honestly be called one-handed. What CAN be promised is narrower and worth promising: the fight is winnable by
the right thumb alone, because standing still is never punished (§6) and every act is aimed by the game.

**The player who never touches the camera** is the normal player here. The answers found: a camera that needs no
touching ([CAM50], above); a fixed room camera (Luigi's Mansion, §4); the hero aimed by the game (Archero).

## 4 · The camera in small rooms

### 4.1 What the craft says

From the fifty camera mistakes (read in a Russian write-up of the talk's first half, translated: the wording is not
the speaker's) [CAM50]:

- 1: using a dynamic camera without need; a simpler fixed camera is often better.
- 2: a camera not suited to the level design; the camera and the layout must support each other.
- 4: a distance at which the line of sight is easily blocked; the closer the camera, the less often it is blocked.
- 5: letting obstacles cut in from the sides; a sudden cut to avoid them breaks the 30-degree rule.
- 7 and 10: letting the camera go inside or through an obstacle; a sphere round the lens, pushed off surfaces.
- 9: narrow things (a column) may pass in front; the player can still follow the character.
- 12: when something is behind the lens, move TOWARD the character, not sideways.
- 13: letting the near plane cut through the character.
- 18 to 20: a hard cut costs the player his bearings; show landmarks, give him time, never cross the 180-degree line.
- 22: always relying on the player to control the camera.

The engines' stock answers:

- **A boom with collision.** Unreal's spring arm "tries to maintain its children at a fixed distance from the parent,
  but will retract the children if there is a collision, and spring back when there is no collision" [UE-ARM]
  (extract).
- **Three ways to clear an obstacle.** Unity's Cinemachine: "Pull Camera Forward", "Preserve Camera Height",
  "Preserve Camera Distance" [CM-DEOCC] (extract). A user's report on the second: it "sometimes choose a lower spot
  inside my player character" [CM-BUG] (extract).
- **Fade the character** when the lens is forced against his back: "when the camera is forced really close to the
  player in enclosed spaces the player fades out so that the player model does not block the player's view"
  [VALVE-FADE] (extract).
- **Fade or cut away what is between**: swap the wall's material for a transparent one (the whole wall goes), cut a
  hole round the character with a stencil, or dither it out [HIDE-OBJ][STENCIL] (extracts). The known fault: a fade
  keyed to distance pops as the camera moves ("when player gets to close to the front wall, camera moves away and the
  wall appears again") [FADE-POP] (extract).

### 4.2 What shipped games set indoors use

| Game | Camera | What it buys | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Luigi's Mansion (all three) | Fixed, into each room through a missing wall: "the original vision was Luigi, vacuum, and the dollhouse 3D camera" [LM-GD]; "the player always looks through one of the walls to see the other three walls in the room" [LM-MW] (extract); "a fixed third-person perspective" [LM3-WP] | No camera control at all; every room is a composed picture; two bodies in one frame | "the dollhouse camera is fixed almost throughout the game" [LM-NL] (extract): the room is always seen from one side, and it must be built for that side |
| Resident Evil 2 (remake) | Over the shoulder, chosen after first-person and fixed-camera builds were tried [RE2-COG] (extract); "a tense claustrophobic camera"; it "pulls in when you get grabbed" [RE2-WCC][RE2-GS] (extracts) | Closeness, dread, the struggle with ONE enemy in the frame | Little is seen to the sides; it assumes a player who aims and turns the camera |
| Silent Hill 2 (remake) | Over the shoulder: "One of our first decisions was to change the camera to a regular over-the-shoulder third-person perspective" [SH2-PSU] (extract) | The same | A boss fight had to be re-staged because the old small stairwell was "too hard to pull off" with it [SH2-GRX] (extract) |
| Alan Wake 2 | "the camera looms in over Alan's right shoulder" [AW2-PCG] (extract) | The same | The same |
| Control | Over the shoulder, with a button to change shoulders [CONTROL-TW] (extract) | The player can clear his own view | One more control |
| God of War (2018) | "an over-the-shoulder camera", one unbroken shot [GOW-WP] | "a more intimate relationship with the character" [CAM-80LV] | "it's tough to see enemies behind you" [GOW-DT] (extract); a critic: most deaths "came from enemies attacking off camera" and the warning arrows were unreliable [GOW-GW] (extract); its makers keep enemies in known quarters round the player [CAM-SL] |
| Bayonetta (cited for its rule) | (its camera was not looked up) | "enemies who are off-screen cannot attack" [CAM-SL] | Enemies must wait their turn |
| Hades, Diablo | High fixed three-quarter view (well known; I opened no source that describes it) | The whole fight in one frame; one stick is enough | No ceilings, and near walls cut or faded (the techniques of §4.1) |
| The Last of Us | Close third person | Not found: the talk I located covers aiming, not rooms | — |

The pattern: the over-the-shoulder cameras belong to games about ONE character, aiming at ONE thing, played with two
sticks. The games with two bodies in a room, or with many enemies and one stick, pull the camera up and fix it.

### 4.3 The arithmetic of this flat on an upright phone (mine)

The game's lens is 72° tall. On a screen of 390 × 844 that is only **37° wide**. On a 16:9 desktop it is 104° wide.

| What the lens sees | Upright phone | Desktop 16:9 |
|---|---|---|
| Width seen, per metre of distance | 0.67 m | 2.58 m |
| Height seen, per metre of distance | 1.45 m | 1.45 m |

**Over the shoulder, two metres behind him:** a strip 1.34 m wide and 2.9 m tall. The phone spends its height on the
ceiling and the floor at his feet, and its width on his back. At three metres back (if the room has three metres to
give: its walls will usually pull the boom in) it is 2.0 m wide. A spirit a pace to either side is out of the
picture.

**Raised, looking down:** the lens 2.4 m up at one end of the room, pitched 40° down, converts that useless height
into DEPTH of floor.

| Along the floor from the lens | Width of floor seen there | A standing figure's height on the screen, about |
|---|---|---|
| 1 m | 1.55 m | 39 % |
| 2 m | 2.06 m | 29 % |
| 3 m | 2.58 m | 23 % |
| 4 m | 3.09 m | 20 % |
| 5 m | 3.61 m | 17 % |
| 6 m | 4.12 m | 15 % |

- The floor is seen from 0.6 m in front of the lens to the far wall, in one frame.
- What is NOT seen is two wedges of floor beside the lens: at one metre the picture is only 1.55 m wide, and a room
  3.65 m wide is seen at its full width only at its far wall.
- If the lens can stand 1.5 m BEHIND the near wall (the wall cut away, as in the dollhouse), the room's own floor
  begins where the picture is already 1.8 m wide, and the full width of a 3.65 m room is in frame from about 3.6 m in.
- A steeper, higher view (60° down from 5 m, the ceiling gone) sees a room whole, but a figure is then a tenth of the
  screen: too small on a phone, and it is the top of his head.
- A wider lens helps and costs straight lines at the top and bottom of the frame: at 80° tall the phone sees 42° wide
  (0.78 m per metre). The standing rule already allows it: "A cramped interior gets a wider lens on a phone" (v24.21).

**How much of the floor each camera shows** (the share of the room's floor inside the frame, lens on the middle of a
short wall, looking down the room's length; counted over a grid, mine):

| Room | Raised (2.4 m, 40° down), lens at the wall | The same, 1.5 m behind the wall | The same, and an 80° lens | Low behind a body (1.6 m, 10° down), lens at the wall | The same, 2 m into the room | Raised at the wall, desktop 16:9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Living room 3.65 × 5.15 m | 61 % | 82 % | 89 % | 46 % | 15 % | 88 % |
| Bedroom 2.85 × 4.70 m | 71 % | 92 % | 96 % | 52 % | 14 % | 88 % |
| 3 × 4 m | 63 % | 88 % | 94 % | 41 % | 5 % | 85 % |
| 4 × 6 m | 61 % | 81 % | 87 % | 50 % | 21 % | 90 % |

A camera that follows a body into the middle of the room (the fifth column) shows a seventh of the floor on a phone;
the room camera set back behind a cut wall shows four fifths or more. That difference is the whole argument.

### 4.4 Recommendation: one room camera

- **Raised three-quarter view, fixed to the room, not to the character.** About 35 to 40 degrees down, from just
  under the ceiling (or through it), on the room's long axis, behind the pair, the near wall and the ceiling cut away
  by rule. It drifts a little (a slow push along the axis, a few degrees of pan) to keep both bodies and the nearest
  spirit in frame, and it answers a drag by a few degrees and eases back (§3.2).
- **Solved for the screen it is on**, as the engine's shots already are: the points that must be in frame are named
  (both bodies, the spirits that are pressing, the doorway they come from) and the distance is found for this aspect
  (v21.8). On a desktop the same rule gives a lower, closer view, because width is no longer the limit.
- **Why it fits.**
  1. It turns the upright screen's long side into the room's depth (§4.3); over the shoulder wastes it.
  2. It needs no camera control from players who have never used a second stick ([CAM50] 22).
  3. Both bodies are in one frame, so a switch moves no camera, cuts through no wall, and costs nobody his bearings
     ([CAM50] 18 to 20). Luigi's Mansion 3, with its two bodies and its fixed room view, is the shipped example.
  4. Up on the stick is always the same way in the room, for both characters.
  5. Nothing attacks from where the player cannot see, because the frame IS the fight (Bayonetta's rule).
  6. It needs no boom, no collision and no fading of the character: the camera is placed by hand where the room is
     known to be clear.
- **Over the shoulder is kept for the films**: the short cutscenes from play (`kit.film`) at the beats (the first
  switch, the twist, the last spirit), where a composed close angle is worth a cut. The plan's "third person
  character view" is honoured in both; this choice between them should be put to him with two pictures, because
  "like final fantasy" may mean the close view to him.

### 4.5 What breaks, and what each needs

1. **The two near wedges** are unseen on a phone. No spirit may enter, wait or strike there; a body that walks into
   one makes the camera ease back or aside; spawn points are on the far half.
2. **The near wall and the ceiling must be able to vanish.** They must be their own meshes (a wall merged into the
   room's static mesh cannot be hidden alone; and hiding a mesh hides its children, v12.4). Ceiling lamps and fans go
   with the ceiling.
3. **Tall furniture near the lens** hides the floor. The room is dressed for this camera: tall things on the far and
   side walls. Anything that must stand near is cut down by rule for the fight, not faded by distance (the pop of
   [FADE-POP] (extract)).
4. **A second room** means a cut. Make it in the black or inside a film (v16.1), keep the same compass (the next
   room's camera looks the same way), and hold the stick's old meaning until it is let go ([CAM50] 18).
5. **The figures are small**: 15 to 39 % of the screen's height, a face about 30 points tall. Feeling is carried by
   the voice, the body and the light; faces belong to the films. The lighter bodies are what is drawn at this
   distance; the full models (within 2.2 m, v26.5) are for the films.
6. **A raised lens shows what first person never did**: the tops of cupboards, the fan, and above all the FLOOR, which
   becomes most of the picture. It needs dressing and painted contact shadows (v9.3). The third-person bodies are
   then judged by every body rule (feet on the ground, v26.3; a walk paced from its own take, v26.0).
7. **A desktop sees 104° of width**: the cut edge of the missing wall and the rooms beyond the doorways are in the
   picture. They must be built or masked for the wide view too (v21.19).
8. **Spirit Vision** is a full-screen pass built round his eyes; from a raised lens its wave must start at his body,
   and its negative world must still let the gold of the ring read as gold.
9. **The passage** (1.55 m wide) and the kitchen cannot hold this camera or two bodies and nine spirits. The fight
   belongs in the living room; a bedroom can take a second, shorter stage.
10. **The hand-over** from and to first person: the hands and their pass, the lens, his look, the HUD; all changed in
    the black, never on screen (§9, C6).

## 5 · Teaching a new control scheme in the middle of a game

### 5.1 What is known

- **Teach by doing, one thing at a time, in few words.** The ten tips of the Plants vs. Zombies talk [PVZ]: blend the
  tutorial into the game ("We strive to make it not feel like a tutorial at all"); have players do, not read; spread
  the teaching out; get the player to do it ONCE; use fewer words (about eight on the screen at a time: "Click on a
  seed packet to pick it up"); unobtrusive messages; ADAPTIVE messages (tips only to players doing the wrong thing);
  no noise (a message that does not matter teaches players to stop reading); let the look teach; use what people
  already know.
- **Tutorials earn their place only where the game is complex.** A study of eight designs across three games and
  45,000 players: tutorials "increased play time by as much as 29%" in the most complex game and "did not
  significantly improve player engagement in the two simpler games" [CHI12]. A two-body fight with a new camera is
  the complex case.
- **Teach in safety, inside the fight.** A boss fight is "a test" and "a story" in eight beats; the third, Business
  as Usual, is the baseline: "If you must teach the player something new, teach it during this beat"; the skills a
  boss tests are taught BEFORE him [BOSS].
- **The world makes the switch necessary.** FF VII Remake's first boss "acts very much as a tutorial": it climbs out
  of the swordsman's reach, so the gunner is needed [FF7R-SCORP] (extract). Revelations 2: the helper sees what the
  fighter cannot [REV2-GR].
- **Controls appear when they are first needed.** Minecraft Dungeons on touch shows movement and melee first, and
  adds a button when its ability is first gained [MS-TOUCH]. Mario Odyssey shows a captured creature's controls at the
  foot of the screen at the capture, with the full list kept in a menu [SMO] (extract).
- **A new kit can be taught in minutes if it is small.** "it's basically like teaching a player a new game in an
  hour", level after level; the fix when one did not work: "Design by subtraction" [ITT-TALK] (extract).
- **Pausing for a prompt: makers disagree.** One team's tutorial "does NOT pause the game or stop the player"; a
  second found pause-tooltip-unpause "felt completely wrong" and used slow motion; a third would lock the player "out
  of full gameplay until they have demonstrated the basic element" [TUT-DIS][TUT-MOK][TUT-TUTS] (extracts). Big games
  slow or stop time while a command is chosen (§1.2, 7).
- **Never by text alone.** "Ensure no essential information (especially instructions) is conveyed by text alone",
  reinforce with visuals and/or speech; "Include interactive tutorials" [GAG].
- **This engine's own tools** (mine): a brief with a START and a looping picture before anything is timed (v8.7,
  v18.2); a film that stops its own clock until an event resolves (`eventWait`, v23.0); a film that cannot be skipped
  the first time (v23.0); objectives completed by a press (v21.9); captions on wall time (v21.30).

### 5.2 A staged introduction that fits two to four minutes (mine)

| Beat | About | What is on the screen | What he is told | Ends when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The turn | 10 s, a film | First person hands over to the room camera in the black; both bodies seen; the count: nine | One line: who he is now and that the room is his to move in | The film ends |
| First spirit: FIRE | 20 s | The stick and ONE button | "Tap FIRE." The world waits for the first tap | The first spirit is spent and bagged |
| Second: the SWITCH | 25 s | The switch button appears, pulsing; a spirit the fire cannot find | The teacher asks for his student by name; the first switch is the only way on | The student has opened his sight and the teacher has burned what it marked |
| Third: the WARD and the CHANT | 30 s | The student's other two buttons, one at a time | One line each, each naming the button's own word | A spirit held at the ward is taken |
| The ring | 15 s | The shockwave button lights for the first time, full | "Now." The first use is given, charged by what the ward held | The ring has gone through them |
| The fight | 60 to 90 s | Everything; no new control | Only adaptive lines (§5.3) | The count reaches two |
| The twist and the finish | 30 to 40 s | §6.3 | The story speaks, not the tutor | The last is bagged |

- Each new control arrives alone, with a spirit that needs it, after the one before has been used once.
- The first use of each is FORCED but never timed: the fight waits (spirits hold off) until the press. Waiting is
  safe; it is the "Business as Usual" beat.
- Words on the screen stay near eight; the voice carries the rest. Every line names the thing by the word on its
  button, never by a word of the writer's (chapter 4's R38).
- Taught this way the teaching takes about a minute and a half of the fight and is itself three of the nine.

### 5.3 Telling him to switch without nagging

What the sources say about companions who instruct:

- Valve's playtests of a companion: "players much preferred to set the pace themselves", and "especially disliked
  virtually any hint of bossiness". Her hints became "only accessible at the player's request". What she says
  unasked is about HERSELF: she "calls for help when she's mobbed" and "will ask for light". And her praise "gives
  players the satisfaction that someone witnessed and appreciated their heroics" [EP1].
- God of War Ragnarök's companions gave puzzle answers too soon; its makers on playing the finished game: "they're
  giving hints way too often"; testers had not complained; an option to "reduce the frequency" came two years later
  [RAG-PS][RAG-80][RAG-GR] (extracts).
- Zelda's fairy and sword spirit are the standing examples of a helper who interrupts; the remake of the second made
  her hints optional: "for the most part, she stays hidden unless called upon" [NAVI][FI] (extracts).
- Adaptive messaging, and no noise [PVZ].

A ladder built from them (the numbers are mine, to be tuned on the build):

1. **The world asks first.** The spirit the teacher cannot find is on the screen; the switch button is there. No
   words for about 5 seconds.
2. **The partner says what HE needs**, in his own voice and in the button's own word: a need of his, not an order.
   Once.
3. **The button itself asks**: after about 8 more seconds it pulses (chapter 4's eye button has this language
   already: a different pulse to open and to close), and he says it again in other words.
4. **The partner does a small version himself**, after about 6 more seconds, and says so kindly. The fight never
   stalls on a player who does not press. (Chapter 4 already opens his sight for him at the big moments, R96.)
5. **Praise the first time it is done right**, never after.

- Never while another line is speaking (one voice at a time, v5.30).
- Never twice for the same need in the same words; each time a need comes back, its prompt is shorter and later.
- Never as a banner that orders what cannot be followed (v12.3).
- Never a prompt for the thing he is already doing.

### 5.4 How long before it drags

No source gives a number for a fight like this. What bears on it:

- "Constant, unchanging combat is fatiguing"; "Long periods of inactivity are boring"; the game that measured it
  holds a peak for 3 to 5 seconds after it crests and then gives 30 to 45 seconds of relief [L4D].
- A boss "should build up and release tension"; its beats are a ramp with a turn in the middle [BOSS].
- Holding a finger on glass tires: "Your finger sticks to the screen, you get uncomfortable"; test at 15, 30 and 60
  minutes [TOUCH-GD]. A fight of minutes is far inside that, but a HOLD of many seconds is not free.
- The plan's own figure: two to four minutes.

A working rule (mine): no stretch of the same activity longer than about a minute; a breath of 10 to 15 seconds after
the teaching and before the last third; the whole between three and four minutes for a player who keeps up; and a
mercy for one who does not (after about four and a half minutes the remaining spirits weaken, so the count runs down
by itself and the fight ends inside five).

## 6 · Forgiving action

### 6.1 The toolbox

| Tool | Who uses it, and how | For this fight |
|---|---|---|
| No lost fight; a cost that is seen | LEGO: "they lose studs (as opposed to lives)", no game over [LEGO-WP]. Kirby's Epic Yarn: a hit scatters beads; the aim was that players "really feel regret when you run into enemies" and "a strong sense of achievement" for a clean level [KIRBY]; its producer had been "extremely opposed to the idea of an action game where the game wasn't over when an enemy got you" [KIRBY-WP] (extract). It Takes Two: "respawn quickly" [ITT-WP] | A spirit that gets through costs something on the screen and on the card (sanity or awareness: minigame events never move wisdom, v7.0), and is said by the teacher. | The warning from the game that removed death | Prince of Persia (2008): the partner always catches him. | A partner who cannot be lost | "he cannot be knocked out or killed" [GOW-WP]; "The secondary character cannot die" [NIER-AI] (extract) | Neither body can be lost. A body that is overwhelmed is covered by the other |

| Pacing that bends, not difficulty | "Algorithm adjusts pacing, not difficulty": estimate how hard-pressed the player is; "If intensity is too high, remove major threats for awhile" [L4D] | A simple pressure number (spirits near, hits taken, seconds since the last good act) decides when the next spirit comes in, never how hard it hits |

### 6.2 How it stays tense when it cannot be lost

- **Something else is at stake.** Chapter 4 put a person in the room whose bar was the fight. Here: the devotee's
  home, the count of nine, and the student himself (the case file has his own guardian come in to protect him). A
  visible thing that worsens when the player is careless and mends when he is not.
- **The count is the clock.** Nine, eight, seven: a number that only the player's acts move is tenser than a timer
  and tests no speed.
- **Engineered near misses** (the table's fourth row): the ward that holds at the last moment, the ring that comes
  ready just as they close in.
- **The partner's voice.** He asks for help when he is pressed [EP1]; he says when it was close.
- **Sound that tightens**: the bed closes in (the engine's `kit.hush` and `kit.pulse` exist), the music adds a layer
  per spirit taken (§6.3).
- **The cost is real on the card.** The rank is decided by how it was played, as in every chapter.

### 6.3 Keeping a climax a climax

The eight beats of a boss fight [BOSS], laid on this one:

| Beat | In the source | Here (mine) |
|---|---|---|
| Build-up | The boss is promised and its skills taught before it | The cleansing in first person; the nine felt before they are seen |
| Reveal | "start the fight off with a BANG" | The turn to third person: the room seen whole for the first time, the count appears |
| Business as usual | The baseline; the place to teach | The first three spirits (§5.2) |
| Escalation | "The difficulty, intensity, and drama begin to rise here" | Two at a time, the kinds that need a different first step, the first full ring |
| Midpoint | A turn: a false victory, a false defeat or a transformation, with a breather | The case file's own: his Lersi comes in and he goes into trance. For a short stretch the student cannot be played: he chants by himself, warded, and the player is the teacher alone, with the staff "trembling ... as if it were spitting fireballs". The loss of the switch is the twist; getting it back is the relief |
| It's ON | "the boss has access to the full range of his attacks" | The last three together; everything the player has, at once |
| The kill | The player is shown the win; it "doesn't need to actually die" | The ninth into the bag: one hold, slow, the room's sound falling away |
| Victory | "explicitly about rewarding the player" | The bag tied; "the air in the house felt lighter"; first person again |

The tools for the last two:

- **A finishing act that cannot be failed.** A hold that fills, never a mash and never a timed press [GAG]; the
  engine's `yield` is this.
- **The last hit slowed and brought close.** Peggle slows and zooms on the last peg, with a drum roll into "Ode to
  Joy" (its makers "focused on improving the presentation", "adding a zoom on the current ball as it neared the last
  orange peg") [PEGGLE-WP][PEGGLE-PCG] (extracts); Smash Bros. uses a "Finish Zoom" "when an attack will likely
  result in a game-winning KO", with its own sound [SSB-ZOOM] (extract). Feedback of this kind is "superfluous from a
  strictly mechanical perspective" and exists "to make actions feel significant" [JUICE] (extract). Here it is a
  short film from play, so its clock is the film's.
- **Music that is built with the fight.** Layers added to a track that keeps playing; sections changed at a phrase's
  end; short stings on the beat for single events [MUSIC-AK][MUSIC-WR] (extracts). Here: a layer for each stage, a
  sting for each spirit bagged, the vocal or the gong held back for the last three, and silence under the last hold.
  (That one well-known action game brings its vocals in at a boss's last phase is from memory.)
- **Relief is part of it.** "Unpredictable peaks and valleys of intensity create a powerfully compelling ...
  experience" [L4D]: the breath before the last third is what makes it a peak.

## 7 · The partner's AI

### 7.1 The rules, each from a shipped partner

1. **He keeps working, at a lower rate.** FF VII Remake's AI fills its gauge slower [FF7R-TAC] (extract). Ellie fires
   only if "the player has instigated a weapons-free situation" or is in danger; her shots at enemies outside the
   player's view do not hurt them; she had to be reined in because "she turned into a killing machine" [TLOU-AI].
   Atreus alone: "all he's doing is enhancing what you are already doing" [GOW-GI], yet "never passive" [GOW-PSB].
   Alyx "always hangs back a little, blasting anything that moves" [EP1].
2. **He never takes the big moment.** "Alyx more or less sits out all of our boss encounters": they are "the
   player's big hero moments", and too much help "dilutes the sense of individual accomplishment" [EP1]. The bots of
   Left 4 Dead "never use Molotovs" [L4D]. Atreus's strongest effects were moved "on to a command button" [GOW-GI].
3. **He cannot be lost, and is not babysat.** "we weren't making an escort-mission game" [GOW-GI]; "he cannot be
   knocked out or killed" [GOW-WP]; Ellie is invisible to enemies while the player is hidden, so stealth is not
   ruined by her "accidentally running out of cover" [TLOU-AI].
4. **He stays near, in the frame and out of the way.** Elizabeth is usually "goal side", just ahead of the player,
   present but not "attached at the hip" [LIZ]. Ellie's standing spots are found by rays against the walkable floor
   and scored; she was first "always to one side" and had to be fixed [TLOU-AI].
5. **When he is stuck, he is moved while nobody is looking.** "If a SurvivorBot ever gets far out of place for any
   reason, it is teleported near the team when no human is looking at it" [L4D]. One reviewer's AI partner got stuck
   in the environment [REV2-GR]: it happens in shipped games.
6. **He is predictable and fair.** "Reliable and predictable decision making"; "SurvivorBots cannot deal friendly
   fire damage"; they "prioritize human teammates over bots" [L4D]. Where a game cheats for him, it cheats "in very
   specific situations", tuned to reduce frustration [TLOU-AI].
7. **He speaks for himself, not at the player.** He asks for what he needs and praises what he saw [EP1]; out of
   sight, Elizabeth would sigh or say "hmm" to draw the player to her [LIZ].

### 7.2 For this pair (mine)

| Left alone | Does | Never does | Says |
|---|---|---|---|
| The teacher | Fires at a slow, steady pace at marked spirits only; keeps his ground near the middle of the room; turns to face what presses the student | Fire the golden shockwave; bag the LAST spirit; walk into the camera's near wedges or stand in a doorway; take a spirit the player has just marked before the player can see it burn | What he needs ("I cannot see it"), once; what was done well, once |
| The student | Goes on chanting if the chant was begun; keeps his ward up at a weaker strength; stays within a pace or two of his teacher | Open or shut Spirit Vision (its strain is the player's to spend); place a new ward; move away to fight | That he is pressed; that he sees one (which is the cue to switch to him) |

- **Idle is designed out by the case file.** Each has an act that is his when nothing else is asked: the teacher
  draws the air and tends the bag; the student chants. Neither ever simply stands.
- **A stall detector.** If the played character has done nothing useful for a set time and the partner has no valid
  act, the ladder's fourth step fires (§5.3): the fight cannot hang.
- **The partner's numbers are derived, not stored**: his rate is a share of the player's own, so a change to one
  moves both (v16.7).
- **Two bodies, one room**: each has a small ring of standing places round the other, scored by distance, by being
  in the frame, by not being between the other and a spirit, and by not being in a doorway; the two never claim the
  same place (the checklist's D2).

## 8 · Feedback

### 8.1 The rules

- **Never by one channel.** "Ensure no essential information is conveyed by sounds alone"; nor "by a fixed colour
  alone"; nor "by text alone" [GAG]. Many will play with the sound off: the picture must carry everything, and the
  sound must carry everything for the player who is looking at his thumbs.
- **There is no buzz to lean on.** The web's vibration call is listed as not implemented on iPhone and iPad
  [VIB-W3C][VIB-CIU] (extracts; one bug report in the same search claims a recent Safari does it: unconfirmed).
  Apple's own advice to "Combine this press state with sound and haptics" [HIG] is for native games; here every
  press state must work as light and sound alone, with a buzz only as an extra where a browser gives one.
- **The press is seen round the finger**, not under it: "a glow, that they can see even when their finger is
  covering the control" [HIG].
- **Playable by sound.** Overwatch's audio team was told to make a game one could "play by sound": a clean mix,
  sounds that can be placed exactly, sounds that carry game information, and voices that describe; each other player
  gets an "importance" in the mix; "every character in Overwatch has their own unique footstep sound"; an enemy's
  footsteps are louder than a friend's [OW-SOUND][OW-GD].
- **Playable without a HUD.** One game set out "to give the player all feedback without the use of an onscreen HUD",
  with voices that warn of incoming attacks [HELLBLADE] (extract).
- **Do not hide the fight to say it is going badly.** The red screen at low health takes away sight exactly when it
  is needed: in games where killing restores health, it makes enemies "harder to see": "an ouroboros of failure"
  [REDSCREEN].
- **The reaction lands on the frame its cause is seen** (this game's rule, v13.0), and a hit freezes both for an
  instant: hit stop, "a rather subtle but powerful technique" in one designer's words, holds the attacker and the
  target still on the first frame of the impact; it is not realistic, and it makes the connection felt [GOW-PSB].
  One series has lengthened that freeze over the years, from 8 frames to 15 for the same blow [SSB-HITLAG]
  (extract).

- One voice at a time (v5.30): combat calls are short, are dropped if a line is speaking, and never queue up behind
  each other.
