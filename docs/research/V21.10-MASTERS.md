# v21.10 · The five masters — the gallery's sources

The record behind episode 3 chapter 3's masters' gallery (docs/V21.10-PLAN.md).
Chad asked for "a proper accurate background documentary/story of each monk, and
the era they were born in (years), and what each were known for". Researched by a
background agent across English and Thai Wikipedia, temple biographies, Thai
newspapers (Thairath, Khaosod, Siam Rath, Silpa Wattanatham), an academic paper
(Mahachulalongkornrajavidyalaya University) and amulet references; every date
cross-checked against at least two sources where two exist. What is LEGEND is
written as legend on the page; what is a guess is written as a guess.

Year conversion: B.E. = C.E. + 543 (the sources' own rule; before 1941 the Thai
year began in April, so a January–March date can read one year lower in old-style
B.E. — Ajahn Mun's January 1870 birth is B.E. 2413 by the modern rule).

The words shipped are the chapter's (`words`: `mThuadName` … `mPhiboonStory2`),
so they are on the sheet's EPISODE 3 tab for Chad to edit.

## 1 · Luang Pu Thuad (หลวงปู่ทวด / หลวงพ่อทวด)

- Title: สมเด็จพระราชมุนีสามีรามคุณูปมาจารย์ (Somdet Phra Ratchamuni Samiramkhunupamachan). Also สมเด็จเจ้าพะโคะ (Somdet Chao Phako), หลวงปู่ทวดเหยียบน้ำทะเลจืด ("who stepped on the sea and made it fresh"). Birth name ปู (Pu); monastic name สามีราโม (Samiramo).
- Born, BY TRADITION, 1582 (B.E. 2125), 3 or 4 March; other proposed years B.E. 2124 and 2131. Died, by tradition, 6 March 1682 (B.E. 2225), aged about 100 (some say 99). Confidence medium: traditional dates, disputed.
- Birthplace Ban Wat Liap, Tambon Di Luang, Sathing Phra, Songkhla; parents Nai Hu and Nang Chan, bonded servants of the rich man Sethi Pan.
- Wat Phako (Wat Phra Ratchapraditsathan), Sathing Phra, Songkhla — his home temple, restored by him; Wat Chang Hai Rat Buranaram, Khok Pho, Pattani — listed as its first abbot; a stupa there holds his remains. Died at เมืองไทรบุรี, i.e. KEDAH (English Wikipedia confuses it with Sai Buri district, Pattani).
- Era: Ayutthaya — born under King Maha Thammarachathirat (r. 1569–1590; English Wikipedia's "Borommatrailokkanat" is wrong), titled by tradition under King Ekathotsarot, died under King Narai.
- LEGENDS: the cobra and the crystal at his cradle (a crystal said to be it is kept at Wat Phako); the sea made fresh on a storm-bound voyage to Ayutthaya; the Dhamma riddle from Lanka solved at court.
- The 1954 (B.E. 2497) amulets: made at Wat Chang Hai by its abbot Ajahn Tim — Phra Khru Wisai Sophon (Tim Dhammadharo, 1912–1969) — a DIFFERENT monk from Luang Phu Tim of Wat Lahanrai; with the layman Anan Khananurak; herbal material, given to those who helped build the new ordination hall.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luang_Pu_Thuat · https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/หลวงปู่ทวด · https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/culture/2933800 · https://gps.mcu.ac.th/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Luang-Pu-Tuad-completed.pdf · https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/วัดช้างให้ราษฎร์บูรณาราม · https://today.line.me/th/v2/article/0MJLj7G · https://en.thairath.co.th/horoscope/belief/2921121 · https://so03.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/jssr/article/view/258658 · (legends only) https://ancientamulet.com/?p=3406

## 2 · Luang Phu Tim Issariko (หลวงปู่ทิม อิสริโก), Wat Lahanrai

- Title พระครูภาวนาภิรัต (Phra Khru Phawanaphirat), 5 December 1964 (B.E. 2507). Birth name ทิม งามศรี (Tim Ngamsri).
- Born 16 June 1879 (B.E. 2422) at Ban Hua Thung Ta But, Tambon Lahan, Rayong (then Ban Khai district, now Pluak Daeng); one source gives 16 May. Died 16 October 1975 (B.E. 2518) at Wat Lahanrai, aged 96, after 69 rains. Ordained 7 June 1906 (B.E. 2449), preceptor Phra Khru Khao of Wat Thap Ma.
- Wat Lahanrai (formerly Wat Rai Wari), Tambon Nong Lalok, Ban Khai, Rayong. A younger relative of Luang Pu Sang Thao of Wat Keng Chin, whose manuals of the old protective arts he studied.
- Era: Rattanakosin, Rama V to Rama IX.
- Known for: austerity (one meal a day, vegetarian, few words, no wealth), years of thudong; the Phra Khun Paen Phong Prai Kuman amulets of B.E. 2515–2517 (1972–74), made for the consecration of the temple's ordination boundary; the powder's ingredients are amulet LORE, unverified.
- Sources: https://www.blockdit.com/posts/67d3cf80a0623b9cf66c7d97 · https://www.luangphor.com/?p=1095 · https://travel.trueid.net/detail/PKejOl23PLAx · https://www.thairath.co.th/horoscope/belief/2572295 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluak_Daeng_district · (snippets) https://www.khaosod.co.th/newspaper-column/amulets/news_4314304 · https://siamrath.co.th/n/359524

## 3 · Somdej Toh — Somdet Phra Phutthachan (To Phrommarangsi) (สมเด็จพระพุฒาจารย์ (โต พฺรหฺมรํสี))

- Born Thursday 17 April 1788 (B.E. 2331) at Ban Kai Chan, Tha Ruea, Ayutthaya. Died Saturday 22 June 1872 (B.E. 2415), aged 84, at Wat Intharawihan (then Wat Bang Khun Phrom Nai), Bangkok. Weekdays check out.
- Abbot of Wat Rakhang Khositaram 1852–1872; declined rank under Rama III; Phra Thammakitti 1852, Phra Thepkawi 1854, Somdet Phra Phutthachan 1864. Novice at about 12; monk 1807.
- Era: early Rattanakosin, Rama I to Rama V.
- LEGEND: his father royal (Rama I or Rama II); the subduing of the ghost Mae Nak.
- Known for: the Phra Somdej amulets (head of the Benjapakee); the Chinabanchon chant — REFINED AND POPULARISED, not composed (thought to come from Lanna); plain, witty preaching and fearless lessons to kings (the stories are legend; NOT Mongkut's preceptor — Thanissaro calls him his "older brother monk"); the giant Buddhas at Wat Ket Chaiyo and Wat Intharawihan (the 32 m standing image, navel-high at his death).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somdet_To_Brahmaramsi · https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/สมเด็จพระพุฒาจารย์_(โต_พฺรหฺมรํสี) · https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/thanissaro/toh.html · https://en.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/amulet/2913510 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinapanjara · https://en.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/amulet/2927530

## 4 · Luang Pu Mun Bhuridatto (หลวงปู่มั่น ภูริทตฺโต), Ajahn Mun

- Born Thursday 20 January 1870 (B.E. 2413) at Ban Kham Bong, Khong Chiam (now Si Mueang Mai), Ubon Ratchathani; birth name Mun Kaenkaew. Ordained 12 June 1893 at Wat Liap, Ubon (Bhuridatto, "one who gives wisdom"). Died 11 November 1949 (B.E. 2492) at Wat Pa Sutthawat, Sakon Nakhon, aged 79, after 56 rains.
- Founded the Thai Forest (Kammatthana) Tradition with Ajahn Sao Kantasilo (1861–1941); decades of dhutanga in Isan, Laos and Burma, about twelve years in Chiang Mai and Chiang Rai; disciples include Ajahn Maha Bua, Ajahn Thate, Ajahn Lee (and Ajahn Chah studied under him).
- UNESCO associated itself with the 150th anniversary of his birth (2020).
- Era: Rattanakosin, Rama V to Rama IX.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajahn_Mun · https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/หลวงปู่มั่น_ภูริทตฺโต · https://en.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/culture/2933647 · https://luangpumun.org/main_e.html · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Forest_Tradition · https://www.pattayamail.com/thailandnews/culture-ministry-honors-venerated-monk-phra-ajarn-mun-284894

## 5 · Luang Pu Phiboon (หลวงปู่พิบูลย์), Wat Phra Thaen, Ban Daeng, Udon Thani

- The man in Chad's photograph, confirmed: it is on bankhundej.blogspot.com's "ประวัติหลวงปู่พิบูลย์" (https://bankhundej.blogspot.com/2011/09/blog-post.html), captioned "หลวงปู่พิบูลย์ วัดพระแท่น … พิบูลย์รักษ์ จ.อุดรธานี".
- Born พิบูลย์ แซ่ตัน (Phiboon Sae Tan — his Chinese father's clan name) at Ban Phra Chao, Tambon Ma-ue, Thawat Buri, Roi Et; father Nai Sa Sae Tan, a Chinese merchant; mother Sopha. NO BIRTH DATE IS RECORDED (Silpa Wattanatham); the Isaan Record PRESUMES the reign of Rama IV (1851–1868). A devotional claim of 135 years is legend.
- Married, raised an adopted daughter, gave his property to her family and ordained; wandered alone across the Mekong to the caves of Laos; founded Wat Ko Kaeo Ko Ket (Kumphawapi), then Wat Phra Thaen (Tambon Ban Daeng, named for an ancient laterite Buddha pedestal) and helped lay out Ban Daeng — roads, land shares, a rice bank, a buffalo bank.
- Detained: questioned in the mid-1920s (legend: caged and sunk at Ko Si Chang for seven days); from about 1931 (B.E. 2474) confined at Wat Phothisomphon, Udon Thani, for about fifteen years — the state saw a possible "phi bun" (holy-man rebel) in a monk keeping the old Lao-style customs during the reform of the Isan Sangha. Died there in 1946 (B.E. 2489). Cremated 1961 (B.E. 2504) at Ban Daeng. Phibun Rak district (1992 / 1997) is named after him.
- LEGENDS: tamed crocodiles, a sacred staff left for him by a mysterious stranger, prophecies.
- Coins: the teardrop coin of about B.E. 2485 (1942), the only one said to be blessed in his lifetime; the B.E. 2504 (1961) cremation coin showing him standing with his staff; later issues.
- Sources: https://bankhundej.blogspot.com/2011/09/blog-post.html · https://bankhundej.blogspot.com/2011/09/blog-post_8530.html · https://today.line.me/th/v3/article/1DX2oRE (Silpa Wattanatham, เชิดชาย บุตดี, July 2013) · https://theisaanrecord.co/2018/02/25/historical-memories-of-thai-laos-politics/ · https://www.108prageji.com/หลวงปู่พิบูลย์/ · https://www.blockdit.com/posts/636092ea9bc71ab629757170 · https://dhammakaya.tv/ประวัติหลวงปู่พิบูลย์-2/ · https://ampoljane.com/2009/06/17/1-2/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phibun_Rak_district

## Put to Chad, not changed

- Episode 1's item text for the LP Phiboon amulet (his own words) says "born during the reign of King Rama 4". The sources say no birth date was recorded and Rama IV is a researcher's presumption; "believed to have been born in the reign of King Mongkut (Rama IV)" would be the safer wording. "Second Batch" matches no source's numbering by that name: the amulet trade counts the 1942 teardrop coin as the first batch (so the second is the 1961 cremation coin, which shows him with his staff, as in the photograph); the temple's own numbering would make it the 1964 coin. His call which, and whether to change the words.
