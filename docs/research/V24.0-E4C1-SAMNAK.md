# v24.0 · The samnak — what the sources say

Research note for episode 4 chapter 1, *The Master I Did Not Know* (docs/V24.0-E4C1-PLAN.md, §3, §4 and §6). Written
27 Oct 2026. Nothing in the game or the plan was changed by this note.

**The job:** check every cultural detail the chapter uses, correct what is wrong, and supply the exact words for the
chant and the Thai lines. The game's standing rule sets the tone: no practice and no practitioner is ever the villain,
so every custom is described the way the people who keep it describe it.

**How it was done.**
- The backbone is the Royal Institute dictionary (พจนานุกรม ฉบับราชบัณฑิตยสถาน พ.ศ. ๒๕๕๔), queried through its own
  search at [dictionary.orst.go.th](https://dictionary.orst.go.th/) (the Office of the Royal Society, "ORST" below). Its
  entries cannot be linked one by one, so each is named.
- Then: the Siam Society, peer-reviewed papers, Thai government pages, Thai news (Thairath, Matichon, Daily News, Thai
  Post, Bangkok Biz News, Thai PBS World), and, marked as such, seller and lifestyle blogs. Thai was read in the
  original.
- Every URL below was opened. A few Thai pages that the summarising tool refused were downloaded directly and read as
  HTML, so their quotes are verbatim. Pages that refused, timed out or sat behind a check are listed in §13 and support
  nothing here.
- Each claim was also read for what would refute it. Where a source pulls the other way, it is under **Second
  reading**.

**Labels.** **VERIFIED**: two independent sources agree. **CORRECTED**: the plan's version is off, and the accurate one
is given. **UNSURE**: one source, sources that disagree, or none. **[BELIEF]**: what devotees or practitioners
believe; true as a report of belief, not proven.

**Still open:** Q4 of the plan (Master Zavier checking the Thai lines) is not replaced by this note.

## 0 · The short answer

- **Samnak** — VERIFIED. It is the right word for a master's place. Never letter his gate สำนักสงฆ์, which is a monks'
  place.
- **The pao** — VERIFIED. The Royal Institute dictionary defines it: reciting a katha, then blowing onto the middle of
  the head "for metta-mahaniyom", to be liked and favoured. That is exactly a trader's blessing. [BELIEF]
- **The teacher's offering** — VERIFIED for what is brought: flowers, incense, candles and money in an envelope.
  "Put what you like" is the custom. The **amount is UNSURE**: the only sourced figure is a symbolic ฿24, for a Sak
  Yant.
- **Greeting** — VERIFIED. Kneel, legs folded to one side with the feet away, head lower than his, and a wai coming
  and going. He returns it.
- **Nang Kwak** — VERIFIED. She faces out to the shop front or door, on her own shelf at eye level or higher. Water
  daily, a red drink, garlands.
- **Takrut — CORRECTED.** It is worn on a cord at the waist, neck or upper arm. "For the till" is in no source.
- **Pha yant** — VERIFIED. Framed on the wall facing the shop front, lower than the Buddha shelf.
- **The chant** — Namo ×3, then the short Nang Kwak katha *เอหิ จิตตัง มหาลาภัง ปิยัง มะมะ มามา* (two independent
  sources). It is a Thai folk formula in Pali-sounding words, not canonical or grammatical Pali.
- **Day colours** — VERIFIED. **Add Wednesday night** (Rahu, 18:00–05:59): a smoky near-black.
- **The takhian tree** — VERIFIED. Dresses hang on the tree and on a rail; three- or seven-colour cloths go round the
  trunk.
- **Landscape** — VERIFIED. Sugar palms on paddy dykes, famously in Phetchaburi (Suphan Buri and Nakhon Pathom
  UNSURE). Late-afternoon storms, mid-May to mid-October.
- **Houses, mor hom, pha khao ma, luk thung** — VERIFIED, with small notes.
- **Phrases** — VERIFIED, with two particle fixes.

## 1 · A samnak consultation

### 1.1 The word "samnak" — VERIFIED

**Accurate:** สำนัก (sǎm-nák) is a place of study and training under a teacher, and Thais use it for a lay master's
establishment. This master is a layman (ฆราวาส). A monks' place is a สำนักสงฆ์, which must not appear on his gate.

- ORST, สำนัก (2): "แหล่งศึกษาอบรม เช่น สำนักวิปัสสนาวัดปากน้ำ" — "a place of study and training, e.g. the
  meditation centre of Wat Paknam."
- ORST, สำนักสงฆ์: "วัดที่ยังไม่ได้รับพระราชทานที่วิสุงคามสีมา" — "a wat not yet granted its consecrated
  boundary."
- ORST, ฆราวาส: "คนผู้อยู่ครองเรือน, คนทั่ว ๆ ไปที่ไม่ใช่บรรพชิตหรือนักบวช" — "a householder; anyone not ordained."
- In use for a lay master's place: [a Chulalongkorn thesis on "สำนักสักยันต์อาจารย์หนู กันภัย"](https://digital.car.chula.ac.th/chulaetd/51630)
  (Wannipa Chuanchom, 2010).

### 1.2 The queue and the wait — UNSURE (the detail)

**Accurate:** people do queue and wait their turn at a well-known master's place. A number is called with คิว (khiu,
"queue"). A whiteboard of numbers is plausible, but no source shows one.

- [Matichon, 9 Apr 2017](https://www.matichon.co.th/local/news_523758): at a lay astrologer-master's in Kalasin
  (โหรมนตรี พุ่มทิพย์, "อาจารย์อ๊อด ผ้าขาว"), "มาเข้าคิวรอทำพิธีเป็นจำนวนมาก" — "many came and queued, waiting for
  the rites": blessings, readings and Sak Yant.
- ORST, คิว (2): "แถวตามลำดับก่อนหลัง เช่น เข้าคิว" — "a line in order of arrival, as in 'to queue'."

### 1.3 The offering to the teacher — VERIFIED (what), UNSURE (how much)

**Accurate:** the visitor brings the core things of Thai worship (flowers, incense, candles; popped rice is the
fourth), plus money for the teacher, usually in an envelope. The money is ค่าครู or ค่ายกครู ("the teacher's fee");
the old word is กำนล. "Put what you like" (ตามศรัทธา, "as your faith moves you") fits the custom. No reliable typical
sum was found. The one sourced figure is a symbolic ฿24.

- ORST, เครื่องบูชา: "ใช้ของ ๔ อย่างเป็นสำคัญ คือ ข้าวตอก ดอกไม้ ธูป เทียน" — "chiefly four things: popped rice,
  flowers, incense, candles."
- ORST, ยกครู: "…นำดอกไม้ ธูป เทียน หญ้าแพรก ดอกมะเขือ ไปบูชาครู" — "…bringing flowers, incense, candles, Bermuda
  grass and eggplant flowers to honour the teacher." This is the rite of becoming a pupil; the yearly rite brings "a
  washbowl, white cloth and money".
- ORST, กำนล: "เงินค่าคำนับครู" — "money paid in respect to a teacher."
- [Ramathibodi Hospital, Mahidol University, "การสักที่เกี่ยวเนื่องกับความเชื่อ"](https://www.rama.mahidol.ac.th/ramalaser/th/knowledge/tattoo4)
  (compiled by Dr Somsak Tanrattanakorn): "เริ่มด้วยหาดอกไม้ ธูปเทียน และค่ายกครู ๒๔ บาท มาขึ้นครูกับพระอาจารย์ที่สัก"
  — "it begins with flowers, incense, candles and a teacher's fee of 24 baht, to present yourself to the master who
  tattoos." That master is a monk, and the figure is old and symbolic.
- **A word to avoid:** ORST, ปัจจัย (2) says money called ปัจจัย is "มักใช้แก่ภิกษุสามเณร" — "usually for monks and
  novices". For a lay master, say ค่าครู.

### 1.4 Greeting and sitting before a master — VERIFIED

**Accurate:** the junior greets first with a wai and the senior returns it at once. Before a revered person who sits
on a mat or a platform, you approach on your knees and sit with your legs folded to one side (พับเพียบ), feet pointing
away and head kept lower than his. The three-fold prostration is for the Buddha. To a teacher you bow once. You wai
when you receive something from him, and again when you leave.

- [Phya Anuman Rajadhon, "Thai Traditional Salutation", *Journal of the Siam Society* 49.2 (1961)](https://thesiamsociety.org/knowledge-hub/uploads/research/528/665c8ae5db4e5.pdf):
  - "The person who is inferior in age or rank … initiates such a movement of the hands and the person receiving the
    salutation immediately reciprocates."
  - "If a high personage is sitting on a rug or mat in the Thai traditional style, he should be approached on the
    knees."
  - The *krap* "is expressed thrice" as worship of the Triple Gem. To "revered parents, teacher or mentor" the *mop
    krap* "is done once only".
  - "When a person is receiving anything from a senior, he will raise his hands as a 'wai'."
- [Thailand Foundation, "Feet Low, Head High", 16 Feb 2024](https://thailandfoundation.or.th/?p=518467): the feet are
  "the lowest and least pure part"; "it is rude to point one's feet, especially the sole, at other people"; footwear
  "should always be removed" on entering a house.
- ORST, พับเพียบ: "อาการที่นั่งพับขาให้ปลายเท้าไปทางเดียวกัน" — "sitting with the legs folded so both feet point the
  same way." ORST, รับไหว้: "ไหว้ตอบ" — "to return a wai."

### 1.5 What a master asks — VERIFIED

**Accurate:** a Thai reading commonly starts from the date of birth, and the day of the week counts on its own,
because each day has its planetary deity (and its colour, §4). "เกิดวันอะไร" ("what day were you born?") is a natural
first question. Many readers also ask the full date and the time.

- [Thairath, 8 Sep 2023](https://www.thairath.co.th/scoop/theissue/2723359): fortune-tellers work "ผ่านวิธีการต่างๆ
  เช่น การดูลายมือ การเปิดไพ่ การดูวันเดือนปีเกิด การดูลักษณะรูปหน้า" — "by reading palms, turning cards, reading
  the date of birth, reading the face."
- ORST, ทักษา: the eight planets of Thai astrology, each with its direction and number (Sun 1 … Rahu 8). The days of
  the week bear the planets' names.
- [Thairath, 16 Mar 2023](https://www.thairath.co.th/horoscope/belief/2581801): the nine planetary deities as "เทวดาประจำวันเกิด" — "the deities of the day you were born". [BELIEF]

### 1.6 How a reading about a business is given — VERIFIED (in outline)

**Accurate:** the master reads from the birth data (sometimes from palms or cards), names what he sees, and may
suggest a remedy: a charm, an offering or merit-making. Practitioners themselves tell clients to think before they
believe.

- Thairath 2023 (above) for the methods. [Matichon 2017](https://www.matichon.co.th/local/news_523758): a lay master
  giving readings ("ดูดวง") beside blessings and Sak Yant.
- [Thairath, 22 Dec 2022](https://www.thairath.co.th/scoop/interview/2584823), interviewing Amarin Suksamai, who
  studies astrology and the occult arts. Charms for trade "มีทั้งผ้ายันต์ ตะกรุด … นางกวัก กุมารทอง" — "include pha
  yant, takrut … Nang Kwak, kumanthong". And: "ก่อนจะเชื่อสิ่งใด ต้องใช้สติในการไตร่ตรอง" — "before you believe
  anything, think it through mindfully", because many beliefs online are made for profit. He also warns against
  charms made from grave materials. Nothing on the chapter's tray is of that kind.

### 1.7 The pao (เป่า) — VERIFIED

**Accurate:** the master recites a katha under his breath, then blows onto the crown of the client's bowed head
(เป่ากระหม่อม, bpào grà-màwm). The Royal Institute dictionary gives the purpose as metta-mahaniyom (being liked and
favoured), which is what a trader asks for. It closes a blessing, while the client's hands are joined in a wai.
[BELIEF]

- ORST, เป่ากระหม่อม: "ร่ายมนตร์คาถาแล้วเป่าลงกลางศีรษะเพื่อให้เกิดเมตตามหานิยมเป็นต้น" — "to recite a mantra or
  katha, then blow onto the middle of the head, to bring about metta-mahaniyom and the like."
- ORST, เป่ามนตร์: "เสกคาถาแล้วเป่าลงไปที่สิ่งใดสิ่งหนึ่งหรือส่วนใดส่วนหนึ่งของร่างกายมีกระหม่อมเป็นต้น" — "to recite
  a katha and blow it onto a thing, or a part of the body such as the crown."
- [Thai Tabloid, 30 Oct 2023](https://thaitabloid.com/archives/152414), on Luang Pu Khram: visitors came "ให้หลวงปู่เจิมหน้าผาก รดน้ำมนต์ เป่ากระหม่อมให้" — "for him to anoint their foreheads, pour holy water and blow on their
  crowns."
- [T-News, 26 Feb 2017](https://www.tnews.co.th/variety/302983), on Luang Pho Thong Suk of Phetchaburi: "ลมปากที่ท่านเป่ากระหม่อมให้กับศิษย์ยานุศิษย์ทั้งหลาย" (spelled so in the source) — "the breath he blew onto his disciples'
  crowns", believed to bring good fortune and protection. [BELIEF]
- Why the head: Anuman Rajadhon (1961): "The Thai deems his head to be sacred", the seat of the *khwan*.
- **Second reading:** no source fixes the number of blows. Three is the plan's staging, not a rule.

### 1.8 Holy water — VERIFIED (optional)

**Accurate:** น้ำมนต์ (náam-mon) is water consecrated in a Buddhist rite, to bathe in, drink or sprinkle. A blessing
may end with it poured or sprinkled. Optional for this chapter.

- ORST, น้ำมนต์: "นํ้าที่เข้าพิธีทางพระพุทธศาสนาเพื่อให้อาบ กิน หรือประพรม เป็นต้น ถือกันว่าเป็นมงคล" — "water
  that has been through a Buddhist rite, to bathe in, drink or sprinkle; held to be auspicious."
- Thai Tabloid 2023 (above): "รดน้ำมนต์" alongside the pao.

## 2 · Shop-fortune items

### 2.1 Nang Kwak (นางกวัก) — VERIFIED

**Who she is:** a charm and a folk goddess of trade. She is a woman seated with her legs folded aside, a money bag in
her left hand, her right hand raised to beckon customers and money in. Her origin is unclear. The katha calls her the
daughter of ปู่เจ้าเขาเขียว, the Lord Grandfather of the Green Mountain. One story makes her Suphawadi, a merchant's
daughter in India; Thai Wikipedia judges that a later invention. [BELIEF]

**How merchants keep her:** on her own shelf, facing **out** toward the shop front or entrance, at eye level or
higher, never by the toilet or under the stairs. She gets water every day (changed daily), a red drink (some add a
green one), fresh garlands and fruit weekly. Sources give 5, 9 or 16 incense sticks. [BELIEF]

- ORST, นางกวัก: "เครื่องรางชนิดหนึ่ง ทำเป็นรูปหญิงนั่งพับเพียบ มือซ้ายถือถุงเงินวางบนตัก มือขวายกขึ้นเสมอตา แสดงอาการกวักมือ … ทำขึ้นด้วยความเชื่อว่าทำให้ค้าขายดี" — "a kind of charm: a woman seated with legs folded aside, the
  left hand holding a money bag on her lap, the right hand raised to eye level, beckoning … made in the belief that it
  makes trade good."
- [Thairath, 16 Jun 2024](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/culture/2793400): "เริ่มจากตั้งหิ้งบูชาหันหน้าออกบริเวณนอกร้าน เตรียมน้ำและเครื่องบูชาสำหรับถวายทุกวัน และเตรียมผลไม้สำหรับถวายอย่างน้อยอาทิตย์ละครั้ง" — "set up
  her shelf facing out of the shop; water and offerings every day, fruit at least once a week." A hand raised above
  the mouth means "กินไม่หมด", more than you can eat.
- [Wongnai, 25 Jul 2022](https://www.wongnai.com/articles/thai-amulet-for-business): "ตั้งนางกวักไว้ที่ร้าน โดยหันหน้าออกไปที่หน้าร้าน" — "keep Nang Kwak in the shop, facing out to the shop front."
- [Thairath English, 15 Jul 2026](https://en.thairath.co.th/horoscope/belief/2946465): she "should face outward toward
  the shop front or entrance", "at eye level or higher", never near toilets; offerings of "red soda, clean water,
  auspicious fruits … fresh flower garlands"; "5 or 9 incense sticks".
- [Grab Thailand merchant blog, 17 Jul 2024](https://www.grab.com/th/merchant-blog/%E0%B8%84%E0%B9%89%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%82%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%9A%E0%B8%B9%E0%B8%8A%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%B0%E0%B9%84%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%94%E0%B8%B5/):
  "น้ำเขียว น้ำแดง น้ำเปล่า มาลัยสด อย่าได้ขาด" — "green drink, red drink, plain water, fresh garlands; never let them
  run out." Incense 9 or 16.
- Origins:
  - [Thai Wikipedia, นางกวัก](https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/นางกวัก): "คติการนับถือนางกวักไม่เคยมีในประเทศอินเดีย
    เข้าใจว่าคงแต่งเรื่อง … เพิ่มความศักดิ์สิทธิ์" — "Nang Kwak was never worshipped in India; the story was
    probably composed … to add sanctity."
  - [Saran Thongpan, *Sarakadee*, 28 Nov 2018](https://www.sarakadee.com/2018/11/28/nang-kwak/): her origin is
    unclear and she is best read as a local folk deity. The oldest small figures date from the late Ayutthaya period
    and stood among market stalls.

### 2.2 Takrut (ตะกรุด) — CORRECTED

**Accurate:** a takrut is a thin sheet of metal (or palm leaf) inscribed with a yant and a katha, rolled into a tube
and **worn on a cord at the waist, the neck or the upper arm**. Takrut consecrated for trade exist. **Keeping one in
the till is in no source found.**

- ORST, ตะกรุด: "เครื่องรางอย่างหนึ่ง ทำด้วยโลหะหรือใบลานเป็นต้น โดยปรกติลงคาถาอาคมแล้วม้วนเป็นรูปกลมยาวและกลวง" —
  "a charm of metal or palm leaf, usually inscribed with spells and rolled into a long hollow tube."
- [Baker & Phongpaichit, "Protection and Power in Siam", *Southeast Asian Studies* 2.2 (2014)](https://englishkyoto-seas.org/2014/02/vol-2-no2-baker-and-pasuk/):
  "tied around the neck, waist, or upper arm."
- [Thai Wikipedia, ตะกรุด](https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/ตะกรุด): worn "คล้องคอหรือคาดเอว" ("hung on the neck or
  tied at the waist"); its purposes include trade and fortune. The article carries a needs-sources tag.
- Thairath 2022 (§1.6): "ตะกรุด ที่ปลุกเสกด้านโชคลาภค้าขาย" — "takrut consecrated for fortune in trade."
- **Fix:** offer it as "a takrut to wear when you open the shop."

### 2.3 Pha yant (ผ้ายันต์) for a shop — VERIFIED (wall), UNSURE (over the door)

**Accurate:** a cloth printed or drawn with a yant (Nang Kwak cloths are the common trade ones). It is framed and hung
on the shop's wall or shelf facing out to the shop front, lower than the Buddha shelf and never under the stairs. Over
a doorway is a common place for protective yants, but only weak sources say so.

- [TrueID Horoscope, 21 Aug 2026](https://horoscope.trueid.net/detail/ZO4dELA8NJLO): "ให้นำใส่กรอบให้สวยงามติดบนหิ้งหรือผนังหันหน้าออกหน้าร้าน" — "frame it and fix it on the shelf or wall facing out to the shop front"; "ต่ำกว่าหิ้งพระ" — "lower than the Buddha shelf"; never "ใต้บันได", under the stairs.
- Thairath 2022 (§1.6): "ผ้ายันต์นางกวัก มีหลายตำราโบราณสืบทอดต่อกัน" — "Nang Kwak cloths come down from many old
  texts."
- [Wikipedia, Yantra cloth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantra_cloth): cloths that protect houses, raised on the
  beams of a new house; Thai cloths dedicated to Nang Kwak.

## 3 · The chant — the Nang Kwak katha

### 3.1 The recommended text (the short version) — VERIFIED (text)

The best-sourced business blessing is the short Nang Kwak katha. Two independent publishers print it identically, and
a seller blog a third time. A Thai master begins, as nearly every Thai chant does, with the homage to the Buddha,
three times.

**Line 1, said three times**
- Thai: นะโม ตัสสะ ภะคะวะโต อะระหะโต สัมมาสัมพุทธัสสะ
- As a Thai says it: ná-moh tàt-sà phá-khá-wá-toh à-rá-hà-toh sǎm-maa-sǎm-phút-thát-sà
- Pali: *Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa*
- Meaning: "Homage to the Blessed One, the Worthy One, the Rightly Self-awakened One." —
  [*A Chanting Guide*](https://origin.dhammatalks.org/books/ChantingGuide/Section0004.html) (dhammatalks.org, the
  source of the game's v21.31 captions).

**Line 2**
- Thai: เอหิ จิตตัง มหาลาภัง ปิยัง มะมะ มามา
- As a Thai says it: ay-hì jìt-tang, má-hǎa-laa-phang, pì-yang má-má, maa-maa
- Pali-style spelling: *ehi cittaṃ mahālābhaṃ piyaṃ mama māmā*
- Meaning (this note's reading, from the dictionary glosses in §3.2; no source translates the line): "Come, hearts —
  great gain — dear to me — come, come."

**Sources for the text:**
- [KTC, "รวม 14 คาถาเรียกลูกค้าเข้าร้าน…", updated 7 Aug 2026](https://www.ktc.co.th/article/knowledge/business/prayer-for-business-growth):
  "ฉบับย่อ นะโม ตัสสะ ภะคะวะโต อะระหะโต สัมมาสัมพุทธัสสะ (3 จบ) เอหิ จิตตัง มหาลาภัง ปิยัง มะมะ มามา".
- [Thairath, 16 Jun 2024](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/culture/2793400): "คาถาบูชานางกวักแบบย่อ … (3 จบ)
  จากนั้นกล่าวคาถา 'เอหิ จิตตัง มหาลาภัง ปิยัง มะมะ มามา'".
- [Shopee blog, 27 Feb 2026](https://shopee.co.th/blog/worship-nang-kwak) (seller): the same short version.

**Romanisation:** written for this note, following the Thai reading of Pali that
[*A Chanting Guide*'s pronunciation section](https://origin.dhammatalks.org/books/ChantingGuide/Section0003.html)
describes ("v as w", "ṁ & ṅ as ng", bh as "a throaty ph"). In the Royal Institute's RTGS spelling, line 2 is "Ehi
chittang mahalaphang piyang mama mama". Tone marks: à low, â falling, á high, ǎ rising, none mid.

### 3.2 What kind of text it is — not Pali

Line 2 is a Thai folk formula. It uses Pali-sounding words in a Thai order, it is not in the Pali canon, and it is not
a grammatical Pali sentence. The caption should say so (the line as said, over a gloss marked as a gloss), never call
it scripture.

Word by word, from the Pali Text Society's *Pali-English Dictionary* (at the University of Chicago's DSAL):
- [*ehi*](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/pali_query.py?qs=ehi&searchhws=yes): "come, come here".
- [*citta*](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/pali_query.py?qs=citta&searchhws=yes) (*citta 2*): "the heart …
  i.e. thought".
- [*lābha*](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/pali_query.py?qs=l%C4%81bha&searchhws=yes): "receiving, getting,
  acquisition, gain"; *mahā*, great.
- [*piya*](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/pali_query.py?qs=piya&searchhws=yes): "dear, beloved … pleasant,
  agreeable".
- [*mama*](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/pali_query.py?qs=mama&searchhws=yes): "gen. dat. of … ahaŋ", i.e.
  "my, to me".
- *มามา* is no Pali form as written. It most likely reads as Thai มา มา, "come, come" (this note's reading).

"ปิยัง มะมะ" ("dear to me") recurs in other Thai trade kathas, e.g. the day-of-birth verses to Phra Sivali, "สะทา
โหนติ ปิยัง มะมะ" ([Shopee blog, 3 Nov 2020](https://shopee.co.th/blog/worship-siwalee), seller).

### 3.3 The full version, and its variants

The full katha is mostly **Thai**: an invocation, then a Pali-sounding tail. Its opening is the same in every source;
the middle varies by teacher's lineage. KTC's text, verbatim:

> นะโม ตัสสะ ภะคะวะโต อะระหะโต สัมมาสัมพุทธัสสะ (3 จบ) โอมปู่เจ้าเขาเขียวมีลูกสาวคนเดียวชื่อนางกวัก
> หญิงเห็นหญิงทัก ชายเห็นชายรักทุกถ้วนหน้า พวกพ่อค้าพาณิชย์ พากูไปค้าถึงเมืองแมน
> กูจะค้าหัวแหวนก็ได้วันละแสนทะนาน กูจักค้าสารพัดกาลก็ได้โดยคล่อง กูจักค้าทอง ทองก็เต็มหาบ
> เป็นร้อยสามหาบมาเรือน สามเดือนเป็นเศรษฐี สามปีเป็นพ่อค้าสำเภา พระฤาษีผู้เป็นเจ้าประสิทธิให้ลูกคนเดียว
> เอหิ จิตตัง ปิยัง มะมะ มามา มหาลาภาภะวันตุเม โชคลาภเงินทอง อันอยู่ จตุทิศา อัฎฐะทิศา
> ไหลมาเทมา สมาทิมา เอหิมามา สัพเพชะนา พหูชะนา

(Lines are broken only where the source has a space, so the text renders exactly as KTC prints it.)

**Meaning (this note's translation; the archaic words are from ORST: แมน "deities", ทะนาน an old measure, หาบ a
carrying-pole load, สำเภา a Chinese junk, เศรษฐี a rich man):** "Om! The Lord Grandfather of the Green Mountain has
one daughter, named Nang Kwak. Women who see [me] greet [me]; men who see [me] love [me], every one. The merchants
take me trading as far as the city of the gods. Trading rings, I take a hundred thousand measures a day; trading all
manner of things, all goes smoothly; trading gold, gold fills the loads, and by the hundred three loads come home. In
three months a rich man, in three years a junk merchant. The Lord Hermit grants it to his only child. [Ehi …] May
great gains be mine. Luck, fortune, money and gold in the four directions and the eight, flow in, pour in … all
people, many people."

[Thairath English](https://en.thairath.co.th/horoscope/belief/2946465) gives a looser rendering of the opening: "Om
grandfather of Green Mountain, with only one daughter named Nang Kwak; women see and love her, men see and greet her;
may business profit without obstacles."

**For a Thai voice** (this note's romanisation), the opening:
- โอม ปู่เจ้าเขาเขียว มีลูกสาวคนเดียว ชื่อนางกวัก — ohm, bpùu-jâo khǎo-khǐaw, mii lûuk-sǎaw khon diaw, chêu naang-kwàk
- หญิงเห็นหญิงทัก ชายเห็นชายรัก ทุกถ้วนหน้า — yǐng hěn yǐng thák, chaai hěn chaai rák, thúk thûan nâa

**Variants (both in [Thairath, 16 Jun 2024](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/culture/2793400)):**
- Luang Pho Doem of Wat Nong Pho (Nakhon Sawan) opens "โอม มหาสิทธิโชคอุดม", reverses the pair to "ชายเห็นชายรัก
  หญิงเห็นหญิงรัก", and closes "โอมปู่เจ้าเขาเขียว ประสิทธิแก่กูคนเดียว สวาหะ".
- Luang Phu Tim's version is longer. It adds Thai prayers such as "ขอให้ซื้อง่ายขายคล่อง" ("may buying be easy and
  selling brisk") and the heart-syllables "นะชาลีติ".
- The pairing of greet and love also varies: "หญิงเห็นหญิงทัก" in KTC against "หญิงเห็นหญิงรัก" in both Thairath
  versions.
- กู is the old blunt "I" of spells. In a caption it is simply "I".

**For the chapter:** the full version is long and its middle is lineage-specific. The short version (§3.1) fits a
10–20 s chant and is the one both sources agree on word for word.

### 3.4 Real Pali inside the full version (optional)

Two pieces of the tail are grammatical Pali and translate cleanly, if the captions need a line that is truly Pali.
Both are in KTC and Thairath, printed run together.
- มหาลาภา ภะวันตุ เม — *mahālābhā bhavantu me* — "May great gains be mine." Said: má-hǎa-laa-phaa phá-wan-tù mee.
- สัพเพ ชะนา พะหู ชะนา — *sabbe janā, bahū janā* — "All people, many people." Said: sàp-phee chá-naa, phá-hǔu
  chá-naa. ([PTS *jana*](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/pali_query.py?qs=jana&searchhws=yes): "people … *bahu(j)jana*
  many people".)

The parsing is this note's: *lābhā* plural, *bhavantu* "may they be", *me* "to me".

### 3.5 An alternative, weakly sourced — the Phra Sivali verse

Phra Sivali is the disciple the Buddha named foremost among those who receive offerings
([Wikipedia, Sivali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sivali): "Etadaggaṃ bhikkhave mama sāvakānaṃ lābhīnaṃ yadidaṃ
Sīvali"), and Thai traders invoke him [BELIEF]. His verse begins "สีวะลี จะ มะหาเถโร เทวะตานะระปูชิโต …". Only one
openable source gives it (the 2020 Shopee blog), and its middle words are corrupt Pali. **Not recommended** without a
second source.

## 4 · The Thai day-of-week colours — VERIFIED (and add Wednesday night)

**Accurate:** the colours come from the body colours of each day's planetary deity. Wednesday is split in two: a
birth from 06:00 to 17:59 is Wednesday (Mercury, green); from 18:00 to 05:59 it is "Wednesday night", the day of Rahu,
whose colour is a smoky near-black.

| Day | Colour | Thai |
|---|---|---|
| Sunday | red | แดง |
| Monday | yellow (cream also given) | เหลือง |
| Tuesday | pink | ชมพู |
| Wednesday, day | green | เขียว |
| Wednesday, night (Rahu) | smoky near-black | ดำสลัว |
| Thursday | orange | ส้ม |
| Friday | sky blue (not navy) | ฟ้า |
| Saturday | purple | ม่วง |

- [Thai Wikipedia, สีประจำวัน](https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/สีประจำวัน): the seven colours; "สีประจำวันของไทยมาจากสีกายของเทวดา" — "the Thai day colours come from the body colours of the deities."
- [Wikipedia, Thai solar calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_solar_calendar): the same seven (Monday "Yellow
  or Cream", Friday "Light Blue"), citing Denis Segaller, *Thai Ways* (2005).
- [Bangkok Biz News, 11 Aug 2022](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/lifestyle/horo/1020461): Wednesday day "06.00-17.59
  น." green; Wednesday night "ราหู (เกิดในช่วงเวลา 18.00-05.59 น.)", colour "สีดำสลัว สีหม่นๆ ไปทางสีดำ แต่ไม่ดำสนิท" — "a dull, smoky colour tending to black, but not pure black." Tuesday "สีชมพู".
- ORST, ราหู: "เทวดาพระเคราะห์ มีอาภรณ์และพาหนะสีคลํ้า" — "a planetary deity whose dress and mount are dark."
- **Second reading:** the Wednesday-night hours come from one source opened. Search snippets of pages that were not
  opened suggest some people count Wednesday night only to midnight. Treat that as a lead, not evidence.

## 5 · Nang Takhian (นางตะเคียน) — VERIFIED

**Accurate:** a *nang mai*, a female tree spirit believed to dwell in big takhian trees (*Hopea odorata*, a hard
timber used for building and dugout boats). She is also called เจ้าแม่ตะเคียน, the Mother of the Takhian. Devotees tie
three- or seven-colour cloths round the trunk and offer women's Thai dresses, hung on the tree or on a rail beside it.
They also offer scents, powder and cosmetics, flowers and marigold garlands, red drinks, water and fruit, but never
raw meat. Many come to ask for luck. Folklore counts her among the more fearsome tree spirits, so a big tree is not
felled without asking leave. [BELIEF]

- ORST, นางไม้: "ผีผู้หญิงที่เชื่อว่าสิงอยู่ตามต้นไม้ใหญ่มีต้นตะเคียนเป็นต้น" — "a female spirit believed to dwell
  in big trees, such as the takhian." ORST, ตะเคียน: *Hopea odorata*, "เนื้อไม้แข็ง ใช้ในการก่อสร้าง และขุดทำเรือ".
- [Thairath, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/culture/2954132):
  - "ผ้าสามสี หรือ ผ้า 7 สี สำหรับผูกหรือถวายแก่ต้นตะเคียน" — "three-colour or seven-colour cloths, to tie on or
    offer to the takhian."
  - "ชุดไทย นิยมถวายชุดไทยสตรี สีส้ม สีทอง หรือสีชมพู" — "women's Thai dresses in orange, gold or pink."
  - "น้ำอบ น้ำหอม แป้งร่ำ" — "scented water, perfume, scented powder."
  - "ห้ามถวายเนื้อสัตว์ดิบ" — "never offer raw meat."
  - (English version: [en.thairath.co.th](https://en.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/culture/2954132).)
- [Daily News, 1 Feb 2026](https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/5556695/), a century-old takhian in Prachin Buri: "ชุดไทยแขวนอยู่ที่ต้นและราว" — "Thai dresses hang on the tree and on a rail", with a box of cosmetics.
- [Pattaya Mail, 29 Dec 2023](https://www.pattayamail.com/news/lottery-fever-sparks-pilgrimage-to-sacred-mother-takhian-tree-trunk-448914):
  offerings of "traditional Thai attire, cosmetics, clay dolls, and red coloured soft drinks"; the trunk wrapped in

- [Wikipedia, Nang mai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nang_mai): Nang Ta-khian is "one of the most hostile Nang mai";

## 6 · The central plains landscape

### 6.1 Sugar palms along paddy dykes — VERIFIED (Phetchaburi), UNSURE (Suphan Buri, Nakhon Pathom)

**Accurate:** the sugar palm (ตาล or ตาลโตนด, *Borassus flabellifer*) grows along paddy dykes, where farmers plant it to
mark boundaries and for shade. Phetchaburi is the province famous for it: palms are on its emblem, and its palm sugar
is well known. Palms appear across the central region, but no source opened names Suphan Buri or Nakhon Pathom in
particular.

- ORST, ตาล: "ชื่อปาล์มชนิด Borassus flabellifer … ตาลโตนด ก็เรียก" — "also called ตาลโตนด."
- [Thailex, Phetchaburi](https://www.thailex.info/THAILEX/THAILEXENG/LEXICON/Phetchaburi.htm): the provincial emblem
  shows "parcels of farmland with palm trees"; "The palm trees are sugar palms that are abundant in this province."
- [Thai Wikipedia, จังหวัดเพชรบุรี](https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/จังหวัดเพชรบุรี): known as a sugar producer because
  of its many palms.
- [Thai Post, 28 Mar 2021](https://www.thaipost.net/main/detail/97567): "ตาล ตาลโตนด หรือ 'โหนด' ไม่ใช่จะมีแต่ที่เพชรบุรี" — "sugar palms are not only in Phetchaburi". In Songkhla "ส่วนใหญ่จะปลูกตามคันนา" — "they are mostly
  planted along the paddy dykes", also in lines that mark land boundaries.
- [Daily News, 3 Apr 2022](https://www.dailynews.co.th/articles/917933/): "ปลูกตามคันนาเพื่อบอกอาณาเขตและอาศัยร่มเงา"
  — "planted along the dykes to mark boundaries and for shade."
- **Second reading:** in the six geographical regions of the National Research Council of Thailand, Phetchaburi is
  Western Thailand, and the Meteorological Department forecasts it with the south's east coast
  ([Wikipedia, Western Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Thailand)). The plan's village is fictional,
  so "the central plains' rice country" stands. If a province is ever named, pick one in the plain proper (Suphan
  Buri, Ayutthaya), where palms grow too, though no source opened here says how many.

### 6.2 The rainy-season afternoon storm — VERIFIED (with a nuance)

**Accurate:** the rainy (south-west monsoon) season runs from mid-May to mid-October. Over land, its rain most often
peaks in the late afternoon (15:00–18:00). In central and north-east Thailand there is also a secondary midnight
peak, so a storm building through a late afternoon is the commonest pattern and a night storm would also be true.

- [Land Development Department, "Seasons"](https://webeng.ldd.go.th/pv/?cd=229): "Rainy or southern west monsoon
  season (mid-May to mid-October)".
- [Limsakul et al., "Diurnal Variations in Summer Monsoon Precipitation over Thailand…", *J. Sustainable Energy &
  Environment* 5 (2014)](https://www.jseejournal.com/media/160/attachment/Diurnal%20Variations%20in%20Summer%20pp.%2013-19.pdf):
  - The leading mode is "the diurnal cycle with an afternoon-evening peak".
  - "Precipitation maximum tends to occur in the late afternoon (1500-1800 LST) over land areas."
  - A second mode shows a "mid-night peak in the central and northeast of Thailand".
- [Kiguchi, Kyoto University seminar, 16 Dec 2005](https://www.eps.sci.kyoto-u.ac.jp/ieshub/kagi21/jp/education/2005/20051216-0000_a.html):
  from 3-hourly station data in central Thailand, "The maximum time is from night time to early morning in the inland
  area of the year."
- [Thai Wikipedia, จังหวัดเพชรบุรี](https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/จังหวัดเพชรบุรี): rain from May to November, heaviest
  in September and October.

## 7 · Rural house types — VERIFIED (stilt house), UNSURE (how common concrete annexes are)

**Accurate:** the central Thai house stands on posts with a steep gable roof and a terrace. The open space under it
(ใต้ถุน) is used for storing tools and farm equipment, for crafts and work, for sitting together and eating, and it
keeps the living floor above floods. Galvanised "zinc" sheet is the everyday roof of newer rural buildings, and
compounds have changed in look while keeping their old layout. No statistic on concrete annexes was found.

- ORST, ใต้ถุน: "ที่ใต้พื้นเรือนซึ่งยกพื้นสูง" — "the space under the floor of a house raised on a high floor."
- [Sirisoda & Loymek, *ISVS e-journal* 10.3 (2023)](https://isvshome.com/pdf/ISVS_10-3/ISVSej_10.3.9.pdf), Thai-Yuan
  houses in Saraburi, central region. Under the raised floor:
  - "Storage: … various items, tools, and equipment"
  - "Workspace: … crafts, carpentry"
  - "Social gathering place"
  - "Flood protection"
- [Wikipedia, Traditional Thai house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Thai_house), citing the National
  Identity Office's *Ruen Thai* (1993): the central stilt house is the commonest; the space under it is for "storage of
  tools or agricultural equipment, parking, eating meals and other activities".
- [Thai Wikipedia, เรือนไทย](https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เรือนไทย): raised against floods and creatures such as
  snakes and centipedes.
- ORST, สังกะสี (2): galvanised sheet "ใช้มุงหลังคาเป็นต้น เช่น หลังคาสังกะสี" — "used for roofing, as in a zinc roof".
- [Pinijvarasin, "Changes in Thai Vernacular Housing Compounds", *NAJUA* 19](https://so04.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/NAJUA-Arch/article/view/45673),
  80 households in Ayutthaya villages: "although the physical appearance of the housing compounds has changed they

## 8 · Mor hom and pha khao ma — VERIFIED

**Mor hom (ม่อฮ่อม):** the round-necked, short-sleeved shirt open all the way down the front, dyed indigo-dark blue or
black. The word is northern (Phrae is its home, at Ban Thung Hong), but the shirt is worn by farm workers across
Thailand, so it suits Ta Sombat.
- ORST, หม้อห้อม (northern dialect): "เรียกเสื้อคอกลม แขนสั้นผ่าอกตลอด มักย้อมสีน้ำเงินเข้มหรือดำ ว่า เสื้อหม้อห้อม,
  เขียนเป็น ม่อห้อม หรือ ม่อฮ่อม ก็มี" — "the round-necked, short-sleeved shirt open all down the front, usually dyed
  dark blue or black; also written ม่อห้อม or ม่อฮ่อม."
- [National Geographic, Lucie Grace, 22 Sep 2023](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/thai-town-reviving-indigo-production):
  "a common uniform for rural and agricultural workers in Thailand", made in Ban Thung Hong, Phrae.
- [Wikipedia, Mo hom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo_hom): the biggest traditional production is at Ban Thung Hong;
  it is sold in markets across Thailand.

**Pha khao ma (ผ้าขาวม้า):** a checked cotton cloth about 60–80 × 120–180 cm, the all-purpose cloth of Thai men.
They tie it round the waist, drape it over a shoulder, wrap the head against the sun, bathe outdoors in it, and use
it as a towel, a mat or a hammock. Women use it to carry a baby or goods. It has been national heritage since 2013 and
is nominated to UNESCO.
- ORST, ผ้าขาวม้า: "ผ้าฝ้ายทอเป็นรูปสี่เหลี่ยมผืนผ้า มักมีลายตาหมากรุก ใช้ผลัดอาบน้ำหรือเคียนพุงเป็นต้น" — "a
  woven cotton rectangle, usually checked, used as a bathing wrap or tied round the waist."
- [Thai PBS World, 10 Mar 2023](https://www.thaipbsworld.com/lifestyle/pha-khao-ma-the-checkered-cloth-woven-into-thai-culture):
  "usually tied around the waist of Thai men"; a handkerchief, a fan, a loincloth "when bathing outdoors", a mat, "a
  head covering"; national cultural heritage in 2013.
- [Thailand PRD, 2 Mar 2023](https://thailand.prd.go.th/en/content/category/detail/id/48/iid/163229): "head cloths,
  belts, sleeping pads"; the Cabinet approved the UNESCO nomination on 28 Feb 2023.
- [Thai Wikipedia, ผ้าขาวม้า](https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/ผ้าขาวม้า): the central region favours tartan-like checks;
  "สามารถใช้นุ่งอาบน้ำ เช็ดตัว คลุมหัวกันแดด หรือทำเปลก็ได้" — "it can be worn to bathe, used to dry off, to cover
  the head from the sun, or made into a hammock"; men in murals drape it on the shoulder.

## 9 · Luk thung (ลูกทุ่ง) — VERIFIED

**Accurate:** Thai country music. It rose after the Second World War in central Thailand, with Suphan Buri its
heartland. Its sound is Western brass and electric instruments with Thai ones such as the khaen and phin, sung in a
country accent heavy with vibrato and the wailing ornament called *luk khor*. Its songs are of rural life, poverty and
love.

- ORST, ลูกทุ่ง (5): "เรียกเพลงชนิดที่คนในชนบทนิยม ว่า เพลงลูกทุ่ง" — "the kind of song country people love." ORST,
  ลูกคอ: "เสียงเอื้อนหรือเสียงครวญในเวลาร้องเพลง" — "the vocal turn or wail in singing."
- [Wikipedia, Luk thung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luk_thung), citing Mitchell, *Luk Thung* (2015):
  - it emerged after the war in central Thailand, and "Suphan Buri in particular became the center";
  - it is "harmonized with Western instruments, mostly brass and electronic instruments", with "khaen and phin";
  - it is sung "with a distinctive country accent and common use of vibrato";
  - its lyrics treat "rural poverty, romantic love, the beauty of rural scenery".
- [Wikipedia, Suraphol Sombatcharoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suraphol_Sombatcharoen): the "King of Luk Thung",
  born in Suphan Buri.
- Note: ORST, แคน places the khaen in "ภาคอีสานตอนเหนือ" (the upper north-east). The score's khaen is an Isan sound;
  in luk thung it is a borrowing.

## 10 · The Thai phrases — VERIFIED (with fixes)

Romanisation is simple, with tone marks: à low, â falling, á high, ǎ rising, no mark mid. Meanings are from the ORST
dictionary and the [thai-language.com dictionary](http://www.thai-language.com/dict). Multi-word lines are checked word
by word.

| Thai | Say it | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| สวัสดีครับ / สวัสดีค่ะ | sà-wàt-dii khráp / khâ | "Hello" or "goodbye"; men end with ครับ, women with ค่ะ |
| นั่งก่อน / นั่ง ๆ | nâng gàwn / nâng nâng | "Sit down first" / "sit, sit" (นั่ง sit, ก่อน first) |
| สิบสอง · สิบสาม · สิบสี่ | sìp-sǎwng · sìp-sǎam · sìp-sìi | 12 · 13 · 14 |
| คิวสิบสี่ค่ะ | khiu sìp-sìi khâ | "Queue fourteen" (how a number is called) |
| ไปดี ๆ นะ | bpai dii-dii ná | "Go well, okay?" — a warm send-off |
| เดินทางปลอดภัยนะ | dern-thaang plàwt-phai ná | "Have a safe trip" |
| เกิดวันอะไร | gèrt wan à-rai | "What day were you born?" (เกิด born, วัน day, อะไร what) |
| โชคดี | chôhk dii | "Good luck!" |
| อาจารย์ | aa-jaan | "Teacher"; also a title for someone learned (RTGS "achan"; "Ajahn" in English) |
| ตามศรัทธา | taam sàt-thaa | "As your faith moves you" ("put what you like") |

- **Particles** (ORST): ครับ (khráp) is the man's polite particle. Women say ค่ะ (khâ, falling) after a statement and
  คะ (khá, high) after a question or after นะ: "ไปนะคะ". So Pa Noi's "Wait over there, ka" is รอตรงนั้นนะคะ (raw
  trong nán ná khá), and "Ajahn say, call anytime, ka" ends in ค่ะ.
- ORST, สวัสดี ๒: "คำทักทายหรือพูดขึ้นเมื่อพบหรือจากกัน" — "said in greeting, on meeting or parting." ORST, อาจารย์
  (2): "คำที่ใช้เรียกนำหน้าชื่อบุคคลเพื่อแสดงความยกย่องว่ามีความรู้ในทางใดทางหนึ่ง" — "a title before a name,
  honouring someone's knowledge."
- thai-language.com: โชคดี "Good luck!"; เดินทางโดยปลอดภัย "Have a safe trip!"; ดี ๆ "very good"; นะ "added to the end
  of a sentence to soften it".

## 11 · How a paddy compound sounds in the late afternoon

- **Frogs after rain.** The banded bullfrog is "often heard calling during or after rain", and is common across
  Southeast Asia ([NParks](https://biodiversitysg.nparks.gov.sg/our-biodiversity/amphibians/banded-bullfrog/)). The
  hush filling with frogs is right.
- **Geckos at dusk.** The tokay is "nocturnal", calls most "at dusk", and "frequently adapts to rural human
  habitations" ([Wikipedia, Tokay gecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokay_gecko)). "Tokay" imitates the call;
  the Thai ตุ๊กแก (dtúk-gae) sounds the same (this note's observation).
- **Swifts round the palms.** The Asian palm swift glues its nest "to the underside of a palm leaf"; its call is "a
  loud shrill scream" ([Wikipedia, Asian palm swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_palm_swift)). The article
  names oil palms; sugar palms are this note's inference.
- **The village loudspeaker.** News is still spread "ผ่านหอกระจายข่าวในทุกหมู่บ้านทั่วประเทศ" — "through the
  broadcast towers in every village nationwide" ([Naewna, 5 Sep 2026](https://www.naewna.com/n/?p=44155)). A distant
  announcement is a true rural sound.
- **Quiet houses.** A 2026 soundscape study in Uthai Thani heard pop music and "the profound silence of empty homes
  due to labor migration" ([Boonrod et al., *Journal of Arts and Thai Studies* 48.1](https://so08.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/artssu/article/download/5890/4735/62926)).
  That study is of a small city, not a farm. That rural compounds are often left to the old and the young is this
  note's inference from it; Ta Sombat watching the visitors fits either way.
- **The radio's luk thung** is right for the region (§9). The TV soap and the dog were not sourced.

## 12 · Notes for the script (§4 of the plan)

1. **The takrut "for the till" (P5; ending A).** CORRECTED: a takrut is worn. Pong offers "a takrut to wear when you
   open the shop". Ending A's line then follows the piece:
   - Nang Kwak: "Put her facing the door, up high, never near the toilet. Give her water every day."
   - Pha yant: "Frame it and hang it facing the door, lower than the Buddha."
   - Takrut: "Wear it on you when you open the shop."
2. **The day of birth (P4, S47).** Wednesday is two days in Thai astrology. If he picks Wednesday, Pong asks "Morning
   or night?" (กลางวันหรือกลางคืน, glaang-wan rěu glaang-kheun). Night, from 6 pm, belongs to Rahu, whose colour is a
   smoky near-black. Or add an eighth tile. Friday is sky blue (ฟ้า), not navy.
3. **Pa Noi's particles.** Requests end นะคะ (ná khá), statements ค่ะ (khâ). She would call "คิวสิบสี่ค่ะ" (khiu
   sìp-sìi khâ), "queue fourteen".
4. **The offering (P5).** A footed tray with flowers (jasmine or marigold), incense and a pair of candles, plus his
   envelope. Call the money ค่าครู (kha khru), never ปัจจัย (the monks' word). No figure is needed: ตามศรัทธา is the
   custom.
5. **The greeting order (P4).** Kneel at the mat, prostrate three times to the altar's Buddha, then make one wai or
   bow to the master. Sit พับเพียบ with the feet pointing away from him and from the altar. Keep the head below his.
   Take the piece with a wai. Keep the hands joined while he chants, and keep the head bowed with the hands in a wai
   at the pao. Wai when leaving; he returns it (รับไหว้).
6. **The pao (ending A).** VERIFIED. It is literally a blessing to be favoured (ORST), which is what a shop needs. The
   three blows are staging, and fine.
7. **"Writes it on a slate in old script" (P4).** UNSURE: no source found. Thai astrologers write a birth chart in
   Thai numerals; Khom letters belong to yants and kathas. Safer: he notes the day and a small chart in Thai numerals,
   or draws a yant in Khom letters for the piece he is about to bless.
8. **The gate sign (O7).** สำนักอาจารย์ … ("Ajahn …'s samnak") is right. Never สำนักสงฆ์, which is a monks' place.
9. **The laundry line (§4.1).** Anuman Rajadhon (1961): "A man will not pass under a clothes line, where women's
   clothing is hung to dry", or "All his sacred endowments and powers appertaining to magical arts will be weakened or
   gone." In a master's compound the line would hang where no path runs under it. A beat if Pong ducks round it, or
   the player's path simply avoids it. [BELIEF]
10. **Pa Noi's skirt (§6.1).** The central Thai word is ผ้าถุง (pha thung). ซิ่น (sin) is marked regional in ORST
    ("(ถิ่น)"). The model spec should read "tube skirt (pha thung)".
11. **The takhian tree (§4.1).** VERIFIED. Add the colour cloths tied round the trunk, dresses on hangers on the tree
    or on a rail, scents, powder and a box of cosmetics, red drinks, marigold garlands, and small dancer or doll
    figurines. Never meat.
12. **The mangoes (ending A).** Mango is a hot-season fruit. A Department of Agriculture study in Khon Kaen gives the
    crop "ในฤดูกาล (ผลผลิตออก มีนาคม-เมษายน)", in season in March and April, with forced off-season fruit in November
    to February and a late crop in October and November
    ([Department of Agriculture report, 2016](https://info.doa.go.th/research/pdf/2559/141_2559.pdf)); with forcing,
    mangoes can be had all year. [KTC's month-by-month list of rainy-season fruit](https://www.ktc.co.th/article/shopping/rainy-season-fruit-delicious-and-useful)
    has none. In season from July to September: guava, bananas, custard apples (น้อยหน่า), longan, langsat and
    pomelo. Or palm sugar from the compound's own palms. Songkhla holds its palm-fruit fair in June and July "เพราะเป็นช่วงที่น้ำตาลและลูกตาลโตนดจะมีผลผลิตมาก", because that is when palm sugar and palm fruit are most plentiful
    ([Thai Post 2021](https://www.thaipost.net/main/detail/97567)).
13. **The presence (§3.5, §6.1).** A breast cloth (ผ้าแถบ) and cropped "dok krathum" hair are also the classic look of
    Nang Kwak figures ([Thai Wikipedia](https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/นางกวัก): "ไว้ผมปีกหรือดอกกระทุ่ม ห่มผ้าแถบ
    นุ่งโจงกระเบน"). With a Nang Kwak on the tray, players may read her as Nang Kwak. That is Chad's call. If it is
    unwanted, give her a plain old blouse instead of the breast cloth.
14. **The khaen and phin** are Isan instruments (ORST). As the score they are fine. In the world, the radio's luk
    thung may carry them (§9).
15. **"Sawasdee… sit, sit"** (นั่ง ๆ / นั่งก่อน) and **"go well"** (ไปดี ๆ นะ) are both natural. VERIFIED.
16. **The storm** building through the late afternoon is the commonest monsoon pattern. VERIFIED.
17. **Fairness.** Practitioners themselves tell clients to think before believing (Thairath 2022, §1.6). The chapter's
    lesson echoes them; it is not set against the practice.
18. **Optional, one source.** The [Cultural Atlas](https://theculturalatlas.org/thai-culture/thai-culture-etiquette)
    says "It is considered taboo to step on the threshold of a door in someone's home", and to step over it, as a
    spirit is believed to live there. It would suit the room's door step. Unconfirmed in Thai sources.
