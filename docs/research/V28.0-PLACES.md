# v28.0 · Episode 4 chapter 5 · research note: the places and the people (Singapore)

Research note for episode 4 chapter 5 (key `e4c5`; casebook S1·14 *The House With Nine Prai* and S1·15 *The Yin Amulet
Collector*), written for the plan (docs/V28.0-E4C5-PLAN.md). Written 20 Nov 2026. Nothing in the game, the plan or any
other file was changed by this note.

**The job:** WHERE and WHO, in Singapore. Case two is a male devotee's home, reached by a lift and a walk to his unit;
its climax (the cleansing) is to be played for a short while in THIRD PERSON, two bodies seen from behind (Chad's R3),
so the home must be a real kind of Singapore home with room for that. Case three is a customer who "came to us" with
about twenty Yin amulets. Chad has said both happen in Singapore (his R2). This note gives what chapter 4 already
built, the real home types that would look different from it, where case three could happen, who the people are, what
the places sound like, and what the hour of day would mean. It decides nothing.

**How it was done.**
- Both case files were read whole first (docs/source/site-case-files.md), then every other case file's "Place" line.
- Chapter 4's place was read from its own sources (masters/v27.0/parts/*.js, docs/V27.0-E4C4-PLAN.md,
  docs/research/V27.0-HDB.md), not from memory.
- Every web source is numbered (§ Sources). Every source cited by number was **opened** and read on 20 Nov 2026 unless
  it is in the list marked **(index)**: those were seen only through the search engine's summary because the page
  refused (403, 404, 405, 503, a bot check) or came back empty. An (index) source never carries a number alone.
- HDB's resale register (data.gov.sg) was queried and counted for maisonettes and executive flats [1]. Eleven floor
  plans in HDB's own lettering (collected at housingmap.sg) and three of a private condominium's five were opened and
  read [7]–[17], [49]. Seven photographs on Wikimedia Commons were opened full size [37]–[43].
- **Quotations.** The reading tool answers with a summary, so every quotation kept here was then checked word for word
  against the page's own text (thirty-seven pages fetched again raw, and the report [54] read as text). Where a
  "quotation" proved to be the tool's paraphrase, the quotation marks came off. Not checked this way: [68] (the page
  refused the second fetch) and the figures of [69].
- **Labels.** **BUILT**: read from chapter 4's or chapter 2's own source. **SOURCED**: stated by an opened source.
  **DATA**: counted by me from the register [1]. **SCALED**: measured by me off a drawing (HDB's 1:100 plans from their
  own strings, about 23 mm a pixel, ± 0.1 m; the condominium's plan from its drawn beds, ± 10 %). **OBSERVED**: seen by
  me in a photograph I opened. **DERIVED**: arithmetic on sourced numbers. **UNSURE**: one weak source, sources that
  disagree, or (index) only. **DEFAULT**: no source found; a suggestion for the plan, to be checked before it is built.
- A plan's printed figures are HDB's grid strings (to wall lines). Clear room sizes are 0.1 to 0.25 m less each way
  (checked on one plan: a room lettered 4000 measures about 3.75 m between its walls).
- **Walls met and not worked round:** propertyguru.com.sg, 99.co, mdpi.com, core.ac.uk and sso.agc.gov.sg (403); HDB
  InfoWEB's lift-upgrading page, its FAQ and a 2025 press release (404); MND's pages (empty without script, or 404);
  megafurniture's maisonette delivery guide (503); a fan wiki on HDB lifts (402); timeout.sg (405); the National
  Archives' photograph record and two roots.gov.sg pages (empty); Wikimedia Commons' rate limit (two refusals: stopped
  after seven photographs).
- **The search tool's budget ran out** late in the work (a limit of 200 searches shared by every reader in the turn).
  What was still unsearched is in §8 (above all: Singapore journalism on who collects Yin amulets).
- **Search queries.** No query carried the owner's name, the practitioner's, any master's, any private person's, an
  email address or the project's name. Two queries, made to find two academic papers by their titles, carried their
  authors' surnames. Said here because the rule for this research is no person's name in a query.
- **The repository is public.** No private person is named or pictured here. The seven photographs, the nineteen plan
  images, one report (PDF) and the register's rows are on the working machine only, in the scratch folder (§7), and are

### 2.3 Option A · an HDB executive maisonette in a 1980s slab block

**What it is.**
- "The maisonette was a two-storey HDB unit" [22]; "the upper floor is focused more on the residents' privacy while
  the lower floor is geared towards its communal purpose" [18]. Executive flats came in from 1983–84 as HUDC's
  successor [2][3][5]; "80% of Executive units were Maisonettes and 20% were Apartments" [3].
- **DATA** [1]: 6,712 executive maisonettes changed hands from Jan 2017 to Nov 2026. Floor area median **147 m²** (p10
  144, p90 153; the commonest figure 146). Lease began 1984–2000, median 1988 (62 % in 1985–89). **96 % were on storeys
  1 to 12.** Most in Tampines, Hougang, Jurong West, Woodlands, Serangoon, Bukit Batok, Yishun, Bishan and Pasir Ris.
- An owner's account of a 38-year-old one: "two lifts per block"; "Our unit faces the corridor so I don't get to open
  my dining room windows often for privacy"; a picture's caption: "Our Floor is Clean and Empty Some Floors There is a
  Jungle of Potted Plants"; no piped gas (LPG tanks) [18].

**The block, the lift and the lift lobby.**
- 1980s slab blocks have "10-13 floors, usually with 10 or 12 units per floor"; facades at least 24 m apart; most
  aligned east–west "to avoid sun"; usually four units and a void deck at the ground [3] (SOURCED for 1980s slabs in
  general; UNSURE for a maisonette block's count). At 7.4 m a unit, ten or twelve make a corridor of **74 to 89 m**
  (DERIVED).
- **The corridor is on every second storey** (OBSERVED in two photographs of maisonette blocks: a parapet on alternate
  storeys, a row of bedroom windows above each [41][42]; DERIVED from the plan: the upper floor has no access balcony,
  only "VOID" over it [7]). So the lift's stops, the lobbies and the unit numbers skip a storey (DERIVED; no opened
  source says it in words; [S4] speaks of the turn "from the lift landing onto the corridor" of a maisonette).
- Older HDB lifts "only stopped at certain floors (e.g. odd/even numbered or every three floors)" [26]; the "first
  blocks with lifts stopping at every floor" were completed in 1992 [3]; upgrading since 2001 has added lifts and
  shafts to older blocks [26][27].
- **A lift lobby as photographed** (a 1977 block's, in 2014; OBSERVED [40]): a stainless two-panel door with two narrow
  vision panels; a block-number plaque; a "do not use lift in case of fire" plaque in four languages; a maintenance
  hotline plaque; a red LED floor indicator; a no-smoking sign; posters; grey wall tiles with a brown band; ceiling
  downlights; the stair beside it with a red-brown steel balustrade. (Its posters name real bodies: generic in the
  game.) At the ground, "the center of the void deck houses its iconic elevators lobbies and dedicated letterbox
  area" [36].
- Lift car sizes of a 1980s block were not found (docs/research/V27.0-HDB.md gives 1200 × 1500 mm for new accessible
  lifts only). The Naga staff is about 1.3 to 1.6 m (the plan's reading of Chad's photograph): it stands upright in
  any lift.

**The corridor (HDB's "access balcony").**
- **1.70 m** from the facade to the parapet's line [7] (SOURCED string); about 1.55 m clear (DERIVED). SCDF's rule
  leaves 1.2 m clear "from the parapet wall to the shoe or movable clothing rack" [31]: racks and pots get about 0.35 m.
- **Two storeys high**: the next slab over it is the corridor two storeys up, about **5.4 m** above its floor (DERIVED:
  two floor-to-floor heights of 2.8 m less a slab). Bedrooms 2 and 3 of each unit look down into it from above the
  dining room's windows [7][41][42]. Chapter 4's corridor was 2.65 m high.
- The facade as photographed (OBSERVED [41][42]): full-height fins between bays from the ground to the roof; pale
  parapets faced in a brick-like tile; brown bands; grilles on some windows; plants on some parapets.
- **Facing the corridor at each unit:** the dining room's window (a 3.9 m bay), the main door and gate, the utility
  room's window [7].
- What lines it: §2.8.

**The door and the gate** [7]: one leaf in an opening of about **0.96 m** (SCALED), swinging in; a two-leaf gate
swinging out over the access balcony; "100 DROP" lettered at the door (the flat's floor 100 mm above the corridor's).
The door is not in the middle of the frontage: it opens into a foyer beside the dining room.

### 2.5 Option C · a 1980s private condominium with walkways and entrance courts

**What it is.** The first condominium was built in 1974; early ones had units of "150-300 sqm (double than size of HDB
flats built in same period)", living and dining separate, "2 entrances for owners and servants", and "most condos
built up to 1980s feature low-rise blocks with large open spaces" [6]. By "the '80s and '90s, having the
