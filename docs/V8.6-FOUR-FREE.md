## 1 · THE TREE STANDS CULL — and the margin has to be ADDITIVE

The same bug as v8.5's characters, on a different object. Every stand carried
`frustumCulled = false`, with the reason in the comment: *"a tree's own box is
a metre tall until the instance matrix scales it"* — true, and the fix was to
disable culling rather than ask for real bounds. But `InstancedMesh` has
`computeBoundingSphere()`, which walks **every instance matrix** and unions the
result. The correct bounds were always one call away.

**The margin is where this nearly went wrong.** Chad asked for generous bounds,
and I first read that multiplicatively: ×1.6. Measured over 12 headings, mean
triangles a frame against no culling at all:

| margin | mean | saves | |
|---|---|---|---|
| exact bounds | 278,987 | 114,957 | 29% |
| **+3 m (ships)** | **297,872** | **96,073** | **24%** |
| +4 m | 307,831 | 86,113 | 22% |
| +8 m | 361,110 | 32,834 | 8% |
| ×1.25 | 358,308 | 35,636 | 9% |
| ×1.60 | 381,942 | 12,002 | **3%** |

A stand's sphere already spans tens of metres — these run **4.3 m to 45.6 m** —
so a multiplicative margin scales the slack with the stand and swallowed the
whole win. What the margin has to cover is a per-TREE uncertainty (a leaf card
past its own instance box, a crown that sways, a re-dealt spot), which is
metres, never a proportion. **A flat three metres keeps 84% of the maximum
saving and is pure insurance**, since `computeBoundingSphere` is already exact
for the geometry, crown and instance scale included.

`plantTrees` is the ENGINE's, so this helps chapters 1 and 3 as well.

## 2 + 3 · THE STATIC MESHES STOP RECOMPUTING THEIR MATRIX

These turned out to be the same lever, and a wrong assumption had to be
corrected first: in this three.js, `updateMatrixWorld` **always recurses into
children** — `matrixWorldAutoUpdate` only skips the maths for that one node,
not the walk. So "stop traversing the hidden film sets" is not available. What
IS available is `matrixAutoUpdate = false`, which skips the per-object compose
AND stops it setting `matrixWorldNeedsUpdate`, which would otherwise force
every descendant to recompute its world matrix too.

**Which objects are safe was MEASURED** (`dbg-movers.mjs`): every object's
local matrix sampled 130 times across the whole opening film and a full day of
play. Of 3,126 objects, **569 ever changed — and 552 of those are bones**. Of
the seventeen that are not, every one is a **Group or Object3D**: a cast
member's group, a crowd copy, the camera rig, the ferry's swell.

**No mesh ever moves**, because the chapter animates by turning groups: the fan
is a Group whose blades are fixed inside it, the toilet door is a leaf parented
to a rotating pivot, and the wall clock is a redrawn **texture** with no moving
geometry at all. So the rule is "meshes freeze, groups and bones never" — safe
by construction rather than by a list that can go stale. It covers the film's
three sets (2,136 of those objects) and the bunk in one pass.

**And it lifts for every cutscene.** A scene may move anything it likes, so the
freeze is released in `snap()` — the engine's own hook when a cutscene takes
the world — and re-applied in `restore()`. Play is long and a scene is short,
so the saving is kept where it matters and the risk removed where it would
bite. It is also re-applied on a timer, because models arrive async long after
build().

Verified by re-running the mover probe on the changed build: **568 movers, of
which non-bone exactly 17** — identical. Nothing that moved has stopped.

## 4 · LIGHTS AT ZERO, AND THE CLOCK'S LAMP

three.js collects a light whether or not its intensity is zero, so a dark lamp
is still evaluated for every fragment. The bunk carries eleven point lights and
**five sit at zero at any moment**. `darkLights()` hides them.

**With hysteresis**, because the number of lights is part of the shader's
identity: flipping one every few frames would recompile the program and
stutter. A light must read zero for 20 consecutive frames before it is hidden,
and returns the instant it is raised. Measured: **0 visibility flips over 400
frames** of steady play — the settling happens once, at a phase change, exactly
like any other lighting cue.

**The clock's light is gone** (`CLOCK_GLOW` 0.55 → 0). Chad was right that it
was not needed, and it was a leftover: it began as the RED of a *digital*
display, and when v7.9 made the clock analog the light was kept as "the small
warm practical over the toilet door" — but there is no lamp fitting there, and
a wall clock is not one. Held at zero rather than deleted, because ten call
sites save, restore and cue it; `darkLights()` then drops it from the shader
entirely, so it costs nothing.

## 6 · THE LAWS

- **A generous margin must be additive when the thing it bounds varies in
  size.** ×1.6 on stands spanning 4 m to 46 m turned a 29% win into 3%.
- **`updateMatrixWorld` always recurses**; only the maths is skipped. The lever
  is `matrixAutoUpdate`, not `matrixWorldAutoUpdate`.
- **Derive the static set by measurement, then freeze by a rule the code can
  state** — here "no mesh moves", which the 130-sample probe confirmed and the
  chapter's construction guarantees.
- **A light at zero still costs**, and hiding it needs hysteresis because the
  light count is part of the shader.
