# v21.23 · The gallery, debugged: the wheel, the portrait, the end of a story

Chad, 22 Oct 2026, verbatim:

> Previously when i tried on desktop and mouse scroll to the bottom, it immediately jumped to the bottom of the next
> page, instead of starting at the top of the next page. Please check if this is still an issue. Do a deep bug check and
> debugging of the monk gallery window frame and its interactions. See whether there are more ways to improve the window
> frame and its experience, visually or in sound effects.

The window is the engine's `kit.gallery` (v21.10, the twenty-ninth seam); only episode 3 chapter 3 opens it (the
masters' gallery in the amulet shop), and the fixture proves it. Nothing here reaches episodes 1 and 2.

## 1 · Is it still an issue? Yes — measured on the live v21.22 before anything moved

Two probes, both against the live build (snapshot of v21.22):

- `dbg-galwheel.mjs` — a REAL mouse wheel (Playwright's `mouse.wheel`), with and without Chrome's smooth scrolling.
- `dbg-galwheel2.mjs` — the same window with wheel events at a real machine's TIMING (a notch every 30 ms, a trackpad's
  60 Hz stream and its momentum), dispatched in the page, because on this box one `mouse.wheel` call takes over a
  second and hides how a spin really arrives.

| scenario (from the top of the first master) | v21.22 |
|---|---|
| a fast spin, on past the end | turned TWICE, ended at the FOOT of the third master |
| one notch at a time, four past the end | turned at the first notch past the end; the new page scrolled 300 px down |
| a trackpad swipe and its momentum | turned; the momentum carried 534 px down the next page |
| at the end, a pause, then a fresh spin | turned; the rest of the spin scrolled the new page to 700 px |
| a sideways trackpad swipe | turned TWO pages |
| a spin with the mouse over the portrait | turned the page instead of scrolling the story |

Why: one notch past the end turned the page at once (50 px was enough), and the wheel handler let every later event of
the same spin through to the new page the moment it had room to scroll — its 450 ms block applied only after that check.

## 2 · Found on the way (each measured or photographed on v21.22)

- **The portrait was drawn too small, and its size depended on how you got there.** `galPaint` sized the canvas from
  the column's on-screen box, which the page turn's sweep (scale .92, rotateY) and the window's entrance (scale .88 ×
  .86) shrink while they run: at 1280 × 800, 286 × 374 on opening and 251 × 335 after an arrow, against 342 × 456 that
  the column holds — and it kept that size until the window was resized.
- **The last line of every story was faded for good.** The words' mask faded the bottom 12 % whatever the scroll: at the
  very end of a page, 36 px of the last paragraph lay inside it.
- **A button reached with Tab did not answer its own key.** Enter on a thumbnail (or the X) pressed NEXT instead, because
  the window took Enter and Space for NEXT whatever held the focus. (A first reading suspected Space after a mouse click
  on an arrow of pressing twice — measured, it does not: cancelling the key's own press stops the button's. The jump
  from master 2 to 4 in that probe was NEXT skipping a master already read, which is what NEXT does.)
- **A cross-fade cut short left the portrait invisible.** Luang Phu Tim's two photographs cross-fade every 6.5 s through
  a 280 ms timer that put the picture back only if the window was still open; closed inside those 280 ms, the canvas
  stayed at opacity 0, and the next opening showed an empty frame on every page with one photograph.
- **On a phone held upright the side arrows sat on the story's words** once the page had scrolled up.
- The sparkles round the portrait used on-screen boxes unscaled, so while the column swept in they could cross the
  picture's edge.
- Page Down / Page Up turned the page from anywhere, where a reader expects them to scroll the story.

Then two review lenses read the window's code (input and state; sound and feel), and every lead was checked against
the code as it stands before anything was touched. Confirmed and fixed:

- **A page read could be lost.** The reading reached the chapter (and the save) only when the window closed, so a phone
  that locked or dropped the tab in the middle of the gallery lost every page read in it.
- **Time away counted as reading.** A page glanced at before switching apps counted on the way back, and Luang Phu
  Tim's photograph changed the instant the window was seen again.
- **The screen could lock mid-story.** Five long stories read without a touch let a phone dim and lock.
- **Held keys ran the pages round.** A held → (or a key still held from walking when the window opened) turned a page
  at the keyboard's repeat rate.
- **A second finger took the swipe over**, and lifting it turned the page; a swipe the system took back (a
  pointercancel) turned it too; and a swipe cut short by a close left the next opening's page shifted, its sweep off.
- **The window sprang back.** The second click of a double-click on the X, or a tap through its fade, pressed the row
  that opens it again; and with the fading window letting taps through, the second tap could land on the mute button
  now under it.
- **CONTINUE under a finger already on its way.** NEXT turns into CONTINUE the moment the last page counts; a press in
  that instant closed the window a tenth of a second into its own celebration.
- **Ctrl and the wheel** (a trackpad's pinch) scrolled or turned, and zoomed the whole page behind the window.
- **A key with Ctrl, Cmd or Alt** (copy, find, zoom) turned pages; Alt or Cmd and ← could leave the game.
- **Tab walked out of the window** to the browser's own bar, where no key of the window answered, Esc included.
- **Space pressed to read on** through the last master's story closed the window instead.
- **A picture that failed to arrive stayed an empty frame** for the rest of the session (its bytes are let go when the
  loading curtain lifts, so one dropped request on a weak connection was final).
- **A pointer lock asked for a moment before the window opened by itself** could land after it: the cursor hidden over a
  window made to be pointed at.
- The sparkles' bursts had no ceiling (a run of turns stacked thousands), and the two light canvases kept their pixels
  after the window had gone (3 MB on a phone, up to 19 on a large screen).
- **Sound**: his answer as the window opens was said in the same instant as its opening bell, and every sound dips
  under a voice, so the bell rang half-heard; the read tick sat under the completing chime; and the close landed on top
  of the objective's chime and his next line.

## 3 · What it does now

- **The wheel reads, and turns only when asked.** It scrolls the words from anywhere over the window (over the
  portrait too). A spin that scrolls into the end of a story STOPS there. The page turns only on a pull that BEGINS at
  the end — the wheel quiet a moment (260 ms), then turned again — and the pull fills a hairline under the story (200 px,
  two notches) before it turns, to where NEXT would go; then the wheel is held until it goes quiet (700 ms at least), so
  nothing of that spin reaches the new page, which opens at its top. Upward the wheel stops at the top. Sideways (a
  trackpad's swipe, shift and the wheel) turns once a gesture. The wheel never closes the window.
- **The end of every story** carries a gold rule and the way on, by name — "Next · Luang Phu Tim" — or, every page
  read, CONTINUE; it presses like NEXT (it IS NEXT, said where the reader's eyes already are). On a desktop the hairline
  fills as the wheel pulls, with "Scroll on to turn the page" under it.
- **The portrait** is sized from the column's layout box: the same size however it was reached.
- **The fades** appear only where there is more: above once the words have scrolled, below until the end.
- **Enter and Space** mean NEXT unless the keyboard has brought the focus to a button, which then answers its own key
  (Tab to a thumbnail, Enter, and that master opens); a mouse press no longer leaves a button holding the focus.
- **The cross-fade** belongs to the page it began on: turned or closed, the picture is put back at once.
- **Page Down / Page Up** scroll the story a screen at a time; past the end Page Down goes on, above the top Page Up
  goes back (a held key only scrolls).
- **The phone's side arrows** step aside once the page has scrolled, and come back at its top.
- **Sound**: each page turn a breath of pitch either side and from the side it comes in; turns closer than 110 ms
  share one sound; the page that completes the set has its chime alone (the tick under it was a click on the bell); a
  soft tick, rising, as the wheel pulls at the end of a story; his answer comes 0.6 s after the window opens, so the
  bell is heard whole; the close is half as loud on CONTINUE, under what follows it.
- **Reading shows**: a ring fills round the page's own thumbnail over the 1.2 s it takes to count; when it counts, a
  short buzz and the master's name lights once. The paragraph of what devotees believe has a small gold rule before it,
  where the telling turns from his life.
- **A page read is kept the moment it is read** — told to the chapter and saved with the run.
- **Time away is not reading**; the photograph does not change while the window is hidden.
- **The screen stays awake** while the window is open (where the browser offers it).
- **Keys**: a held key turns one page; Ctrl, Cmd or Alt with a key is the browser's; Tab stays in the window; Space
  reads on a screen at a time and is NEXT only at the end of the story.
- **Touch**: a second finger is a pinch, never a swipe; only a finger lifted turns the page.
- **Opening and closing**: not under a live minigame; not again for 0.6 s after it closes; the fading window still takes
  the taps meant for it; a press in the 0.8 s after the last page counts is not taken; a pointer lock that lands late is
  let go.
- **The wheel with Ctrl** does nothing (no scroll, no turn, no zoom of the page behind).
- **A picture that failed** is asked for again (at most every 4 s); the bursts are capped at 320; the light canvases
  give their pixels back when the window goes.
