# v5.08 · THE HOME ALTARS, AND MASTER ZAV IN THE ROUND

Two of Chad's uploads, two jobs.

## The Vietnamese altar

> *"use this altar for the living room altar top & bottom … For the bedroom
> altar, use only the top one."*

Chad's Sketchfab altar is two meshes in one file: a floor cabinet and a
wall shelf with carved brackets below a deity's picture. Which is which is
MEASURED at load — the tier whose bounding-box centre sits lower is the
cabinet — rather than read off names that a re-export could swap. The file
is scaled by one rule everywhere (its full height to 2.15 m), so the shrine
is the same object in the flat and in the bedroom.

**The flat (ch4, ch5).** Both tiers stand against the kitchen-corner wall
where the lacquer shelf hung: grounded, backed to the wall, centred on the
shelf's z. Measured in world: the cabinet 0.69 m wide, 0.38 deep, 0.83
tall; the shrine 0.96 wide, hung 1.14–2.15. On arrival the primitive slab
hides and the oil lamp, its flame and the two oranges are re-seated on the
cabinet's top surface, near its front edge — every position taken from the
model's own box, not typed in. The cabinet's box joins the chapter's
blockers BY REFERENCE (`BLK`), so a piece of furniture that arrives after
the walls were counted is still something the player cannot walk through.

**The bedroom (ch2).** The shrine alone, on the +x wall at the old shelf's
spot. The first hang put the candle and the oranges at the bottom of the
bracket, floating in air — the tier's box starts at the bracket tips, and
the BOARD, where things stand, is 0.42 m above that (read off the flat,
where the model's own cups show where the board is). The shrine is hung so
the board lands at 1.67, the old shelf's height, and the candle, the
oranges and the electric candle's light go on it.

The texture is 331 KB JPEG from a 2 MB PNG; the file 493 KB. Not
preloaded: the shelf stands in until it lands.

## Master Zav, in the round

> *"a real 3d model in the middle rotating, and the various equipment boxes
> are shown around the character … Follow the boxes that we had before."*

The equipment screen is Chad's Guild Wars screen now. Master Zav himself —
his own model, a static sculpt of 990k triangles brought to 40k with the
base colour kept and the maps the CSP-safe loader cannot restore dropped,
1.27 MB — turns in a cell in the middle of the panel, and the five worn
boxes flank him in two columns: his head, neck and RIGHT hand on the
viewer's left (he faces the player), his body and LEFT hand on the right.
The bag, the drag, the tap-then-tap, the double-tap and the keyboard are
the v3.1 interaction model untouched; only what the boxes stand around
changed. Verified by driving it: a drag of the beads into the bag, a
double-click sending them back.

How it draws: a SECOND `WebGLRenderer` on its own small canvas inside the
panel, with its own three lights and a 30° camera 3.6 m out (which sees
1.93 m at the figure, so a 1.75 m man fits whatever the panel's width).
Rendered only while the panel is open. Drag on him to spin him; four
seconds after you let go he turns on his own again. The model is fetched
the first time the panel opens and kept; until it lands — and on a
machine where it never does — the old silhouette drawing behind the
canvas is the figure.

Two traps:

- The slot boxes came out 150 px tall in the new columns. `aspect-ratio`
  in a flex column does not make a height DEFINITE, so the icon's 52%
  height fell back to an inline SVG's 150 px intrinsic height and the
  button grew to hold it. An explicit height fixed it.
- The figure cell is 280 px, not 300: at 300 the bag's third row fell off
  a 760 px screen.
