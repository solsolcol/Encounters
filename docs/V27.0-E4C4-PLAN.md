## 1 · The case file (S1·13, verbatim from docs/source/site-case-files.md)

**The Woman Who Rose From Her Wheelchair** · *case one · spiritual work* · Period: ten years of cultivation · Place:
with Grandmaster Pho.

> Over the past ten years of cultivation under Grandmaster Pho, I have seen many cases — helping devotees, doing spiritual work, and during periods of my monkhood. This is one I will never forget.

> An elderly Indian lady was brought to see him. She had been unable to walk properly for years and used a wheelchair. She had seen many doctors and no clear explanation had been found. She had also been to many spiritual practitioners and nothing had improved. Eventually she came to Grandmaster Pho.

> Through his practice he perceived that a spirit was sitting on her back, and he believed this was connected to her years of difficulty. He also saw that she had once served as a medium of Mae Kali, and that because of past karma and matters left unresolved, the spirit had continued to oppress her. > During the blessing her whole demeanour changed and she entered a deep trance associated with Mae Kali. As it deepened, the spirit let out a loud cry and was forced from her back. Afterwards she opened her eyes, and then, to everyone in that room, she stood up. She could walk. From that day she walked again.

**The lesson.** I want to be careful about how this one is heard. Not every unexplained physical problem is spiritual,
and medical checkups matter — she had already been through the doctors before she ever came to us, and that order is
the right one. If you or someone you love cannot walk properly, see a doctor first and keep seeing them. This account
is what I witnessed, not a promise of what will happen to anyone else. Beyond that, the case taught me three things I
still use. Look carefully at a person's history, their previous practices and their circumstances before reaching any
conclusion. Sensitivity only lets you experience something; cultivation is what teaches you how to examine it. And
experience teaches you not to jump to conclusions — including the conclusion that something is spiritual at all.

- **A · Grab him and hold him back.** He catches Ravi round the chest; Ravi fights him, shouting for his mother; Mala
  screams at them both. The cry peaks; the thing tears free of her back and is gone (the wave, white); Amma opens her
  eyes on a room of people struggling. She stands up out of the chair, and her son is not beside her, because the
  student is holding him. Ravi goes still; then goes to her. Pho, after: **P:** "You kept the blessing. You forgot the
  son. His fear was only love." **Z** (thought): "I saw a man in the way. He was her son."
- **B · Throw the water at the thing on her back.** He throws the whole bowl at it. The crimson thing lets go of her
  and comes at HIM: the vision fills with it, his heart stalls, the picture goes white. Pho's hand leaves her head for
  the one moment it takes to push it back (a single word); her trance falters; Pho's hands return; the cry rises again and
  the thing is forced away. She opens her eyes and stands, and the student is on the floor. **P:** "Your part was the
  water and the room. The work was mine. Today you were lucky." **Z** (thought): "I saw it, and thought it was mine to
  fight."
- **C · Go to him: she is safe; stay with him.** He steps between Ravi and the chair, hands open, and speaks low:
  **Z:** "She's not being hurt. It's leaving her. Stay with her. Hold her hand." Ravi kneels by the chair and takes her
  hand; Mala comes to the other side. The cry; the thing is forced from her back (the wave, white); she opens her eyes,
  looks at her son, and stands, holding his hand. **P:** "You saw what was there, and you told him only what you knew.
  Nothing more." **Z** (thought): "I didn't promise him anything. I told him what I saw."
- **D · Keep your place.** He stays where he is: the bowl steady, his sight shut, his eyes on his teacher. Ravi reaches
  the chair; Pho speaks his name once, calm, without looking up (**P:** "Ravi. Hold her hand."), and Ravi does. The
  cry; the thing is forced from her back; she opens her eyes and stands. **P:** "You held. That is where it starts. But
  there were two of them afraid in this room, and you are the one who could see." **Z** (thought): "I kept to my
  part. Next time I'll see the people too."
- In every ending, at the release: Pho dedicates the merit of the night to all beings, the released one among them
  (§2.8; spoken, captioned; no water poured). In the vision the green shapes still in the room ease and go.
- In every ending, after she stands: her first steps, to her altar; Mala puts the sacred ash on her forehead
  (*thiruneeru*, §2.4); she lights a second wick of the lamp herself (her own gesture, as her goddess's devotee). Then
  the student closes his vision (the wave home to him). **P** (to the family, whichever ending): "Keep seeing her
  doctors. What happened tonight is what happened tonight. It is not a promise." The lesson card follows.

## 5 · Spirit Vision: the specification (engine: the fiftieth seam, main.js `vis` + src/vision.js)

- **Opening:** the on sound (a rising breath of energy, a low thump as it leaves him); the wave runs out from his eyes
  over 1.3 s (the room in the first half second, the far windows by the end); at its front a thin line of cyan light
  in which every edge of the room burns; behind it the world INVERTED (the colours as seen, 1 − c), its edges faintly
  drawn, the camera's motion blurred slightly, the corners darkened slightly; spirits appear as the front reaches them.
- **Closing:** the off sound (the same breath, backwards); the wave comes home over 1.0 s from the far windows to him,
  undoing the vision as it passes; the instant it is home the world is drawn exactly as without it.
- **Sound while open:** its bed (a low choir of breath, a far ringing, the room's own sound pulled thin: the world's beds
  ducked 45 %) and its music (a slow, dark pulse); both rise with the wave and fall with it.

  chapter raises it further the longer the vision stays open and in the fight; closing lets it settle to 74 over 4.5 s,

  GOLD for Grandmaster Pho's aura (his alone, the standing rule) — a subtle rim, never a solid figure.
