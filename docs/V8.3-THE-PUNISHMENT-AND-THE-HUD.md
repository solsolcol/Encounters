## 1 · THE VOICE, AND THE PROMPT RULE UNDER IT

The encik has had two shouted lines since v7.1 and no voice — they were the
sergeant's. He is **Hilmi** (`klqxhYh2Np93AvKxFz0b`), Malaysian English,
middle-aged, Chad's pick after four rounds of rejection, and what actually got
there is a **prompt rule, not a casting one**.

The workspace's Malay and Malaysian voices are booth-recorded voice-over
artists. Chad's three rejections, in order: *"none of them sounds old"*, *"zul
sounds like a young boy, and yatin sounds 80 years old"*, *"none works, they
sound too polished"*. The third was mine, not the library's. Every take had
been prompted with a stage direction —
`[a furious sergeant-major in his fifties, shouting across a parade square]` —
and a stage direction gets a professional **performing** anger, which is
exactly what "polished" names. Written as the bark itself instead — short
bursts, full stops between them, CAPITALS on the stressed words, no direction
at all — the same voice landed on the first take.

**This is the opposite of Aaron's rule** (v5.28: a tag that names an emotion,
never a volume), and it is written into `encik`'s row in `src/voicelines.js`
so the next person to regenerate his lines does not undo it.

Two lines, Chad's own words:

| key | words | secs |
|---|---|---|
| `e1knock` | Ah! Take your time some more! Whole lot, knock it down! Twenty push-ups! Go! | 7.31 |
| `e1backbunk` | Now! Go back to your bunk! I want standby bed, now! | 4.44 |

Both peak-matched to the sergeant's shouted takes (−3.65 dB), both encodings.

## 2 · ONE MODEL IN THE BUNK

*"i want all the bunkmates to be the same green admin tshirt one, not the blue
botak one."* `BUNK_MEN` was three `admintee` and three `botak`; it is six
`admintee` now. The botak recruit keeps the two jobs he was cast for at v8.0 —
the ferry seats and the parade square — where a blue PT tee among a hundred
strangers is right and a bunk of identical roommates is not.

**And the bunkmate changed rig.** He was `fbonosling` — the rifle model with
its sling removed — and Chad's *"bunkmate is green admin tshirt not the fbo
one"* settles what he always should have been. He is `admintee` now, on the
model's own `Idle_9` and its own `Talk_with_Hands_Open`, so the retargeted
talking take v8.1 built for him is no longer needed and neither is the file:
`fbonosling` leaves the chapter's asset list. `fbosling` (with the rifle) stays
— he is the sentry in the film.

That also decided the push-ups. `tools/retarget.mjs` carries **rotations only,
no hips translation**, and the admin tee's own `push_up` drops the hips 0.668 m
from its idle. A retargeted push-up would have had him doing press-ups standing
upright in mid-air. Everyone in the bunk being one model means everyone plays
the model's *own* take.

## 3 · THE PUNISHMENT BEAT

*"Even if player manages to fall in on time, i still want this to happen."*

`onTheLine()` no longer runs straight to the standby bed. Both the on-time and
the late branch now land in `punishBeat()`, a phase of its own (`punish`, with
its own objective line — TWENTY PUSH-UPS — THE WHOLE SECTION):

```
 0.0   e1knock — "Ah! Take your time some more! ... Twenty push-ups! GO!"
 6.9   the whole section drops        idle_to_push_up
 9.6   push_up, x3                    (PU.REP 1.63 s each)
14.5   push_up_to_idle
17.7   back on Idle_9
18.2   e1backbunk — "Now! Go back to your bunk! I want standby bed, now!"
23.1   beginStandby() — the section marches back in
```

`PUNISH_LEAD` is 6.9 s because that is where "GO!" lands in the take: they drop
on the word, not after the sentence. Every rig and every crowd copy takes the
same three cues through `crowdPlay` (v8.2), so the buddy, the bunkmate and all
six recruits go down and come up together.

The late branch dropped its own private push-ups — it had them since v8.0 and
they are now the shared beat — and ends at `after(6.0, ...)` into the same
place.

**A resume lands mid-beat correctly**: `applyPhase('punish')` calls
`punishBeat(true)`, which sets the phase, clears the waypoint and goes straight
to `beginStandby()`. The shout is spent; a Continue must not make a player
listen to it twice, and must not leave the section face-down on the balcony.

## 4 · THE OBJECTIVE AND THE TIMER, LIT

*"Make the timer and instructions way more obvious with glowing pulsating
effects."*

The objective box is the same size it has always been — the geometry is
deliberately unchanged, because at desktop widths near 1100 px it sits between
the stat rows on the left and the button column on the right with only a few
pixels of air, and a wider box would have run into one of them. What changed is
everything else:

- the box **breathes** (`objGlow`, 2.4 s) — the border and a sodium halo swell
  and settle;
- a **light crosses its face** every 5.2 s (`objSweep`, a gradient on `::after`);
- the ground is a warm gradient rather than flat charcoal, and the stencil
  OBJECTIVE label is sodium with its own glow;
- the **timer ticks in scale** (`objTick`), and under ten seconds reddens and
  beats at 0.78 s (`objTickLow`) instead of the old 1 s fade;
- the box **flashes** the moment the words change (`objFlash`, one 0.95 s
  pulse, driven from `paintObjective` — the class is pulled and re-added with a
  reflow between, because re-adding a class that is already there does not
  restart a CSS animation).

`prefers-reduced-motion` already kills every animation in the file globally, so
none of this reaches a player who has asked for stillness.

## 5 · A MARK OVER EVERYTHING YOU CAN ACT ON

*"Make all the interactable objects, people, much more obvious with exclamation
marks and more effects to show they are interactable. It is very hard to see
now."*

He is describing a real property of the code rather than a taste. The interact
badge names a thing only once **both** conditions hold: the player is inside
that hotspot's radius, *and* the hotspot is on screen. Until then nothing on
the HUD says the thing exists. In a bunk twelve metres long with five things to
touch, that is a player walking the room hoping something lights up.

`#hotmarks` is a new layer, and `paintHotMarks()` fills it beside
`paintWaypoint()` in the frame. For every hotspot that is **enabled** and in
front of the lens within `HOTMARK_FAR` (16 m) it projects a diamond with an
exclamation mark in it, jade like the badge, pulsing; smaller and dimmer with
distance; and `near` — bigger, brighter, with a ring rippling outward — once
you are inside the radius the badge would fire at. It hides with an open event,
exactly as the badge and the approach prompt have since v7.2.

On a phone the marks are 44 px rather than 34: the screen is a third of a
desktop's width and the same diamond reads as a speck on it.

**Chapters 1–5 declare no hotspots**, so `hotspotList()` returns an empty array
there and the layer paints nothing. Episode 1's own pile prompt (`.ibadge`) is
deliberately untouched — it is frozen base-game feel (v6.17), and restyling it
is Chad's decision to make separately.

## 6 · WHAT WAS MEASURED

- `dbg-hud.mjs` — resumes into `fallin` and `free` on both crops, reads the
  objective's text, timer and bounding box, and counts the marks with their
  transforms. On desktop: 1 mark at the toilet door in the fall-in, 2 (the
  entrance and the notice board) in free play, the box clear of the stat rows.
  On the phone the marks only show for things roughly ahead, and that is the
  lens rather than the layer — the camera's 72° is VERTICAL and fixed, so a
  portrait phone is a centre crop at a third of the width (AUDIT-2026-09 Part
  One). It is still far more than the badge, which needs radius AND aim.
- `dbg-punish2.mjs` — walks the player onto the yellow line and samples the
  phase, the voice log and how many of the bunk's figures have their crown
  below waist height. Measured on the shipped build:

  | wall | phase | figures down | voices |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0 s | fallin | 7 (the sleepers, the floor) | — |
  | 3 s | **punish** | 7 | `e1knock` |
  | 24 s | punish | **15** — the floor plus all eight | |
  | 42 s | punish | back to 7 — **nobody left face-down** | `e1backbunk` |
  | 48 s | **standby** | 7 | |

  (The wall clock is about twice the chapter clock on this box: the chapter
  clock is capped at 0.5 s a frame and the renderer draws ~1 fps. The FIRST
  run of this probe gave the beat 46 s and concluded the phase never advanced;
  nothing was wrong with the beat. LEARNINGS carries it.)
