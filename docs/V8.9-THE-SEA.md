## 1 · THE MISSING BUNKMATES

`BUNK_MEN` ended with:

```js
].filter(m => !LOW || m.low);
```

and two of its six entries carried `low: false`. `LOW` is `IS_PHONE`. So on
**every phone — the only device Chad plays on** — the bunk built four men and
two of the beds v8.1 had given owners went back to being nobody's.

The filter was written at v8.1, when no rig in this chapter was culled at all
(`frustumCulled = false` on every `SkinnedMesh`, because a skinned mesh keeps
its own bounding sphere computed once from whatever pose the file loads in).
v8.4 gave every rig a real `CULL_SPHERE` covering every pose of every clip it
can play, so **a man the lens is not pointed at now costs nothing**, and the
reason for the filter went with it.

Priced rather than assumed (`dbg-v89men.mjs`, the render wrapped because
main.js renders twice a frame and three.js resets `renderer.info` at the top
of each pass — v8.4's law):

| | triangles |
|---|---|
| one restored recruit in shot | **44,539** |
| the bunk down the whole −x bed row, all six built | **255,161** |
| what v8.6 left this chapter at | 297,872 |

The room did not get dearer than its own worst frame. It only stopped being
half empty.

Two of eight faced along the room; the other six faced the aisle. Chad read

where they stand rather than by a talking take nobody can hear" — but a
conversation between two men and a roomful of men all facing one way does not
read as a conversation, it reads as two men stood the wrong way round.

Both are on the aisle now (π/2 ∓ 0.35), each canted 0.35 rad toward the other,
so the conversation survives as a **lean** rather than as a turn. Re-measured:
all eight point at the aisle or, for the sergeant at the door, along the room,
which is where he has always faced.

## 3 · THE TOILET DOOR

The block was never sealed. `walktest` has asserted since v7.4 that every cell
of it is reachable, and a flood fill re-run for this build confirms it. What
trapped him was the **leaf**: at `DOOR_AJAR` 0.35 rad it hung across most of a
0.9 m opening in an unlit tiled block, and a doorway you cannot see is a wall.

v7.5's answer was a hotspot that swung the door and stepped the player
through. Chad's answer is better and he asked for it directly: the door
simply stands open. `DOOR_PLAY = DOOR_OPEN` at build and in `reset()`; the
`wcdoor` hotspot, `useDoor()` and the word `hotDoor` are gone; `dooropen2`
left `PLAY_LINES` with them (it is scene A's cue now, and the film warms its
own). The two CUTSCENE beats are untouched — the film shuts the door to AJAR
at `step(0)` and scene A swings it open on `dooropen2`, both setting the angle
themselves.

Verified by measurement rather than by eye: the open leaf runs from its hinge
at (−3.750, 4.080) to (−3.686, 3.182), perpendicular to the wall and clear of
the entire aperture (x −3.75 … −2.85 at z 4.08); and a flood fill of the real
collision grid from the spawn reaches the doorway, inside the block, the
shower and back out.

## 4 · THE SEA

v7.9's sea was a **vertical sheet** — one painted gradient with 220 white
dashes on it, hung 16 m off each side of the cabin. A gradient standing up in
front of a window has no perspective in it, so no amount of paint on that
sheet could ever have made it read as water. On the phone crop at 13 s and
19 s it came back as a flat blue-green band with a few blurred dashes.

It is real geometry now. Five layers, near to far:

1. the **hull** and its gunwale, so the water starts at a boat;
2. the **wake** — a foam strip alongside, scrolling fastest, because it is
   the nearest thing in frame and carries most of the sense of way being made;
3. the **water**: a disc under a seamless crest tile (every crest drawn nine
   times, at every wrap, so the tile is seamless by construction) scrolled
   slowly, with a second larger slower copy over it — two layers at different
   rates is what breaks the tiling a single sheet always shows at a grazing
   angle;
4. the **haze** cylinder, which takes the far water into the sky and hides
   the disc's own rim;
5. the **sky dome**, a painted tree line, and four ships on it.

Three things had to be measured to get there, and each is a law worth keeping.

### 4.1 THE CAMERA'S FAR PLANE IS 160 m

`new THREE.PerspectiveCamera(72, …, 0.08, 160)`.

The first pass built the water at radius 400 and the dome at 420 and was
simply **clipped**: a hard edge across the sea at 160 m with the real camp
showing past it. Every radius is now set by that number — dome 155, water 148,
haze 136, shore 132, furthest ship 128.

And the pocket MOVED, from (−70, −60) to (−70, −420). At 92 m the camp was
fine while the sea was two walls; open the windows onto real water and the
bunk block and its trees sit on the horizon in the middle of the Johor Strait.
Past 160 m from everything else, **distance does the hiding** — no flags, no
visibility bookkeeping, nothing to remember to switch off. Camp 426 m away,
jetty 461, parade 443. Fog and the far plane are both measured from the
CAMERA, which is inside the cabin, so nothing else about the set changed.

### 4.2 THE GUNWALE MUST SIT UNDER THE SILL

The eye is 3.76 m above the waterline, so the sea it can see runs from the
horizon down to whatever the boat's own side hides.

The first pass capped the hull at y 1.12 — 0.19 m under the seated eye — and
every ray steeper than **9.6°** then landed on the gunwale's deck rather than
on water. Ten degrees of sea in a 72° lens: the flat band the frame came back
with. Capped at 0.80, under a sill dropped from 1.06 to 0.86, the same
arithmetic gives **30.4°**. Three times the sea, for one number.

The window went with it: 0.64 m of glass became 1.06 (sill 0.86, head 1.92),
which from the seated eye 0.64 m away subtends 83° against a 72° lens — so
the contemplative shot is all window.

### 4.3 PAINT A FILM SET AT HALF THE BRIGHTNESS YOU WANT ON SCREEN

The renderer is **ACESFilmic at exposure 1.42**, which scales linear radiance
by 1.42 / 0.6 = **2.37 before the curve**, and the curve then rolls everything
bright toward white. Worked through the real transform:

| painted | arrives as |
|---|---|
| `#87b2d6` (an ordinary sky blue) | `#d5eaf2` — nearly white |
| `#314c78` | `#4e8bc8` — a proper sky blue |

That is why the first pass's window looked out on a white void, and it was
found by measuring (`dbg-sky.mjs` reported `toneMapping: 4`,
`toneMappingExposure: 1.42`) rather than by tuning colours until something
looked right. Every basic-material colour in the sky, the haze, the shore and
the car-park ground is now the INVERSE. Nothing dark needed it — water, hull
and tree line live where the curve is nearly linear.

A second, smaller instance of the same class: **a greyscale map MULTIPLIES the
material's colour**, so a mid-grey seat weave took the seats' blue to a third
of itself and the first render came back with black seats. The cloth tile is
near-white with its weave in the strokes' alpha. And `matChrome` — a metal
with no environment map has nothing to reflect and renders near black, which
is what the first pass's grab rails did; they are a light dielectric now.

### 4.4 AND THE CABIN IS NOT BAREBONES

A carpet and a seat cloth with weave in them, a grab rail across every row,
parcel shelves with bags on them and a lip rail, skirting, a sill ledge, two
life rings, a painted passenger notice, muster and exit signs, an
extinguisher, a life-jacket locker, and kit on the floor and on the spare
seats. All boxes, tubes and one canvas; all static, so v8.4's culling drops it
the moment the lens turns away.

One thing was tried and taken back out: a pale antimacassar over the whole
headrest. At a 1.31 m eye the headrest in front is 1.06, so it filled the 9 s
shot with a grey slab. It is a thin band on the crown now.

## 5 · THE PARADE SQUARE GROUND

One flat cream colour with three thin white lines on it. On the phone crop the
block and the ranks were standing in a white void.

A Tekong square IS a car park most of the week, so it gets what one has:
asphalt with its aggregate, worn patches (drawn nine times so the tile wraps)
and seal joints, and a grid of painted bays over the half the camera pans
across — 2.5 m bays, 5.0 m deep, back to back with a 6.0 m aisle, from z −46
forward so they fall behind and beside the ranks rather than under them.

392 dividers and 8 head lines in **one InstancedMesh** — a car park's worth of
paint in one draw call. The lines are GEOMETRY rather than paint in the tile
because a 10 cm stripe on an 11.5 m tile is two texels and would alias to
nothing.

## 6 · WHAT WAS RUN

Twelve harnesses, in foreground pairs: `chapter`, `walk`, `cine`, `leak`,
`text`, `fixture`, `hosted`, `menu`, `csp`, `sound`, `census`, `pile`. All
green. Chapter 1 is the control inside `cine`, `pile`, `leak` and `hosted`.

Probes, all against the real hosted build and all capturing `pageerror` and
console first (v8.5's law): `dbg-bunk.mjs` (who faces where),
`dbg-v89men.mjs` (what the restored pair costs), `dbg-v89door.mjs` (the door's
angle, and a flood fill of the collision grid), `dbg-leafpos.mjs` (where the
open leaf stands), `dbg-sky.mjs` (every sky sphere, the tone mapping, the
exposure), `dbg-sea.mjs` and `dbg-film.mjs` (the frames).

One probe law, paid for again: **a camera moved by hand under a PAUSED film is
put back before the shot** — `cineSeek` re-applies every track whose t0 has
passed, on every frame. The ferry set had to be photographed from PLAY, with
`ferryRoot.visible` forced on, which is free now that the pocket is 420 m out.
