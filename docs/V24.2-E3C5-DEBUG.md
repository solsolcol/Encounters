## 1. The soft-lock (v24.1)

THE CAUSE. The water is the engine's `craft` kind with one `hold` step (1.2 s, "Hold to pour their water"). On a
phone a finger held half a second is a LONG PRESS: the browser fires `contextmenu`, opens its own menu over the game
(and, on text, starts a selection), and CANCELS the touch — `pointercancel` ends the hold. Nothing in the game refused
that menu except the canvas while a rifle is wanted (v12.2), and no surface refused selection or the iOS callout. The
same long press interrupts every hold in the chapter: the water (1.2 s), the lighter (1.4 s), and the three openings
(`yield`, filled while held).

WHY EVERY PROBE PASSED. The v23.0 play-through (`dbg-e3c5flow.mjs`) dispatched PointerEvents on the overlay in code;
a dispatched event never passes through the browser's gesture detection, so no long press, no menu, no cancel. And
headless Chromium sends no long-press `contextmenu` even for a real CDP touch held 2.6 s (measured on the live v24.0:
`menus: 0`, poured). The v18.3 law ("a minigame probe drives the REAL input path") was half-kept: the hotspots were
tapped for real, the gestures were not — and even the real path cannot show this browser behaviour, so the check is
now on what the game does WITH a long press's contextmenu (refused, and the text under the finger unselectable).

THE FIX (engine, every chapter — a long press is never the browser's in this game):
- `shell.html`: `html, body` carry `-webkit-user-select: none; user-select: none; -webkit-touch-callout: none` (the
  game has no text input; the volume slider is a range).
- `src/main.js`: a document `contextmenu` listener refuses the menu on a touch device anywhere but a link (the
  credits'), and with a mouse only while a minigame is open; a desktop keeps its menu everywhere else.
- The hold already keeps its progress across a lifted finger ("let go, it waits"), so a hold interrupted by anything
  else (a system gesture) is resumed by pressing again.
