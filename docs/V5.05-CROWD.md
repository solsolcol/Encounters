# v5.05 · TWO IN THE CROWD WHO ARE NOT ENCIK

Chad's note, in full: *"the 2 character models with animations fbx files
here in this folder … I want the medium event audience character, for 2 of
them to be replaced by this 2 models. Because there are too many repetitive
ones now."*

He was right, and the reason is structural rather than a matter of taste.
Since v4.7 every seated person in chapter 3's tentage has been ONE scan —
Encik Lim Cheng Teck, duduk — cloned about nineteen times, tinted a little
differently each time and mirrored on every third seat. v4.8 gave the whole
set a life by pointing every clone at one of four offstage skeletons running
the file's own idle. All of that works, and none of it changes the fact that
it is one man nineteen times: tints do not make faces, and a mirror does not
make a different person.

## What arrived

Two Mixamo FBX takes, each a whole character with one clip:

| file | character | clip | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Sitting Clap.fbx` | Ch41 — white top, dark trousers | 4.50 s, 109 keys | 47.5 MB |
| `Sitting Angry.fbx` | Ch31 — navy sweater, ponytail | 3.33 s, 81 keys | 54.5 MB |

## Getting them shippable

Converted with the repo's own `node_modules/fbx2gltf` binary, then run
through gltf-transform. The measurements, because the ratios are the useful
part and the absolute numbers are not:

| stage | clap | angry |
|---|---|---|
| FBX in | 47.5 MB | 54.5 MB |
| GLB out of FBX2glTF | 38.2 MB | 47.6 MB |
| shipped | **917 KB** | **1040 KB** |

Where the weight went, in order of size:

1. **The maps nothing can display.** Each character shipped a 4096² normal
   map (19–24 MB), a 4096² packed AO/metal/rough, and two diffuse sheets.
   The engine's CSP-safe `rescueTextures()` in `main.js` only ever restores
   the BASE COLOR map — every other map renders white in production. So all
   of them are DROPPED, not merely resized: a map that cannot survive the
   policy is pure download. `roughness` is set flat at 0.85 and `metalness`
   at 0, which is what a person in a car park looks like anyway.
2. **The body sheet** to 512 JPEG at q84 — 12 MB to 28 KB. It is a
   background face at three metres.
3. **The hair/eyelash sheet** stays PNG, because it carries the cutout
   alpha that makes hair cards read as hair, and JPEG has no alpha. 256²,
   which is where it stops mattering: 303 KB and 494 KB, still the largest
   single thing in each file.
4. **Geometry** welded and simplified with meshopt at ratio 0.28, error
   0.02: ~50k triangles down to ~14k. Hair resists collapsing (alpha cards
   have seams meshopt will not cross) and stays the largest mesh in both.

Reference weight: `encik.glb` is 818 KB for 4200 triangles and one 512
texture. These two sit in the same class for four times the geometry,
because they carry a skeleton and a clip and he carries a bake.

## How they are wired

`SPECIALS` in `ch3.js` names the two seats, beside `ODD` and `BACK` where
the rest of the seating plan lives:

```
{ key: 'sitclap',  i: 1 * 8 + 4 }   // row two, right of the aisle
{ key: 'sitangry', i: 2 * 8 + 2 }   // row three, the far side
```

Those two indices were not picked by eye. The seating scatter skips the odd
chair and its neighbours, thins the front row, and drops every fifth seat,
so only nineteen of the thirty-two are taken; and `LOW` thins the crowd
again by skipping odd indices. Both chosen seats are occupied, even (so
they survive `LOW`), in the two middle rows, and on opposite sides of the
central aisle — which is where the walkable lanes run and therefore where a
face is actually seen.

Three things follow from that, and each is a small contract:

- **The encik loader leaves those two seats alone.** It skips them when it
  rebuilds the crowd, and it does not hide their primitive placeholder.
- **The placeholder is hidden only when the real model lands.** A download
  that never finishes costs a nicer face, never an empty chair — the same
  rule every other model in this game loads under.
- **Order does not matter.** Whichever of the three loaders arrives first,
  the other two find the world in a state they can work with, because the
  special groups also carry `userData.seat` and are therefore `kept` by the
  encik loader's filter.

Unlike the crowd, these two get a skeleton and an `AnimationMixer` each. The
shared-pose trick exists because thirty skeletons is thirty; two is two.
Their mixers are updated with `dt * crowdLife`, so they freeze and thaw with
everybody else when a scene stills the tent.

## The trap this build found

Sizing and grounding from POSED bones is the standing law and both of them
follow it. But the *crown* the encik crowd measures to is not the crown you
would write today: it tests `/Head/i`, which on a Mixamo skeleton also
matches `HeadTop_End` — so the height it normalises is the top of the skull.
Measured with `HEAD_RE` (`/Head(_\d+)?$/`, correct for finding the head
JOINT) these two normalised to the neck instead and stood a visible head
taller than everyone around them.

So the special loader deliberately uses the crowd's own loose `/Head/i`,
with a comment saying why. **When a new thing is placed among existing
things, the measure that matters is the one the existing things used** — not
the one that is technically right.

Measured before: crowns at 1.577 and 1.572 m. After: 1.348 and 1.316, which
is where the encik crowd sits.
