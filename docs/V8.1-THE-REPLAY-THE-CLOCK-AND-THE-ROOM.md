## 1 · THE REPLAY THAT WENT MUTE

**Measured first**, against the shipped v8.0 build (`dbg-replay.mjs`):

```
 before replay:  phase free   dayClock 2.00    sayLine('b1day')  -> true
 after  reset :  phase free   dayClock 0.80    sayLine('b1day')  -> false
                                               sayLine('k1board') -> false
```

The chapter refuses a line while one is already speaking, and it books that
window as a TIME on its own clock:

```js
speak.until = dayClock.t + (SECS[name] || 2.5) + 0.25;
```

`reset()` puts `dayClock.t` back to zero for the new run — and left
`speak.until` holding a time from the run that just ended. So the new day had
to catch up to it before anything could speak again. In the probe above that
was a couple of seconds. In a real playthrough the day clock reaches a minute
or two before you replay, so **the entire replayed chapter was mute**: the
buddy, the bunkmate, the notice board, the shower, and every line the sergeant
says. Exactly Chad's report.

Why a same-chapter replay hits it and a fresh load does not: `setChapter(key)`
returns early when the key is already current, so the chapter is **not
rebuilt** — `build()` does not run, the closure survives, and only `reset()`
puts the world back. Anything `reset()` forgets, the next run inherits.

The fix is `speakReset()`, called from `reset()`. `freeWarned` and `freeTimer`
go with it for the same reason.

**After**: `sayLine('b1day')` -> `true` on the replayed run.

The general shape is worth keeping: **a value stated in a clock has to be
cleared by whatever resets that clock.** `todo`, `tweens`, `arrivedAt` and
`fallTimer` were all already in `reset()`; `speak` was added at v8.0 and the
list was not re-read.

## 2 · THE COUNTDOWN YOU CAN SEE

The evening section always ran on a 70-second clock — it just ran it
privately, off `dayClock`, with nothing on screen. The lights went out with no
warning a player could read, which is the whole of Chad's complaint.

It runs on **`kit.timer`** now: the same seam the fall-in has used since v7.1,
which paints `M:SS` beside the objective line and turns it red under ten
seconds. The timer is what ENDS the phase, so what the player reads is what
the chapter obeys, rather than a display beside a second clock that could
drift from it.

- `FREE_SECS` 70, `FREE_WARN` the last 25 seconds (was: a warn at 45 elapsed,
  the same moment).
- Going to bed early still cuts it short — `beginLightsOut` stops the timer.
- `applyPhase('free')` restarts it, so a Continue in the middle of the evening
  comes back with a countdown instead of none.
- `reset()` clears it.

Measured on the build: `__enc.kitDebug().timer` reads 70 the moment the phase
opens, and the HUD shows `OBJECTIVE  Look around the bunk before lights out
1:08`.

The 70 seconds itself did not change. Making it visible changes how it FEELS —
a deadline you can see is a different thing from one you cannot — and that is
a design call, so it is Chad's to move if he wants it longer.

## 3 · A MAN AT EVERY BED

Nine beds, one of them his, and until now three people in the room: the
sergeant at the door, the buddy, the bunkmate. Six made beds belonged to
nobody while the narration said twenty of us to a bunk.

Six recruits now stand at the foot of the other six beds.

**They all STAND, and that is forced rather than preferred.** The whistle
empties the bunk onto the balcony (v7.4), and `mkCrowd` gives a copy exactly
ONE action — so a man parked on a seated take has nothing to switch to and
would ride the fall-in sitting in mid-air. Standing men fall in.

**Two models, and that is casting.** The first pass put the FBO rig in the
mix and the photographs settled it: it wears full battle order — helmet, vest,
field pack — and six of him standing about a bunk at ten to ten at night reads
as a deployment, not a bunk. The sergeant wears it because he is on duty and
the bunkmate has worn it since v7.5; everyone else is in what a recruit wears
in his own bunk. So: **three `admintee` on `Idle_9`, three `botak` on
`Walking` parked at t = 0.122** — the frame the parade square is built on
(feet closest, hands lowest), because the botak has no standing idle at all.
Nothing new is downloaded: both files are already in this chapter.

Numbers, all measured rather than eyeballed:

| | |
|---|---|
| x | ±3.25 — the foot of the bed, and **0.26 m clear** of the blocker column `solid()` puts round the mattress (±3.51), so nobody stands in the walking lane |
| z | the six beds' own: −3.00, −1.50, 0.00 on the −x row; −2.20, 2.20, 3.35 on the +x row |
| the pair at −3.00 / −1.50 | face each other across 1.5 m — the conversation told by where they stand, since a looping talking take with no sound to go under it reads worse than two men turned toward each other |
| `LOW` | keeps four of the six, dealt before the crowds are built |

Wired into the three places the room already knew about:

- `fallOut(on)` -> `bunkCrowdPlace(on)`. Measured on the build: all eight men
  (six new, plus the buddy and the bunkmate) move from their beds to x 7.40,
  z −1.70 … 4.25 at 0.85 m spacing, and back again.
- `setNightRoom(on)` -> `bunkCrowdShow(!on)`. Measured: the crowd is hidden at
  lights out, with the rest of the day cast.
- `applyState` -> the same, so a Continue at three in the morning does not
  restore six men standing over the sleepers.

They are not blockers, so `walktest` is unaffected by construction.

## 4 · AND THE BUNKMATE HAS NEVER MOVED WHEN HE TALKS

Found while reading the interaction handlers for §1. Both his lines call

```js
bunkmate.play('mixamo.com', …)
```

and `assets/fbonosling.glb` ships `Running | Walking |
Gesture_with_Hand_on_Gun | Gun_Hold_Left_Turn | Idle_6 | Rifle_Charge_inplace`
— **there is no clip of that name.** `mixamo.com` was the FOUR-animation admin
tee's own FBX name, and it was left behind at v7.5 when the bunkmate stopped
being the admin tee and became the FBO without a rifle. `rig.play` of a take a
rig does not have is a no-op by design, so from v7.5 to v8.0 he stood dead
still through every line he said, with no error anywhere. (v8.0 fixed the
sibling half of the same slip — his return-to-idle asked for `Idle_9`, which
he also does not have.)

The FBO carries no talking take at all. Its one gesture,
`Gesture_with_Hand_on_Gun`, was **photographed rather than assumed** and is a
CROUCH — a man kneeling with one leg forward — so it cannot serve.

So `Talk_with_Left_Hand_Raised` is transplanted from `fbosling`, and **which
tool does the transplant was measured, not guessed.** The two files share the
same 27 `mixamorig:` joints, which is exactly the case where copying by name
looks safe — and it is not: their rest ROTATIONS differ by up to 22° at the
forearms, 21° at the feet, 17° at Spine2, 16° at the shoulders, 13° at the
head. That is the v5.20 law meeting the v7.6 one: two rigs can pose a body
identically and still hold their bones at different rest orientations, and
copying by name copies the numbers and loses the meaning. It went through
`tools/retarget.mjs` in world space.

Verified by render, front and side, four frames across the clip: upright,
hands gesturing at waist height, geometry intact, feet planted, no tearing.

`assets/fbonosling.glb` 4,067,948 -> 4,134,304 bytes, six clips -> seven. The
chapter names it `TALK_NOSL`, and the sergeant keeps
`Talk_with_Left_Hand_on_Hip`, so the two men do not talk with the same hands.
