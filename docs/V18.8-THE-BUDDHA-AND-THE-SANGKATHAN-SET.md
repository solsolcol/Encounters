## The Buddha (a Phra Buddha Chinnarat scan, 588,799 triangles)

**Where.** Every Buddha in episode 3 was the Thai kit's, drawn through each
chapter's `thai('buddha', …)` — so the swap is in that one helper (`chadBuddha`),
and every placement keeps its place and size: Chad's statue is baked on its
base centre and sized at load to the kit's 0.527 m before the placement's own
scale. e3c1: the principal image (now ×4.5, 2.37 m — LARGE), the two flanking
it, the six small images in rows inside the temple, the six in the courtyard
gallery. e3c2: the great principal image (now ×9.0, 4.74 m to its arch's tip
under a 7.2 m ceiling, sitting the pedestal's top tier), the four on the altar
row, the gold-leaf Buddha. No tint (the kit's needed one; his gold is his own).

**Looking perfect, measured by photograph.**
- Every triangle and every map, the metal-roughness sheet included (that sheet
  IS the sheen): `buddhahd`, 8.2 MB.
- A lighter cut floored at ~123k (the atlas's islands are seams the simplifier
  won't cross) and read as a FACETED face — not the normal map (dropping it
  changed nothing) but normals split along the seams, which polished metal
  shows; recomputed across the seams it is clean (`smoothn.mjs`). A 30k
  topology-free cut of the same vertices is the far level (`buddhalod`).
- The world's reflection environment is 0.05 on purpose; the statue's material
  takes the scene's environment at its own strength, bracketed in the hall:
  0 bronze-dark, 0.3+ pale, **0.15** gold.

**Phone cost.** A THREE.LOD per image. The three the player stands before —
e3c1's principal, e3c2's principal, the gold-leaf Buddha (`hd: true`) — are the
full statue while big on screen (under 4× their height away), the smoothed cut
further, the 30k cut when small (12×). Every other image (under a metre tall)
is the smoothed cut near and the 30k cut beyond 4× its height. First pass had
every image full-detail when near: 2.6M triangles at e3c1's altar.

**The gold-leaf patches** (e3c2) were positioned against the kit statue's body;
on landing each is now cast along its own line onto the real surface (front:
chest and base; back: the statue's back).
