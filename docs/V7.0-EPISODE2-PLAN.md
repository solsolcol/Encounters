## 1 · The five chapters

Every chapter keeps the spine the engine knows — world, opening film,
ONE four-choice decision, four cutscenes, teaching card, rank — and
everything before the decision is played. Deltas are the trial's ×3
(episode 1's practice), capped ±30; *critical* marks the trial's rule
(wisdom ≤ −15). **Reaction challenges move Sanity and Awareness only;
Wisdom is the card's alone** (the trial's rule, adopted).

### E2C1 · THE WORST BED — day 1, and the first 3 AM

**Place.** Hawk Company's bunk as the film shows it: double-deck beds in
two rows, lockers, fans, the notice board, a red 03:00 wall clock, the
balcony over the parade square, and at the front the toilet-and-shower
block. His is the bottom bunk nearest that door.

**Film (~60 s).** Ferry, gates, "bed one — next to the toilet", locker,
lights out. *"The worst spot in the room, and the one nobody wanted."*

**Play — day.**
- **FALL IN** (objective + timer): the whistle; run to the line before
  the count ends. Late = the sergeant and push-ups.
- **STANDBY BED** (reaction test, Chad's design): items appear one after
  another, fast, each for a short window — pillow, blanket fold, boots,
  mess tin, water bottle, helmet, field pack, name tag — and the player
  TAPS each before it fades, the pace rising, all inside 60 s. Each item
  is a 3D model or an icon from Chad's image. Misses cost the timer;
  a failed layout means the whole bunk does it again (a sanity cost and
  the sergeant's line). Awareness on the result.
- **THE BUNK** (hotspots): the shower block in daylight, the bunkmates
  (the buddy who recurs), the notice board ("Wk 2 — live firing").
- **LIGHTS OUT** → time skip. The bed is a hotspot; interacting lies him
  down in play.

**Play — night.** 03:00. The shower starts. **HEARTBEAT · FEAR CONTROL**
on the HUD's own ECG: tap on the beat, five beats; misses take sanity.
Then the decision (the trial's THE SOUND):

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | GET UP AND OPEN THE TOILET DOOR | 0 | +15 | +6 | Investigating can provide information, but moving straight toward uncertainty is not automatically the wisest first step. |
| B | STAY STILL AND LISTEN | +9 | +18 | +18 | Hearing something frightening is an observation. Deciding what caused it is an interpretation. |
| C | WHISPER TO YOUR BUNKMATE — "YOU AWAKE?" | +6 | +15 | +15 | Check simple human explanations before building a more complicated interpretation. |
| D | IT'S A GHOST — BLANKET OVER YOUR HEAD · *critical* | −15 | −9 | −18 | Fear is a signal, not proof. |

**Scenes.** A: the walk to the block, the running shower, nobody — and for
one frame at the end of the corridor, the soldier standing (the film's
image). B: the fan, the clock, the water stopping by itself. C: the
buddy's "huh?". D: the blanket over his face, the sound going on.

### E2C2 · THE MORNING AFTER — day 2, morning only

**Place.** The **live-firing range**: 100 m lanes, the butts, figure-11
targets, safety bays, the tower. Plus the bunk at breakfast.

**Film (~40 s).** 05:30, the bunk waking, the block in daylight (the
tiles still wet at the drain — a detail, not a scare), the armskote, the
SAR 21 into his hands.

**Play.**
- **TALK TO THE BUNK** (hotspots, before the range): four platoon mates
  about last night. Each has a line — the buddy heard nothing, one says
  "the 3 AM one? heard before, never looked", one laughs, one older
  re-enlistee says "don't play play with these things".
- **STRIP AND ASSEMBLE** (reaction test, timed): the rifle's parts in
  order, tapped in sequence, the buddy fumbling his.
- **THE RANGE** (rifle mode, blanks, objectives + timer): the detail,
  the tower's commands — *"Shooters, watch your front. Ready."* —
  figure-11s rising at 100 m and 50 m, fire on *"Fire!"*, stop on
  *"Cease fire!"*, the score read aloud. This is where aim, fire, recoil
  and reload are learned with nothing to fear. The score is a line and a
  tease, never a stat.

**Decision** — after the range, the section in the smoking corner, the
story of last night out loud. The trial's WHAT CAN YOU ACTUALLY CONCLUDE
set, which is exactly a conversation about an experience:

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | "SOMETHING HAPPENED. I DON'T KNOW WHAT." | +18 | +24 | +30 | Document before interpreting. Wisdom can include: I don't know yet. |
| B | "THE BUNK IS HAUNTED. I'M SURE." · *critical* | −12 | −9 | −30 | Confirmation bias can make ordinary events feel like proof. |
| C | "I WAS TIRED. I IMAGINED IT." | +9 | −21 | −21 | Scepticism is not the same as dismissal. |
| D | ASK THE ENCIK WHAT HE THINKS | +15 | +21 | +30 | Good guidance expands your thinking; it does not replace your judgement. |

**Scenes.** A: the section quiet, the buddy nodding, the rifle cleaned.
B: the story growing in the telling, the bunk laughing, and that night
every drip in the block is proof. C: "tired lah", and the older man
looking at him a beat too long. D: the encik's answer, which is not an
answer — plumbing, tiredness, "and if it's not, don't disturb it" — the
episode's lesson said early by someone who has done twenty years.

### E2C3 · THE BLANKET — day-2 night, then day 3

**Place.** The bunk only — the cheapest chapter and the most intimate.

**Film (~30 s).** Lights out, day 2. The clock. The fan. Not asleep.

**Play — night.** Lying (the play pose). The blanket takes weight at the
foot of the bed and PULLS. **STAY DOWN** (Chad's design): the body wants
to sit up — a bar that fills by itself, and the player MASHES a button
as fast as they can to keep it down while the blanket goes inch by inch
and "SIT UP" / "LOOK" flash as temptations; eight seconds. Hold it, and
the film's image: over the edge of the bunk above, **a grey face looking
down**, then not. Fail, and he sits up: the sanity chunk lands, presence
rises, and the face is closer. *"I simply stayed there and didn't react."*

**Play — day 3.** Verify: **ASK THE BUNK** (hearsay in daylight — which
is why the decision's A, a witness woken at 3 AM, is worth more); **THE
PLUMBING** (a tracing puzzle: the cold feed to the timer valve — nothing
visibly wrong, the trial's own outcome); **THE PROGRAMME** (route march,
SOC, field camp; the buddy: "sleep when you can").

**The days blur** (montage: march, range, SOC wall, boots, bed, faster
each time), and the decision on the last of those nights, the trial's
VERIFY set:

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | WAKE YOUR BUDDY AND ASK | +15 | +24 | +27 | Seek independent information before drawing conclusions. |
| B | CHECK THE PLUMBING AGAIN | +12 | +27 | +24 | Failure to find an ordinary explanation is not proof of a supernatural explanation. |
| C | IT MUST BE SUPERNATURAL · *critical* | −21 | −6 | −24 | Unexplained does not mean supernatural. |
| D | SLEEP. IT'S 3 AM. | +21 | +9 | +30 | You do not have to solve everything immediately. |

D is the source's own ending: sleep takes him mid-thought; morning; *"Once
I stopped giving the experience my attention, it stopped giving me its
attention too."* The chapter closes on the posting: infantry, outfield,
three days two nights.

### E2C4 · THE PRESSURE — outfield, night one

**Place.** Secondary jungle at night (the v6.15 trees, dense and fogged,
plus undergrowth), a track in, the section's harbour and shell scrapes.
The torch is what you see by.

**Film (~50 s).** The tonner, the drop-off at last light, single file,
the whispered brief. *"I was at the very rear of my section, facing
away from the others, looking out into the dark."*

**Play — the exercise (rifle mode, objectives).**
1. **MOVE TO THE HARBOUR** — the section commander's bearing; torch
   discipline (red filter; white light draws the sergeant).
2. **DIG IN** — the shell scrape as a mash event; the buddy beside him.
3. **FIRST STAG** — the rear, facing out; the radio check (*"Charlie Two,
   this is Charlie Zero, radio check, over"* — answer within four
   seconds), the micro-sleep (the screen dips; tap), a branch on the left.

**THE PRESSURE.** Weight on his leg — the phone vibrates, the camera
nudges. The look down is a reflex the game performs; the torch shows the
boot and nothing. The pressure stays. **HOLD THE BEAM** (stabilise): keep
the torch steady on the leg and, at the edge of the light, the film's
image — a grey hand on the trouser, an arm, **a crouched soldier against
the tree**. Swing the beam onto it: gone. *"For a moment, I thought I saw
something. Then whatever was there disappeared."*

**Decision**, still in the scrape — the trial's HALLWAY set, whose
teachings are about light, darkness, other people and provocation, and
fit a jungle at 2 AM unchanged:

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | PUT THE TORCH ON IT BEFORE YOU MOVE | +9 | +18 | +21 | Courage does not require deliberately removing your own safety advantages. |
| B | REACH DOWN INTO THE DARK | −12 | +9 | −9 | Making observation harder is not bravery. |
| C | ASK YOUR BUDDY IF HE FELT IT | +6 | +21 | +24 | Independent context can narrow possibilities without forcing a conclusion. |
| D | "WHO'S THERE?" | −6 | +6 | −12 | Provocation and investigation are not the same thing. |

**Scenes.** A: the beam held; the figure fully in it for a second — old
uniform, grey face, no expression — then only the tree. B: the hand into
cold leaf litter, and something moving away from the fingers. C: "felt
what?", and the two of them looking out together until relief. D: his
voice across the harbour, the section commander hissing him quiet, the
pressure lifting exactly as if answered.

### E2C5 · THE CYCLIST — outfield, night two

**Place.** The ambush position across a shallow gully from the other
platoon's harbour. Same jungle set, second position.

**Film (~40 s).** Day two in ten seconds; the ambush line at dusk; the
brief: live rounds tonight (Chad's call), *"Nobody fires until I say."*

**Play — the exercise.**
1. **MOVE TO THE AMBUSH LINE** — bearing, red light.
2. **THE AMBUSH DRILL** — the enemy party crosses the killing ground;
   *"Shooters, watch your front. Ready."* … *"Fire!"* — a timed event:
   on the order, not before (early = "you've compromised the section"),
   not late; live rounds, the flash lighting the jungle for a frame, the
   silhouettes dropping; *"Cease fire!"* The player has just been
   trained that a moving shape is answered with the rifle.

**THE STAG.** Two in the morning. The rear. The radio check. Then:

**THE CONFUSION** (Chad's beat). Movement across the gully — a shape,
upright, crossing the other platoon's scrapes. The section sees it too:
*"Contact! Someone there — two o'clock!"* from the next scrape, the
buddy's rifle coming up. The radio: *"Charlie Zero, negative. No
friendlies out. No enemy party. Stand fast."* Everyone says someone is
there; the radio says nobody is. No fire order comes. The shape keeps
crossing — too smooth for walking, a hand-span off the ground, no
vegetation moving — and the torch's edge finds **a bicycle under it,
and a rider that gives off its own light**.

**THE PLAYED MOMENT.** The rifle is in his hands, loaded.
- **FIRE**: the shot, the flash, the figure GONE — and back, nearer, on
  a different bearing, the chain now faintly audible. Each shot: a sanity
  chunk, presence up, closer. *"Who fired?"* After the third it is at the
  gully's lip, stopped, facing him, and **the bell rings once**. Live
  rounds change nothing: it does not come back because bullets fail; it
  comes back because he engaged it.
- **HOLD**: the lowered rifle as a hold event, FIRE flashing as the
  temptation; it crosses the whole gully at walking pace, close, at the
  edge of the torch, and as it passes its head turns to him — no face —
  and the bell rings once. Longer and nearer than the firing branch's.
- **TRACK** (focus challenge, under both): keeping the torch on a moving
  thing earns awareness, whether or not he fires.
- **REPORT** (the radio button): *"Charlie Zero, this is Charlie Two,
  contact — negative, not enemy, over."*

Then it is THERE, at the lip, in the torch, and **the decision opens with
it still coming, on a clock** — a bar that is its approach; reach the
scrape before you choose and a sanity chunk lands (never wisdom):

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching (NEW, on S1·05's sentences — for Chad's approval) |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | KEEP YOUR ARC. REPORT IT. CARRY ON. | +15 | +21 | +28 | Carry on with your duties, keep your mind clear, stay grounded. |
| B | PUT THE TORCH ON IT AND GO CLOSER | −9 | +18 | −6 | Not everything needs to be challenged or investigated. |
| C | FIRE AT IT UNTIL IT STOPS COMING · *critical* | −24 | +6 | −30 | Do not engage, do not provoke, and do not feed your own fear. |
| D | GET UP AND RUN · *critical* | −21 | −15 | −24 | Your own experience and somebody else's account are two different things. |

**Scenes.** A: the arc held, the radio, it goes into the trees; then the
other platoon crashing out through the harbour — *"Eh, what is that? The
bicycle, it's floating! Look over there!"* — the exercise called, and at
the tonner their story told AT him, kept separate from his own. B: the
walk across the gully, the empty scrapes, tyre tracks that are not
there, and the thing behind him now. C: the flashes, the reappearances,
the last close enough to see there is no face, the whole section
shouting at HIM, and in the morning the other platoon's account, which
now sounds like proof and is not. D: the run, the branches, straight into
the other platoon running too, the tonner's headlights, the sergeant
counting heads.

**The ending** (~40 s, over the tonner at dawn): *"Something pressed on
my leg. Another platoon saw a man on a bicycle. Those are two different
things, and I only know one of them. I don't know what it was. I don't
have to."* — and the film's own card: **"What you can't see doesn't mean
it doesn't exist. Stay calm. Observe. Don't let fear control your
response."** Then the EPISODE COMPLETE card.
