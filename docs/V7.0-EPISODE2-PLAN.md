# EPISODE 2 · THE 3 AM SHOWER — the plan

*Status: PROPOSAL. Nothing in this document is built. Chad asked to see the
plan before anything is executed; the DECISION items at the end are his.
This draft was reviewed once against the source files and the engine
before he saw it; the corrections it took are folded in, not listed.*

Episode 1 is complete and **v6.17 is the base-game reference standard**
(Chad's call, 8 Sep 2026: "good enough as the base game reference with a
complete episode 1" — recorded in CLAUDE.md with this document). Episode 2
is the first thing built on top of it, and it is built for a different
player: the boy of the void deck is a young man now, and he has been
enlisted.

Chad's brief, in his words: *"more gameplay elements ... more than just
simple click on one item, get a cutscene and go to next chapter ... more
exciting, complex and dynamic ... playable missions where the game turns
into a first person shooter with night objectives to simulate outfield
training, and after a while, that's when the ghost cyclist appears ...
initial confusion, shoot the cyclist, and it keeps reappearing ... more
minigames? more quick time events? reaction based events? ... step up to
another level in gameplay."*

This document answers four questions: is there enough story; what the five
chapters are; what the new gameplay is and what it costs the engine; and
what only Chad can decide.

---

## 0 · Is there enough story? Yes — and here is exactly where it comes from

Master Z's forty-three case files contain **two** from his army years, and
they are the two Chad named:

- **S1·04 The 3 AM Shower** — BMT, Hawk Company, Pulau Tekong. The bed at
  the front of the bunk beside the toilet, "the one nobody wanted". For
  several nights at almost exactly three the shower turns itself on. One
  night he gets up to look: running, nobody there, straight back to bed.
  That night something tugs his blanket; he stays still and does not react.
  Then training exhausts him, he sleeps through, stops paying attention —
  and it stops. *Lesson: rule out plumbing first; if nothing is asking
  anything of you, don't engage, don't provoke, don't feed your own fear;
  acknowledging a presence can create a connection that was not there.*
- **S1·05 The Ghostly Cyclist** — infantry training, a jungle outfield
  exercise. Rear of the section, facing out. Real weight pressing on his
  leg; torch straight down, nothing; the pressure stays; he swings the beam
  and it is gone. **What he did not know until later**: the same night
  another platoon saw a figure on a bicycle floating across their shell
  scrapes, panicked, and evacuated. *Lesson: you feel before you see;
  ordinary explanations first; your experience and somebody else's account
  are two different things; carry on with your duties.*

And the **2D trial game's own Episode 2** is five fully written decision
sets for the shower story — every choice, its sanity/awareness/wisdom
deltas and its teaching, in Master Z's voice (`docs/source/trial-game-
chapters.md`): THE SOUND, THE HALLWAY, THE BATHROOM, VERIFY THE HOUSEHOLD,
WHAT CAN YOU ACTUALLY CONCLUDE. The trial set them in a house; the site's
file says it happened in a platoon bunk, so the game moves them back to
Tekong. Core lesson, verbatim: *"Fear is not proof. Investigate safely,
improve viewing conditions, look for ordinary explanations, preserve
evidence, and accept uncertainty when the facts do not justify a final
answer."*

So the arithmetic: two true stories, five written decision sets, and Chad's
gameplay brief. Three of the trial's sets carry the three bunk chapters
nearly verbatim (its HALLWAY and BATHROOM sets fold into one chapter, with
the hallway's "lights on or off" becoming something the player DOES rather
than picks). The outfield chapter needs **one new decision set**, written
on the trial's own archetype table (docs/SOURCE-NOTES.md — provocation is
always the worst move, asking for help always the best) and submitted to
Chad for his words. The fifth chapter is the trial's CONCLUDE set, and it
is set at HOME, on book-out, in the flat episode 1 already built.

**One departure from the source, and it is Chad's ask, so it is named
rather than hidden.** In S1·05 Master Z never saw the cyclist: he felt a
weight on his leg, and the bicycle is what ANOTHER platoon reported. In
the game the player sees it — that is the brief ("shoot the cyclist, and
it keeps reappearing"). The plan keeps the file's truth inside that: his
own encounter is still the pressure on the leg; what crosses the gully is
what the other platoon is about to run from; and the chapter's lesson
turns on keeping the two apart. It is DECISION 1 below, because it is the
only place this episode says something the case file does not.

**The escalation axis**, the way episode 1's was DISTANCE → SMALLNESS →
INVERSION → INTRUSION → RELEASE:

| # | chapter | axis | what it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | THE WORST BED | ROUTINE | the army takes his days; the shower takes his nights |
| 2 | THE CHECK | CURIOSITY | "curiosity got the better of me" — he goes to look |
| 3 | THE BLANKET | CONTACT | he looked, and that night it touched back; he does not react; exhaustion ends it |
| 4 | THE SHELL SCRAPES | ENGAGEMENT | a rifle in his hands and something in the dark — the one chapter where provocation has a trigger |
| 5 | BOOK OUT | DISCERNMENT | what can you actually conclude — written down at Ma's table |

Chapter 4 is the loudest and chapter 5 is the quietest, exactly as THE
LESSON closed episode 1 — and the site's own reading order (S1·04 then
S1·05, BMT then infantry) is the play order.

## 1 · The five chapters

Every chapter keeps the shape the engine and the harnesses know — a world,
an opening film, ONE decision of four choices, four cutscenes, a teaching
card, a rank — because that spine is what `chaptertest`, the sheet, the
saves and the selector are built on. What changes is everything BEFORE the
decision: instead of walking to one object, the player plays through the
day and the night, and what they did in play can tilt the outcome (§3.8).

**On the numbers.** Every delta below is the trial's, scaled the way
chapters 2 and 3 of episode 1 were scaled — about ×3 (chapter 2's trial
row +5/+4/+9 shipped as +15/+12/+25), rounded to the nearest whole number
with halves away from zero, and capped at ±30. They are starting values;
the build tunes them so one rank formula serves ten chapters.

### E2C1 · THE WORST BED

**Place.** Hawk Company's platoon bunk on Tekong: one long room, sixteen
beds in two rows, a locker per man, the fans, the notice board, the
balcony corridor over the parade square, and at the FRONT of the room the
toilet-and-shower block with its swing door. His bed is the first one, its
head against the toilet wall. One location serves chapters 1–3, at three
times of day (morning, lights-out, 3 AM) — the same economy as episode 1's
flat serving chapters 4 and 5.

**Film (~60 s).** The ferry's wake and the island coming up; the gates;
the bunk assignment; "bed one — next to the toilet"; his locker; lights
out; the fan; the room breathing. First words in the young man's voice:
*"My bed was at the very front of the platoon bunk, next to the toilet.
The worst spot in the room, and the one nobody wanted."* The equipment
panel shows the OLDER figure from this chapter on (`ZAV_FIGURE` is keyed
by episode).

**Play — day.** The routine as gameplay, short and physical, and the
chapter that teaches every daylight system:
- **FALL IN** (objective + timer): the whistle from the parade square;
  the HUD says where and how long; run the corridor and the stairs to the
  line before the count ends. Late means the sergeant's line and the
  whole platoon's push-ups — a sanity cost you can hear.
- **STANDBY BED** (minigame, timed): ninety seconds. Blanket folded to
  the width of the board, hospital corners, boots on the line, locker to
  the layout on the notice board. A tap-to-place minigame with a
  countdown; a miss means the bunk does it again.
- **THE BUNK** (hotspots): the shower block (the taps, the drain, the
  cubicle — you can look now, in daylight; it is just a shower), your
  bunkmates (three or four, each with a line; one is the buddy who recurs
  through the episode), the notice board (the week's programme — also
  the chapter's foreshadowing: "Wk 3 — live firing").
- **LIGHTS OUT** → time skip: the room in blue dark, fifteen men breathing,
  the fan ticking. The bed is a hotspot; interacting LIES HIM DOWN in
  play (§3.9 — a play-time pose, not a cutscene; he can look where a man
  on his back can look). A minute of nothing. Then, at three, the shower.

**Play — night.** The sound is the decision. The four choices are the
trial's THE SOUND, re-worded for a bunk:

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching (Master Z, verbatim) |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | GET UP AND OPEN THE TOILET DOOR | 0 | +15 | +6 | Investigating can provide information, but moving straight toward uncertainty is not automatically the wisest first step. |
| B | STAY STILL AND LISTEN | +9 | +18 | +18 | Hearing something frightening is an observation. Deciding what caused it is an interpretation. |
| C | WHISPER TO YOUR BUNKMATE — "YOU AWAKE?" | +6 | +15 | +15 | Check simple human explanations before building a more complicated interpretation. |
| D | IT'S A GHOST — BLANKET OVER YOUR HEAD | −15 | −9 | −18 | Fear is a signal, not proof. |

**The four scenes.** A: the walk to the door in the dark, the running water
louder with every step, the door, the cubicle — running, nobody — and the
walk back that is longer than the walk there. B: the lying camera, the
sound, the fan, the time; he counts the beds by their breathing; the water
stops by itself. C: the buddy's mumbled "huh?", nothing else; a little
information, no answer. D: the blanket over his face, the sound going on
and on, his own breathing the loudest thing in the room.

### E2C2 · THE CHECK

**Place.** The day is the **live-firing range** — the first new set: the
100 m lanes, the butts, the figure-11 targets, the safety bays, the tower.
The night is the bunk again.

**Film (~45 s).** Rifle draw at the armskote: the SAR 21 into his hands
for the first time — the weight of it, the sergeant's words about it. The
range at nine in the morning, heat already on the tarmac.

**Play — day. RIFLE MODE, introduced in safety.**
- **STRIP AND ASSEMBLE** (minigame): the rifle's parts in order, a tap
  sequence with a timer; the buddy beside him fumbling his. Half of the
  episode's key mechanic learned as a puzzle.
- **THE RANGE** (rifle mode, daylight; objectives + timer): the detail,
  the safety drill, then the shoot: figure-11 targets rising at 100 m and
  50 m on a timer, scored — a marksmanship score the sergeant reads out.
  This is where the player learns aim, fire, recoil and reload with
  nothing in the dark to fear. **The score is never a stat delta**: a bad
  shot gets the sergeant's line and a bunkmate's tease, nothing else.

**Play — night.** Curiosity. The shower again at three, and this time he
gets up (the chapter is called THE CHECK because the source says he
checked). The walk to the front of the room IS the trial's HALLWAY set,
played rather than picked: **the light switch** is a hotspot on the way.
Flip it and the block is lit (awareness up; the wet floor readable);
leave it and walk in with the torch alone (sanity cost; the trial's "the
darkness magnifies every reflection"). That switch is this chapter's only
played conduct (§3.8). Either way the door, the block, the shower RUNNING
— and as he reaches for it, stopping by itself.

The decision is the trial's THE BATHROOM, set in a wet shower block at 3 AM:

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | LOOK WITHOUT TOUCHING ANYTHING | +3 | +24 | +21 | Observe the original state before you interfere. |
| B | TEST THE TAP | +6 | +15 | +6 | Look for ordinary explanations — but observe before altering evidence. |
| C | OPEN THE CUBICLE AND SEARCH IT | +3 | +21 | +15 | Eliminating one possibility does not prove the next possibility. |
| D | "WHO'S IN THERE?" | −9 | +3 | −15 | Timing can feel like proof without actually proving causation. |

**The four scenes.** A: the droplets on the tiles, the wet drain, the tap
handle's position — a slow inspection, and the source's "I went straight
back to my bed". B: the tap turned — water, normal, and he has moved the
handle; whatever it was pointing at, he will never know now. C: the
cubicle door, the wet empty stall; one possibility gone, nothing proven.
D: his voice in the tiled room, silence, and a shampoo bottle falling off
the wet shelf a second later — the only jump in the chapter, earned by
provocation.

### E2C3 · THE BLANKET

**Place.** The bunk, and nowhere else — the cheapest chapter to build and
the most intimate to play.

**Film (~30 s).** Straight from the block back to the bed. Lying down. The
fan. Not asleep.

**Play — night, first.** The **blanket tug** — the beat the source gives
us that the trial does not have. He is lying (the play-time pose); the
blanket at the foot of the bed takes weight and pulls. A **HOLD-STILL
event** (§3.3): the screen says nothing except a small "don't react" and a
bar that empties if the finger or the mouse drifts, while the temptation
prompts flash ("SIT UP" / "LOOK") and the blanket goes, inch by inch; the
breath sound; the pull stops. Let go, sit up, and the sanity chunk lands
and the chapter's presence rises (§3.7). Master Z: *"I stayed where I was
and tried not to react."* The engine's first reaction event, and the
lesson in its purest form: the winning move is to do nothing, and doing
nothing is HARD.

**Play — day.** Verify. Three things to do in the daylit bunk, any order,
each a hotspot or a minigame:
- **ASK THE BUNK** — walk the room and ask; four men, four lines, and
  none of them an answer ("bro, I sleep like a log", "the 3 AM one? heard
  before, never looked"). Hearsay in daylight — which is exactly why the
  decision's A, a bunkmate woken WHILE it is happening, is worth more.
- **THE PLUMBING** (minigame): the shower's pipework panel — trace the
  cold feed to the timer valve and its solenoid, the thing that COULD do
  this. Nothing visibly wrong with it. That is the trial's own outcome:
  *"No obvious visible fault appears, but that does not rule out hidden
  or intermittent mechanical issues"* — the puzzle is the tracing, and
  the result is honestly inconclusive, because the teaching it leads to
  is about NOT finding the ordinary cause.
- **THE PROGRAMME** — route march, SOC, field camp: the notice board says
  what is coming, and the buddy says it plainer: "Sleep when you can."

**The days blur** (a short film between day and decision — the montage the
source describes: "training became so exhausting that I simply slept
through the nights"): the route march, the range, the SOC wall, boots,
the bed, the fan, faster each time. The decision opens on the last of
those nights: the trial's VERIFY set.

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | WAKE YOUR BUDDY AND ASK | +15 | +24 | +27 | Seek independent information before drawing conclusions. |
| B | CHECK THE PLUMBING AGAIN | +12 | +27 | +24 | Failure to find an ordinary explanation is not proof of a supernatural explanation. |
| C | IT MUST BE SUPERNATURAL | −21 | −6 | −24 | Unexplained does not mean supernatural. |
| D | SLEEP. IT'S 3 AM. | +21 | +9 | +30 | You do not have to solve everything immediately. |

D is the source's own ending and the highest wisdom in the chapter — the
first time in the game that the best choice is to close your eyes. Its
scene: the sound, the fan, the breathing, and sleep taking him mid-thought;
then morning, and the line that ends the shower story: *"Once I stopped
giving the experience my attention, it stopped giving me its attention
too."* The chapter closes on the posting: infantry. Outfield next week.

### E2C4 · THE SHELL SCRAPES

The chapter Chad's brief is about. It is the biggest build in the episode
and it is built in two halves: **the exercise**, a night first-person
mission with objectives and no ghost in it at all, and **the stag**, when
the mission is over and he is alone at the rear of the section, and
something presses on his leg.

**Place.** The jungle harbour: secondary forest at night (the v6.15 tree
kit at last used for what a jungle needs — dense, tinted, fogged), a track
in from the drop-off, the section's harbour area with its shell scrapes,
and across a shallow gully, the other platoon's scrapes. The real
darkness: the world at a tenth of chapter 1's light, the torch the only
thing you see by. Sky through the canopy in patches.

**Film (~50 s).** The three-tonner, the drop-off at last light, the
section moving in single file, the section commander's brief in a whisper,
the sky going. His line: *"That night I was at the very rear of my
section, facing away from the others, looking out into the dark."*

**Play — THE EXERCISE (rifle mode, night objectives).** Three objectives,
each a HUD line and a waypoint, each a different verb:

1. **MOVE TO THE HARBOUR** — follow the section commander's bearing along
   the track. Torch discipline: red filter only; white light draws the
   sergeant's voice. A navigation beat that teaches the dark.
2. **DIG IN** — the shell scrape: a MASH event (dig, rest, dig) that fills
   a bar; his buddy in the next scrape; the sergeant checking arcs. The
   player's own scrape is the chapter's home.
3. **THE AMBUSH DRILL** — the "enemy party" crosses the killing ground:
   silhouettes on the track, the section commander's whispered "wait ...
   wait ..." and then the FIRE order. A **TIMED event**: fire on the
   order, not before (early = "you've compromised the section", a sanity
   cost and the sergeant), not late. Blanks; muzzle flash lights the
   jungle for a frame; the silhouettes drop. The drill ends, the
   all-clear, the section stands down to stag rotation. **Nothing
   supernatural has happened yet.** This is the point: the player has
   spent five minutes learning that the rifle is what you answer the dark
   with — and learning what a shape crossing the killing ground means.

**Play — THE STAG.** Two in the morning. He has the rear. Facing out. The
section behind him asleep. The jungle bed: insects, drips, nothing else.
A **clock of small demands** so the player is never idle: the radio check
("Charlie Two, radio check" — a TAP event with a four-second window, or
the section commander's voice sharpens), a micro-sleep (the screen dips;
the tap brings it back; fatigue is the chapter's texture, as it was the
source's), a sound to the left that is a branch.

Then **THE PRESSURE** — his own encounter, the file's. Something presses
on his leg: the phone vibrates, the sound is a weight settling, the
camera nudges as if pushed. The look down is a REFLEX the game performs
(the camera dips to his leg under the player's hand for a second — play's
pitch stops short of one's own feet, and a man does not decide to look at
what is on him); the torch shows nothing. The pressure stays. The torch
is the player's now: swing it — and it is gone. Silence. Presence rises.

Then **THE CONFUSION** — Chad's "initial confusion", built on what the
drill just taught. Movement across the gully, over the other platoon's
scrapes: a shape, upright, crossing. Everything the player has learned
says enemy party. The radio: "Charlie Two, movement, your two o'clock."
The section commander: "Negative. No friendlies out. No enemy party.
Stand fast." No fire order comes. The shape keeps crossing — too smooth
for walking, a hand-span off the ground, no sound of vegetation — and the
torch's edge finds a bicycle under it. **This is the played moment:**
- **FIRE** (the drilled instinct; a blank; the flash): the figure is gone
  — and back, nearer, on a different bearing, the chain now faintly
  audible. Each shot: a sanity chunk, presence up, and closer. The
  sergeant hisses "who fired?" After the third it is at the lip of the
  gully, stopped, facing him. Blanks, in reality and in the game: it does
  not come back because bullets do not work on it; it comes back because
  he engaged it. *"Acknowledging a presence can create a connection."*
- **HOLD** (the lowered rifle — a HOLD event, the "don't react" bar with
  FIRE flashing as the temptation): it crosses the whole gully at walking
  pace, close, at the edge of the torch, and as it passes its head turns
  to him — no face — and the bell rings once. This is the hold branch's
  spectacle, and it is longer and nearer than the firing branch's, because
  a thing that is not being shot at has no reason to hurry.
- **REPORT** (the radio button): "Charlie Two, contact — no. Not enemy."
  The section commander's answer is the calmest voice in the chapter.

Either way it is THERE now, stopped at the gully's lip in the torch, and
the decision opens in that moment — the world frozen under the panel the
way chapter 1's freezes at the pile. **What do you do?**

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching (NEW — for Chad's approval) |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | KEEP YOUR ARC. REPORT IT. CARRY ON. | +15 | +21 | +28 | Carry on with your duties, keep your mind clear, stay grounded. |
| B | PUT THE TORCH ON IT AND GO CLOSER | −9 | +18 | −6 | Not everything needs to be challenged or investigated. |
| C | FIRE AT IT UNTIL IT STOPS COMING | −24 | +6 | −30 | Do not engage, do not provoke, and do not feed your own fear. |
| D | GET UP AND RUN | −21 | −15 | −24 | Your own experience and somebody else's account are two different things. |

The four teachings are Master Z's sentences from S1·05's lesson, one per
choice; the deltas sit on the trial's archetype bands (report-and-carry-on
is the "ask someone experienced" band, the best; fire is provocation, the
worst; the torch is "investigate alone"; running is the one panic entry).
**The played conduct counts too** (§3.8): shots fired at it, the torch
swung at it, the position held, the report made — capped at ±10 so the
card still decides the rank and play tilts it.

**The four scenes**, and where the other platoon's story lands in each
(the source: *"What I did not know until later"*): A: the arc, the radio,
it goes into the trees, the dawn; at the tonner the other platoon's story
is told AT him — a man on a bicycle over their scrapes, and they ran —
and he keeps it separate from his own. B: the torch beam over the gully,
the empty scrapes, tyre tracks that are not there, and the walk back that
is the same walk as chapter 1's. C: the flashes, the reappearances, the
last one close enough to see there is no face — the whole section awake
and shouting at HIM, and in the morning the other platoon's account, which
now sounds like proof and is not. D: the run, the branches, straight into
the other platoon's harbour — who are running too, shouting about a
bicycle — the tonner's headlights, and the sergeant counting heads.
Teaching D is the sentence about two accounts because he learns theirs
by running into it.

### E2C5 · BOOK OUT

**Place.** Home — the flat of chapters 4 and 5, in Saturday afternoon
light, reused whole. Ma at the table. The young man in the doorway with a
field pack instead of a school bag — the door we know, and the figure who
has aged behind it. The most affordable chapter in the episode.

**Film (~40 s).** The ferry back, the bus, the lift, the door — the same
door as chapter 4's film, opened from the other side of two years. Ma's
"eat first".

**Play.** Quiet, on purpose, after chapter 4's noise — and quiet is a
choice Chad can overrule (DECISION 12):
- **THE TABLE** (hotspot): sit, and the shower story and the bicycle story
  come out to Ma — she is the "experienced perspective" the trial's ch5
  names, and the tang-ki's absence is deliberate (episode 1 already spent
  him; the site's Master Z at this age is on his own).
- **WRITE IT DOWN** (minigame — the trial's ch5 A, made playable): a
  notebook page; the night's facts as cards; sort each into OBSERVED or
  ASSUMED. "The shower was running" → observed. "Something wanted me to
  look" → assumed. "Another platoon saw a cyclist" → observed, THEIR
  observation. "It followed the section" → assumed. Scored on the split;
  this IS the lesson, and a player who cannot sort them is told why.
- **THE DRIP** (world clock): the flat's bathroom tap drips once, on the
  chapter's poltergeist clock, exactly once — the trial's own consequence
  ("an ordinary water droplet makes you freeze"). A player whose chapter-3
  choice was "it must be supernatural" (the run's save keeps the letter,
  §5) gets the drip closer to the decision. Nothing else happens in the
  flat. Nothing is there.

The decision, the trial's CONCLUDE set:

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | WRITE DOWN WHAT HAPPENED. LEAVE THE CAUSE OPEN. | +18 | +24 | +30 | Document before interpreting. Wisdom can include: I don't know yet. |
| B | IT WAS A GHOST. BOTH TIMES. | −12 | −9 | −30 | Confirmation bias can make ordinary events feel like proof. |
| C | I WAS TIRED. I IMAGINED IT. | +9 | −21 | −21 | Scepticism is not the same as dismissal. |
| D | ASK MA WHAT SHE THINKS | +15 | +21 | +30 | Good guidance expands your thinking; it does not replace your judgement. |

**The four scenes.** A: the notebook closed on the table, the pen laid on
it, the afternoon. B: the drip in the bathroom and him freezing at it —
his own conclusion now shaping what a tap means. C: "must be tired", and
Ma, from the kitchen, "the bathroom floor wet, who bathed?" — someone
else noticing what he has decided was nothing. D: Ma's answer, which is
not an answer — a possibility, and another, and "you don't have to know
today". The episode's last line to camera, S1·04's: *"Knowing how to do
something does not mean we should. Understanding when not to interfere is
just as important as knowing what to do."* Then the EPISODE COMPLETE
card, the CASE CLOSED stamp, the trail to episode 3.

## 2 · The gameplay, in one table

What is new, where it first appears, and where it recurs — so no system
is built for one moment.

| system | first | recurs | what the player does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hotspots** (many interactables, not one pile) | c1 | every chapter | walk up, E / tap, a prompt per thing |
| **Objectives + waypoints + timer** (HUD) | c1 fall in, standby bed | c2 range, c4 mission | knows what to do next and how long there is |
| **Minigame overlay** (2D, phone-first) | c1 standby bed | c2 strip/assemble, c3 plumbing, c5 notebook | tap-sequence puzzles, one screen each |
| **Time skip** (day → lights-out → 3 AM in one chapter) | c1 | c2, c3, c4 | the same set at three times of day |
| **Lying pose in play** | c1 lights out | c3 blanket | the bed is a hotspot; look where a man on his back can |
| **Events** — tap / hold / mash / timed | c3 blanket (hold) | c4 dig (mash), fire order (timed), radio (tap), the rifle held (hold) | act within a window, or hold still while the game tempts you not to |
| **Rifle mode** (aim, fire, recoil, reload, targets) | c2 range (daylight) | c4 (night) | drag / mouse to aim, FIRE button or click, R / button to reload |
| **Torch** (spot light on the camera, white / red) | c2 the walk | c4 | F / TORCH button; where it points is what you see |
| **Haptics** (phone vibration) | c2 range shots | c3 the tug, c4 the pressure and the shots | felt, not seen |
| **Chapter-driven presence** (drain without a ghost mesh) | c3 | c4, c5 | the drain follows what the chapter says is near |
| **Played conduct on the card** | c2 (light on/off) | c4 (shots, torch, hold, report) | what you did in play is a line on the outcome card |
| **NPC cast** (bunkmates, sergeant, section commander, Ma) | c1 | all | lines, looks, a few walks — the ch2-mother / ch5 tang-ki precedent |

Running exists already (Shift, since v3); no stamina bar is planned —
it would be one chapter's, and the rule is that nothing is built for one
moment. Nothing in that table replaces the four-choice decision. It is
what the player does to REACH it, and it is why the decision lands: by
chapter 4 the player has been trained, by the game, to answer a shape in
the dark with the rifle — and the lesson is that this once, they must not.

## 3 · What the engine must grow — the seams, honestly costed

Episode 1's law: *a chapter declares it, chapter 1's current value is the
default, so nothing moves.* Every seam below is optional, declared by the
chapter, and absent from chapters 1–5 — and each is proven on the FIXTURE
chapter (`chtest.js`) before any episode-2 chapter uses it, so
`fixturetest` keeps saying "the engine is chapter-agnostic" about the new
systems too.

### 3.1 Hotspots — `stage.hotspots[]`
Today the engine drives ONE interactable, `stage.pile`, with one prompt
and one `interact()` that opens the decision. A chapter may instead (or
also) return a list: `{ pos, radius, words: {approach, act}, onInteract }`.
The engine finds the nearest in range, shows its prompt, routes E / the
tap to it. `pile` stays exactly what it is — chapters 1–5 never see the
list. **Cost: small-medium.** The proximity lines (`near`/`close`) learn
to read a hotspot's own words.

### 3.2 Objectives, waypoints, timer — `api.objective(text)`, `api.waypoint(pos|null)`, `api.timer(secs, onEnd)`
Three HUD elements in `shell.html` (hidden unless used; `texttest` sees
them only when a chapter shows them), three verbs on the chapter kit
(`CHCTX`) and the cutscene `api` so a film can set the first objective.
The fixed words ("OBJECTIVE", the timer's format) are `strings.js` rows;
each objective's own text is the chapter's, in its DATA block, so
`textsync` finds it. **Cost: small.**

### 3.3 Events — `api.event({ kind, window, prompt })` → promise
One system for the quick-time family, four kinds: **tap** (act within a
window), **hold** (keep holding; a bar that empties on release or when the
finger / mouse DRIFTS beyond a few pixels — the blanket, the rifle),
**mash** (fill a bar — dig), **timed** (act inside a window that opens
later — fire on the order, the radio check). Resolves `{ ok, t }`.

**On the phone**, where the left 45 % of the screen is the move stick and
the rest is the look drag, an event never shares those zones: a TAP or
TIMED event puts a labelled button (RESPOND, FIRE) ABOVE the touch layer,
which swallows its own touches; a HOLD or MASH event suspends the stick
and the look for its duration (he is lying down or holding a position —
there is nowhere to walk) and takes the whole screen as its surface. On
the desktop: Space / E / the mouse.

**Inside a cutscene**: the verb exists in the cutscene language so a film
can hand the player a moment and continue — and while an event is
pending, Space, E and Enter belong to the EVENT, not to the skip; the
Skip button (the only tap-skip since v6.6) stays, and a skip resolves a
pending event as `{ ok: false, skipped: true }` so `cinetest` can never
hang on one. **Cost: medium** — and it is the system every later episode
will lean on most.

### 3.4 Minigame overlay — `api.minigame(builder)` → promise
A new screen state beside `inventory` and `menu` (`mini`): the engine
freezes play, the drain and the ghost exactly as the inventory does,
mounts the chapter's DOM into one panel, and unmounts on resolve. The
chapter builds the puzzle (standby bed, strip-and-assemble, plumbing,
notebook) out of plain DOM and the game's own CSS classes so it looks
like the equipment screen, not a web page. No canvas, no library, works
under the strict CSP. **Every word a minigame shows lives in the
chapter's DATA block** (a `mini` field beside `words` and `lines`), which
is the shape `textsync` discovers — so the puzzle's labels reach Chad's
sheet like every other chapter string; the builder reads them, never
holds them. Each minigame is a chapter's, so it costs the engine nothing
after the first. **Cost: medium** for the overlay, then small per puzzle.

### 3.5 Rifle mode — `viewmodel: { kind: 'rifle', asset }`
The largest addition since the cutscene language, and the only one that
touches the render loop. A chapter declares it and the viewmodel scene
swaps the arms rig for a rifle rig (arms + weapon, one asset, sized from
bones the way the arms were at v3.8). Aim is the existing look; **fire**
is a click, or on the phone a FIRE button — one at the LEFT edge above
the stick, so the thumb that is aiming keeps aiming, and its mirror at
the right edge (the convention of every mobile shooter, because the free
thumb changes with what you are doing); TORCH and RELOAD are small
buttons at the top right, clear of the look. A raycast from the camera
against `stage.targets` (a list the chapter keeps — figure-11s on the
range, silhouettes on the track, and the cyclist); recoil is a kick on
the viewmodel and a nudge on the pitch; muzzle flash is a point light
for two frames plus a sprite; brass and smoke are cheap sprites; reload
is R / a button and a viewmodel take. Ammo is a HUD counter. Targets
report `hit(point)` to the chapter, so the range scores and the cyclist
answers. **Blanks** in the outfield: the same flash and sound, and the
chapter decides what a hit means. What it must NOT do: no enemy that
shoots back, no damage model, no hit-scan against the world's geometry
beyond a blocking test — this is a training exercise, not a war. **Cost:
large.** Tested by state, not by eye (`__enc.fire()` on the harness box,
hits counted through `stage.targets`), because SwiftShader cannot judge
feel and must not be asked to.

### 3.6 Torch — `torch: { color, angle, red }`
A spotlight parented to the camera, toggled by F / a HUD button, with a
red-filter mode the outfield asks for. Chapter 2's walk and chapter 4
use it; the fixture proves it. On a chapter that declares nothing, no
light exists. **Cost: small.** One trap known in advance: a spotlight on
a fogged, tree-dense scene is a draw-call and shadow question — shadows
OFF for the torch by default, on only if the box can afford them.

### 3.7 Chapter-driven presence — `ctx.setPresence(k)`
A chapter with `ghost: null` (chapters 3, 4 and 5 of episode 2 all are;
none has HER) can still say how near the unseen thing is, 0..1, and the
DRAIN reads it. This is not the small seam it looks: today `ghost: null`
turns the drain off outright (`ghostDrainRate` returns 0 on `GH.off`),
`updateGhost` zeroes her presence every frame when she is off, and the
same presence number drives her loops (`ghostloop`, `whisper`) and her
banner — so a chapter's presence must reach the drain ALONE, and her
sounds and banner must stay hers and stay silent in a ghost-less chapter.
The blanket, the pressure on the leg, the cyclist's approaches and
chapter 5's drip all speak through it. **Cost: medium** — the
seventeenth leak, closed before it opens: the drain is the game's, and
the ghost was only ever one way to drive it.

### 3.8 Played conduct — `stage.conduct()` → `{ s, a, w, note }`
Read when the choice is made and added to the choice's deltas; the
outcome card shows a second line under the three bars ("In the field:
you fired on it three times. −6 sanity, −8 wisdom."). Capped at ±10 per
stat per chapter, so the card's choice always decides the rank and play
tilts it. (This game's rank is the weighted blend of all three stats —
`finish()`, 0.3 / 0.3 / 0.4 — the deliberate divergence from the trial's
wisdom-only pass recorded in SOURCE-NOTES; the cap is what keeps a
played chapter on the same formula as a picked one.) **Cost:
small-medium** (card markup + strings).

### 3.9 Pose and time of day in play — `api.pose('lying'|'standing')`, `api.daylight(preset, secs)`
Chapter 2's lying camera is a CUTSCENE camera today (`PILLOW`, driven by
`camTo`); there is no way to lie down in play. A play-time pose lowers
the camera to the pillow, limits the look to what a man on his back can
see, and stands him up on a tap or a key — the bed hotspot in chapters 1
and 3 uses it, and the hold event runs on top of it. `applyDaylight()`
exists and runs once per chapter; it becomes callable with a tween so a
chapter can go morning → lights-out → 3 AM without a rebuild. Chapters
1–5 call neither. **Cost: small-medium.**

### 3.10 Haptics
`navigator.vibrate` behind one function, silent where unsupported.
**Cost: tiny.**

### 3.11 The episode plumbing — EPISODES-PLAN.md's recommendations, put to Chad here
Decided at v6.0: keys `e2c1`..`e2c5`, `episode: 2` in each DATA block,
`ZAV_FIGURE` keyed by episode. RECOMMENDED there and decided nowhere yet
(DECISION 11): **a folder per episode** (`src/chapters/e2/`, a one-line
glob in build.py and textsync); the young man's voice as its own speaker
(`who: 'jamesTeen'`) with its own bus, since the v5.26/28 bus is tuned to
Aaron's crest; **a per-episode pack tier** (the bunk's sounds are three
chapters' — the rule that puts them in the SHARED pack every player
downloads at boot is exactly the growth v4.2 was built to stop); the
**single-file build episode-1-only** (the fallback stays real, csptest
stays honest, and it stops growing); `leaktest` building all ten chapters
plus the fixture until that hurts. The sheet gains an EPISODE 2 tab by
itself (`textsync` already discovers chapters).

### 3.12 Harnesses — "adding an episode must not add a harness"
That law is about CHAPTERS and EPISODES, and it holds: `chaptertest`
learns the new declarations (hotspots inside bounds, every target /
hotspot / event kind exists, every minigame's words have rows) and runs
the five new chapters the way it runs the first five; `leaktest` builds
them; `fixturetest` proves the new seams on the fixture; `statetest`
proves the new save fields (§5). Engine SYSTEMS have had harnesses of
their own before (the menu and selector got `menutest` at v5.12), and
rifle mode plus the four event kinds are engine systems, not chapter
content. Recommendation: extend `fixturetest` first; add ONE harness
(`playtest`, for the play-time systems) only if the fixture cannot carry
them without becoming unreadable — DECISION 10. Never one per chapter.

## 5 · Scoring, saves, the sheet

- **Stats and rank are unchanged**: sanity / awareness / wisdom, the same
  `RANK_OF` and the same weighted score, the episode card's mean of five.
  Deltas follow episode 1's practice (§1, "On the numbers"), so one
  formula serves ten chapters.
- **Conduct** is capped (§3.8) so it can tilt a rank one step at most.
- **Fainting** stays a fail state — in the field it reads as "you froze",
  which is what the sergeant counting heads makes of it.
- **Saves**: `worldState()` is `{ v, ch, stats, inv }` today. It gains
  `phase` (day / night / which objective, so resume lands in the right
  time of day), `conduct` (the chapter's running counts, so a resume
  mid-stag does not forget the shots), and `choices` (the letter picked
  in each sealed chapter — chapter 5's drip reads chapter 3's). Old saves
  load with all three absent, and `statetest` gets a case for each.
- **The sheet**: an EPISODE 2 tab appears by itself; the new UI words
  (OBJECTIVE, the timer, FIRE / TORCH / RELOAD / RESPOND, the conduct
  line) are `strings.js` rows; every objective, hotspot and minigame word
  is in its chapter's DATA block; every new voice line is a VOICE LINES
  row. Chad's edits keep flowing through `textsync import`.

## 7 · Risks, named

- **A shooter whose lesson is "don't shoot."** The design's answer is
  that not firing is an ACTIVE, tense thing to do (the hold bar, FIRE
  flashing, the radio to reach for), never a passive one; that the hold
  branch gets the LONGER, NEARER sighting, so the player who does the
  right thing is not shown less; that the rifle is taught in daylight so
  the player trusts it; and that the game never punishes shooting with a
  game-over, only with what the source says happens — it comes closer. If
  play-testing shows the hold reads as "the game took my gun away", the
  fix is in the event's feel, not the lesson.
- **The harness box cannot judge feel.** Rifle mode and the events are
  proven by state (`__enc`), and the feel is judged by Chad on his phone —
  earlier and more often than episode 1's chapters were, because a
  reaction window that is right on a laptop is wrong on a thumb.
- **Phone first, always.** The event buttons sit above the stick and look
  zones and swallow their touches; a hold suspends both; FIRE is on the
  side the free thumb is; the cyclist's crossing comes TOWARD the player
  as much as across, so the portrait centre crop (docs/AUDIT-2026-09.md
  Part One) never loses it; every event window is tested on a 390-wide
  viewport.
- **Authenticity.** Chad served; the plan's army (Hawk Company, the bunk,
  the SAR 21, the range, shell scrapes, stag, "Charlie Two") is from the
  case files and common knowledge, and every command, word and layout
  should be checked against his memory before it is voiced. A wrong
  detail in an NS story is the kind every Singaporean player notices.
- **Asset weight.** A jungle at night with a torch is the heaviest scene
  the game will have drawn. The v6.15 kit's `lowKeep` and the fog do the
  phone's share; shadows are off for the torch by default.
- **The cyclist's look.** A translucent soldier on a bicycle is either
  the best image in the game or a ragdoll on a prop. It is built early
  (v7.5's first checkpoint), photographed alone (the v6.17 law), and
  shown to Chad before a scene is written around it.
- **Scope.** Seven releases is the honest count. Anything cut should be
  cut from the LIST of minigames and events (each is a chapter's, cheap
  to drop), never from the kit — the kit is what makes episodes 3–10
  possible without this conversation again.

## 8 · DECISIONS — Chad's

1. **The player SEES the cyclist** (the brief), where the case file says
   Master Z only felt the pressure and heard about the bicycle later.
   The plan keeps both truths (his encounter is the leg; the bicycle is
   what the other platoon runs from) — confirm this is the departure he
   wants, since it is the only place the episode goes past the file.
2. **The shape: three bunk chapters + one outfield + one home** (the plan
   above), or **two bunk + two outfield + one home** (chapter 3 becomes a
   first outfield night with only the pressure on the leg, chapter 4 the
   cyclist night)? Recommendation: 3+1+1 — the source's shower story has
   three real beats (the sound, the check, the tug and the exhaustion) and
   the cyclist is one night; stretching it over two risks padding the
   best chapter.
3. **Chapter 5 at home with Ma**, or on Tekong (the medic, the encik, a
   book-in Sunday night)? Recommendation: home — it reuses a built set,
   ages the boy in front of the door we know, and Ma is the trial's
   "trusted older person" already in the cast.
4. **Blanks or live rounds in the outfield?** Recommendation: blanks (it
   is an exercise; the flash and the sound are identical; and it makes the
   point that it reappears because he engaged, not because bullets fail).
5. **How much of the cyclist is shown** — a silhouette with no face, seen
   only at torch range (recommended), or a lit, detailed figure?
6. **Played conduct on the outcome card** — show it as a line (recommended)
   or fold it silently into the deltas?
7. **The young man's voice**: picked from candidates the v5.14 way when
   E2C1's lines are written — Chad's ear.
8. **The sergeant's speech**: Singlish words in a neutral voice
   (recommended, since the workspace has no local voice) or standard
   English?
9. **The rifle rig**: a bought first-person arms-and-rifle pack (best) or
   the existing arms holding a rifle model (cheaper, stiffer). Chad
   sources either. And **the ferry**: modelled, or implied (coastline,
   wake, engine sound)? Recommendation: implied.
10. **Harness**: one `playtest` for the engine's play-time systems if the
    fixture cannot carry them — yes or no in principle (§3.12).
11. **The episode plumbing** (§3.11): the folder per episode, the young
    man's own speaker and bus, the per-episode pack tier, the single-file
    build going episode-1-only. All four recommended; none built.
12. **Chapter 5's weight**: quiet by design (the table, the notebook, the
    drip), or grow the notebook into a full case-file assembly — the
    facts, the witnesses, the timeline, the open questions — so the last
    chapter carries a real puzzle?
13. **Chapter titles** (working): THE WORST BED · THE CHECK · THE BLANKET ·
    THE SHELL SCRAPES · BOOK OUT. His to change, on the sheet.
14. **The new decision set** (E2C4's four choices and teachings) — written
    on Master Z's own sentences; his to approve or re-word before it is
    voiced.

Nothing is built until these are answered. When they are, v7.0 starts with
the engine kit on the fixture, and the base game — v6.17, the reference —
is proven untouched before episode 2's first world exists.
