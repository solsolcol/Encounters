## 0 · The story material, now including the films

the two Chad named — and each exists three times: as the site's text, as
the site's VIDEO, and (for the shower) as the trial game's five decision

  the front of the bunk beside the toilet; the shower turning itself on
  at three for several nights; he checks once — running, nobody; the
  blanket tugged; exhaustion; it stops. **Video** (39 s): a bunk of
  double-deck beds, a red 03:00 wall clock, the wet corridor, and **a
  pale soldier in uniform standing in the shower corridor and, later,

  *"I stopped checking. I stopped reacting. I simply slept through it."*
- **S1·05 The Ghostly Cyclist.** Text: infantry outfield; rear of the
  section; real weight on his leg; torch — nothing; the pressure stays;
  he swings the beam and it is gone; another platoon saw a figure on a
  bicycle floating over their shell scrapes and evacuated. **Video**
  (48 s): **a grey-skinned soldier in an old uniform crouched at the
  tree with a hand on his leg**, then standing behind him — gone when
  the beam swings; **a luminous, translucent soldier on a bicycle
  riding slowly through the other platoon**, who scramble and point;
  their own words on the soundtrack: *"Eh, what is that? The bicycle,
  it's floating. Look over there!"*; and the lesson card: **"WHAT YOU
  CAN'T SEE DOESN'T MEAN IT DOESN'T EXIST. Stay calm, observe. Don't let
  fear control your response."**
- **The trial's Episode 2** (unchanged on the new site): THE SOUND, THE
  HALLWAY, THE BATHROOM, VERIFY THE HOUSEHOLD, WHAT CAN YOU ACTUALLY
  CONCLUDE — twenty choices with deltas and teachings in Master Z's

So the entity is not the game's invention: **Master Z's own films show a
pale soldier at the bedside and a glowing cyclist in the trees.** The
game shows what the films show. The one liberty is the brief's — the
player, not only the other platoon, sees the cyclist — and the films
make that a small step, not a leap.

**The shape: three bunk chapters, two outfield nights.** The trial's
five decision sets and the two files fit five chapters like this:

## 1 · The five chapters

Every chapter keeps the spine the engine and the harnesses know — a
world, an opening film, ONE decision of four choices, four cutscenes, a
teaching card, a rank — and everything BEFORE the decision is played.

**On the numbers.** Deltas are the trial's scaled about ×3, the way
episode 1's chapters 2 and 3 were (chapter 2's +5/+4/+9 shipped as
+15/+12/+25), rounded, capped at ±30. **Critical mistake** marks the
trial's rule (a wisdom delta of −5 or worse, so −15 here) — see §5.

**The reaction challenges** (the trial's five, done in the 3D world —
§3.3) follow the trial's own rule: **they move Sanity and Awareness, never
Wisdom.** Wisdom is the card's alone.

### E2C1 · THE WORST BED

**Place.** Hawk Company's platoon bunk on Tekong as the film shows it:
double-deck beds in two rows, a locker per man, the fans, the notice
board, a red digital clock on the wall, the balcony corridor over the
parade square, and at the FRONT the toilet-and-shower block with its
swing door. His bed is the bottom bunk nearest that door. One location
serves chapters 1–3 at three times of day.

**Film (~60 s).** The ferry's wake and the island coming up; the gates;
the bunk assignment; "bed one — next to the toilet"; his locker; lights
out; the fan; the clock. First words in the young man's voice: *"My bed
was at the very front of the platoon bunk, next to the toilet. The
worst spot in the room, and the one nobody wanted."* The equipment panel
shows the OLDER figure from here on.

**Play — day.**
- **FALL IN** (objective + timer): the whistle from the parade square;
  run the corridor and the stairs to the line before the count ends.
  Late means the sergeant's line and the platoon's push-ups.
- **STANDBY BED** (minigame, 90 s): blanket to the board's width,
  hospital corners, boots on the line, locker to the layout.
- **THE BUNK** (hotspots): the shower block in daylight (just a shower),
  the bunkmates (the buddy who recurs), the notice board ("Wk 3 — live
  firing").
- **LIGHTS OUT** → time skip: blue dark, fifteen men breathing, the fan.
  The bed is a hotspot; interacting LIES HIM DOWN in play (§3.9).

**Play — night.** 03:00 on the wall. The shower. **HEARTBEAT · FEAR
CONTROL** — the trial's own challenge for this exact chapter, in our
HUD: the sanity ECG (v2.9) is the pulse; tap on the beat, five beats,
and sanity holds; miss, and the drain takes a chunk per miss. Then the
decision, the trial's THE SOUND in a bunk:

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching (verbatim) |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | GET UP AND OPEN THE TOILET DOOR | 0 | +15 | +6 | Investigating can provide information, but moving straight toward uncertainty is not automatically the wisest first step. |
| B | STAY STILL AND LISTEN | +9 | +18 | +18 | Hearing something frightening is an observation. Deciding what caused it is an interpretation. |
| C | WHISPER TO YOUR BUNKMATE — "YOU AWAKE?" | +6 | +15 | +15 | Check simple human explanations before building a more complicated interpretation. |
| D | IT'S A GHOST — BLANKET OVER YOUR HEAD · *critical* | −15 | −9 | −18 | Fear is a signal, not proof. |

**Scenes.** A: the walk to the door, the water louder with every step,
the running shower, nobody — and, as the film has it, **for one frame at
the end of the corridor, someone standing** — then the tiles. B: the
lying camera, the fan, the clock; the water stops by itself. C: the
buddy's "huh?", nothing else. D: the blanket over his face, the sound
going on and on.

### E2C2 · THE CHECK

**Place.** Day: the **live-firing range** (100 m lanes, the butts,
figure-11 targets, the safety bays, the tower). Night: the bunk.

**Film (~45 s).** The armskote; the SAR 21 into his hands; the range at
nine in the morning.

**Play — day. RIFLE MODE, learned in safety.**
- **STRIP AND ASSEMBLE** (minigame, timed): the rifle's parts in order.
- **THE RANGE** (rifle mode, objectives, timer): the detail, the safety
  drill, the shoot — figure-11s rising at 100 m and 50 m, scored aloud
  by the sergeant. The score is a line and a tease, never a stat.

**Play — night.** The shower, and this time he gets up. **The light
switch** on the way is the trial's HALLWAY set played: flip it and the
block is lit (awareness up), leave it and go in by torch (sanity cost).
In the wet block, **SPIRITUAL FOCUS POINT** — the trial's challenge for
this chapter — as a torch-hunt: five details glimmer in the dark for a
moment each (the drops on the tiles, the drain, the tap's handle, the
cubicle latch, the wet footprint that is not his); put the torch on each
before it fades. Awareness. Then the water stops by itself as he reaches
for it, and the decision, the trial's THE BATHROOM:

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | LOOK WITHOUT TOUCHING ANYTHING | +3 | +24 | +21 | Observe the original state before you interfere. |
| B | TEST THE TAP | +6 | +15 | +6 | Look for ordinary explanations — but observe before altering evidence. |
| C | OPEN THE CUBICLE AND SEARCH IT | +3 | +21 | +15 | Eliminating one possibility does not prove the next possibility. |
| D | "WHO'S IN THERE?" · *critical* | −9 | +3 | −15 | Timing can feel like proof without actually proving causation. |

**Scenes.** A: the inspection, and "I went straight back to my bed". B:
the tap turned, water, normal, the handle moved — evidence gone. C: the
cubicle, empty and wet. D: his voice in the tiles, silence, a bottle off
the shelf a second later — and in the frosted glass behind it, for a
breath, **a shape standing**, the way the trial's own consequence video
puts one there.

### E2C3 · THE BLANKET

**Place.** The bunk only.

**Film (~30 s).** Back to bed. The fan. Not asleep.

**Play — night.** Lying (the play pose). The blanket takes weight at the
foot of the bed and pulls. **HOLD TO STABILISE** — the trial's hold
challenge as a body: a bar that empties if the finger or the mouse
drifts, "SIT UP" and "LOOK" flashing as temptations, the blanket going
inch by inch, the breath; and if he holds, **the film's image**: over the
edge of the bunk above, a grey face looking down, then not. Let go, and
the sanity chunk lands, presence rises, and the face is closer. Master Z:
*"I simply stayed there and didn't react."*

**Play — day.** Verify: **ASK THE BUNK** (four men, four lines, none an
answer — hearsay, which is why the decision's A, a witness at 3 AM, is
worth more); **THE PLUMBING** (minigame: trace the cold feed to the
timer valve; nothing visibly wrong — the trial's own outcome, and the
teaching it leads to is about NOT finding the cause); **THE PROGRAMME**
(route march, SOC, field camp; the buddy: "sleep when you can").

**The days blur** (montage film: march, range, SOC wall, boots, bed,
faster each time). The decision on the last of those nights, the trial's
VERIFY:

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | WAKE YOUR BUDDY AND ASK | +15 | +24 | +27 | Seek independent information before drawing conclusions. |
| B | CHECK THE PLUMBING AGAIN | +12 | +27 | +24 | Failure to find an ordinary explanation is not proof of a supernatural explanation. |
| C | IT MUST BE SUPERNATURAL · *critical* | −21 | −6 | −24 | Unexplained does not mean supernatural. |
| D | SLEEP. IT'S 3 AM. | +21 | +9 | +30 | You do not have to solve everything immediately. |

D is the source's own ending and the chapter's best. Its scene: the
sound, the fan, sleep taking him mid-thought; morning; *"Once I stopped
giving the experience my attention, it stopped giving me its attention
too."* The chapter closes on the posting: infantry. Outfield — three
days, two nights — next week.

### E2C4 · THE PRESSURE

The first outfield night: a night mission with objectives and no ghost
in it, then the stag, then his own encounter from the file.

**Place.** Secondary jungle at night (the v6.15 tree kit, dense and
fogged, plus undergrowth), a track in from the drop-off, the section's
harbour with its shell scrapes. The world at a tenth of chapter 1's
light; the torch is what you see by.

**Film (~50 s).** The three-tonner, the drop-off at last light, single
file, the whispered brief, the sky going. *"That night I was at the very
rear of my section, facing away from the others, looking out into the
dark."*

**Play — THE EXERCISE (rifle mode, objectives).**
1. **MOVE TO THE HARBOUR** — the section commander's bearing along the
   track; torch discipline (red filter; white light draws the sergeant).
2. **DIG IN** — the shell scrape as a MASH event; the buddy in the next
   scrape; arcs checked.
3. **FIRST STAG** — the rear, facing out. The clock of small demands:
   the radio check (TAP within four seconds), the micro-sleep (the
   screen dips; tap), a branch on the left.

**THE PRESSURE.** Weight on his leg — the phone vibrates, the camera
nudges, the sound of something settling. The look down is a REFLEX the
game performs (play's pitch stops short of one's own feet); the torch
shows the boot, the leaf litter, nothing. The pressure stays. Now the
torch is the player's: **HOLD TO STABILISE** again, but the other way —
keep the beam ON the leg while the bar asks you to, and the film's image
arrives at the edge of it: **a grey hand on the trouser, an arm, a
crouched figure against the tree**, seen only where the beam is not
quite pointing. Swing the beam onto it — gone. Silence. *"For a moment,
I thought I saw something. Then whatever was there disappeared."*

The decision opens there, still in the scrape — the trial's HALLWAY set,
whose four teachings are about light, darkness, other people and
provocation, and fit a jungle at 2 AM without a word moved:

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching (verbatim) |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | PUT THE TORCH ON IT BEFORE YOU MOVE | +9 | +18 | +21 | Courage does not require deliberately removing your own safety advantages. |
| B | REACH DOWN INTO THE DARK | −12 | +9 | −9 | Making observation harder is not bravery. |
| C | ASK YOUR BUDDY IF HE FELT IT | +6 | +21 | +24 | Independent context can narrow possibilities without forcing a conclusion. |
| D | "WHO'S THERE?" | −6 | +6 | −12 | Provocation and investigation are not the same thing. |

**Scenes.** A: the beam held, the figure fully in it for a second — the
old uniform, the grey face, no expression — then only the tree; the rest
of the stag with the torch in his fist. B: the hand going down into the
dark, cold leaf litter, and something that moves away from the fingers.
C: the buddy's "felt what?", and the two of them looking out together
until the relief. D: his voice across the harbour, the section commander
hissing him quiet, and the pressure lifting exactly as if answered.

### E2C5 · THE CYCLIST

The second night, and the chapter Chad's brief is about: the platoon
moves to the ambush position on the far side of the gully from the
other platoon's harbour; the drill; then the thing on the bicycle.

**Film (~40 s).** Day two in ten seconds (the move, the heat, the
recce), then the ambush position at dusk: the killing ground, the other
platoon's scrapes across the gully, the section commander: "Nobody fires
until I say."

**Play — THE EXERCISE.**
1. **MOVE TO THE AMBUSH LINE** — objective, bearing, red light.
2. **THE AMBUSH DRILL** — the "enemy party" crosses the killing ground:
   silhouettes on the track, "wait ... wait ..." and the FIRE order — a
   TIMED event: fire on the order, not before (early = "you've
   compromised the section") and not late; blanks; the flash lights the
   jungle for a frame; the silhouettes drop; all clear. **The player has
   just been trained, by the game, that a shape crossing the killing
   ground is answered with the rifle.**

**THE STAG.** Two in the morning. The rear again. The radio check. Then:

**THE CONFUSION** (Chad's "initial confusion"). Movement across the
gully, over the other platoon's scrapes: a shape, upright, crossing.
Everything the drill taught says enemy party. The radio: "Charlie Two,
movement, your two o'clock." The section commander: "Negative. No
friendlies out. No enemy party. Stand fast." No fire order comes. The
shape keeps crossing — too smooth for walking, a hand-span off the
ground, no vegetation moving — and the torch's edge finds **a bicycle
under it, and a rider that gives off its own light.** The film's cyclist.

**THE PLAYED MOMENT.** The rifle is in his hands.
- **FIRE** — Chad's loop, exactly: a blank, the flash, and the figure is
  GONE — and back, nearer, on a different bearing, the chain now faintly
  audible. Each shot: a sanity chunk, presence up, closer. "Who fired?"
  from the sergeant. After the third it is at the lip of the gully,
  stopped, facing him, and the bell rings once. Blanks, in reality and in
  the game: it does not come back because bullets fail; it comes back
  because he engaged it — *"acknowledging a presence can create a
  connection."*
- **HOLD** — the lowered rifle as a HOLD event, FIRE flashing as the
  temptation; it crosses the whole gully at walking pace, close, at the
  edge of the torch, and as it passes its head turns to him — no face —
  and the bell rings once. The hold branch's sighting is LONGER and
  NEARER than the firing branch's: a thing not being shot at has no
  reason to hurry.
- **SPIRITUAL FOCUS POINT** rides under both: keeping the torch on a
  thing that moves is the awareness challenge, so a player who tracks it
  calmly earns awareness whether or not they fire.
- **REPORT** — the radio button: "Charlie Two, contact — no. Not enemy."
  The calmest voice in the chapter answers.

Then it is THERE, at the gully's lip in the torch, and **the decision
opens in the live moment with the thing still coming** — DECISION 4
proposes this as the game's one timed decision, the way the trial's
30-second clock works, but diegetic: a bar that is its approach, and a
sanity chunk (never wisdom) if it reaches the scrape before you choose.

| | choice | S | A | W | teaching (NEW, on S1·05's sentences — for Chad's approval) |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| A | KEEP YOUR ARC. REPORT IT. CARRY ON. | +15 | +21 | +28 | Carry on with your duties, keep your mind clear, stay grounded. |
| B | PUT THE TORCH ON IT AND GO CLOSER | −9 | +18 | −6 | Not everything needs to be challenged or investigated. |
| C | FIRE AT IT UNTIL IT STOPS COMING · *critical* | −24 | +6 | −30 | Do not engage, do not provoke, and do not feed your own fear. |
| D | GET UP AND RUN · *critical* | −21 | −15 | −24 | Your own experience and somebody else's account are two different things. |

**Scenes.** A: the arc held, the radio, it goes into the trees; then the
film's own beat — the other platoon crashing out through the harbour,
*"Eh, what is that? The bicycle, it's floating! Look over there!"* — and
the exercise called; at the tonner at dawn their story told AT him, and
him keeping it separate from his own. B: the walk across the gully, the
empty scrapes, tyre tracks that are not there, and the thing behind him
now. C: the flashes, the reappearances, the last close enough to see
there is no face, the whole section awake and shouting at HIM, and in
the morning the other platoon's account, which now sounds like proof and
is not. D: the run, the branches, straight into the other platoon — who
are running too — the tonner's headlights, the sergeant counting heads.

**The ending** (~40 s, over the tonner ride out at dawn): the young man's
own conclusion, which is the trial's fifth set said as one thought —
*"Something pressed on my leg. Another platoon saw a man on a bicycle.
Those are two different things, and I only know one of them. I don't
know what it was. I don't have to."* — and the film's card to camera:
**"What you can't see doesn't mean it doesn't exist. Stay calm. Observe.
Don't let fear control your response."** Then the EPISODE COMPLETE card,
the CASE CLOSED stamp, the trail to episode 3.

## 3 · What the engine must grow — the seams, costed

Episode 1's law: *a chapter declares it, chapter 1's current value is the
default, so nothing moves.* Every seam is optional, declared by the
chapter, absent from chapters 1–5, and proven on the FIXTURE chapter
first so `fixturetest` keeps saying "the engine is chapter-agnostic".

### 3.1 Hotspots — `stage.hotspots[]`
Today the engine drives ONE interactable, `stage.pile`. A chapter may
also return `{ pos, radius, words: {approach, act}, onInteract }` items;
the engine picks the nearest in range, shows its prompt, routes E / the
tap. `pile` is untouched. **Small-medium.**

### 3.2 Objectives, waypoints, timer — `api.objective`, `api.waypoint`, `api.timer`
Three HUD elements, hidden unless used; the fixed words in `strings.js`;
each objective's text in the chapter's DATA block so `textsync` finds
it. **Small.**

### 3.3 Events and challenges — `api.event({ kind, window, prompt, stat })` → promise
One system: **tap**, **hold** (a bar that empties when the finger or
mouse drifts), **mash**, **timed**, and the trial's three challenges as
world-native kinds — **heartbeat** (tap on the HUD ECG's beat, five
beats), **focus** (put the torch or the reticle on N glimmers before
each fades), **stabilise** (hold a beam or a barrel on a mark for five
seconds). Every kind resolves `{ ok, score }` and the chapter maps the
score to SANITY or AWARENESS (never wisdom — the trial's rule, adopted).
Difficulty is a chapter's number, so a later episode can tighten it the
way the trial's tiers do.

**Phone**: a TAP/TIMED event puts a labelled button ABOVE the touch layer
that swallows its own touches; a HOLD/MASH/STABILISE event suspends the
stick and the look for its duration and takes the whole screen. Desktop:
Space / E / the mouse.

**Inside a cutscene**: the verb exists in the cutscene language; while an
event is pending, Space, E and Enter belong to the EVENT, not the skip;
the Skip button stays; a skip resolves a pending event as failed-and-
skipped so `cinetest` never hangs. **Medium** — the system every later
episode leans on.

### 3.4 Minigame overlay — `api.minigame(builder)` → promise
A new screen state (`mini`) beside `inventory` and `menu`: play, drain
and ghost frozen; the chapter's plain-DOM puzzle mounted in one panel in
the game's own CSS; its words in the chapter's DATA block (a `mini`
field) so they reach the sheet. **Medium**, then small per puzzle.

### 3.5 Rifle mode — `viewmodel: { kind: 'rifle', asset }`
The largest addition since the cutscene language. The viewmodel scene
swaps the arms rig for a rifle rig (arms + weapon, sized from bones as
at v3.8); aim is the look; FIRE is a click, or on the phone a button at
the LEFT edge above the stick (the aiming thumb keeps aiming) with a
mirror at the right; TORCH and RELOAD small at the top right. A raycast
against `stage.targets` (figure-11s, silhouettes, the cyclist); recoil
on the viewmodel and the pitch; a two-frame muzzle light and sprite;
brass and smoke sprites; reload as a take; ammo on the HUD; targets
report `hit(point)` to the chapter. No enemy that shoots back, no damage
model. **Large.** Tested by state (`__enc.fire()`, hits through
`stage.targets`), never by SwiftShader's eye.

### 3.6 Torch — `torch: { color, angle, red }`
A spotlight on the camera, F / a button, red-filter mode; no shadows by
default. **Small.**

### 3.7 Chapter-driven presence — `ctx.setPresence(k)`
A chapter with `ghost: null` (all five here — HER is episode 1's) says
how near the unseen thing is and the DRAIN reads it. Not small: today
`ghost: null` returns 0 from `ghostDrainRate`, `updateGhost` zeroes her
presence every frame, and the same number drives her loops and banner —
so a chapter's presence reaches the drain ALONE and her sounds stay
hers. **Medium** — the seventeenth leak.

### 3.8 Played conduct — `stage.conduct()` → `{ s, a, note }`
Read when the choice is made, added to the deltas, shown as a second
line on the outcome card ("In the field: you fired on it three times.
−6 sanity."). SANITY and AWARENESS only, capped at ±10, so the card's
choice keeps the rank and play tilts it. **Small-medium.**

### 3.9 Pose and time of day in play — `api.pose('lying'|'standing')`, `api.daylight(preset, secs)`
