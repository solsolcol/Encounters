Chad, on episode 2 chapter 4 the morning after v14.2:

> Do the same level of debug for ep 2, chapter 4.
>
> For chapter 4, the voicelines keep referring to lane 5, when it should say

Two reports and one mandate. This document is the record of the two reports;

## 1 · THE LANE

### What he heard

`t4who` — the range officer's challenge — said:

> CEASE FIRE! Who fired? **Lane five.** What are you firing at?

and it was cued twice: once in the confusion beat in play, and once in
**scene C**, where the player has just fired three rounds himself.

### Why it was written that way, and why that stopped being good enough

The confusion beat was internally consistent. One line above the cue,
`beginConfuse` fired a scripted round —

```js
queueFn(() => { if (worldSfx) worldSfx('rifleshot', 0.75); });          // and lane five fires anyway
```

— so lane five HAD fired, and the tower was right to say so. The design
intent was a line that panics and shoots despite `k4cyc`'s "DON'T SHOOT!
DON'T SHOOT!".

From the player's seat that reading is not available. He hears a rifle he did
not fire and then the range officer challenging a lane he is not standing on,
which reads as the game addressing the wrong man. And in **scene C** it is
not a reading problem at all but a plain defect: the player fires three real
rounds through `kit.weaponShot()` and the tower blames lane five.

### The fix, and why it is better rather than only asked-for

The challenge now belongs to the **player's own first round** (`onFiredAtIt`),
and the scripted lane-five shot is gone:

```js
      sayLine('n4back');
      /* v14.3: AND THE TOWER CHALLENGES HIM. ... */
      if (shots <= 1) { queueGap(0.6); tower('t4who'); }
```

Three things follow from that one move.

1. **The line is true when it is said.** It is his round, so it is his lane.
2. **`onFiredAtIt` gains the answer it never had.** Putting a live round into
   a target area the range party has just called empty drew complete silence
   from the range, through three releases. It is the one thing a range
   officer cannot let pass, and now he does not.
3. **`k4cyc` lands better.** "DON'T SHOOT! DON'T SHOOT!" is now said to the
   only man on the range who can — instead of being immediately contradicted
   by a round nobody can be held to answer for.

Once only: `if (shots <= 1)`. Three challenges for three rounds is a machine,
not a man. It is QUEUED, so it falls in behind his own "It's coming back" and
behind whatever radio traffic is still running, and `openDecision` drops it
if the bell comes first (v12.3's rule).

What is left in the confusion beat is the three shouts and the tower's
negative — the gap the whole chapter is built on — untouched.

## 2 · THE TRIGGER KNOWS WHERE HE IS STANDING

### The engine's share — the nineteenth seam

`kit.weaponBlock(msg)`. A string forbids firing and **is what the HUD says**;
`null` allows it again. It defaults to null, so the fixture's nine weapon
promises and episode 1 — which declares no weapon at all — are untouched by
construction.

```js
function weaponFire() {
  if (!weaponDecl || state !== 'play' || !weaponWant()) return false;
  /* the chapter's own rule, before anything is spent: no round, no take,
     no flash, no raycast — and the reason on the screen */
  if (weaponBlock) { weaponBlockSay(); return false; }
```

It sits **before** the fire-gap clock as well as before the spend, so a
refused press costs nothing and is not rate-limited into silence by the
weapon's own cadence.

**The message is the chapter's, never the engine's.** What is forbidden and
why is a chapter's business, and a chapter's word goes through its own
`words`, which is where `textsync` puts it in front of Chad. The engine
hard-codes no lane.

The refusal is not only a dead press. `#nofire` — a red stencil under the
reticle, which is where the eye already is when the trigger is pulled —
carries the chapter's sentence, with `hudfail` (already in the engine's
unconditional warm set since v8.8) and a hard triple buzz. It is rate-limited
to one in 900 ms so a held trigger cannot flicker it, and its entry animation
is **restarted** on every refusal past that window, so a second, deliberate
press is visibly answered rather than silently ignored. And `body.wpnBlocked`
dims the FIRE button, so the rule is on the screen **before** it is tested —
a player who looks at the HUD never has to discover it by pressing.

A dead trigger with nothing to read is exactly the failure v12.2 spent a
release fixing ("shooting does not seem to work"). There it was a real bug;
here it is a rule; the difference has to be visible.

`weaponSetup` and `weaponTeardown` both clear it, so a chapter's fire rule is
never inherited by the next one.

**The fixture declares it and `fixturetest` proves it** (v12.0's rule — a
seam proved by the fixture first is a seam the chapter cannot hide a bug in).
Two new promises, 58 in all: a blocked press returns false with the round
count **exactly where it was** and the words on the screen, and clearing it
puts `body.wpnBlocked` and the element back.

### The chapter's share

```js
const FIRE_R = 3.0;
...
const wantBlock = (!kit || !kit.weaponBlock) ? null
  : (phase === 'line' || offLine > FIRE_R) ? DATA.words.noFire : null;
if (wantBlock !== blockNow) { blockNow = wantBlock; kit.weaponBlock(wantBlock); }
```

`noFire` is **"Shooting only allowed at lane six"** — Chad's own sentence, in
the chapter's words, on his sheet.

**DERIVED on the frame, never set by a phase** (the v11.6 law). A resume, a
replay, a reset or a cutscene that puts him somewhere else all land on the
right answer on the next frame, because there is no stored flag to leave
behind. `blockNow` exists only so the verb is called on a CHANGE — it paints
a body class and would otherwise run sixty times a second for nothing — and
`reset()` clears it with the rest of the run state (the v8.1 law, an eighth
time).

**Why 3.0 m and not `ZONE_R`.** The arrival circle is 1.35 m and the glow
drawn round it is 2.1. Arriving is a **moment** and has to be precise;
standing on your lane is a **state** the player holds through the whole of
the serials, and a rule that punishes a shuffle is a rule that reads as a
bug. 3.0 m is still well inside the 2.6 m lane pitch's neighbour, so a man
who has wandered onto someone else's lane is refused — which is the range's
own rule, and the one the chapter's whole drill is about.

**It does not replace the early-fire penalty, it complements it.** The block
is about PLACE; `onShot`'s `if (!hot)` branch (−6 sanity and `e4wait`) is
about TIME. A player standing on his lane who fires before the order is still
reprimanded and still pays.

## 4 · THE DEBUG PASS

Seven dimensions read the chapter in parallel — cutscenes, phases, world,
audio, lifecycle, engine, text — and every candidate was then read back
against the current code before anything was changed. The rule from v12.3
holds: a finding that has not been verified is a LEAD, not a bug, and about
three in five survive.

### 4.1 · THE CYCLIST NOBODY COULD SEE

The largest one, and the one no harness could have caught.

`cycStart(p, fadeIn)` ends with `cycAlpha(fadeIn ? 0.02 : 1)` and leaves the
ramp to `cycFrame` — which is called from `updateNotes` BELOW
`if (getState() !== 'play') return`, so it does not run under a cutscene.
`cycStart`'s own comment says exactly that: *"a SCENE still sets the alpha
outright through `cycAlpha`, because `cycFrame` does not run under a cutscene
and a ramp there would never advance."* v13.2 then wrote three scene calls
that rely on that ramp.

Measured, by walking every scene's real timeline against a replay of
`cineSeek` (`dbg-scenealpha.mjs`, plain Node, no browser):

| scene | v14.2, as shipped | v14.3 |
|---|---|---|
| A | full → fades out 4.4–7.1 → gone | unchanged |
| **B** | **FULL for 13.7 s, then GONE for the rest** | full 0–2.2, fades out to 3.0, gone, fades in 13.7–14.5, full to the end |
| **C** | **GONE (alpha 0.02) for the whole 18.4 s** | fades in and out at each of its seven beats, full in between |
| D | full throughout | unchanged |

Both inverted. Scene B stood him at full alpha, bell ringing, through the
whole walk to the "empty ground" its own outcome card describes, and then put
the thing behind the 180-degree turn — the payoff frame — at two per cent.
Scene C, where the player fires three rounds, whips round and gasps, had no
subject in it at all from its first frame to its last.

And two per cent is worse than faint: v12.4 measured that this one baked
29,707-triangle mesh is not drawn correctly through the transparent path, so
what little was there had no head and no torso. This is Chad's own v12.4
report — *"The body is missing, and this happens across many outcome
cutscenes too"* — re-made in a different way by the release that came after
the fix.

Fixing it made a second defect visible for the first time, which is the
honest cost of a fix like this: scene B's payoff was placed at z −2.6, and the
berm is a solid box at z −3.2…−1.6, 0.9 m tall. `ghostcyclist.glb` is
0.720 × 1.575 × 1.906 with its length on z, so 1.55 m of his length and
0.9 m of his height were inside the earth mound, with a sandbag through the
saddle. He stands at −5.2 now: his whole length downrange of the berm's face,
4.4 m from the lens, still between the camera and the line.

### 4.2 · THE RESUME

Six defects, all on the Continue path, all invisible to the suite.

- **A Continue after the third round left the chapter unfinishable.** There
  are two live ways to ring the bell in the played moment: `cycFrame`'s
  far-edge test, which is unreachable for the last pass (`cycStopped` is set
  when `PASS_SPD[pass]` is 0, and it is 0 for pass 3), and `beginMoment`'s own
  backstop, which is gated on `shots === 0`. The third round's bell comes from
  a THIRD place — `onFiredAtIt`'s `after(3.5, …)` — and that timer lives in
  `todo`, which no save carries. So a resume with `shots = 3` restored a
  stopped cyclist with every bell path dead: no decision, no prompt, and
  `kit.presence(0.55)` draining until the faint, which rewinds the save to the
  start of the chapter. The live path's own timer is armed on the resumed path
  now.
- **A round fired during the confusion skipped `beginMoment` entirely.**
  `onFiredAtIt` calls `setPhase('moment')` to re-stamp the ammo receipt
  (v12.3), and `setPhase` also assigns `phase` — so the hand-over's
  `if (phase === 'confuse')` was already false when it fired. The objective
  stayed on "Hold it in your sight. Do not fire." for the rest of the chapter
  while the player kept firing, the presence never rose, and `b4float` — the
  only line that says somebody else sees it — was never heard. The intended
  path was the one path most players will not take.
- **A MISS during the confusion was scored as nothing at all** — neither arm
  of the branch matched and it returned before the reprimand. The same one
  line closes it.
- **The cease-fire had no bookmark**, so for its six seconds the save still
  read `serialN` and a Continue replayed a serial already paid for. `bank()`
  dedupes by NOTE and one serial has two possible notes, so a replay that
  scored differently banked the other one in full on top.
- **A Continue in the confusion discarded the whole reveal** — `dropTodo()`
  emptied the schedule the sting, the shouts and the tower's negative live in,
  and `t4neg`'s own registry row says "the chapter's whole gap is in this
  line". It replays the sighting now.
- **A resumed moment fired no flare**, so the climax ran on a black range.

### 4.3 · THE SOUND

- **"It's coming back" could never play.** `sayLine` refuses while another
  voice is live; traced in chapter-clock seconds, the confusion's queue runs
  to 21.28 s while the moment opens at 13.6, so for the whole first eleven
  seconds — and past every round a normal player fires — the line was
  refused. Every other call site in the chapter had been converted to the
  queue for exactly this reason; this one was missed. (It was missed again in
  this release: the comment written above it three hours earlier asserted an
  ordering that had never been traced.)
- **Three sighting shouts had no measured length.** `sayLine` states its
  one-voice-at-a-time window in the chapter's own `SECS`, and a name the table
  does not carry books the 2.5 s fallback — so `k4cyc` (3.42 s, "DON'T
  SHOOT!") released the queue 0.67 s early and the PA key-up landed on its
  last word. Two more of the same in e2c1 (`b1hurry`, `k1hurry`). All five
  were measured when they were made and simply never entered.
  **`chaptertest` now fails any chapter that speaks a line its SECS table
  does not carry** — a data check, no new harness, and it catches the whole
  class.
- **The first early shot deleted the fire order.** `lineQ.length = 0` wiped
  the queue, and the comment above it names the trigger window as "while the
  tower is still giving the order" — which is precisely when the order is
  sitting in that queue unspoken. The reprimand is unshifted in front of it
  now, so it interrupts without eating anything.
- **The bicycle bell rang through the outcome card, the teaching and the rank
  screen.** `mixBeds` keys it to `cyc.a` on the strength of "`cyc.a` is 0
  everywhere else" — and `restore()` breaks that, putting the alpha back to
  the snapshot taken AT THE DECISION, where the bell beat had already faded
  him to 1. So every ending, including scene A, whose whole point is that the
  thing goes away.
- **The flare burned in silence** in the film and all four endings: a film
  drives the light through `setFlare` and never sets `flareOn`, and the hiss
  was gated on `playing && flareOn`. It is keyed to the light now.

  reachable, since nothing roots the player during the moment — the ghost

## 5 · ONE FINDING THAT DID NOT SURVIVE

The lifecycle pass raised a **breaks-the-chapter** finding against this
release's own fix: that every fade of the cyclist draws him with no head and
no torso, because v12.4 measured that this one baked mesh "is not drawn
correctly through the transparent path… independent of alpha". If that held,
the scene fades added here would be a regression, and so would every fade in
play since v13.2.

It does not hold. Photographed on one frame with one property changed at a
time — the rider side-on, lit, 4 m from the lens:

| case | result |
|---|---|
| `transparent: false`, opacity 1 | whole rider |
| `transparent: true`, opacity 1, depthWrite on | **identical** — whole rider |
| `transparent: true`, opacity 0.5, depthWrite **on** | whole rider, half-transparent |
| `transparent: true`, opacity 0.5, depthWrite **off** | whole rider, half-transparent |

So the fade is safe on the shipped build, and the claim in the loader's
comment is retired — the code it justifies is kept, because holding a
fully-visible figure opaque is right either way, but the sentence that said
this model cannot be made transparent is no longer true of it.

Worth keeping as a habit: **an audit reasoning from a note in the code is
reasoning from a claim, not from a measurement.** Both of those are worth
checking, and the note is the one nobody re-checks.
