# v13.1 · THE MUZZLE FLASH, AND THE AMMO POINT THAT IS REAL

Chad, with three Sketchfab models:

> "See if you can use this muzzle flash effect when shooting the rifle, it
> should show this for a split second."
>
> "Also use a mix of these 2 models, one is ammo crate, one is a bunch of
> magazines and bullets."

All three land in episode 2 chapter 4. `src/main.js` gains the flash seam and
nothing else; episode 1 declares no weapon at all, so it cannot reach any of
it, and the FIXTURE declares a weapon with no model and no flash, which is
what proves the default is nothing.

## 2 · The prep, and the two laws it paid for

`tools/prepmuzzle.mjs` and `tools/prepammo.mjs`.

| out | size | triangles |
|---|---|---|
| `assets/muzzle.glb` | 1659 KB → **94 KB** | 16, both variants kept |
| `assets/ammocrate.glb` | 8358 KB → **609 KB** | 40,426 → 12,610 |
| `assets/ammomags.glb` | 3078 KB → **184 KB** | 4,634, untouched |

**THE ALPHA WAS NEVER CARRYING ANYTHING.** The flash file ships three
textures for two materials, and measured, **tex1 and tex2 hash identically
over RGB** — tex1 is simply tex2 plus an alpha channel. Since the engine
draws the cone ADDITIVELY, where black adds nothing and the core glows, the
alpha is redundant twice over. Two images ship instead of three, as 512 px
JPEG.

**`flatten()` DOES NOT BAKE.** The first pass of `prepammo` measured the raw
POSITION accessors after `flatten()` and reported the crate as 0.480 × 0.813
× 0.620 m — a crate standing on end. `flatten()` removes the *hierarchy* and
leaves every mesh node holding its own local transform, and both Sketchfab
files hang their meshes off a root with a quarter turn about X. The fix is
`clearNodeTransform()`, and the tool now ASSERTS that every node is identity
afterwards rather than trusting it. Asserted too, because a crate that comes
out on its side would pass every other check: the base is on y = 0, it is
centred in x/z, its length is the number asked for, and **it is wider than it
is tall**.

**METALNESS COMES DOWN** (the v8.9 law, met twice more): both files ship
metalness 1.0, and a fully metallic material with no environment map has
nothing to reflect and renders near black. Photographed raw, the magazines
were a black silhouette with three lit brass tips. At 0.30 they read as dark
polymer with brass in the open one.

## 3 · The flash: the engine's share

`weaponFlash` — the point light on the WORLD camera that has thrown the
flash's light since v12.0 — is untouched. The new thing is the CONE, and the
two are deliberately different objects in different scenes: the light lights
the range, the cone lights nothing and is only seen. Both run off the one
`weaponFlashT` clock, so they cannot disagree about when a shot happened.

Four new fields on `WEAPON_DEFAULTS`, all defaulting to nothing:

| field | default | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| `flash` | `''` | the asset key; empty means no cone is ever created |
| `flashPos` | `[0.135, -0.227, -0.671]` | the apex, in `weaponProp`'s own frame (plain viewmodel metres) |
| `flashSize` | `0.18` | the cone's LENGTH in metres; its rim is 1.09× that across |
| `flashSecs` | `0.09` | how long it is on screen |

**`flashPos` IS MEASURED, not chosen.** It is the KRISS Vector's barrel tip,
read off `main_Vector_D_0`'s SKINNED vertices at the engine's own rest pose —
`getVertexPosition`, the most-negative-Z vertex, and the 18-vertex cluster at
that face averaged for the bore axis. A SkinnedMesh's `Box3` is its BIND
pose (the v5.21 / v8.4 law), so a bounding box could not have answered this.

The cone is a child of `weaponProp`, so it inherits the aim's slide and the
v11.3 weapon swap for free. Three lifecycle traps, each handled rather than
hoped:

1. **Either half may land first** — the model and the flash are separate
   fetches — so `weaponFlashPlace()` is idempotent and both loaders call it.
2. **`weaponPropDrop()` disposes every mesh it can reach**, and the flash is
   a child, so it is taken out of the prop BEFORE that sweep.
3. **The CSP rescue writes to the parser's materials, asynchronously**, and
   by then the meshes are wearing ours. A parser-material → ours map plus
   `rescueTextures`' own `onMap` callback carries the texture across; the
   synchronous path (`src.map` already set) is taken at build time. Neither
   alone is enough.

Two cards are dealt alternately and each is rolled about the bore, so a
magazine's worth of shots is not one picture repeated — on the flash's **OWN**
deterministic stream (`flashSeed`), deliberately, so the recoil's sideways
scatter stays bit-identical to v13.0 and the feel Chad signed off on is not
perturbed by a cosmetic addition.

## 4 · The ammo point

The primitives v13.0 built — the two steel boxes, the tray of eighteen
standing rounds, the four loose ones and the four magazines — now live in ONE
group, `ammoOld`, and the swap is a single flag. That shape is the lesson of
v9.1 and v9.2, where the superseded list was enumerated by hand and missed a
piece twice (the bedding, then the posts and rails). The table, its legs and
the red lamp are NOT in the group: they are not superseded.

On the table: **two crates, stacked**, at the −x end where the steel boxes
stood, the upper skewed 0.09 rad because nobody stacks a crate square — and
the top one's lid carries the model's own loose rounds where the film's
second shot can see them. Then **two sets of magazines** along the near half,
where a man actually draws them.

Both models arrive in real metres with their origin on the base centre, so
every number in the chapter is a place on the table and none is a scale
factor. The crate's length is on its own Z, so a quarter turn lays it along
the table — a NODE rotation, which quantization permits where a write to the
vertices would not (the v9.0 law, used the other way round).

The primitives stay standing until **every** model has landed, so a failed
download costs a nicer prop and never the chapter (the v4.7 rule), and a
half-dressed table never appears.
