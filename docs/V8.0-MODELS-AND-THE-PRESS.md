## 1 · THE PRESS THAT DID NOTHING

**Measured before anything was changed**, against the shipped v7.9 build, at
the first frame of play (`dbg-e2talk.mjs`, reading the new `__enc.packDecoded()`):

```
 samples decoded at the start of play : 46
 b1day  k1board  k1three  n1shower  n1board  decoded : false false false false false
 first  call to play each of them              : false false false false false   (nothing played)
 same call 1.5 s later                         : true  true  true  true  true
```

`snd()` returns `null` for a sample that has not decoded — `sndBuf()` kicks
the decode and the call plays nothing — and until now only a FILM's cues were
warmed (`whenDecoded(introSamples())`). Every line a hotspot can ask for was
cold. So the first press of E at a soldier was **silent by construction**.

And it got worse from there: `sayLine` booked `speak.until` for the full
length of the line it had just failed to play, so every press for the next
three or four seconds was refused too — silently, with no click, no badge
change, nothing. Press, silence; press, silence; press, and by then the bytes
had landed. Exactly the report, and the "not always" is the decode race.

What was NOT the cause, checked rather than assumed: the badge geometry. A
grid over the toilet-door hotspot (`dbg-e2spot.mjs`) offers it at every
distance inside its radius up to about 34° off-aim, which is correct
behaviour — a hotspot must be on screen to be offered.

**The fix, in three parts:**

- **`ctx.warmSounds(names)` — the seventeenth engine seam.** A chapter may
  decode ahead of the player. It only ever RECORDS: a chapter is built long
  before its sound pack has downloaded, so warming at build time would decode
  nothing (the first attempt called `packWarm` straight through and threw,
  because the whole audio section is initialised further down the file than
  the first chapter build — `packBufs` was still undefined). The pack loader
  drains the set the moment bytes land, and `enterWorld` drains it again for a
  replayed chapter whose pack is already in memory. Chapters 1–5 never call it.
- **`sayLine` books its window only when a line actually STARTED** —
  `worldSfx` hands back the source, or null.
- **and a cold press is HELD, not dropped.** The decode is already running, so
  the line fires the moment it lands (given up after three seconds rather than
  arriving out of nowhere), and what the line was going to make happen — the
  talk take, a follow-up line — rides with it as `onStart`, so the animation
  and the voice cannot come apart when one of them waits.

Measured after: **68 decoded at the first frame of play**, every talk line
among them, and the first call to each one plays.

One more bug fell out of reading those handlers: the bunkmate is the FBO rig,
whose rest take is `Idle_6`, and both his lines sent him back to `Idle_9`
when they finished — a take he does not have. `rig.play` of a missing take is
a no-op by design, so since v7.1 he has been frozen in the last frame of his
talking take after every line he says.

## 2 · THE ADMIN TEE, AND WHY IT WOULD NOT SHRINK

Chad's file: 593,914 triangles, one material, an 8192-pixel base colour, nine
clips authored on its own rig. Run through the standing `prepwoman.mjs`
recipe it came out at **45 MB with 592,763 triangles** — the simplifier had
removed 0.2 % of the mesh at a ratio asking for 7 %.

The tell is in the vertex count: 1,190,700 vertices for 593,914 triangles,
where a closed mesh of that size has about 297k. Measured, there are **297,665
unique POSITIONS, each duplicated exactly 4.00 times**, and **2000 of 2000
sampled triangles are flat-shaded** — every triangle carries its own normals.
gltf-transform's `weld()` is bitwise across every attribute, so a per-face
normal makes every vertex unique; nothing welds, and meshopt will not collapse
an edge across an attribute seam. The simplifier was being told the mesh was
all seam.

`tools/deflatten.mjs` is the step that has to come first, and it measures
before it acts:

1. sample the corner normals — a mesh that is mostly flat gets de-flattened,
   a smooth one is copied through untouched (Chad's botak measures 1 flat
   triangle in 2000 and passes straight through);
2. drop NORMAL and weld, so what is left to agree on is position, uv, joints
   and weights — a real UV-island border still splits (13 % of the duplicate
   groups here have genuinely different UVs) and the texture cannot tear.
   Checked before doing it: within a duplicate group the SKINNING is identical
   in 60,001 of 60,001 sampled groups, so welding cannot disturb the rig;
3. recompute smooth normals BY HAND. gltf-transform's `normals()` is the wrong
   tool and quietly undoes step 2 — it unwelds the primitive and writes
   per-face normals, taking the mesh straight back to 1,781,742 loose vertices
   with no index buffer, and the simplifier removed 589 triangles again.
   Area-weighted vertex normals are fifteen lines and exact.

1,190,700 → 341,992 vertices (3.48×), and the simplifier then does its job.

Shipped: **2448 KB, 44,539 triangles, 2048 JPEG, all nine clips** (resampled:
17,271 keyframes for an 11 s clip down to 3,406). The old four-animation file
was 4831 KB. Nothing is retargeted onto this rig any more, so v7.6's tearing
cannot come back.

The botak: **1687 KB, 29,907 triangles**, five clips, no de-flattening needed.

Both were verified the way v6.17 says to — **photographed on their own**, every
clip at four frames, front and side, before any of it was wired into the
chapter. No tearing, no detached limbs, texture intact.

## 3 · WHERE THE RECRUITS WENT

The botak took the ferry seats, the jetty walk and the parade square. The
jetty and the square read right on the first try. **The cabin rendered
empty** — and the riders were all there, visible, hips at 0.604 m, exactly on
the seat pans.

Measured (`dbg-sit.mjs`, head-above-hips sampled 40 times across each clip):

```
 Chair_Sit_Idle_M  dur 10.75  head-hip 0.58 at 0.00-0.125 and 0.83-1.00
                              …and 0.27 from 0.20 to 0.75
 Sit_and_Doze_Off  dur 17.38  head-hip 0.53-0.55, the whole way through
```

`Chair_Sit_Idle_M` is not an idle. For more than half its length the man
folds right over with his head down at his knees — so ten riders parked on
evenly spread fractions put seven of them below the seat backs. Each take now
carries its own **upright window** and a rider is parked inside his take's,
never across the clip at large.

The same measuring answered the parade square, where the botak has no
standing idle at all and its `restpose` is an A-pose with the arms held out.
Sampling `Walking` sixty times, **t = 0.122** puts the feet 0.136 m apart —
the closest they come — with the hands at their lowest, 0.880 m. That frame,
parked, is a man standing with his feet together and his arms down. Every rank
stands on the same frame, because men at attention are meant to match; the
life comes from a few centidegrees of yaw.

## 4 · AND SOMEBODY DOES THE PUSH-UPS

Chad, back at v7.1: *"suddenly it says you the last one with 20 push ups, and
there is push up sounds, but im not actually doing anything on my screen."*
The sound had nothing to look at because no rig in the chapter carried the
take. The new tee carries three of them. The buddy now drops on the line
beside you while the sergeant's line lands: 2.71 s down, three reps at 1.63 s,
3.21 s back up — 10.8 s, inside the 15.5 s the beat has before the squad is
sent back. `fallOut(false)` puts him on his idle whatever happened, so a
skipped phase can never leave a man face-down in the bunk.
