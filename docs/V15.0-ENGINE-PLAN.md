> After you are done with all these and deployed what you were working on,
> next i want you to think of how to dramatically optimize the game without
> resorting to compromising visual quality or fidelity in any way. It has to
> be technically sophisticated, like increasing the multi threading
> capabilities, improving the game engine, low level optimizations, engine

> these highly technical improvements that are objectively superior. Think of
> what even i have not thought of. Plan it out, have steps and milestones and
> checkpoints, save memory along the way to avoid context loss and memory
> compaction, keep executing continuously until you are fully done and deploy
> it.
>
> You should not resort to cheap methods that may have unintended effects,
> like compressing things, reducing resolution, etc. They should be real
> technical improvements to the core engine.

## 1. The rules this work is held to

1. **Zero visual change.** The same pixels, proven by a pixel diff of fixed
   camera poses before and after (tolerance 0 unless a float-level
   difference is explained). No resolution or pixel-ratio change, no mesh or
   texture compression or decimation, no LOD that swaps visible detail, no
   smaller shadow maps, no fewer lights, no lower frame cap.
2. **Zero behaviour change.** Timings a cutscene depends on, sounds, save
   format, harness contracts — all identical. The 24 harnesses stay green.
3. **Measured, not assumed.** Every change has a before and an after number
   from the same probe on the same box (`dbgprof.mjs`, §3).
4. **Both builds.** The strict-CSP single-file build keeps working; anything
   that needs a real worker file degrades to today's path there.
5. **iPhone first.** Chad plays on an iPhone 13 (iOS Safari, WebGL2). A
   feature Safari lacks must fall back to today's path, never to less.

## 3. How it is measured

`dbgprof.mjs <label> <chapters…>` — for each chapter: into play, six seconds
to settle, then twenty seconds with the view turning 0.2 rad a second:

- CDP `Performance.getMetrics` deltas per second of play: script, task,
  layout and style-recalc milliseconds, and layout/style counts;
- a CPU sampling profile (0.5 ms): self time per function, the garbage
  collector's share, idle;
- the renderer's own counters summed over BOTH passes of every frame (the
  world and the viewmodel): renders, draw calls and triangles per frame;
  programs, geometries and textures resident; the JS heap.

Output `prof-<label>.json`. SwiftShader draws on the CPU in the GPU process,
so frame RATE on this box says little about a phone's GPU; the main-thread
numbers (script, GC, layout, style) are the engine's own overhead and are
what these changes are judged on, with draw calls and state changes as the
GPU-side proxy.

## 5. Record

- CP0 — v14.16 shipped (869da6a, deploy 6ab6faa87189d8aed14df67e). v15
  starts from it. `dbgprof.mjs` written; baseline in `prof-base1416.json`.
- CP1 — THE BASELINE, read. This box renders on SwiftShader (0.1–1.1 fps),
  so main-thread script is tiny next to the GPU: 2–5 ms of script per
  second, 5–15 ms per frame. The GPU proxy is the number that matters, and
  `dbgtris.mjs` (the scene frustum-culled exactly as three does, triangles
  summed by subtree) found where it goes: in e2c3's harbour **1.09 M of the
  1.1 M triangles a frame are TREES**, in all four directions, because an
  InstancedMesh is culled as a whole and a stand surrounds the player; in
  ch3 the trees are 234 k and the seated crowd 255 k (70 skinned meshes with
  `frustumCulled = false`, drawn whichever way the player looks).
- CP2 — **M1 INSTANCE CULLING, DONE.** `cullEachInstance` / `cullInstances`
  in main.js: each tree stand's full instance list is kept, and before the
  world is drawn the instances whose own sphere is in the frustum are packed
  to the front in their original order; a shadow-map frame draws the full
  list. Switch `OPT.instCull`. Proven by `dbgpix.mjs` (the same frozen frame
  drawn off and on in one task, every pixel compared, eight headings):
  **identical at every heading**, e2c3 1.09–1.39 M → 0.32–0.95 M triangles
  (31–71 % fewer), ch3 18–24 % fewer.
- CP3 — **M2 SPHERE CULLING, DONE.** `cullBySphere(root, cx, cy, cz, r)`
  (engine, and a CHCTX seam): a root three cannot cull (a skinned clone posed
  from a skeleton elsewhere) is taken off the camera's LAYERS while a
  generous sphere round it is outside the frustum — not its visibility (the
  chapter's) and not its boundingSphere (three's sort key). Shadow frames and
  the warm frame restore everything. Chapter 3's seated crowd registers each
  sitter (1.6 m about his middle; he fills 1.30 m by ~0.6). Switch
  `OPT.sphereCull`. `dbgpix.mjs sphereCull ch3`: **identical at all eight
  headings**; facing away from the tent a further 59–63 % fewer triangles and
  about half the draw calls (on top of M1); facing the crowd, unchanged.
- CP4 — **M3 SHADOW TRIM, DONE.** `shadowCasterSync()` under the curtain
  (before the compile) and every 120 frames: a light declared to cast turns
  its shadow off while the world holds NO caster (hidden meshes counted), and
  back on the moment one appears. Measured on a phone (390 × 844, touch): all
  five episode-2 chapters have **0 casters** (everything is
  `castShadow = !LOW`) while 95–493 receiving meshes sampled the moon's empty
  map — nine PCF compares per lit pixel for a map that always answers "lit".
  `PHONE=1 dbgpix.mjs shadowTrim e2c1 e2c5`: **identical at all sixteen
  headings**; e2c1 on the phone now reports no light casting; ch1 (97
  casters) keeps both of its shadow lights. Switch `OPT.shadowTrim`.
- CP5 — **two exact CPU trims.** `darkLights()` walks a LIST of the lights
  (refreshed every 30 frames and on every stage rebuild) instead of
  traversing the whole scene graph every frame to find ~11 of them; every
  light it ever hid is in the list because it was in it when hidden (and the
  loop's early-out is `continue`, not the traversal callback's `return`).
  `loopVol`/`loopPan` re-aim an AudioParam only when the target MOVES: an
  exponential approach re-aimed at the same target is the same curve, so a
  repeat only queued another automation event for every bed, every frame.
- CP6 — **binary sound packs, DONE and verified.** build.py writes each pack
  twice: the JSON-of-base64 the single-file build inlines, and for the hosted
  build a binary one ('MZP1', a JSON index, the files byte for byte). The
  engine reads either (`packParse`); a binary pack's sounds are views into
  one buffer, copied only when one is decoded. Netlify already brotli-
  compresses the JSON in transit (4.82 MB over the wire for 6.43 MB), so this
  is NOT a smaller download and is not claimed as one: it is a 6 MB JSON.parse
  and every per-sound base64 decode taken off the phone's main thread, and a
  quarter less memory held for sound all session (heap at e2c1 after the
  journey 133 → 89 MB, uncollected). All 1,177 sounds checked byte for byte
  against their source files and the JSON packs: 0 mismatches. csp, hosted,
  sound, menu green; `dbgsound16.mjs` unchanged (every opening line, every
  loop retired, the chair scrape a sample).
- CP7 — **covered frames, the letterbox, the empty hand pass — coded;
  letterbox PROVEN.** `worldCovered()` (title, the chapter card once forced
  solid, a film/scene held on black, the episode card): nothing is drawn;
  the logic runs, the matrices are brought up to date, shadow redraws wait
  for a drawn frame, the warm frames are forced. `letterboxArm()`: once both
  bars measurably cover their edges, both passes are scissored to the band
  between them (1 px into each bar); off before the bars leave.
  `dbgletter.mjs`: band = 78 % of the height in ch1, ch3 (desktop) and e2c1
  (phone), **identical inside the band** at three film times each. The hand
  pass is skipped when no mesh under the hands is visible. The suite already
  runs visibly faster (census 58 → 41 s, csp 69 → 52 s): the software
  renderer no longer draws under the title and the cards.
- CP8 — rescueTextures looks before copying (csp green: the strict build's
  rescue still works); the download ahead drains the stream instead of
  building each file as one buffer.
- CP9 — **in-play shader compiles, measured and fixed (to be re-measured).**
  `dbgfirstsight.mjs`: HER FIRST APPEARANCE in ch1 compiled **8 programs
  mid-play** (her light going up changes the point-light count, which is part
  of every lit shader) and the worst frame was 8.6 s on the probe box.
  `warmLightStates()` compiles, under the curtain, the states the engine's own
  lights reach: her light on while she fades (transparent) and when solid,
  the torch on, the muzzle flash on; three keeps every variant per material,
  so the moment in play is a lookup.
- CP10 — **matrix skip, coded.** `Object3D.prototype.updateMatrix` remembers
  the ten numbers and the parent it composed from; bitwise the same → no
  recompose and no dirty flag, so a subtree where nothing moved costs one
  comparison per node instead of a compose and a forced multiply. Proof to
  run: `dbgmat.mjs` (incremental vs three's forced full recompute, bitwise,
  in films and in play).
