## PART ONE · THE VIEWPORT

### The cause is one number

`src/main.js:585`

```js
const camera = new THREE.PerspectiveCamera(72, innerWidth / innerHeight, 0.08, 160);
```

In three.js that 72° is the **VERTICAL** field of view, and it never
changes — the resize handler (`main.js:5154`) only updates `aspect`. So
the vertical slice of the world is IDENTICAL on every device, and the
horizontal slice is whatever the screen's shape gives:

| | aspect | horizontal fov | width seen at 3 m |
|---|---|---|---|
| desktop 1920x1080 | 1.78 | 105° | 7.75 m |
| laptop 1440x860 | 1.67 | 101° | 7.30 m |
| **`cinetest` 500x340** | 1.47 | 94° | 6.41 m |
| iPad portrait 820x1180 | 0.69 | 54° | 3.03 m |
| **iPhone portrait 390x844** | 0.46 | **37°** | **2.01 m** |
| iPhone landscape 844x390 | 2.16 | 115° | 9.43 m |

A portrait phone sees **3.6x less width** than a laptop, and exactly the
same height. Nothing is squashed; the sides are simply not there.

**Why it was never caught:** every cutscene check in the suite runs
landscape. `cinetest` uses 500x340. There has never been a portrait leg.

### But "phone is worse" is WRONG, and this is the important part

The phone is a **centre crop**. Whether that helps or hurts depends
entirely on whether the shot's subject is centred. Four moments rendered
at both shapes (`view-<ch>-t<time>-<shape>.png`, gitignored):

| shot | on a phone |
|---|---|
| ch2 t=22 · bedroom, altar left + door centre | **loses** the altar and the wardrobe |
| ch3 t=30 · the tentage | **loses** the altar, Guan Gong and the tang-ki entirely |
| ch4 t=10 · the flat's altar | **BETTER** — desktop wastes half the frame on a dark wall; the crop removes it |
| ch5 t=31.5 · the note on the table | **BETTER** — the teapot, teacup and TV clutter are cropped away and the note becomes the subject |

Roughly half and half. Chapter 3's is the worst case in the game: the
chapter's entire premise — a man at an altar who has stopped being
himself — is off-screen on a phone.

### What NOT to do

**Do not widen the lens globally on narrow screens.** It was the obvious
first answer (raise the vertical fov below a reference aspect, clamped, so
the horizontal field stops collapsing: portrait 37° -> ~54°, desktop
mathematically unchanged). It would rescue chapters 2 and 3 and **damage
chapters 4 and 5**, putting back exactly the clutter the crop removes. One
global setting cannot be right for both.

**Do not pull the cameras back.** Half this game happens in a bedroom and
at a dining table. There is nowhere to dolly to, and the v5.03 collision
audit exists because cutscene paths obey no collision.

**Deeper letterbox bars do not help.** `.cineBar` is a CSS overlay
(`shell.html:400`, `height:11vh`), so it crops HEIGHT only and recovers no
width. Purely a look choice, not part of the fix.

### What to do instead

1. **A portrait contact sheet, first.** Every cutscene in all five
   chapters, sampled every ~2 s, rendered at phone shape, into one sheet
   Chad can scroll on his phone. That turns "they look vastly different"
   into a specific list. The sample above suggests a MINORITY of shots are
   actually broken, not all of them.
2. **Fix those shots individually**, by aiming or nudging the camera so
   the subject is centred. Centring helps desktop too — chapter 4's shot
   proves it in reverse.
3. **A portrait leg in `cinetest`**, so a shot that loses its subject on a
   phone fails a test instead of reaching a player.
4. **Optionally a `fovTo` verb.** The cutscene language (`A(c)`,
   `main.js:3983`) has `camTo`/`yawTo`/`pitchTo` and **no FOV control at
   all**. One shot that genuinely needs width on a phone and cannot be
   solved by aiming would want it.

## PART TWO · CONFIRMED BUGS

### 1. Chapter 3's medium never moves his head — in THREE cutscenes

Scenes write the head through the PRIMITIVE placeholder:

```js
stage.medium.userData.head.rotation.x = 0.10;
stage.medium.userData.head.rotation.z = 0.42;  // ch3.js:2966-2967
```

`medium.userData.head` is set at `ch3.js:912` on the primitive figure. But
when Chad's rigged Taoist master lands, `ch3.js:1531` runs

```js
for (const c of [...medium.children]) c.visible = false;
```

— every primitive child, that head box included, is hidden, and the real
head bone lives in the local `medHead` (`ch3.js:1524`). So every scripted
head move writes to an invisible box.

Affected, all silent:

| lines | scene | the beat |
|---|---|---|
| 2639, 2708 | `intro` | his head thrown back, and its reset |
| 2775, 2790 | `scWatch` (A) | tipped at the aisle |
| 2966–2967, 3016–3017 | `scJoin` (B) | **"head tilted the way no one tilts a head, and his eyes are SHUT"** — chapter 3's signature scare |

The AMBIENT sway is fine: `ch3.js:2106` drives `medHead` directly, reading
the primitive head's position as its input. Only the scripted tilts are
lost. This is open task #128, which described it as one scene; it is three,
and it includes the chapter's biggest moment.

### 2. Chapter 4's ceiling fan does not stop

`scProvoke`, the "everything stops at once" beat:

```js
tr(8.0, 8.6, k => {
  duck('v4room', 1 - k); duck('clock', 1 - k); duck('fan', 1 - k);
}, rawK);
tr(8.6, 11.0, () => {}, rawK);        // three seconds of nothing
```

The fan's SOUND ducks to silence and holds three seconds. `stage.fanSpeed`
is never touched, so the blades keep turning at full speed on screen
through the whole silent beat. The chapter has the control built for it
(`ch4.js:1487-1488`) and no scene ever calls it — the only unused stage
setter in the chapter.

**Chapter 5's identical beat does it correctly**, same flat, same fan:

```js
tr(16.5, 17.1, k => { stage.fanSpeed = 1 - k; }, rawK);   // ch5 scDismiss
tr(21.0, 22.0, k => { stage.fanSpeed = k; }, rawK);
```

### 3. Adjusting the volume during a cutscene skips the cutscene

`main.js:3974`

```js
addEventListener('pointerdown', e => {
  if (state === 'cine' && cine && !cine.paused && cine.t > 0.8 && !e.target.closest?.('#mute')) {
    skipCine();
  }
});
```

Only `#mute` is excluded. The volume slider (`#volWrap`, shown on
`body.finePtr` — desktop and touchscreen laptops) is live during scenes, so
reaching for the volume loses the scene. Phones are unaffected: the slider
is `display:none` there.

### 4. A light dip can be left stuck dimmed in chapter 4

`ch4.js:1281`

```js
function polterUpdate(dt) {
  if (getState() !== 'play') return;
  if (dipT >= 0) { ... }               // a light-dip in progress
```

The state guard sits ABOVE the in-progress dip, so opening the decision
panel (or starting a cutscene) mid-dip freezes the ceiling light at its
flicker value instead of letting it finish. The room stays dim behind the
panel. The guard belongs on the CLOCK and the trigger, not on the running
animation.

## PART THREE · FRAGILE, NOT YET BROKEN

### 5. Two scenes finish their last line with almost no headroom

`cineEnd()` calls `stopCineVoices()`, and `c.dur` is the max TRACK `t1`
with stings NOT counted (`main.js:3842`) — so a line still playing when the
last track ends is cut off mid-word. Measured across all 20 choice scenes,
nothing is cut today, but:

| scene | scene ends | last line ends | headroom |
|---|---|---|---|
| ch1 `scLeave` | 7.70 | `vrelief` 7.67 | **0.03 s** |
| ch2 `scCall` | 23.50 | `vrelief` 23.42 | **0.08 s** |

Both are `vrelief`, which got **1.05 s LONGER** at v5.18. Any future
re-voicing truncates them, and nothing checks it. A one-line assertion in
`chaptertest` (last spoken cue end <= scene duration) would hold it.

### 6. Chapter 5's cast never looks at the player in any cutscene

`ch5.js:1254`

```js
const lookWeight = () =>
  lookOverride !== null ? lookOverride : (getState() === 'play' ? 1 : 0);
```

Default OFF in cutscenes, opt-in per scene via `stage.castLook` — and **no
ch5 scene ever sets it**. The v5.03/v5.04 head-tracking works in play and
is off in every film, including `scGuide`, where the tang-ki teaches across
the table for 33 seconds. The reasoning for defaulting off is sound (a film
cuts to cameras that are not a person; this chapter's hard cut goes UNDER
the table) — but the opt-in was built and never used. **Chad's call**, not
a defect.

The same shape: **`stage.medRate` in ch3** (added at v5.19 as "the only
honest way for a scene to say he does not move") is never set by any scene.
Harmless — the default follows the ritual — but the seam is unused.

### 7. A touchscreen laptop gets phone-grade frame pacing

`main.js:4935`

```js
const FRAME_MIN_MS = HAS_TOUCH ? 1000 / 61 : 0;
const SLOW_EVERY_OTHER = HAS_TOUCH;      // half-rate drift for the soft stuff
```

`main.js:131-141` states the principle explicitly — "LOW (reduced quality)
needs a small screen too, or a touchscreen laptop gets phone-grade
rendering for no reason" — and `LOW = IS_PHONE` obeys it. These two do not.

### 8. Latent: ch1's parked amulet loader has no `alive` guard

`ch1.js:675`. Every other async load in every chapter guards
`if (!alive) return;` against landing after `dispose()`. This one does not.
Dead code today (`SHOW_AMULET = false`, `ch1.js:667`), so it cannot bite —
but flipping that flag would ship a leak.

## PART FOUR · TWO THINGS TO RAISE, NOT BUGS

- **The game starts MUTED on desktop**, unmuted on touch (`main.js:1844`,
  `let muted = !HAS_TOUCH`). `LEARNINGS.md` records this as Chad's call. A
  first-time desktop visitor hears none of the 95 sounds unless they spot
  the speaker icon. Worth revisiting only if he wants to.
- **Chapters 2–5's cutscenes are never played by any harness.** `cinetest`
  drives chapter 1's four scenes and one chapter-2 film-cover check;
  `leaktest` only BUILDS every chapter; `chaptertest` validates data
  without a browser. So 16 choice cutscenes and 4 opening films are never
  exercised. That gap is why #1 and #2 survived, and it is why v5.19's
  frozen tang-ki survived before them.

## PART FIVE · WHAT IS HEALTHY (measured, for the record)

- **All 20 choice scenes across all five chapters** were played through the
  real UI: no page errors anywhere.
- **No spoken line is cut off** by its scene ending (table in Part Three).
- **No overlapping spoken lines** anywhere — every scene and film scanned
  cue-by-cue against the measured take lengths in `src/voicelines.js`.
- **No chapter adds an event listener or a timer**, so `rebuildStage()`
  leaks neither. All six chapters have `dispose()`.
- **Save/load is well guarded**: `?ch=` deep links neither read nor write
  the save; only `state === 'play'` autosaves; a save naming an unknown
  chapter falls back rather than bricking the boot; `applyState` rejects
  non-finite numbers (`+null` would be an instant faint).
- **The v5.19 mixer trap is correctly handled in ch2, ch3 and ch5** — every
  mixer runs above the `getState() === 'cine'` early return. ch2 even
  documents why it needs no per-scene look gate.
- **`packLoad(nextChapterKey())` at chapter 5** passes `null` and resolves
  harmlessly against the already-loaded shared pack.

## How this was measured

Two throwaway probes (`dbgaudit1.mjs`, `dbgaudit2.mjs` — gitignored by
`dbg*.mjs`, so they die with the session):

- **dbgaudit1** drives every chapter through the real UI (start, skip the
  film, walk to the interactable, open the decision, pick each choice),
  reads `__enc.cine.dur()`, and compares it against the last spoken cue's
  end computed statically from the chapter source + `src/voicelines.js`.
- **dbgaudit2** plays each chapter's opening film at two viewports
  (1440x860 and 390x844), seeks to the same instants and screenshots both.

Both are worth rebuilding as real harnesses — dbgaudit1 IS the missing
chapter-2-to-5 cutscene test named in Part Four.
