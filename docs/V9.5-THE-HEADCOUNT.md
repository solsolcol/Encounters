# v9.5 · THE HEADCOUNT, THE TOGGLE ROPE, AND THE MAN WHO IS NOT THERE

Chad, the day v9.4 went live, changing the direction of the whole episode:

> I want a slight update in direction, everything in episode 2, despite all
> these new interactions and minigames, should all still link back to
> spiritual stuff. I don't want to veer too far off into a military sim game.

That is the rule this release exists to serve, and it is worth writing down
as a standing one rather than a note on one build: **every mechanic in
episode 2 is a horror beat wearing a soldier's uniform.** The fall-in, the
push-ups, the standby bed and the count-off are not army colour — they are
the ordinary, repeatable, countable routine that the chapter uses to make one
thing stand out. A recruit's day is *exactly* the right setting for this
story precisely because it is so heavily counted: when the numbers do not
come out, everyone in the room knows.

## The shape of it

Six changes, all in `src/chapters/e2/e2c1.js`. Chad's words for each:

1. **A tenth man on the line.** *"at fall in, add another FBO soldier who is
   semi-transparent (set opacity) who's a ghost, next to the line of
   bunkmates."*
2. **The count-off.** *"after doing pushups, Encik will shout and ask
   everyone to report strength (headcount). Everyone counts, one after
   another, but ends up with one additional headcount. Encik shout, why is
   there an extra headcount, we only have X recruits! Nevermind, i want
   standby bed, now!"* — replacing the existing `e1backbunk`. Then the
   section runs back as before, **and the ghost fades away in its spot.**
3. **The bed zone starts the test.** *"When the player goes back into his bed
   area of effect, i want it to automatically steer the camera view back to
   face the bunk (just like the first time the aoe triggers) and
   automatically trigger the standby bed game menu, and remove the existing
   'tap on bed' interaction as it is not needed."*
4. **The extra toggle rope.** After the standby bed: *"eh bed one recruit,
   why your standby bed got an extra set of toggle rope? You better return it
   to your buddy!"* and then the player, to himself: *"I didn't know where
   this toggle rope came from, I'm sure I only had one...nevermind..."*
5. **A shorter evening.** *"reduce the waiting length of the timer."*
6. **Lights out empties the bunk.** *"When lights out happen, encik and
   sergeant immediately starts to walk out of the bunk to the parade square
   to call it a night."*

## Why the count-off is the right beat

The arithmetic is the horror, and it costs nothing to stage. The section is
**nine men including the player** — six `admintee` recruits in `BUNK_MEN`,
the buddy, the bunkmate, and him. So the count runs one to nine, every number
correct, every man accounted for — and then a tenth voice answers. Nobody has
to see anything. The player counted himself; he knows the number; the number
is wrong.

It also pays off three ways later in the chapter, which is what makes it
worth more than a scare:

- The tenth man was **visible** during the fall-in, half there, and nobody
  reacted — so the player is the only one who saw him.
- He **fades on his spot** as the section runs back, which is the chapter
  saying he was there and is not.
- The **extra toggle rope** on the standby bed is the same fact in an object:
  one more of something than there should be. The encik reads it as
  carelessness. The player knows he packed one.

By the time the shower turns itself on at three in the morning, the chapter
has already told the player twice, in the flattest possible bookkeeping
language, that there is one too many of something in this bunk.

| `n1rope` | jamesTeen | "I didn't know where this toggle rope came from. I'm sure I only had one… never mind…" |

| `c1ten` | — | **the tenth voice** |

**The count-off cost two generations, not eight.** Both four-word takes came
back with exactly three internal silences over 0.2 s, so `masters/v9.5/slice.py`
reads each take's own envelope, cuts at the silences, and **asserts that every
slice holds exactly one word** before writing it. Cut points are never typed
in: a hard-coded time is a guess that survives only until the file is
regenerated.

**The tenth voice is the buddy's, treated** — pitched down 10 % (uncorrected,
so it is also 11 % slower), a hollow double at 60 and 180 ms, and a 3.4 kHz
lowpass. Measured against the living take: dominant **172 Hz against 237**,
spectral centroid **1107 Hz against 1630**, energy above 3 kHz **9.5 %
against 18.4 %**. It has to be recognisably a man in that line and
unmistakably not one of these men. It sits **6 dB under the cast** on
purpose — it is the one voice nobody is sure they heard.

## 5 · WHAT SHIPPED, AND HOW IT WAS MEASURED

All six of Chad's beats are in `src/chapters/e2/e2c1.js`. `src/main.js` gains
only the fourteen `STING_SAMPLE` rows and the fourteen names in
`TEEN_TAKES`/`CAST_TAKES` — no new seam, so episode 1 is untouched by
construction and was run as the control (all 24 harnesses green).

### The tenth man on the line

`fbosling` — the SERGEANT'S own asset, so he costs no download (`assetBytes`
caches by key) and `CULL_SPHERE.fbosling` already covers every pose he can
hold. He stands at the +z end of the rank at (14.0, 5.4): the section runs
z −4.2 … 4.2 and he closes it 1.2 m past the last man, which is the rank's
own spacing. That end is also the one the seniors do NOT use — their route in
goes round the −z flank — so nobody walks through him.

He does not run out with the section. The fade runs at the WHISTLE, while the
player is still on his way out of the bunk: measured on the shipped build,
alpha **0.055 at the whistle → 0.442 at +1.2 s → 0.840 at +4.7 s → 1.0**, and
the furthest recruit is formed up inside six seconds. So the rank has ten men
in it and nobody saw the tenth arrive. A ghost who fades in under your eye is
a special effect; one who is already there is the beat.

The v4.9 ghost treatment (grey, half-alpha, no shadow, no depth write) is now
`ghostify(rig)` in one place, with a material list kept on the rig so a ghost
can write its own alpha every frame. `ghostAlpha(rig, k)` hides the GROUP at
0, so a ghost who has faded away costs nothing to draw.

### The count-off

A count-off is a CADENCE, and it could not be laid out with `after()` at times
typed in by hand: `sayLine` refuses a line while another is still speaking, and
on a box drawing one frame a second several `after` slots flush in the same
tick — the first would speak and the rest would be swallowed. **A silent
count-off is the whole beat gone with no error.** So it runs off its own queue
(`countQ`/`runCount`) gated on the SAME clock the refusal is stated in, with a
`give`-held line holding the queue rather than losing the number (the v8.0 law).

Measured on the shipped build, in chapter-clock seconds:

| | t |
|---|---|
| `e1count` (the order) | 1.0 |
| `n1one` … `c1nine` | 6.85 → 16.98, about 1.0–1.4 s apart |
| the hang (`GHOST_BEAT` 0.9) | 17.0 |
| `c1ten` | ~18.0 — about **1.9 s** after "Nine!", against ~1.0 s between the others |
| `e1extra` | 19.55 → 29.35 |
| `beginStandby()` | 29.55 |

`e1extra` REPLACES `e1backbunk` as the order back to the bunk. The older take
stays in the pack and in the registry, because a RESUME into this phase has
already spent the shout and goes straight to the bed (`punishBeat(true)`).

X is NINE because of who is actually in the room: six recruits at the six beds
that are not his, the buddy, the bunkmate, and him. Every number in the count
is correct — there is no mistake to find in it — and then a tenth voice says a
number after it.

### The bed zone starts the test

`reachedBed` now turns the lens for BOTH arrivals and, on the standby one,
stands the test up itself 0.9 s later (the turn takes 0.55 s: the player sees
where he is before a panel arrives over it). Measured: yaw 2.400 → **4.928**,
which is `camFace(HIS, LINE_X)` to three decimals — dead on target — phase
`standbybed`, and the event arrives as a BRIEF, so nothing is timed until
START. `BED_STANDBY` is gone from `bedWants()` and `interactPile()` with the
word `objBedTap`: v8.7 made the player press the bed because reaching the
circle used to start a timed test while he was still walking, and v8.7's own
brief panel solved that properly, so the press had become a thing to do for
its own sake.

### The toggle rope

Measured through the real match (4 slots, 4 drops, `done: 4`, `wrong: 0`):
`n1bedok` → **`e1rope`** → **`n1rope`** → `beginFree()` at chapter-clock 23.1,
with the objective going back to "Stand by your bed — wait for orders" through
the engine's OBJECTIVE COMPLETE beat. Every gap is stated in the lines' own
measured lengths rather than in numbers typed in, so a re-generated take
cannot make two voices talk over each other.

It is the headcount again, in an object: the section counted one man too many
and his bed has laid out one item too many, and neither has an explanation.
He says "never mind" both times, which is the chapter.

### A shorter evening

`FREE_SECS` 70 → **45**, `FREE_WARN` 25 → 18. Measured on the shipped build:
the objective reads "Look around the bunk before lights out" and the timer
reads **0:45**. The evening now carries the ~15 s rope beat in front of it, so
what follows is shorter than the wall clock says it used to be either way;
there are four things to look at in the bunk and none takes more than a few
seconds.

### Lights out empties the bunk

`seniorsOut()` WALKS them where `putSergeant` stands them — two different
things, and the fall-in has needed both since v8.2. They take the route the
fall-in already proved, at `WALK_SPD` 1.95 m/s, on lanes ±0.35 (inside the
gangway's clear window of ±1.10). Measured over ten seconds from
`beginLightsOut()`: the sergeant (1.3, −2.9) → (5.59, 0.35) and the encik
(−1.5, −3.05) → (2.79, −0.35), both on `Walking`, leg 1 of 5, and **2.8 m
apart at every sample**. `setNightRoom(true)` hides them at 11.4 s whether or
not they have arrived; `reset()` stands them back at the door.

### Reset

`reset()` clears `countQ`, `countAt` and the ghost — the v8.1/v8.2/v8.7/v9.2
law a fifth time. A replay that inherited either would open the new morning
with a ghost already standing in an empty rank, or with numbers arriving out
of nowhere during the walk to the bed.

## 6 · TWO THINGS WORTH WRITING DOWN

**A real click cannot reach a HUD button while the canvas holds the pointer.**
The first probe of this release spent its entire run in
`page.click('#mute')` — Playwright resolved the button, found it visible and
enabled, and was told on every retry that `<canvas id="scene">` intercepts
pointer events. It never got past line one and reported nothing. Dispatch the
click IN the page (`el.click()`), or use `__enc`'s own hooks
(`evPress()`, `evDrop()`), and never let a probe's default timeout be minutes.

**Drive the phase, do not play the day.** The replacement probes called
`stage.headcount()`, `stage.beginFree()` and `stage.beginLightsOut()` directly
against a booted chapter and read every beat in about five minutes and two,
against an hour that produced nothing. The chapter clock runs at roughly 0.4×
wall on a SwiftShader box, so a 30-second beat is a 75-second wait — playing
the whole day through is not a measurement, it is a queue.

(Both probes did let the chapter BOOT first — v9.3's law: a beat fired before
the chapter's first frame is re-applied as a RESUME.)

## 7 · THE DOWNLOAD

The fourteen takes add ~1.1 MB to `dist/` and land in the SHARED pack, not
e2c1's. That is not new and not a mistake in this release: `build.py` moves a
sound out of shared only when no chapter-external source names it, and every
voice line in the game is named in main.js by `JAMES_TAKES`/`TEEN_TAKES`/
`CAST_TAKES` — the sets that route a voice to its bus and its duck. All 37 of
e2c1's existing lines are shared for the same reason. Changing it would mean
taking a voice off its bus, which is not a trade worth making for a few
hundred kilobytes; if the boot download ever needs the space, the fix is to
teach `build.py` about those three sets, not to touch the audio graph.
