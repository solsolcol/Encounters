# v9.5 · THE HEADCOUNT, THE TOGGLE ROPE, AND THE MAN WHO IS NOT THERE

Chad, the day v9.4 went live, changing the direction of the whole episode:

> I want a slight update in direction, everything in episode 2, despite all
> these new interactions and minigames, should all still link back to
> spiritual stuff. I don't want to veer too far off into a military sim game.

That is the rule this release exists to serve, and it is worth writing down
as a standing one rather than a note on one build: **every mechanic in
episode 2 is a horror beat wearing a soldier's uniform.** The fall-in, the
push-ups, the standby bed and the count-off are not army colour — they are
the ordinary, repeatable, countable routine that the chapter uses to make one
thing stand out. A recruit's day is *exactly* the right setting for this
story precisely because it is so heavily counted: when the numbers do not
come out, everyone in the room knows.

## The shape of it

Six changes, all in `src/chapters/e2/e2c1.js`. Chad's words for each:

1. **A tenth man on the line.** *"at fall in, add another FBO soldier who is
   semi-transparent (set opacity) who's a ghost, next to the line of
   bunkmates."*
2. **The count-off.** *"after doing pushups, Encik will shout and ask
   everyone to report strength (headcount). Everyone counts, one after
   another, but ends up with one additional headcount. Encik shout, why is
   there an extra headcount, we only have X recruits! Nevermind, i want
   standby bed, now!"* — replacing the existing `e1backbunk`. Then the
   section runs back as before, **and the ghost fades away in its spot.**
3. **The bed zone starts the test.** *"When the player goes back into his bed
   area of effect, i want it to automatically steer the camera view back to
   face the bunk (just like the first time the aoe triggers) and
   automatically trigger the standby bed game menu, and remove the existing
   'tap on bed' interaction as it is not needed."*
4. **The extra toggle rope.** After the standby bed: *"eh bed one recruit,
   why your standby bed got an extra set of toggle rope? You better return it
   to your buddy!"* and then the player, to himself: *"I didn't know where
   this toggle rope came from, I'm sure I only had one...nevermind..."*
5. **A shorter evening.** *"reduce the waiting length of the timer."*
6. **Lights out empties the bunk.** *"When lights out happen, encik and
   sergeant immediately starts to walk out of the bunk to the parade square
   to call it a night."*

## Why the count-off is the right beat

The arithmetic is the horror, and it costs nothing to stage. The section is
**nine men including the player** — six `admintee` recruits in `BUNK_MEN`,
the buddy, the bunkmate, and him. So the count runs one to nine, every number
correct, every man accounted for — and then a tenth voice answers. Nobody has
to see anything. The player counted himself; he knows the number; the number
is wrong.

It also pays off three ways later in the chapter, which is what makes it
worth more than a scare:

- The tenth man was **visible** during the fall-in, half there, and nobody
  reacted — so the player is the only one who saw him.
- He **fades on his spot** as the section runs back, which is the chapter
  saying he was there and is not.
- The **extra toggle rope** on the standby bed is the same fact in an object:
  one more of something than there should be. The encik reads it as
  carelessness. The player knows he packed one.

By the time the shower turns itself on at three in the morning, the chapter
has already told the player twice, in the flattest possible bookkeeping
language, that there is one too many of something in this bunk.

## The fourteen new voice lines

ElevenLabs flow `zvRcxDJbxeJDS6aCA903`; `masters/v9.5/make.sh` is the recipe
and carries the measured reason for every pick. Peak-matched to **−3.8 dBFS**,
where the whole episode-2 cast sits.

| id | who | what |
|---|---|---|
| `e1count` | encik | "STAND UP! ALL OF YOU! I want STRENGTH! Number off! From the right! NOW!" |
| `e1extra` | encik | "TEN? Who say TEN? I only got NINE recruits in this section. NINE! … Never mind. Go back to your bunk. I want STANDBY BED. NOW!" — **replaces `e1backbunk`** |
| `e1rope` | encik | "EH! BED ONE RECRUIT! Why your standby bed got an EXTRA set of toggle rope? You better RETURN it to your buddy!" |
| `n1rope` | jamesTeen | "I didn't know where this toggle rope came from. I'm sure I only had one… never mind…" |
| `n1one` | jamesTeen | "One!" |
| `c1two` … `c1nine` | buddy / bunkmate | the count, alternating |
| `c1ten` | — | **the tenth voice** |

**The count-off cost two generations, not eight.** Both four-word takes came
back with exactly three internal silences over 0.2 s, so `masters/v9.5/slice.py`
reads each take's own envelope, cuts at the silences, and **asserts that every
slice holds exactly one word** before writing it. Cut points are never typed
in: a hard-coded time is a guess that survives only until the file is
regenerated.

**The tenth voice is the buddy's, treated** — pitched down 10 % (uncorrected,
so it is also 11 % slower), a hollow double at 60 and 180 ms, and a 3.4 kHz
lowpass. Measured against the living take: dominant **172 Hz against 237**,
spectral centroid **1107 Hz against 1630**, energy above 3 kHz **9.5 %
against 18.4 %**. It has to be recognisably a man in that line and
unmistakably not one of these men. It sits **6 dB under the cast** on
purpose — it is the one voice nobody is sure they heard.
