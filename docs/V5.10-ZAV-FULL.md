Chad, on the v5.09 seam work: *"why dont u use the model file that i
originally sent you that was not compressed? … it is still better than

Two asks. Both shipped. This file is the build's memory.

## 1. Why the original could not simply be used

His file (`Master_Zavoptimized.glb`, 2.8 MB, 990,192 triangles) is
Draco-compressed. three.js decodes Draco in a Web Worker that it builds
from a **blob: URL**, and the strict CSP the single-file build is tested
under forbids blob: — the same rule that shaped the hand-parsed GLB
loader and the texture rescue. So the upload's own compression cannot run
in this game, and without any compression the same geometry is 22.6 MB.

**meshopt** (`EXT_meshopt_compression` + `KHR_mesh_quantization`) is the
way through: its decoder is a plain WebAssembly module inlined in three's
own bundle, and `'unsafe-eval'` in the policy permits it. Measured on the
strict build (`dbg-cspzav.mjs`): the figure decodes, no WebAssembly
refusal, only the long-standing blob: image refusals that
`rescueTextures()` exists for. `zavLoader()` is the one GLTFLoader in the
game with `setMeshoptDecoder`; nothing else needed it.

## 2. What the full model costs, and the cut that ships

Three cuts of the same original (`tools/prepzav-full.mjs` then
`tools/packmeshopt.mjs`; the texture handling is settled in 2b below):

| cut | triangles | meshopt file | quantized, no decoder |
|---|---|---|---|
| full | 990,192 | 7.3 MB | 22.6 MB |
| 30 % | 297,048 | 2.5 MB | 7.2 MB |
| 15 % | 148,528 | 1.4 MB | 3.8 MB |

For scale: v5.09 shipped 39,606 triangles in 1.2 MB, because its
attributes were plain 32-bit floats — quantization alone is why the 15 %
cut, with nearly four times the triangles, costs the same download.

At the 900 px close-up Chad judged on, full and 30 % are
indistinguishable; 15 % is very close. In the panel the man is 300 px
tall. **The 30 % cut is what ships** (`assets/zav.glb`, 2,573,860 bytes):
it is the original's look at a third of the full file's download and a
third of its per-frame cost in a second renderer on a phone. The full and
15 % files are kept in the scratchpad pipeline and are a file swap plus a
build away if Chad wants either.

### 2b. The black streak, the birthmark, and why the paint is now untouched

Chad, on the three candidates: *"why do all of them have the black streak
across the eyebrow that was not in the original"*. They did, and it was
the v5.09 halo strip's doing: a pale rim pixel was UNCOVERED and then
refilled by the padding from the nearest covered pixel — and beside an
eyebrow the nearest covered pixel is the brow's dark, so the skin along
that seam was repainted black. Changing the pass to REPAINT a halo pixel
with its island's interior mean removed the streak and drew a muddy
brown patch in its place ("now it looks like he got a birth mark"): near
the brow that mean includes brow pixels.

So the v5.09 retouching is RETIRED. What ships touches no painted pixel:
only the **padding** of the empty fill between islands, which by
construction can change nothing inside an island — it removes the cream
bleed at every seam and nothing else. The halo strip and the seam
feather survive in `tools/prepzav-full.mjs` behind `BAND` and `BLEND`,
both 0 for the shipped file; the lesson is in LEARNINGS. Verified against
the original at the same crop (`shot-zav-final-compare.png`): the same
face, the same faint seams the scan always had, no streak, no mark.

## 3. Standing there before you ask

`zavPrefetch()` runs once, on the first entry into play (both paths: the
chapter card and the resume), through `requestIdleCallback` (an 8 s
timeout; `setTimeout` 2.5 s where the API is missing) so it never sits on
the boot-critical path beside the chapter's own loads. It creates the
panel's renderer on the hidden canvas, fetches and parses the model
without needing the panel, and then `zavWarm()` renders ONE 64 px frame
off-screen — shaders compiled, geometry and texture uploaded — so the
first frame the player sees is not the slow one. Opening the panel finds
`has3d` already set; `zavInit()`/`zavLoad()` on open are no-ops.

Order of arrival is handled both ways: a model parsed before the panel's
scene exists is adopted by `zavInit()` (`if (zav.model && !zav.model.
parent) zav.pivot.add(zav.model)`), and a scene built before the model
lands gets it from `zavLoad()`'s callback.

Measured on this box's software GPU: `has3d` is true before the panel is
opened nine seconds into play (`dbg-inv.mjs`).
