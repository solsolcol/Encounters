# v12.2 — THE RANGE THAT WORKS, AND THE MEN WHO ARE AIMING

Chad, the morning after v12.1 shipped:

> There are so many bugs in this chapter. In this chapter, it should
> immediately start with the rifle equipped in hand, and on screen the rifle
> is already there. Player should not be allowed to unequip the rifle. Player
> should not be able to use the torch, or switch to normal hands. Just for
> this chapter, the rifle stays on at all times. Also the entire range
> programming is broken, aiming is broken, targets dont drop when shot,
> nothing advances, shooting does not seem to work, there is no proper recoil
> when shooting, and it is not obvious what the player should do. Also, i
> thought i previously provided a static standing soldier who is in an aiming
> pose with rifle pointing outwards? All the shooters should use this model.
>
> I need you to comprehensively find bugs, breakages, problems and issues
> with this chapter from a gameplay perspective.
>
> The ghost cyclist should also move faster.

Six complaints about the range, and every one of them has a separate cause.
That is the thing worth writing down first: "the entire range programming is
broken" is not one bug with six symptoms — it is six bugs that happened to
land in the same forty seconds of play. Each is below with the measurement
that found it.

---

## 1 · "nothing advances" — one conjunct gated the whole chapter

`updateDay`, v12.1:

```js
if (phase === 'line' && drawn
    && Math.hypot(yaw.position.x - HIS.x, yaw.position.z - 0.35) < 1.3) reachedLine();
```

`drawn` became true only when the player walked to the AMMO POINT and pressed
its hotspot. A player who walked straight to lane six — which is the only
thing anyone wants to do on a range, and which the objective's own waypoint
pointed at the moment he had his kit — arrived at the firing point and
**nothing happened at all**: no load drill, no tower, no serial, no timer, and
an objective that never changed.

And the same flag governed the rifle: without it, nothing was in the hand
slot, so `weaponAvail()` was false, so `weaponFire()` returned at its first
gate. **"Nothing advances" and "shooting does not seem to work" are the same
line of code.**

Gone with the beat it belonged to (§2). In its place the firing point is a
**zone on the ground** — v8.7's bed-zone recipe, sodium rather than red — and
`ZONE_R` is BOTH the radius the rim is drawn at and the radius tested, so what
is shown and what fires cannot drift apart.

## 2 · The rifle never leaves his hands

Chad's ask is one deleted word. `weaponAvail()` in main.js reads:

```js
const id = weaponDecl.item; if (!id) return true;
```

so a weapon with no inventory id is ALWAYS available. Dropping `item: 'rifle'`
gives all three halves of what he asked for at once: the rifle is out on the
first frame of play, there is nothing in the bag to unequip, and there is no
path back to bare hands, because the weapon's own arms replace the hand and
the torch whenever it is up.

The TORCH keeps its `item` and is never issued, which is the opposite trick:
`torchAvail()` is then false, so there is no `hasTorch` on `<body>`, no torch
button, no F and no beam — while `kit.torchOn()` from a scene is ungated by
design (v11.6: "a film owns its light"), which is what scene B still needs
when he walks out with it.

The ammo-point draw beat, its hotspot, its two words and `drawKit()` are all
gone. No engine change was needed for any of it.

## 3 · "no proper recoil" — the Shoot take never once moved

Measured on the shipped `assets/rifle.glb`:

| take | keys run | first movement |
|---|---|---|
| Draw | 4.17 – 4.67 s | 4.20 |
| **Shoot** | **3.37 – 3.57 s** | **3.40** |
| Reload | 0.03 – 3.33 s | 0.07 |
| Hide | 3.60 – 4.13 s | 3.63 |

v12.0 found this for the Draw and answered it by setting the RATE for "the
half-second that moves" — but an action still has to TRAVERSE the dead 4.17 s
to get there, which at rate 2.6 is 1.6 s of frozen rifle. Shoot is worse: at
the shipped rate 3.2 the gun would first move **1.05 s** after the trigger,
and `fireGap` is **0.42 s**, so every shot reset the clip to zero and the
moving fifth of a second was never reached at any rate of fire.

Every weapon take now starts at its own first key, measured at load rather
than assumed. Shoot's 0.20 s of motion plays in 0.125 s.

And the CAMERA had no recoil either. v12.0's whole answer was:

```js
pitch.rotation.x = Math.min(pitchHi, pitch.rotation.x + weaponDecl.kick);   // kick 0.016
```

0.016 rad is 0.9°, which on a 72° lens is ten pixels of an 844-tall phone —
imperceptible as a kick — and it never came back, so twenty rounds walked the
aim 18° up the range. Both halves wrong. There is a real spring now
(`weaponRecoilFire` / `weaponRecoilStep`): an impulse of 0.032 rad (1.8°, 21 px
on the wide lens and 51 px on the aim) plus 0.010 of deterministic sideways
scatter, applied to the camera as a **delta** so the player's look still owns
the base, decaying on **wall time** so it settles in the same fifth of a second
on a hot phone as on a desktop. It returns in FULL: on targets this small a
permanent climb is a punishment, not a feel.

## 4 · "aiming is broken" — the arithmetic says it was

A Figure 11 board here is 1.05 m × 1.5 m. The banks are at 62, 98 and 132 m
(the range is compressed because the camera's far plane is 160 m; the tower
still says a hundred, two hundred, three hundred, because that is what a range
officer says). Through a **72° VERTICAL** lens on a 390 × 844 phone that is
11.7 px per degree, so:

| bank | angular size | pixels on a phone |
|---|---|---|
| 62 m | 0.97° × 1.39° | **11 × 16** |
| 98 m | 0.61° × 0.88° | 7 × 10 |
| 132 m | 0.46° × 0.65° | 5 × 8 |

…and there was **no reticle at all**, so nothing on the screen said where the
shot would go. Three things answer it:

- **`#reticle`** — four ticks and a dot, each with a dark outline so it reads
  on a white flare and on black tarmac alike. It flashes red on a hit, because
  otherwise there is no way to tell a hit from a miss until the board falls a
  quarter of a second later.
- **AIM mode** (`weapon.zoom`) — the lens narrows to 30°, which is 2.4× and
  makes the near boards 39 px tall. Right mouse, Q, or the new AIM button on a
  phone. The look's own sensitivity scales with the lens, or a narrow field is
  unusably twitchy.
- **Aim assist** (`weapon.assist`) — when the single centre ray misses, every
  listed target is measured for how far OFF-AXIS it is, less the angle it
  subtends, and the nearest inside a 1.7° cone counts. The standard console
  answer, and invisible: the player aims at the shape and the shape falls.

All three default to nothing in `WEAPON_DEFAULTS`, so the fixture and anything
shipped before this release are unchanged, and episode 1 declares no weapon at
all.

## 5 · "targets dont drop when shot" — two serials could not be finished

- **Serial 3** asked for **two** hits and the range has exactly **one** moving
  target, whose `hit` flag latches: `if (!t || t.down || t.hit) return;`. The
  second hit was unreachable by construction, so the serial could only ever
  end on its 30-second clock. The mover comes back up 2.4 s after it is hit
  now, which is what a real moving-target serial does anyway.
- **Serial 2** raised each pop-up ONCE for 3.4 s and then stopped for ever, so
  a serial needing three hits gave three three-second windows at 98 m and a
  single miss made the count unreachable. The sequence loops while the serial
  is live, each board comes back with its hit cleared, and the window is 4.6 s.

## 6 · Three clocks were on the clamped `dt`

`flareFrame`, `moverFrame` and `cycFrame` took the engine's `dt`, which is
clamped to 0.05 s — so below 20 frames a second the flare, the trolley and the
cyclist all ran at a fraction of speed, and it is worst exactly when the phone
is hottest (v9.3's clock-mismatch law, met for the fourth time). They take the
chapter's own wall delta now, as `dayClock` has since v7.1.

## 7 · The cyclist moves like a bicycle

`PASS_SPD` was `[1.35, 1.6, 1.9, 0]` m/s. 1.35 m/s is a **slow walk**, and the
model is a man on a bicycle, so it read as a statue being dragged sideways —
52 m at that speed is thirty-eight seconds for one pass. `[3.6, 4.2, 4.8, 0]`
puts a pass at eleven to fourteen seconds and still leaves the last one
standing still, which is the beat.

Its MESHES rather than its group now go into `shootables()`: the plain ray is
recursive and finds either, but the assist cone measures a bounding sphere and
a Group has no geometry to measure — so the one thing in the chapter the
player most needs to be able to hit would have been the one thing assist could
not help him hit.

## 8 · The men on the line are aiming

Chad: *"i thought i previously provided a static standing soldier who is in an
aiming pose with rifle pointing outwards? All the shooters should use this
model."*

He did, at v7.0. `docs/E2-SOLDIER-MODELS.md` §107 specified it as **`fboaim`**
and cast it at exactly this position — *"the only figures in the set that show
a shouldered weapon; on a firing line nobody moves, so a statue is not a
compromise, it is correct"* — and it was **never prepped**. So v12.1 dressed a
live range with `fbosling` and `admintee`: men standing about with their rifles
slung, which is the one thing a range never looks like.

`tools/prepaim.mjs` ships it: **33.9 MB → 1.84 MB, 47,318 triangles**,
photographed on its own before anything was measured (v6.17's law). Three
things decided the recipe:

1. **No rig, no clips.** Not one scene ever drove `buddy`, `safety` or
   `detail`, so the six of them have been standing still since v12.1 anyway —
   just in the wrong pose. Being static also makes them nearly free: one
   parse, seven clones that SHARE the geometry and the material.
2. **Its material is metalness 1 with a 4096 px metallic-roughness sheet.**
   Dropping that sheet without dropping the factor renders him NEAR BLACK
   (v8.9's law) — a black cut-out at night, which is the failure this chapter
   can least afford. `setMetallicFactor(0)` is not optional.
3. **He aims DIAGONALLY in his own frame** — measured, the muzzle runs out
   toward +x+z at 38°, so neither axis of the bounding box is the aim and
   neither is the body's depth. The tool measures the aim from the chest slab
   (muzzle 0.866 m out against the pack's 0.385 m back, two independent
   measures agreeing to 1.000) and bakes the turn, because a firing line where
   everyone points 38° off the range is worse than no statue at all.

The output contract, asserted on the baked vertices and BEFORE `quantize()`:
boots on y = 0, the origin under the **body** (never under the box — a rifle
held out in front pushes the bounding box a quarter of a metre downrange and a
line is spaced by shoulders), and the rifle aiming down **−z**.

One law paid for here, and it is why the assert re-measures instead of
trusting the maths: the turn was written the wrong way round first
(`atan2(x, -z)` instead of `π − atan2(x, z)`), which put him 104° off, and
nothing but the re-measurement would have caught it before a render.

Seven statues, one per lane but his own, with height, yaw and depth dealt from
the chapter's own stream so the row is not a photocopy. The safety officer
keeps a RIG: he is the only man behind the line and the only one not shooting,
so a slung rifle and a breathing idle are right on him and wrong on everybody
else. `admintee` leaves the chapter — **2.5 MB off the download**.

## 9 · "it is not obvious what the player should do"

- The firing point is a **glowing zone on the ground**, breathing, with three
  radar waves travelling out of it on wall time.
- **WAIT FOR THE ORDER is its own objective**, not a silence: the tower takes
  four seconds to give the order and every round inside those four seconds
  costs six sanity, so the screen says so — and it says it WITHOUT a
  completion banner (`{ complete: false }`), because a HUD that congratulates
  you for a beat you have not done yet is the v8.7 lie in its other direction.
  It is also the drill the whole chapter turns on: what the ghost exploits at
  the end is that you were taught to wait to be told.
- The reticle, the hit flash and the haptic tick on a scoring hit.
- The boards carry a faint self-light: the range between flares is very dark
  and an olive board at a hundred metres was a black rectangle on black
  tarmac. A real target area is lit; this is the cheap version of lit, and it
  costs no light and no shadow pass.

## What episode 1 sees

Nothing. It declares no weapon, so `weaponSetup` never runs; no torch, so the
torch changes cannot reach it; no objective and no events. The reticle and the
weapon HUD are shown under `body.weaponUp`, which only a chapter with a weapon
can set. Episode 1 is the control and is run as one.
