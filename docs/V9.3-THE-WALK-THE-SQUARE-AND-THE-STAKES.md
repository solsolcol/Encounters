## 1 · THE MINIGAMES HAVE STAKES

The old sequence test had **no timing check at all** — the whole press
handler was `case 'sequence': e.hits++; evNote(true); evNextItem(); break;`.
Any press, at any moment in the slot, scored. That is why it was too easy:
it was not a timing test, it was a counter.

Every press is now GRADED against the window, on one ladder shared by both
kinds (`EV_BANDS`):

| error, as a fraction of the zone | award | what it says |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 0.03 | +3 | PERFECT |
| ≤ 0.07 | +2 | EXCELLENT |
| ≤ 0.12 | +1 | GOOD TIMING |
| ≤ 0.20 | 0 | SLIGHT MISS |
| ≤ 0.34 | −2 | MISSED — SANITY LOST |
| beyond | −4 | TOO LATE — SANITY LOST |

A negative band is **paid immediately** — `kitAward(stat, delta)` on the
spot, a red wash over the screen, `hudfail`, a triple haptic — rather than
netted out at the end, because a penalty a player does not feel while
playing is not a penalty. Pressing early is BROKEN, not free; letting a slot
time out unpressed is BROKEN; a second tap on one heartbeat is a miss. The
final award pays only what the running total still owes (`e.paid`), so
nothing is charged twice.

Both of e2c1's tests were re-armed against the reference game's difficulty
tiers (docs/MZTRIAL-NOTES.md): the standby bed at `zone 0.70` (its PRACTICE
tier) accelerating 0.88 per item to a floor of 0.62 s, the 03:00 heartbeat
at `win 0.13 / zone 0.62`. The fixture declares the same shape, so
`fixturetest` proves the ladder rather than only the chapter that asked for
it — six new checks, including that an early press costs Sanity and that a
well-timed run outscores a badly-timed one.

## 2 · WHY A SLEEPER MEASURES PERFECT AND STILL LOOKS LIKE HE FLOATS

Chad has now said this twice, and v9.2 answered it by measuring the same
thing again. Measured on the shipped build, all eight sleepers have their
lowest skinned vertex at y **0.548–0.549** against a mattress top of
**0.550** — on it, to a millimetre, each on his own bed. Two releases of
re-measuring the same way is the tell that **the measure is the wrong one.**

What he is seeing is the absence of a **contact shadow**. Every rig in this
chapter is built `castShadow = !LOW`, and `LOW = IS_PHONE` — so on the only
device Chad plays on, nothing under a sleeping man is darkened at all, and a
body touching a surface with no shadow under it reads as hovering over it.

Two fixes, neither of which costs a light (v8.4–v8.6 spent three releases
getting this chapter's frame cost down):

- **`SLEEP_SINK` 0.035** — a body sinks into a mattress. Resting exactly on
  the undeformed surface is geometrically right and looks wrong; 3.5 cm is
  what a shoulder does to a bunk mattress, and it closes the hairline of
  daylight under him.
- **`sleepShade`** — a painted soft ellipse laid on the mattress under him,
  sized from his own body. A DRAWN shadow, so it is there on LOW exactly as
  on desktop.

The shade is registered on the bed's own `restOn` list rather than given a
height copied once, because Chad's bunk model arrives long after `build()`
and `settleBed` re-seats everything resting on that deck — the v9.2 trap,
one object later.

**And the first version of the shade was wrong in a way worth writing down.**
It measured the body with `Box3.setFromObject`, which on a SkinnedMesh
reports the **BIND pose** — and this rig's bind pose is a Mixamo T-pose
STANDING UP. Every rig's shadow came out sized for a standing man and placed
a metre off the bed. It is measured from the posed skin now
(`getVertexPosition`), which is the v5.21 / v8.4 law met a third time.

Verified on the shipped build: all eight bodies at 0.514 = deckTop 0.549 −
0.035, all eight shades at 0.553 = deckTop + 0.004, each within 0.18 m of
its own body.

## 3 · WHY A MARCHING MAN GLIDES

> why does the sergeant float and glide to the parade square instead of
> walking? It already has walking animation.

He does play it. The glide is a **CLOCK MISMATCH**, and it is invisible on a
fast machine:

```
marchTick(d)          runs on WALL time, capped at 0.5 s a frame
r.mixer.update(dt)    runs on the ENGINE's dt, CLAMPED to 0.05 s
                      (src/main.js: Math.min(clock.getDelta(), 0.05))
```

Above 20 fps the two agree and the legs keep up. Below it they diverge by
exactly `d/dt` — 1.3× at 15 fps, 2× at 10, 4× at 5 — so the body outruns the
stride by that factor and the man skates. **It is worst precisely when the
phone is hot**, which is the only condition Chad plays in.

The take's rate is now driven from the ground the body actually covered this
frame: the mixer advances the clip by `dt × ts` seconds, carrying
`natural × dt × ts` metres of stride, and we want that to equal `spd × d`.

`STRIDE` is measured, not guessed — each take ISOLATED (every other action
stopped: a clip blended under an idle reads HALF its stride, which cost one
wasted pass) and stepped by `action.time`, 48 poses, one foot's travel in the
group's own frame, doubled for the two steps in a cycle:

```
Walking   sergeant 1.437   encik 1.368   admintee 1.416   botak 1.333
Running   sergeant 2.678   encik 2.547   admintee 2.606   botak 2.455
```

One number per take (1.39 / 2.57): the 4 % spread between rigs is far under
what an eye can see, where the mismatch it replaces reached 400 %. Note that
`RUN_SPD` 2.6 already sat dead on the run take's own speed — which is why
only the WALKERS were ever reported as gliding.

**And they walk faster**: `WALK_SPD` 1.35 → **1.95**. The seniors' route to
the square is about 21 m; at 1.35 that is ~16 s plus their stagger, longer
than the fall-in's own 17 s timer, which is what "they take too much time"
names.

**One latent bug fell out of reading it.** The walk take is now STARTED in
`marchTick`, not merely rated: `marchTo` asks for it once, at step-off, and
`rig.play` is a no-op on a rig whose model has not landed yet — the loader
then puts that man on his IDLE, so anyone whose bytes arrived mid-march
glided the entire way with no walk at all. `rig.play` returns early when he
is already on the take, so this costs nothing per frame.

`stage.marchRate()` reports what was actually written, the v5.29
`seatStats()` / v9.2 `ambient()` move: a compensation nobody can read is a
compensation nobody can check. Measured on the shipped build, 57 writes
across one fall-in.

## 4 · THE SQUARE: THREE FLAGPOLES AND STREET LIGHTS

Three poles stand between the last bay divider (x 31) and the far block's
face (x 33) — where a camp actually puts them, and dead centre of what the
rank looks at: from the line at x 14 facing +x they are 12.7° off axis. The
centre pole is 9.2 m with the **Singapore flag**, drawn not downloaded
(CSP-safe, no bytes): 2:3, the top half red (#EE2536, Pantone 032C) over
white, with the white crescent and its ring of five stars in the hoist half
of the red band. The two flanking 7.6 m poles carry plain unit colours.
They are deliberately NOT anyone's real crest — a made-up insignia on a real
parade square is worse than none, and Chad can send the actual colours.
Every flag waves by moving its own vertices, never its matrix, so v8.6's
`freezeStatic` has nothing to say about it.

Six **street lights** in two rows down the square at z ±12, arms reaching in
over the tarmac. No real LIGHT is added; what makes them read is an emissive
head and a painted pool on the ground. **They are ON throughout**, and that
is not laziness: this chapter's declared daylight is EVENING (v7.5), so the
square is dark from the first frame of play to the last. Keyed to `nightK`
they were dark through the whole fall-in — the one beat they exist for. The
first pass' 1.9 emissive blew the head to flat white and is 1.45 now.

Nothing added here needs a blocker: the player's bounds are maxX 20,
z −3.7…7.2, so both the poles and the lamp rows stand where he can never
walk. That is what keeps `walktest` and the fall-in route exactly as they
were.

**And fifteen of the square's twenty-two trees were inside the building.**
Found while photographing the square for the flagpoles — two crowns poke
through the far block's face in the wide shot. The block is a solid box
spanning x 33…43, z −23…23, and the stand was laid out along "the far edge
of the square" before the block grew to that size, so two thirds of this
chapter's trees were parsed, built, instanced and drawn entirely within an
opaque box where nothing could ever see them. They line the SIDES now, just
outside the painted bays, with the rest tucked round the block's ends.

## 6 · ONE PROBE LAW, PAID FOR FOUR TIMES

Three consecutive probes reported that the stride compensation never ran —
`strideRate` called ZERO times in a march the sergeant completed. It was not
the game. **A beat fired before the chapter's first frame is re-applied as a
RESUME**: `updateDay`'s `if (!booted) { booted = true; applyPhase(...) }`
runs on frame one, sees the phase the probe just set, and re-enters it with
`snap = true` — which teleports everyone to the line instead of walking
them. A probe must let the chapter BOOT before it drives a phase. The same
run, with six seconds of play first, logged 57 rate writes.
