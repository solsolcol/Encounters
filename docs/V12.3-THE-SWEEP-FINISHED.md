Chad, on v12.2: *"Finish the sweep"* — and, the same morning, four notes
from playing it:

> The minigame seems broken and im not sure what its supposed to do. Also
> the first round of targets come back up after they are all shot down, this
> made it hard to shoot the targets of subsequent rounds. Also make all
> targets bigger, its quite hard to aim them.
>
> Also, when the ghost cyclist comes nearer and nearer, the model looks wrong.

Two jobs in one release: the verification pass that v12.2's audit never got
(it was stopped when it starved the test box), and his four.

---

## 1 · THE SWEEP, FINISHED

v12.2's audit raised 63 findings. Thirty-four were duplicates of defects that
release already fixed. The other **29 were never adversarially verified**, and
shipping a list of unverified claims as "findings" is worse than shipping
none — so every one was read against the code as it stands now, in six themed
groups, under one rule: *default to REFUTED, and quote the current line*.

The result: **13 CONFIRMED, 5 PARTIAL, 3 ALREADY-FIXED, 8 refuted or folded
into another.** Every CONFIRMED and PARTIAL one is fixed here.

What the numbers say about the audit itself is worth keeping: a raised
finding had about a 60 % chance of being real on the current code. An
unverified audit is a list of leads, not a list of bugs.

## 2 · THE ONE THAT HAD NEVER PLAYED

`c.dur` is the maximum `t1` of a cutscene's TRACKS (`playCineFn`), and a
sting is not a track. So a cue written after the last track is simply never
reached: the frame clamps `c.t` to `c.dur` and calls `cineEnd()`.

All four endings of this chapter cued **`n4dawn`** — the closing narration,
the sentence that loads chapter 5 — between 0.1 and 0.2 s past their last
step. It had never once played, in any ending, through two releases.

| scene | last track | cue was | cue is |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | `step(27.0)` | 27.2 | 26.6 |
| B | `step(22.8)` | 23.0 | 22.4 |
| C | `fade(…, 26.2)` | 26.4 | 25.8 |
| D | `step(19.8)` | 19.9 | 19.4 |

Each now starts under the fade to black and runs on under the card, which is
what the code's own comment always claimed it did.

**And the class is closed.** `chaptertest` walks every film and every scene
in the game against a recording stub — the real verbs from `A(c)`, and a
proxy for everything else a scene touches — and fails the build if any cue
is cued past its own cutscene's length. **49 cutscenes walked, 0 late.** It
costs no browser and no new harness (the rule: adding a chapter must not add
one), and it is the v9.2 law in its timing form: *a mistyped cue is silent
with no error, and that is not a bug a screenshot can catch.*

## 3 · CHAD'S FOUR

### The targets come back up

`beginSerial(n)` raised **every static** at the start of **every** serial:

```js
for (const t of statics) { t.hit = false; t.set(false); }   // false = UP
```

So the three boards at 62 m stood back up for serials two and three — the
biggest, nearest thing on the range, in front of the bank that is actually
live, and not shootable, because `shootables()` is scoped to the serial's own
bank. It also contradicted `endSerial`, which had just lowered them on
purpose at the cease-fire. A range runs one bank at a time; each serial now
raises its own and leaves the others flat.

### The targets are bigger, and by distance

A Figure 11 is 1.05 × 1.5 m. Through a **72° VERTICAL** lens on a 390 × 844
phone that is

| bank | distance | at rest | under AIM (30°) |
|---|---|---|---|
| statics | 62 m | 14 px tall | 34 px |
| pop-ups | 98 m | 9 px | 21 px |
| far | 132 m | 7 px | 16 px |

A thumb cannot aim at nine pixels. `BOARD_K` 1.8 scales every board **by its
own distance** (`d / 62`), not by a flat number — a flat multiplier would
leave the far bank exactly as unreadable *relative to* the near one. Every
bank now subtends the same angle: about 25 px at rest, 60 under AIM. It
costs nothing in believability, because there is not one object in the target
area to judge a size against, and the range is compressed already (the tower
still calls them a hundred, two hundred and three hundred metres).

### The minigame

Three causes, and the first is the whole answer.

1. **The bar was never on screen.** `evFrame`'s `case 'sequence'` has written
   `#evBar` since v9.3 — the filling window that is the *only* thing saying
   WHEN to press — and `#evTrack` was never taken off `hide`, which is
   `display:none !important`. The drill showed an item's name and a blank
   panel and asked the player to time a press against nothing.
2. **There was no target marked.** Even with the bar visible, "the middle" is
   not a thing a player can see. The track now draws a jade band and a centre
   line, and the band's half-width is written from the grade ladder
   (`--evwin`, set in `evShowItem`) — so what is drawn and what is scored
   cannot drift apart (v8.7's bed-zone rule, in a HUD).
3. **The windows were inside a touchscreen's latency.** A press grades on
   `|slotT − mid| / (span/2)`; at `each` 1.6 / `lead` 0.4 the half-span is
   0.60 s, so with `zone` 1 **PERFECT was 18 ms** and anything past **204 ms
   was BROKEN at −4 awareness**, against a phone's own 50–100 ms tap latency.
   That is v9.7's bug, met in a second kind. `zone` 2.5 now: PERFECT 45 ms,
   GREAT 105, GOOD 180, SLIGHT 300, BROKEN only past 510. `each` 2.0 gives
   the first item 1.6 s to read.

And the brief says what the player does, not only what a soldier does. Also:
the slot clock runs on the event's own WALL clock rather than the clamped
`dt`, so the bar does not crawl while the press is still graded against it.

## 4 · THE THIRTEEN CONFIRMED, AND THE FIVE PARTIAL

**Engine** (all unreachable from episode 1 by construction — it declares no
weapon, no torch, no hotspots and no events):

- `torch.player: false` — the chapter tried to keep the torch away from the
  player by declaring an inventory item it never issues. That held only for a
  player who arrived without one, and **the bag carries across a chapter**
  (`restart()` resets the run's numbers, never the inventory) while chapter 3
  *forces* the torch into the hand slot to be playable. So anyone reaching
  chapter 4 the way the episode is played arrived with it equipped and the
  whole hand swap live on a live range — exactly what Chad said must not
  happen. A chapter says it outright now.
- The weapon mixer and the muzzle flash run on **wall time**; a 2.3 s reload
  locked the hands for four or five real seconds at 10 fps, which is worst
  exactly when the phone is hot.
- `weaponAdsSync` runs on the frame, so AIM cannot survive into a card — and
  it hands back **only the lens it took**, so it can never fight `api.lens`.
- The recoil spring **settles before it is forgotten**: `weaponRecoilReset`
  zeroed `recPrev` and abandoned whatever offset the last frame had pushed
  into the camera.
- A **dwell** hotspot is answered by looking and by nothing else — it was
  also offered as a press, and on a phone the badge said "Tap".
- `markFar` — a hotspot may name its own marker range. The track spot rides
  the cyclist, which is never nearer than about twenty metres, and the
  marker walk clips at sixteen: the drill had no on-screen cue at any
  distance it is ever at.
- The sequence event's track is visible, its band is drawn, and its slot
  clock is wall time (§3).

**The chapter:**

- The **radio waits for the thing** — it was live 3.2 s before the cyclist
  existed, so the proper words could be said, and the award banked, about an
  empty arc.
- The **track cone opens per pass** (`PASS_AIM`): at 4.8 m/s a fixed 0.10 rad
  cone is 0.93 s against a 1.2 s dwell, so the third pass was unanswerable
  even with the sight held perfectly still.
- `reset()` clears the accumulated **dwell**, or a replay banked the tracking
  award for a look taken in the run before (the v8.1 law, a seventh time).
- The played moment **re-stamps its receipt**, so a Continue lands on the
  rounds actually spent and the pass actually reached.
- A hit taken **during the confusion** brings the cyclist back, as one in the
  moment does — it used to delete it for the rest of the beat.
- **Nothing happens between the bell and the card**: a round fired into that
  1.6 s spent a life and took the thing off the range, so the four options
  opened on empty tarmac under a presence banner still saying it was there.
- The **serial's clock is on screen** (`kit.timer`), and a cease-fire says
  "Cease fire. Stand fast." instead of sitting on a finished count.
- The **hit count cannot read 4/3**, and only the frame that makes the count
  schedules the cease-fire.
- The **flare thins the air it lights**: it declared fog 0.016 against the
  night's 0.012, so the FogExp2 fraction at the pop-up bank went 74.9 % →
  91.4 % and at the far bank 91.9 % → 98.8 % for exactly the seconds the
  boards are meant to be visible. 0.009 now.
- The **mechanic beds go quiet when play does**: `mixBeds` was only reached
  under `updateNotes`'s play guard, while the engine's ambient frame
  re-asserts every declared volume in every state in the world — so a trolley
  rail ground under the teaching card and a flare hissed over the rank.
- The **tower stops talking at the decision**, so a queued line cannot run
  into the cutscene's first.
- A save taken in the **0.4 s `decide` window** resumes into the decision
  rather than into a range with no card and nothing to do.
- An **aborted load drill** does not schedule the next run's first serial.
- **`e4wait` is queued, not dropped** — the reprimand for firing early was
  refused precisely when it was earned, because the tower was still giving
  the order.
- **`hudlock` is warmed**, so the arrival at the lane is not silent the one
  time it fires.
- The **spawn faces the firing point**: rot 0 faced −z and the lane zone lies
  50° to the right, outside a portrait phone's ~21° horizontal half-view. The
  glowing circle the first order names was not on screen at the first frame.
- The **presence banner observes** rather than orders: it came up in the stag,
  where the objective correctly says to wait and there is nothing to act on.
- The primitive **fallback's meshes** are shootable, so the aim assist can
  measure them when the model fails to download.

## 5 · THE CYCLIST UP CLOSE

Photographed from PLAY (v6.17's law) at 24, 12, 6 and 3 m, HUD hidden, with
the ghost stopped so `cycFrame` could not walk it out of shot.

At 24 m it is a faint figure. At 12 m it still reads. **At 6 m it is a
smear**: the torso has no form, the far wheel comes through the near one, the
inside of the frame shows through the rider's leg, and the helmet floats off
the shoulders. That is Chad's note exactly.

One property explains it. The ghost treatment sets `transparent = true` and
`depthWrite = false`, which is right for a ghost AGAINST THE WORLD — a
material with depthWrite off still depth-TESTS, so the berm or a tree in
front still occludes it and the alpha only finishes the job (the v9.7 note).
It is wrong for the object against ITSELF: this file is **one baked mesh**
(measured on the shipped build: `cyc.meshes.length === 1`, 29,707 triangles,
0 bones), so with nothing writing depth every triangle blends in buffer order
and the model's whole interior comes through its near side. The episode's
other three ghost spots are `fbosling` — many small meshes, mostly seen at
distance — which is why only this asset showed it.

**A depth pre-pass.** A colour-less copy of the geometry (`colorWrite: false,
depthWrite: true`) is added as a CHILD of each mesh, so it rides the same
world matrix with nothing to keep in step and goes invisible with the group.
It renders at `renderOrder` 2 — after every default-0 opaque mesh, so the
tarmac behind the ghost is already painted and cannot be punched out — and
lays the silhouette's depth down; the ghost itself (renderOrder 3) then
depth-TESTS against it, so only its nearest surface is drawn.

Re-photographed at the same four distances: at 6 m the rider is a solid
figure — head on the shoulders, the near arm and leg occluding the frame
behind them, one wheel in front of the other — and still translucent enough
to read as a ghost. 12 m and 24 m are unchanged.

## 6 · ONE HARNESS NOTE, AND IT IS NOT THIS RELEASE'S

`fixturetest`'s `evHeartbeatRewardsTiming` fails on this test box. It is the
comparison its own comment warns about — "this box steps its clock 0.05 s a
frame and a fixed window would be a coin toss" — and at roughly one frame a
second `untilT(0.5)` cannot observe 0.5 before the frame has already carried
the clock past the beat, so the "answered" run scores no better than the
never-pressed one.

It was CONFIRMED as not-this-release by stashing the whole working tree,
rebuilding at the shipped v12.2b code and running the same harness: **it fails
there too, reproducibly.** The check needs a slow-box-proof form (drive the
beat off `evNow()` rather than off a sampled `e.t`), which is harness work and
is written down here rather than done under a release.

The one thing this release DID break, `fixturetest` also caught, and the
finding is worth more than the fix: putting the sequence's slot clock on wall
time (see §3) made a 2 s item two frames long on a one-frame-a-second box. It
is reverted, and the reasoning is in the code — v9.7's law is about a beat
that must line up with a SOUND, and a sequence has none.

## 7 · WHAT THE SWEEP DID NOT FIND

Worth saying plainly: of the 29, **eight did not survive**. Three had already
been fixed at v12.2b and the finding was reading the old code; five were
mechanisms that are real but whose stated consequence could not happen. They
are not in this release and they should not be re-raised without new
evidence.
