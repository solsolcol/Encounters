## The office (14 / 9.4–19.4 s of the film)

- The model is turned a half turn and offset `(1.967, 0, 0.997)` so the
  dual-monitor desk the film is about stands where the drawn desk stood — the
  camera path and the stand-in agree, and the drawn office stays as the
  stand-in until the file lands (v4.7).
- **The code is on the model's own monitors.** The Computers mesh was split
  into connected pieces (`masters/v19.0/screens.mjs`): 24 panels, each
  measured for its centre, the way it faces (toward the nearest keyboard) and
  its width; his two panels carry the scrolling-code texture 18 mm in front
  of their faces.
- **Night, painted, not lit.** Every material becomes an unlit one whose
  colour is multiplied by three terms: a low blue ambient, the screens' pool
  round his desk, and one panel left on far off. A real light would change
  the light COUNT and rebuild every lit shader in the chapter (v15.1). The
  first numbers (ambient 0.085, a 3.6 m pool) read as DAY in the
  photograph; 0.045 and 2.5 m read as night.
- The city at night is round the whole floor now (a cylinder of the old
  painting), so every window has something in it.

## The camera business (19.4–33.6 s)

The warehouse is gone — its racks were the "gates" — and the shot is
Chad's: a light-oak table against a dark warm backdrop (a lit room was a
brown box that pulled the eye), the laptop at the back with the orders
chart on its own screen material, and nine of his cameras set out in two
rows, one slow pan from the left end of the rows onto the laptop's screen.
- The cameras' file metalness (0.49) with nothing to reflect rendered them
  bronze; with the room environment at 0.9 they rendered WHITE. Satin
  plastic: metalness 0.1, roughness 0.45, environment 0.22.
- The pan ends on the SCREEN, not the laptop's base centre: the lid stands
  0.12 m behind it, and on a phone the screen was half out of frame.
- The chart climbs on the film's own clock (`chart.at`), not a frame count,
  which crawled on a slow box.

## The plane (42.4–50.2 s)

- **The file is half a cabin**: one block of three seats and the wall beside
  it, open on the aisle side and at both ends — photographed, the sky stood
  in the cabin. It is mirrored about the aisle (the floor's open edge, x
  −1.113 in the file → a 0.76 m aisle) for the block across, and a ceiling
  with a light strip and two bulkheads (a curtain and an exit sign forward)
  close the box.
- Every seat number was RAYCAST through the file in node
  (`masters/v19.0/holes2.mjs`): the five windows (one per row), the cushion
  top, the backrest's face as it leans back, the seat back's top.
- **Nine passengers** (six on phones) from episode 1 chapter 3's tentage —
  `sitwoman`, `sitman`, `sitangry`, `sitshout`, `sitclap` — each parked on a
  quiet frame of its own take, seated by `fitSeat` on its POSED SKIN: the
  hips over the seat's middle, the lowest skin under the hips set 6 mm ON the
  cushion, the back brought to the backrest's face at each height.
  Photographed from the aisle and from the front: thighs on the cushions,
  feet on the floor, nobody sinking.
- Their culling spheres are built from the posed skin in world space, then
  in each mesh's own frame (v18.5's law): the woman in the aisle seat was not
  drawn at all, because a Meshy mesh hangs under a 0.01 armature and the
  default sphere was 1.9 cm.
- **The sky** is a whole sphere now (`makePlaneSky`): deep blue overhead
  coming down to ~8° above the horizon (a window sees about twenty degrees
  of sky, and the first painting's blue was all above that), a dawn band, a
  cloud sea with lit tops and lavender undersides, towers of cumulus kept
  out of the sun's column, and THE SUN PAINTED LAST — the first pass painted
  the clouds over it. A 520 px bloom whited the window out; 300 px does not.
- The drawn wing is retired: through Chad's window it read as a flat blade
  cutting the sky in two, and the ask was the sky.
- The camera opens from his seat looking down the cabin at the rows ahead,
  then turns to his window and pushes in, ending nearly level (−0.08) so the
  window is sky and sun rather than all cloud sea.

## The title card after a film (engine, every chapter)

`playChapterCard(then, afterFilm)` → `whenWorldReady(..., silent)`: the card
that follows an opening film says no "Loading…". The film's own curtain has
already waited for the world, so this second wait is a re-check that ends in
a moment and only ever flashed the word. A card with no film before it (a
resume, the fixture) still shows the word while it genuinely waits.
