## Standing rules that bind it

- The masters' photographs are used "cleanly, with no alterations ... in
  respect to these masters" (v21.8): the source's own bytes, never cropped,
  never stretched, never drawn over. LP Phiboon's file is the image Chad
  attached, byte for byte (sha1 3f6e2df4d5999d5c4b6a2bffe7f643652f3084b2,
  318 x 500 JPEG), credited to bankhundej.blogspot.com.
- In the gallery too: each image whole, at its own aspect; glow, rays and
  sparkles go AROUND and BEHIND a portrait, never across a face.
- A master's title is spoken in full (v21.8); on screen the printed form may
  stand. The stories are the sheet's words (the chapter's `words`), so Chad can
  edit them.
- A new engine seam is proved by the fixture first (`fixturetest`), and is
  unreachable from a chapter that does not call it.

## Design

**The wall (ask 1).** Six portraits in the one row (v21.9's `MSTR` band,
1.90–2.40 m, every frame the same 0.50 m tall), recentred on the shelf-shrine:
Luang Pu Thuad, Luang Phu Tim at prayer, Somdej Toh, Luang Phu Tim on his
bench, Ajahn Mun, LP Phiboon (the end nearest the door; the row keeps its
alternation of the three framed PNGs and the photographs). LP Phiboon's frame is
a FINE one, richer than the plain mouldings: a two-step gilt profile, a crimson
velvet liner between it and the photograph, gilt corner blocks. The
shelf-shrine's brackets stand clear of the frame tops (they were in the gaps
beside Somdej Toh, who is no longer the row's middle); the calendar moves back
along the wall so the row's far end has air; the row's tap box comes from its
measured extent.

**The gallery (ask 2) — the twenty-ninth seam, `kit.gallery(opts)`.** A window
over a dimmed world, a screen state of its own (`gallery`), so every "is it
play?" gate closes under it. One PAGE per master: the portrait on the LEFT in a
gilt frame (a PNG that carries its own frame is drawn as it is) on a breathing
halo with slow light rays behind it; on the RIGHT the name (and the Thai name),
LIVED (years, C.E. and B.E.), ERA, TEMPLE, KNOWN FOR (three lines), and the
story. Luang Phu Tim's page carries both his photographs (pips to switch, and a
slow cross-fade). Turning: the side chevrons, the thumbnail strip, a swipe, the
mouse wheel, the arrow keys; a page turns with a 3D sweep, a burst of gold
sparks round the frame and its own sound. A sparkle canvas (gold motes rising,
four-point glints round the frame) clipped so nothing crosses a portrait. The
footer: five round thumbnails (a gold tick on each one read), "n of 5 read",
and the primary button — NEXT until every page is read, then CONTINUE, which
wakes with a ring of light and its own chime. A page counts as read once it has
been on screen 1.2 s (wall time). Esc / the X close it at any time; a gallery
closed early resolves `{ seenAll: false }` and remembers nothing itself — the
chapter passes back what was read when it opens again.

**The gate.** The masters' spot opens the gallery and counts as looked at ONLY
when it resolves with every page read; until then the browse stays at n/4 and
the owner cannot call. Closed early, the spot stays live and reopens on the
first unread page. The objective cannot advance without it.

**Sounds** (e3c3's own, passed in `opts.sounds`, the engine's UI sounds the
fallback): open (a bell and a gold swell), flip (a page and a soft chime),
all-read (a resolved chime), close; and a soft bed while it is open, the
shop's radio and the mall ducked under it (the chapter's `mixBeds`).

**CP2 (research).** A background agent, 148 tool calls, English and Thai sources;
the record and every URL in docs/research/V21.10-MASTERS.md. It confirmed the
photograph IS Luang Pu Phiboon of Wat Phra Thaen, Ban Daeng, Udon Thani (it is on
bankhundej's biography post, captioned). The pages: name, second name, the Thai
name on a plaque under the portrait, LIVED (C.E. and B.E.), ERA (the reigns),
TEMPLE, three KNOWN FOR, and a three-paragraph story — legends said to be legends
(the sea made fresh, the cobra's crystal, Somdej Toh's royal father, Luang Pu
Phiboon's crocodiles and staff), traditional dates said to be traditional (Luang
Pu Thuad 1582–1682), and Luang Pu Phiboon's birth said to be unrecorded. Ajahn
Tim of Wat Chang Hai (the 1954 Luang Pu Thuad amulets) is named as a different
monk from Luang Phu Tim. 61 words in e3c3's `words` (galLabel, m<Name><Field>).
Put to Chad, not changed: his episode-1 item text for the Phiboon amulet ("born
during the reign of King Rama 4", "Second Batch") — see the research doc's end.
