## B · The lersi masks, a fifth thing to look at

**Is the line true of the shop?** Yes, measured: the lersi masks hang on their own
two shelves on the back wall at x −3.0, the lower shelf at 1.25 m and the spires at
2.65 m; the altar's row of Buddha statues stands on its cabinet from 0.94 to 1.76 m.
The masks are above the Buddhas, which is what he notices (a lersi is a hermit, and
on a Thai altar the Buddha stands highest) — so nothing in the shop moves; the line
is about the shop as it is.

**The design.**
- A look-spot like the other four (`pressAim`, v21.9: E, the badge, a click under
  pointer lock, or a tap on its mark or on the masks themselves; no timer): id
  `lkLersi`, key `lersi`, on the masks' middle, its mark above them; its tap box the
  masks and their shelves.
- His line `z3lersi` (Brian, eleven_v3, Chad's words exactly, one tag naming an
  emotion — `[puzzled]`): picked by measurement from four takes, his v16.0/v18.6
  chain, its measured length in SECS and the registry.
- The order counts five: `Look around the shop · {n}/5` (six, once §C joined it: `· {n}/6`).

**The gate, and why nothing can get ahead of the gallery.** The browse ends — the
owner's call, his order and his "!" — only when ALL FIVE (six, with §C) looks are spent
(`looked.size >= LOOK_N`, and `LOOK_N` is the number of keys in `LOOKS`). The masters
count as looked at ONLY when the gallery closes with every page read (v21.10), and
nothing closes the gallery early (v21.30). So whatever order the five are pressed in:
- the gallery must be read through, every page, whether it comes first or last;
- the owner's call is queued BEHIND the last look's line (masters' or lersi's), and his
  order and mark come only from the END of his call (v21.21);
- from the press of any look until its line has been said in full, and while the owner
  introduces the masters and while he answers, nothing in the browse can be pressed
  (`browseHeld`, v21.21).
The lersi spot goes into `LOOKS` and its hotspot carries the same gate as the others
(`phase === 'browse' && !browseHeld()`), so it adds one more required look and no new
path. Nothing about the gallery changes.

## C · The shelf of Buddhas, a sixth

**Which shelf, and is the line true of it?** Chad's display shelf (v21.8) stands three times on the staff's wall
behind the staff's counter, at x −4.33, z −0.95 / −2.25 / −3.55, facing into the shop: five rows of four images
each — photographed on its own: Buddhas (the gold one, the Emerald Buddha, the one under the naga, the bronze) stand on
EVERY row, among monks, hermits, a guardian and deities, and the top row carries a monk and a hermit beside two
Buddhas. In a Thai shrine the Buddha images stand highest, so his thought — "all the Buddha statues should be placed
at the top row instead... nevermind..." — is about the shelf as it is; nothing moves.

**The design.** A look-spot exactly like the others, `lkShelf`, key `shelf`, on the shelf by the door (the first of
the three, `SHELF_ZS[0]`): the staff man stands in front of the middle one and the counter's own mark sits in front of
the back one, so the front shelf is the one a mark can stand over without crowding either. Its anchor on the shelf's
upper rows (the counter hides the lower ones from the floor), its mark just over its top, its tap box the shelf. His
line `z3shelf` (Brian, eleven_v3, Chad's words — a comma after "Hm", a space after the first ellipsis and the capital
on "Buddha" for the read; not one word moved; one tag, `[thoughtful]`). The order counts six.

**The gate is unchanged** — the same reasoning as §B: `LOOK_N` is the number of keys in `LOOKS`, so the owner calls
only when all six are spent, the masters only when the gallery has been read through, and every look holds the
browse until its line is over.

**Saves.** The count rides the phase string (`browse:wall,tim,…`); `lersi` joins it.
A save from before v22.5 holds at most four looks: Continue lands in the browse with
the lersi masks and the shelf still to do (and the owner calls after them), however far
through the four it was. A save past the browse (the owner's order, or later) lands
where it always did.
