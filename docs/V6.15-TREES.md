## The four kinds out of three files

Chad's three Sketchfab models are ~11.8 MB of source. The low-poly file
carries TWO trees, so the game gets **four kinds** out of them:

| key | what it is | triangles | shipped | crown ÷ height |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tree1` | realistic tree | 12,799 | 586 KB | 1.03 |
| `tree2` | oak tree | 6,106 | 180 KB | 0.98 |
| `tree3` | trees low poly · tree4 | 3,519 | 338 KB | 0.56 |
| `tree4` | trees low poly · tree6 | 1,862 | 285 KB | 0.78 |

1.36 MB shipped for the set, and a chapter pays for it once however many
trees it plants — the kit is parsed once per session and every stand shares
the geometry.

`tools/preptree.mjs` is the recipe, v5.20's `prepwoman.mjs` plus four steps
that are about trees rather than people:

1. **`metalRough()`** — two of the three arrive as
   `KHR_materials_pbrSpecularGlossiness`, which three.js REMOVED from
   GLTFLoader: it renders flat white and says nothing. (v5.17's study table,
   the same trap, the third time it has been paid for.)
2. **Base colour only** — the CSP-safe `rescueTextures()` only ever restores
   base colour, so a normal or specular map is pure download.
3. **Leaves are CUTOUTS** — their sheets keep alpha (PNG 512), their material
   is MASK with a cutoff, never BLEND: blended foliage sorts wrongly against
   itself from every angle and costs fill for nothing. Bark is JPEG 512 and
   single-sided; leaf cards stay double-sided.
4. **Baked to a contract** — Y-up, trunk centred on the origin, base on
   y = 0, height exactly 1.0. A chapter then plants a tree by saying how tall
   it is, and the four kinds are interchangeable at any placement. The
   transform is baked into the vertex data BEFORE quantization, because a
   quantized attribute is an integer array no runtime matrix can be applied
   to.

## Why the first pass looked bad

Chad, mid-build, on the first shipped-looking render: **"why the trees look
so bad?"** He was right, and the cause is worth writing down.

Step 4 assumed the files were Z-up, because all three arrive Z-up out of
their FBX. But `flatten()` runs before it and bakes whatever corrective
rotation the Sketchfab wrapper carried — so some come out of it **already
Y-up**, and a blind Z-up remap tips those on their side and then normalises
by the wrong span. Measured on that pass: a tree asked to be 7.9 m tall
rendered a **19.2 m crown**, lying down, with the trunk sticking out
sideways.

So the up axis is **found, never assumed**. What tells the axes apart is the
TRUNK: along the up axis the far end is a crown and the near end is a stick.
For each of the three axes and each of the two directions, take the outermost
twelfth of the model and measure how far its vertices sit from that axis,
divided by the span; the smallest of the six is the base of the trunk, and up
is away from it. The prep tool prints the axis it chose and the crown ratio
it got, so a bad bake is visible in the console before anything is rendered.

After it, crowns come out at **0.56–1.03 × their own height**, which is what
a tree is.

The second half of "they look bad" was placement. The old spots were laid
out for small blobs — the East Coast memory's trees stood five to nine metres
in front of a lens looking at the water, and the playground's flanked the
tracking shot. A real crown as wide as it is tall closed the sky over both
shots. Pocket one is replanted along both BANKS and behind him, leaving the
window to the sea, the sun and the path open; the playground's two nearest
are pushed out.

## The kit (main.js)

One seam, `plantTrees(parent, spots, opts)`, added to `CHCTX`:

```js
treeStands.push(plantTrees(world, spots.map(([x, z, s]) => ({ x, z, h: 7.6 * s })),
  { seed: 11, tint: new THREE.Color(0.30, 0.34, 0.30), shadow: true, roughness: 0.95 }));
```

- **Parsed once.** `treeKit()` memoises one promise for the whole session; a
  stand planted in chapter 3 after chapter 1 has run costs no download and no
  parse. Each kind is kept as a list of `{ geo, mat, m, leaf }` — the mesh's
  own world matrix `m` is kept because a file may put its trunk and its
  crown in different nodes.
- **One InstancedMesh per (kind, part).** The instance matrix is
  `M · part.m`, so a part keeps its place inside its own tree.
  `frustumCulled = false`, because a tree's own bounding box is one metre
  tall until the instance matrix scales it.
- **Dealt from a seed.** Kind, turn (a full 2π), lean (±0.03 rad on two
  axes) and height (×0.88–1.18) come from one deterministic stream per spot,
  `sin`-hashed on the spot's index and the stand's `seed`. The same seed
  always deals the same forest, so a render is reproducible and a harness is
  not flaky — but the four stands use four different seeds, so no two places
  in the game get the same arrangement.
- **Tinted per chapter's light.** `tint` multiplies the model's own colour:
  the void deck at midnight takes (0.30, 0.34, 0.30), the East Coast evening
  (1.30, 1.16, 0.94), the playground by day (1.22, 1.26, 1.10), the car park
  at ten in the morning (0.94, 0.98, 0.90). `fog: false` takes a stand out of
  the world's fog, which is what chapter 1's memory bubbles need — they stand
  forty metres out in a midnight fog they are not in.
- **Disposed carefully.** `group.userData.disposeTrees()` disposes the CLONED
  materials only and clears the group: every geometry and every map belongs
  to the shared kit and must survive the chapter. A chapter calls it BEFORE
  its own geometry/material sweep, or the sweep walks into the kit and takes
  the textures out from under the next chapter.

## Verified

- `chapter`, `leak`, `text`, `cine`, `csp`, `hosted` green. `leaktest`
  matters most here: it builds every registered chapter and disposes it in a
  loop, and the shared kit is exactly the kind of thing that leaks — 0
  growth per cycle.
- `csptest` matters second: the kit loads through `assetBytes` +
  `GLTFLoader.parse` + `rescueTextures`, no blob, no data URI.
- Rendered in all four places: the void deck at night, the East Coast memory
  at evening, the playground memory by day, chapter 3's car park at ten in
  the morning. 165k instanced triangles across chapter 1's 27 trees; 30
  instanced parts in chapter 3.
- Deployed and byte-verified, 65/65 (61 files plus the four new assets).
