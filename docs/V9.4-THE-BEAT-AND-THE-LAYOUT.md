## 3 · THE HEARTBEAT IS A MUSIC GAME

### The schedule, laid out once

It used to be `lead + beat * period` — one spacing for the whole test,
computed on the fly. "It should get faster and faster per beat" cannot be said
that way, so `evBeatTimes()` lays the times out **up front** and everything
downstream reads the table: the press grades against `beats[beat]`, the frame
draws the ring contracting onto it, and a slow frame cannot drift the schedule
out from under the player.

Chapter 1 of episode 2 declares `n: 8, bpm: 68, accel: 0.90, minPeriod: 0.42`.
Measured on the shipped build:

```
beats  1.600 2.394 3.109 3.752 4.331 4.852 5.321 5.743
gaps    0.794 0.715 0.643 0.579 0.521 0.469 0.422
```

The last beats land a little over twice as fast as the first, and the floor
stops it outrunning a thumb.

### Undershoot and overshoot grade the same

A press is graded on `|t − beat| / win` — the distance from the beat's centre
as a fraction of the window — so being early by 90 ms and late by 90 ms are
the same band. That is Chad's "if the player undershoots, or overshoots the
circle, it should show missed."

### The answer the thumb gets

- **`#evBurst`** — a ring of light thrown outward on the frame the press
  lands: jade for a hit, white-hot for a PERFECT, red and shaken for a miss.
- **`#evCombo`** — the run beside it, going sodium and breathing at 4×,
  because a music game's reward is the run and not the single tap.
- **Haptics keyed to the band**: a PERFECT is a double tick under the thumb
  (`[18, 40, 18]`), a great one is a single firm 38 ms, a good one 24, a miss
  a hard triple `[34, 28, 34]`. The hand can tell them apart without reading
  the screen, which is what "haptic feedback when the player accurately taps
  at the right time" actually means on a phone.
- **Three new sounds** (§5): `beatperfect`, `beathit`, `beatmiss`.
- The pulse ring goes jade the moment it is inside the window (`#evPulse.near`),
  so the eye has the target as well as the ear.

### Five sanity a miss, and never refunded

`missCost` is a FLAT price per miss, which the ladder cannot express — its two
failing bands are −2 and −4. When a chapter names one, every missed beat costs
exactly that, whether it was a near miss or a beat never answered at all, and
it is taken **on the frame it happens**.

**And the two halves then stop being one sum.** `evResolve` used to pay
`sum * per − paid` — what the ladder says, less what was already taken. With a
flat cost of 5 against a band of −4, `paid` overshoots `sum` and that
expression comes out POSITIVE: eight missed beats would have *handed back*
8 sanity for failing. So when a flat cost is named, the misses are settled and
the end pays only what the HITS earned (`e.pos`), capped by `award.hi`.

Measured on the shipped build, eight beats never answered:
`band [-4 × 8], sum -32, delta 0`, and sanity 100 → **60**. Worst case is
−40, which is a faint, and that is the stake.

`fixturetest` asserts both halves — the exact −10 for two missed beats, and
that the end award is never negative-of-a-refund — so the arithmetic cannot
quietly come back.

## 4 · THE STANDBY BED IS A LAYOUT

A new event kind, **`match`**: items on the left, slots on the right, dragged
one onto the other.

- **The columns are shuffled independently**, so the two orders never line up
  and the player has to read what he is holding.
- **A right drop** locks the tile into the slot with `matchok`, a green wash,
  a 32 ms buzz and a jade burst.
- **A wrong drop** shakes the slot red, fires `matchbad`, takes
  `wrongCost` 4 awareness **on the spot** with the red wash and a triple
  buzz, and sends the tile back. Same immediacy as the graded ladder.
- **The score is TIME.** `fast` 11 s is full marks and `slow` 30 s is none;
  `secs` 40 is the clock and running it out pays the award's floor (−6).
  Awareness runs −6 … +12.

Four items, not eight, because a drag needs a target big enough for a thumb
and four rows fit a phone twice over. They are Chad's four photographs — a
green army towel, black tactical boots, an olive field dressing, a coil of
green cord — and **the photographs themselves could not be reached from this
session** (not on the container's disk, not in Drive), so each is DRAWN in
canvas at 96 px from the picture: no download, CSP-safe, and legible at the
44 px a tile shows. Swapping a real image in later is one `icon:` line each —
`evIconNode` already takes an asset key.

An icon is a **function** returning a node, because both columns draw every
item and one canvas cannot be in two places at once.

### The engine seams this cost

None that a chapter can see beyond the new kind. Inside:

- `evBuildMatch` / `evMatchDrop` / `evMatchHover` / `evDragTo` / `evSlotAt`.
- The drop is resolved on RELEASE, and `evRelease()` receives no coordinates —
  so `evMove` keeps `lastX/lastY` and `evPress` seeds them, for a tap that
  never moved.
- **Pointer lock is released while a match runs.** Under lock a mouse's
  `clientX/clientY` freeze, so the tiles could never be picked up; the next
  click on the canvas after the event re-locks through the path that has
  always done so.
- `__enc.evDrop(tileId, slotId)` and `__enc.evState()` for probes and
  harnesses, so the seam is checkable without synthesising pointer events.

**One trap, paid for once.** `#evMatch` ships with `class="hide"`, and `.hide`
is `display:none !important` — which beats `#event.match #evMatch{display:flex}`
outright. The columns built correctly, the slots measured **0 × 0**, and every
drop silently missed because `elementFromPoint` at a zero-size rect returns
the overlay. The class has to come OFF in `evBegin`; a kind's own CSS cannot
lift an `!important` on its own.

## 5 · THE SIX SOUNDS

ElevenLabs flow `8XvYllS6U1pAsL5Yl3cE`, `eleven_text_to_sound_v2`, two takes of
each, picked by MEASUREMENT — there are no ears in a session. For a rhythm
game the measure that matters is **decay, not character**: at the end of the
accelerating ladder the beats land 0.42 s apart, so a cue still ringing when
the next beat arrives turns the test to mush.

| cue | take | why |
|---|---|---|
| `beathit` | `_b` | `_a` is SILENT (peak −61.4 dBFS). `_b` is a TRAIN of ticks, not one; its loudest falls −49 dB within 80 ms — the single dry tick asked for |
| `beatperfect` | `_b` | `_a` does not decay at all (−3 dB at 0.4 s, a sustained shimmer); `_b` is −13 by 0.35 s |
| `beatmiss` | `_b` | decays faster than `_a` (−16 dB at 0.25 s against −13) |
| `matchok` | `_b` | both latch in 0.37 s; `_b`'s transient is 1.2 dB hotter |
| `matchbad` | `_a` | BOTH takes are a flat sustained buzz with no decay, so both had to be cut; `_a` is marginally less flat |
| `matchdone` | `_b` | `_a`'s chime is still at −9 dB after a second; `_b` resolves to −23 — a flourish that ENDS |

**Every cue is cut from 20 ms before its own peak**, so the transient is the
first thing the ear gets: a UI sound whose attack is 100 ms in reads as late
however short the file is. Then peak-matched to **−2.0 dBFS**, where v8.8 put
the four HUD cues these play beside (hudok −1.4, hudlock −1.6, hudnext −2.0,
hudfail −2.4). Both encodings, `-map_metadata -1`. 55 KB of mp3, 44 KB of
opus, all six.

`masters/v9.4/make.sh` is the recipe and `masters/v9.4/urls.txt` the sources.

**They are warmed when a chapter asks for an event, not at boot.** `snd()`
returns null for a sample that has not decoded (the v8.0 law), so the first
press of the first beat would have been silent; but chapters 1–5 declare no
events at all and must not pay the decode. `EV_SOUNDS` goes into `WARM_WANT`
and through `packWarm` inside `kitEvent()` — a test's own briefing, or its
`lead`, is more than enough time.
