## 1. The openings, the slits and the "missing colour" were ONE cause

The v16.8 bake turned some of the model's faces INWARD. The temple was drawn
one-sided, so every inward face was culled — and what Chad saw through the
hole was the hall behind it: the "empty space openings", the vertical slits
down the pillars, the see-through waist of the base, the black triangle over
the front door, and most of the "spots missing the red gold colouring" (a
culled face shows whatever lies behind it, usually limewash or sky).

Every temple material is drawn TWO-SIDED now, and the shader turns the normal
to face the viewer (`gl_FrontFacing ? vWN : -vWN`), so a reversed face is lit
exactly as the right way round would be.

That exposed the second half: the bake had written each sloped face of the
roof, the gables and the capitals TWICE (once facing up, once down). One-sided,
one copy was always culled; two-sided, the two coincident copies fight for
every pixel. `relabel.mjs` now keeps the first copy of each set of three
corners — **6,817 duplicate faces dropped** (473,896 → 467,079 triangles,
2.86 MB; still every unique triangle of the model).

## 2. The colours

- **The pillars**: first red all over at his word, then — on seeing it —
  WHITE ("now it looks like a chinese temple"). White limewash with gold only:
  the gilt lotus capital, a gold ring, a band of gold stencil on the white, a
  gold rule, a gold line over the gilt foot. No red on a pillar. (The
  "spots missing colour" on them were the inward faces of §1, not paint.)
- **The front door's surround is gilt**: it is welded into the WALL piece in
  the source (the back door's is a piece of its own, which is why only the
  back was gold). Its triangles are measured — z −22.3…−19.3, x −6.7…7.5,
  y 33.3…58.15 in model units — and moved to the `frame` part, which now
  gilds a door surround all over (gold on the faces, a red reveal in the
  hollows by the baked occlusion). The windows keep their gold/red/gold
  frames.
- **The facade over the door** (the wall's upper face at each end, above
  y 50.5) carries the red-and-gold stencil, so the front reads as one gilded
  front, not a white panel under a red gable.
- **The mosaic** is one green glass in 0.22-unit cells: the gold and blue
  chips at the old cell size were the "jagged lines".

## 3. The plants

The bougainvillea pots and the bodhi's small vase are gone; the pack's
**lotus bowl** stands in their places (six round the court, one small one at
the bodhi). Every other plant on the grounds is the pack's shortest bush, the
1.0 m **hedge**, in one size: six along the south wall, five along the west,
three by the east path — no more mixture of kinds.

## 4. The thrones (monk and Ajarn)

Measured (dbg-seat.mjs, every take, nine frames each) before anything moved:

- **The seat was measured on the robe.** v16.8 put the seat's top at the
  LOWEST skin in a box round the hips — and on a man in a robe, that is the
  cloth hanging between his knees, 15 cm under where he sits (monk: drape
  3.52, seat 3.67; Ajarn: drape 3.52–3.64, seat 3.64–3.72). So the seat
  stood a hand's width under him and he hovered over it.
- **The footrest's gilt lip** stood 2 cm over the height his soles were put
  at (soles 3.235, lip 3.256), and was only as deep as a stand-in's feet, so
  his toes went over its edge into the dais.

Now (`seatOnSkin(r, seat, takes, restore)`): every take he sits in is
measured on his posed skin at five frames; his soles go on the footrest's
gilt top (`AJ_RISE` 0.36 → 0.38); the cushion's top is the middle of the
takes' SEATS (the skin under and just behind the hip joint — the cushion
takes the few centimetres between takes, as a cushion does); the seat runs
from just behind his back to a hand short of his knees, with a gilt lip; the
footrest runs from behind the seat to 10 cm past his toes and as wide as his
feet; and a red-and-gold BACK stands just clear of his back. Built round the
man, not a box he is put on.

## 5. The rod, the whisk, the masks

- **The rod** was hung on the HAND bone, which on a Mixamo rig is the
  wrist. It is gripped 60 % of the way from the wrist to the middle fingertip
  (`handGrip`), its butt 6 cm out of the back of the grip.
- **The whisk** hung off the same wrist bone pointing at the lens — a bundle
  floating beside a hand that was not holding it. It is gripped in his fist,
  the handle across the line of the fingers and the stalks standing up out of
  it as a brush is held, tipping toward the one blessed on each throw.
- **The masks**: the two tiers OVERLAPPED — the upper box stood over all but
  the front 6 cm of the lower one's top, so the front row stood half inside
  the upper tier's face — and every mask stood 2 cm into its gilt lip. A real
  step now (lower 0.70 deep, upper 0.30 on its back half), each mask on its
  lip and inside its own step.

## 8. v17.2 — the whisk under the hand, and the monk who slid when he spoke

Chad, on v17.1: *"The stick should be below his palm, not on top of it"*, and
*"When the monk speaks, he moves, and causes his legs to collide into the
seat."*

- **The whisk** lies under his open hand now (3.5 cm below the hand bone,
  stalks running out past the fingertips with almost no rise, tipping up only
  on a throw of water).
- **The slide** was in the animation files, measured in the throne's own
  frame: `Sitting_Answering_Questions` sits his hips **0.30 m further back
  and 0.14 m to one side** of where `Sit_Thumbs_Up_Right` sits them — each
  take was recorded in its own chair — and the Ajarn's `Chair_Sit_Idle_M` is
  0.31 back and 0.17 the other way. So every change of take slid the whole man
  across the throne and his legs through the seat. `seatOnSkin` now records
  each take's offset from the take the seat is built round, and `rootComp()`
  cancels it every frame, weighted by the takes playing (a take it did not
  measure counts as no offset, so the walk out is unchanged). Measured after:
  the monk's hips move 1.5 cm between rest and talk (were 33 cm), the
  Ajarn's 2.3 cm across all three takes; photographed talking from the front
  and the side, seated on the cushion with his legs clear of the seat.

## 9. v17.3 — the dog, the shutters, the front wall, and no green

Chad, on v17.2, four notes: *"Remove this dog"*; *"There is some overpainting
here that extends out from the window frame"*; *"Remove the green, there is
no green at thai temples"*; and, with purple marks on the front, *"the red
gold pattern is a little overdone ... remove the red gold pattern from that
area and keep that area white. Thats the inner wall below the roof"* —
confirmed with him before it was done.

- **The dog** is gone, with its hidden blocker and its breathing.
- **The "overpainting" was the open SHUTTERS.** Each side window's leaves
  stand out from the frame within the same depth of the wall the reveal paint
  keys on (`wt < 0.83`), so they were painted as reveals — lacquer with a gold
  lip, and at their depth almost all lip: flat gold wedges beside every
  window. Found by painting each branch of the wall shader a debug colour.
  The reveal paint now also requires the point to be INSIDE an opening
  (`winSD < 0.25`), so the leaves take the wall's limewash.
- **The front wall** behind the porch is limewash from the white lower wall
  up to the beams. The wall above y 50.5 is stencilled at the BACK end only
  now. The scalloped red-and-gold valances hanging over it (the `screen`
  part) keep their pattern, and so does the gable above the top beam. His
  marks lay under the valances' edges, on the wall itself.
- **No green**: the bargeboards' inner mosaic is gold glass; the roof's eave
  rows are a deep red glaze; the bell tower's and the ordination hall's
  second tiers are deep red tiles; the bodhi's green cloth band is saffron.
  Chad's naga keeps its file. A shader turns any texel whose green stands
  over its red and blue to a deep gold of the same brightness (`ungreen`), so
  its own gold trim still reads brighter. The stand-in naga's mosaic and hood
  are gold too. Left as they are: the trees, the plants and the green
  Thao Wessuwan (Chad's own model, and his colour by tradition).

## 11. v17.5 — the ordination hall's roof sits on its walls

Chad, with a phone screenshot from beside the temple's base: *"Look at the
roof of the other building, fix it."* The hall's lower roof started at the
wall top's height out at the EAVE, 1.2 m beyond the wall, so where the slope

And each slope was a zero-thickness plane: from his spot the eye lay almost
in the plane of the lower slope, so it showed as a thin red line and the roof
seemed gone.

Now the lower tier's eave is SOLVED so its slope meets the wall top (eave
5.21, the slope 39.6°). Each slope is a slab: tiles on top, a dark wooden
underside, gilt edges. The upper tier rests 0.6 m above the lower one over a
short white band, the way a stacked Lanna roof does. The gables, the gilt
bargeboards and the naga blades are computed from the same eave and ridge, so
they moved with it. Photographed from his angle, from under the eave, from the
gate and from the temple's base.
