This file is the build's memory: what the crack lines actually were (not
aliasing — antialiasing was already on), what did NOT fix them and why,
what did, and the layout that replaced the five boxes.

## 1. The crack lines

**Diagnosis, in the order it was measured.** Three renders of the same
face, same lights, at 900 px:

1. The shipped 40k-triangle model, the ORIGINAL 990k-triangle upload, and
   a re-simplification with UV borders locked all showed the same light
   lines in the same places. So not the simplifier, and not the triangle
   count.
2. The same model with its texture REMOVED (a plain grey material) showed
   no lines at all — only the facets of a 40k mesh. So not shading, not
   normals, not aliasing: **the lines are painted in the texture.**
3. The atlas itself, cropped at the face: a photogrammetry scan's atlas of
   several hundred small islands over a cream fill, and every island's rim
   is a few texels of LIGHTER blur where the baker sampled that fill.
   Every seam on the face and hair draws that rim from both sides. On the
   robe the rim is cream on cream and invisible, which is why Chad saw it
   only "in the hair and face".

**What did not work, and why it is written down:**

- *Padding the islands outward* (repainting the fill from each island's
  edge colour) — the standard fix for seam bleed. No change: the light
  rim is INSIDE the island, not in the fill.
- *Blanket erosion* of 2, 4, 6 texels then padding. 2 was too little; 4
  and 6 ate the thin islands whole (an eye strip, hair wisps) and refilled
  them from whatever lay nearest — white patches on the hair — and turned
  the seams into hard tone steps.
- *Feathering colour across every seam* (the mesh knows which UV edge is
  which other UV edge: same two 3D points). Made it worse: a UV seam is
  often also a CONTENT edge — hairline, eyelid, lip — and averaging black
  hair with skin drew grey bands.

**What shipped** (`prepzav3.mjs`, three passes over the 1024 atlas, using
the FULL-resolution mesh's UVs for a coverage mask):

1. **Halo strip.** A rim texel (within 6 of an island border) is dropped
   only when it is lighter than the island's own interior 8 texels in, by
   more than 4/255. 14,905 texels of 366,898 rim texels went. Thin
   islands have no interior to compare against and are left alone.
2. **Padding** repaints what went, and the fill, from the interior — 24
   passes.
3. **Restricted seam feather.** Every seam edge pair (36,199 of 1.48 M
   edges) is walked; each side is blended toward the pair's average over
   4 texels, sampled 3 texels in — but ONLY where the two sides differ by
   less than 30/255. An exposure step is feathered; a hairline stays a
   hairline.

Then the v5.08 pipeline unchanged: no normal/ORM maps, 0.04 simplify,
JPEG q86 at 1024. (Superseded one release later: v5.10 keeps these
texture passes — with the halo strip corrected to a repaint — but ships
30 % of the ORIGINAL's triangles, meshopt-packed: docs/V5.10-ZAV-FULL.md.) The file: 1,232,860 bytes (was 1,266,300). The seams
are faint tonal steps now rather than light lines. They are not zero —
the scan's islands were lit differently when they were photographed, and
no per-texel filter can relight a face. If Chad wants them gone entirely
the honest route is a retexture of the head in a paint tool, not code.

## 2. Four boxes, and where the names went

`GEAR_SLOTS` is `head · neck · body · hand`. The two hand slots became
one: whatever he holds — the beads or the phone — goes in it. `ITEM_DEFS`
points both at `'hand'`. The Light box is gone (Chad: "its not needed
anymore").

**Saves from before wore two hands and carried three rows**, and nothing
an old save held is lost: `applyState()` folds `rightHand`/`leftHand`
into `hand` (the first that held something takes it; the other goes to
the bag), and a fifteen-slot bag's tail beyond slot 10 is moved into the
first free slots. `statetest` has the case (`oldSaveFolded`). The v:1
legacy seed in the same harness still uses the old two-hand shape on
purpose — it is the guard that old saves keep loading.

**The name sits UNDER the box now** (`.gslot` wraps `.slot` + `.cap`),
not inside it over the icon. The boxes are 74 px (56 before), the ghost
icons 56 % and items 66 % of that, the captions 0.62 rem mono. The bag is
ten slots, two rows of five. The keyboard walks the worn boxes as two
columns of two (left/right hop ±2, up/down ±1).

**The figure cell is deliberately narrow — 176 px.** The boxes were far
from him because the canvas was wide: at a 30° lens the canvas height
sets what is seen vertically (1.93 m at the figure) and the width follows
the aspect, so a wide cell was mostly air either side of a 0.6 m man. At
176 × 300 the canvas sees about 1.1 m across, a shoulder's width of air
each side, and the columns sit against that. The whole three-column grid
is centred as one piece in the panel (`justify-content: center`). Phone:
150 px cell, 64 px boxes, both bag rows fit a 664 px screen.

## 3. The polish

- **A lit room, not three lamps.** The figure scene now carries the same
  procedural `RoomEnvironment` the game's metal reflects, at intensity
  0.45 (the game uses 0.05, just enough to keep metal from going black).
  A scanned man under three directional lights is flat; with soft light
  on every side of him he reads as a person in a room.
- **A plinth.** A jade pool of light under his feet (a radial-gradient
  canvas texture on a disc, additive-looking, `depthWrite` off) and a
  thin jade ring that turns AGAINST his spin at half speed. The
  counter-rotation is the whole trick: two things turning opposite ways
  read as a display turntable, one thing turning reads as a bug.
- **The box lights up when an item lands** (`.slot.flash`, one 0.6 s
  ring animation): `inv.flash` is set by `invDropAt` and the quick-move,
  painted once as a class, then cleared, so the animation runs exactly
  once per landing and never on an unrelated repaint.
- The worn boxes got a faint radial gradient and a larger jade glow on
  a worn item; the drag ghost grew to match the boxes.

Nothing in the v3.1 interaction model moved: drag, tap-tap, double-tap,
keyboard, the swap-never-destroy rule.
