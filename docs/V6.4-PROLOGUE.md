# v6.4 — THE PROLOGUE: "the things I picked up"

Chapter 1's opening film. It plays on New game (and on every other path
that enters chapter 1 from its beginning: a `?ch=ch1` link, the selector,
Continue into a run that has not yet entered it), BEFORE the chapter card,
exactly as chapters 2–5's films do — through `intro(c, s, api)`, the entry
point v4.0 added and chapter 1 alone never used.

## The idea, refined

Chad's structure is right and is kept whole: three things picked up, then
the one that was different, then the note goes up into the sky. What the
refinement adds is a RULE that makes it a film rather than a montage:

**He looks down at the world, and the world has never once looked back.
Until it does.** For fifty seconds his eyes are on the ground — the
camera lives at ankle height, at the level of the things he finds, and his
FACE IS NEVER SHOWN: a hand comes down into frame, a pair of shoes arrives,
a small silhouette walks out of a pool of light. The first time the film
shows his face is the last beat, when a hell note drifts past it in slow
motion and, for the first time in his life, something he did not pick up
comes to him. The withheld face is the whole cinematic move; everything
else serves it.

Three more things the refinement decides:

1. **The memories are POCKETS OF LIGHT in the dark** — chapter 4's flashback
   recipe (black-box bubbles forty metres off the playable world), because
   the boy's memories should not be sets, they should be islands: a patch
   of grass, a stairwell corner, a stretch of pavement, each lit its own
   colour and nothing beyond its edge. And the colours make an arc:
   afternoon GOLD (the leaf) → fluorescent GREEN-WHITE (the toy) → sodium
   ORANGE (the money) → and then the real night, BLUE-BLACK with fire (the
   note). Warm to cold; day to midnight; found to given.
2. **The present tense is chapter 1's own world**, at the exact spot the
   player will be standing when play begins. The film's last ground shot is
   the block at night from the grass, notes drifting; the close-up stands
   him at the spawn point; the note rises from there. The card comes up,
   and the night the player walks into is the night they just watched.
3. **The note that passes his face is HER note.** The chord that plays as
   it crosses him is `strings` — the dread chord chapter 1 uses when she
   first appears (scene A) — so the film plants her leitmotif on the note
   itself, and a player who reaches scene A has heard it once before
   without knowing what it meant. She is not shown. Chapter 1's terror is
   DISTANCE (CLAUDE.md); the prologue keeps her at infinite distance.

## Camera language

- Shots 1a, 2a-foreground, 3a: the LENS AT THE THING'S HEIGHT. Ankle-level,
  the found object large in the foreground, the boy arriving or standing
  small. The audience is on the ground with what he finds.
- Shot 1b: the only push-in on him, and it goes to his HAND, not his face.
- Shot 2b: the one high angle — a crane down from god's-eye to shoulder
  height in one continuous move, the film's most elaborate camera; it is
  spent on the line about nobody watching.
- Shot 3a: the one lateral track, kerb-height, a classic street glide; the
  reveal of the money is the camera finding it, not a cut.
- Shot 3b: a HOLD. He leaves the frame; the frame stays. The first stillness.
- Shot 4: the only wide, and the first time the player sees the block.
- Shot 5: the only close-up of a face in five chapters of first-person, and
  an ORBIT — the camera position on an arc about his head, yaw and pitch
  aimed at him each frame, so the note passes between lens and eyes.
- Shot 6: the crane-and-track up to the moon; the camera rises 1.6 → 3.2 m
  while it follows, so the note's climb reads on the block behind it.
- Roll (`camera.rotation.z`) is used twice, both small: a 2° dutch on shot
  2a's stairwell, and a 3° lean that straightens through shot 5's arc.

### Where the film differs from the plan, and why

- **His face is not "never shown" — it is turned down, or above the
  frame.** The rule that the camera lives at the height of the things he
  finds survived intact: 1a and 3a are at the ground, 1b's push-in goes to
  his hand, 2b looks down on the crown of his head. But a boy bending to a
  leaf, seen from behind it, shows a face in the frame's upper edge for a
  moment, and cropping him to hide it cost more than it bought. The
  close-up at 45.0 is the first FULL view of his face, which is the move
  that matters.
- **Shot 3b holds through the pocketing; he does not walk out of the
  light.** The walk–pick–pocket take ends standing, and a walk out on top
  of it read as a second walk. The stillness the plan wanted is the hold
  itself: the pool of light, the boy in it putting money away.
- **The climb runs up the moon's line.** The plan's "streaks past the
  block, then the stars, then the moon" put the note behind the block's
  roof for a second; aiming the keys along `MOON_POS` keeps it against the
  sky the whole way and lands the last frame with the paper a fleck beside
  the moon over the block's edge.
- **The stand is `look`, not `alert`.** At 0.12× `alert` reads as a
  flinch; `Look_Around_Dumbfounded` parked at 4.3 s and crawling is a boy
  standing still and breathing.
- **The take player is not `tangPlay`.** Every take is PARKED at the frame
  the cine clock asks for — `stage.boyPose(a, ta, b, tb, k)` sets each
  action's `time`, weights the blend, runs `mixer.update(0)` and lays the
  head look on after it — and nothing is ever played. A seek, a skip and a
  box drawing one frame a second all land on the same pose, which is what
  `cinetest` relies on when it seeks to 48.3 and asks whether his head is
  on the note. There is no `boyRate`: the slow motion of the stand is the
  take's own time argument crawling.
- **`Running` is not in the file.** Five takes ship (`walk`, `alert`,
  `pick`, `walkpick`, `look`); the sixth was dropped with the bind clip in
  `tools/resampleclips.mjs` (gltf-transform `resample` + `prune`), which
  also cut the clips' redundant keys. `assets/young.glb` is 2,227,824 bytes
  at ratio 0.20 on a 1024 JPEG sheet — judged at the close-up.
- **The leaf lay under the lawn.** Two millimetres under it (y 0.018 against
  the disc's 0.02), invisible with no error; it sits at 0.04 now. The
  bear's grab first produced a giant: `fitTo` set an absolute scale on a
  clone that was already fitted, and now multiplies. The in-hand props
  first came out a hundred times too small: the hand bone's frame is in
  the rig's CENTIMETRE units (world scale 0.0129), so a group parented to
  it is scaled by the inverse of the bone's world scale, and bone +y runs
  down the fingers.
- **The head turned away from the note.** Measured: the head bone's local
  +x is his right and positive `rotation.y` turns him LEFT, so a target on
  his right needs negative yaw. (Chapter 5's `headLook` may share this
  sign; noted, not touched.)
- **The pass is centred on the EYES.** With the group at z 17 the head
  joint sits at z 16.71; the eyes are ≈ 16.58, and the note's keys pass
  eight centimetres in front of them, with a small lift for the frame's
  parallax. Before that it went through his face.
- **Pocket 3's tracking camera drove through his legs** on the first
  pass; its path moved to the road side of the kerb.
- **The lights inside hidden pockets cost play nothing:** three.js does
  not collect a light under an invisible parent, so the memory sets exist
  in the scene through play at zero cost, and the fog never moves (the
  bubbles occlude the world).

the title away from `#cineFade` as well as the card, and taps through the

One harness found a real change to the base game. `invtest` expects a
desktop new game to land in play with the mouse LOCKED (the v3.1 fix: the
Start click is the gesture), and with a film in between it did not: a
film releases the lock when it starts so the Skip button can be reached,
and nothing re-locked — which is how chapters 2–5's films have always
ended, unnoticed because a run reaches them from a click on a card and
Chad plays on a phone. Now `skipFilmOrScene` re-locks after a FILM
skipped by click, tap or key, because that gesture is the one Chromium
allows a lock request from; a film that runs to its end has no gesture,
and play begins unlocked as before, with the edge-turn look and one click
to lock. Never from `__enc.cine.skip()`: a lock request with no gesture is
refused and marks the page as unable to lock at all (`lockBlocked`), so
the suite's `toPlay` skips with a real key press (E — Escape is excluded
from activation in Chromium) and arrives in play the way a player does.

Two harnesses failed once on the first full pass beside heavy neighbours
and both were the harness, not the game. `menutest`'s "the film is heard"

re-armed" reads the reveal a frame-driven observer saw at the first play

first frame now and prints the frames it saw when the check is false.
Both passed on the re-run under the same pairing.
