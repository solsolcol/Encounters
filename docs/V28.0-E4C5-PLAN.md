## 1 · The case files (S1·14 and S1·15, verbatim from docs/source/site-case-files.md)

#### S1·14 — The House With Nine Prai

*2026-09-18 · Classification: case two · cleansing*  
`Period Ten years of cultivation · Place A devotee’s home · Nature Case two — cleansing · Runtime 0:45`

**Hook.** The moment we stepped out of the lift I caught an extremely heavy, foul stench. We were not even near his unit yet. I asked the two followers if they could smell it. Neither of them could smell anything at all.

> A male devotee had been offending people outside, and at home he kept experiencing disturbances. When we investigated, what we found was darker than anyone expected.

> The moment we stepped out of the lift I caught an extremely heavy, foul stench — and we were not even near his unit yet. I asked the two followers with us whether they could smell it. Neither could smell a thing. Grandmaster Pho said the spiritual sense of my nose was already open, and that was why it reached me and not them.

> Once inside, Pho detected nine spirits in the house. He believed those who bore a grudge against the devotee had spilled corpse oil of nine Prai there. As the cleansing began, my own Lersi came in to protect me and I went into trance. I stayed in that state throughout, my mouth chanting something I did not know at the time.

> Pho began drawing from the air, like a vacuum, as though pulling the presences in — then blowing them into a black bag and tying it. He moved through every part of the house doing this. One of the followers holding the Naga ritual stick said he could feel it trembling in his hands, as if it were spitting fireballs. When it was finished the air in the house felt lighter. The bag was tied tightly and disposed of according to the practice, the devotee gave his thanks, and the case was closed.

**The lesson.** Three things this case taught me. First, spiritual sensitivity is not only about seeing — it can come through smell, through physical sensation, through forms of perception you would not expect. Second, and just as important: experiencing something unusual does not automatically prove what caused it. I smelled something the others could not, and that is a fact about my experience, not proof of any particular explanation. Third, and this is the one I would underline: curiosity is not protection, sensitivity is not mastery, and experiencing something is not the same as understanding it. Cultivation, control, experience and discernment — that is the real path, and there is no shortcut through any of it.

#### S1·15 — The Yin Amulet Collector

*2026-09-21 · Classification: case three · objects*  
`Period Ten years of cultivation · Place With Grandmaster Pho · Nature Case three — objects · Runtime 0:40`

**Hook.** He arrived carrying around twenty Yin amulets and asked us to clear them. He had been collecting for a while. Lately he felt disturbed, his luck had collapsed, his health was poor, and he kept smelling something foul he could not place.

> A customer came to us carrying around twenty Yin amulets. He had been collecting them for some time, and lately he felt disturbed — his luck was very low, his health was bad, and everything around him felt heavy. He kept catching a foul smell he could not trace.

> The moment we looked at what he had brought, I caught it too. An extremely heavy stench — and it was not coming from anywhere in the room. It was coming from the amulets themselves.

> When Grandmaster Pho examined them, we saw many presences attached to the pieces. Each amulet seemed linked to something different: some angry, some hungry, some simply looking for energy. Pho began the ritual, chanting and sprinkling holy water with a fan blessed in the traditional Thai way. As the water touched them, the presences reacted — screaming, coming away from the amulets. He kept going until the heaviness in the air gradually weakened.

> The amulets were gathered into a black bag, which he sealed tightly with sacred string and chanted over so that nothing could escape. The foul smell went. The customer said he felt lighter, and his mind felt calmer.

**The lesson.** The problem here is not simply whether an amulet is Yin or Yang. The real questions are much plainer: what exactly are you bringing into your home, where did it come from, and do you actually understand what you are keeping? Do not collect spiritual objects the way people collect ordinary things. If you do not understand what you are inviting into your environment, do not bring it home — curiosity may make you want to collect something, but understanding should come before possession. And please note this part carefully: never automatically assume that poor health or a run of bad luck is spiritual. Health problems need to be medically assessed, and that comes first. Wisdom and discernment will protect you more than any object will.

## 3 · The story, and its place in the episode

**The chapter in one breath.** His nose opens before his eyes do. At a devotee's home he smells what nobody else can,
and it leads to nine presences put there by somebody's grudge; when the cleansing begins his own Lersi comes to protect
him, and for the first time he watches his teacher work, and himself, from outside. Days later the same stench comes
off twenty pieces on a table, and a frightened man asks HIM what it means. What he does with a sense that proves
nothing by itself is the chapter.

**Title (the casebook's own, until Chad names one):** THE HOUSE WITH NINE PRAI. Key `e4c5`, episode 4, id 5: it closes
the episode *The Teacher In White*.

**Two cases, in the casebook's order** (v23.2), and why two cases are one chapter: case two GIVES him the experience
(the smell, the trance, the sight of his teacher's work) and its lesson in his teacher's mouth; case three TESTS that
lesson, on a quieter stage, with nobody to hide behind. The spectacle is in the first; the turning point is in the
second (§4.8).

**It opens where chapter 4 left him** (v24.56): chapter 4 ended that night in her flat, the student told what his sight
did and did not prove. This chapter opens on the next case: another lift, another home, and the first line is about his
NOSE, so nothing re-shows chapter 4's journey.

**What it leaves for later chapters** (v26.0: an ending never runs ahead). Episode 5, *The Signal And The Translator*, is
S1·16 to S1·20: cases four to eight, one a chapter, and its hinge is the END of case four: "I had thought I was only
there to assist my teacher." So in this chapter he is still, in his own mind, the one who carries the bag and the
string. Nobody tells him otherwise. The chapter stops at the customer leaving and the bag sealed: no line about what he
will become, no case four, no "next time you will". The collector's question is the first time a stranger asks HIM
(§4.8), and the chapter does not comment on that: episode 5 does.

**Tone** (v23.0, v27.0): spiritual education, the dread of episodes 1 and 2 kept: the smell before anything is seen, nine
still figures already standing there when his sight opens (v9.5: a ghost who is already there is the beat), the lights
of the home going one by one. No jump scares, no gore, no film ghosts.

**Nobody is the villain** (v21.30). The devotee is a blunt man who has quarrelled with people and says so himself; he
is frightened and he asked for help. The collector is curious, not a fool. Whoever bore the grudge is never seen. The
nine are not enemies: they were put there. Grandmaster Pho is never short with anyone.

## 7 · The voices: instructing, prompting, and never nagging (Chad's R4: "Lots of voicelines that instructs the player well, prompts the player to do what he needs to do")

### 7.1 · The prompt ladder (one rule for every need, in play and in the fight)
A NEED is something the game is waiting on: a first use, a switch, a place not yet found. For each need:
1. **The world asks first.** What needs doing is on the screen, with its button. No words for 5 s.
2. **Someone says what HE needs**, once, in the word on the button (R38): the teacher to his student, a follower to
   either of them.
3. **The button asks**: after 8 s more it grows and shrinks (chapter 4's R47), and the line is said again in other,
   shorter words.
4. **The other one does a small version himself** after 6 s more, and says so kindly. Nothing ever hangs.
5. **The first time it is done right, it is praised.** Once. Never after.

And always: one voice at a time (v5.30); a prompt never cuts a line that is being said; never the same words twice
running; each time a need returns its prompt comes later and shorter; no prompt for what he is already doing; a banner
never orders what cannot be followed (v12.3). Every spoken prompt is also text above the thumbs, so a muted game is
still told.

### 7.2 · Who says what
- **Grandmaster Pho** speaks to his student while the student is played: what to do, by the button's word.
- **The followers at the door** speak while Pho is played (a master is not ordered about by a tutor's voice): they call
  what they see ("On your left, Master!"), and they cry out for the student ("Brother is in trouble!").
- **Mr Koh** says only what a frightened man says. He never instructs.
- **Master Zavier** has no spoken words in the fight (he is chanting): his thoughts are heard at four moments only,
  as the man telling the story.
- **The murmur** is his voice with no words in it, checked by a transcriber, uncaptioned (v25.4).
