## 1 · The edges of every take

Chad blamed the compressor, and the first thing done was to try to agree
with him: the v5.26/v5.28 bus (gain 3.5 → compressor −18/12/4 → limiter
−3/0/20) was rebuilt in an `OfflineAudioContext` and softened — slower
attack, lower ratio, the limiter out. The artefact did not move (±0.3 dB).
Then the FILES were measured: the first and last 40 ms of every one of his
79 takes against the RMS of the body of the line. eleven_v3 returns a take
trimmed to its last audible sample, so nearly every one begins ON signal and
ends on it, and several end while the voice is still loud — `v5fearB1`'s
last 40 ms sit **+14 dB** over its own body, `v2D` +9, `vlost` +5. A buffer
that starts or stops on a non-zero sample is a click; through ×3.5 and a
limiter it is a chuff. And a syllable that ends at full level IS "cut off
prematurely" — the file simply stops.

The fix is the smallest possible one: every voice source now gets an
**8 ms fade-in and a 50 ms fade-out** on its own gain node, before the bus
(`VOICE_FADE_IN`, `VOICE_FADE_OUT`, `voiceEdges()` in main.js). Measured
through the real chain, every positive tail goes negative (v5fearB1
+14.2 → −1.5 dB) and the level cost is 0.2 dB. The bytes are untouched — the
masters stay the proven fallback — and the bus is untouched, which keeps
v5.28's measurements true.

Four play-time paths built their own source before this (`say`, `speak`, the
scare, the opening line); they all go through one `mkVoice(name, buf)` now,
so the edge is one decision rather than four. Cutscene lines go through
`snd()`, which applies the same edges when `isVoice(name)`.

The second half of "cut off prematurely" was the engine's own doing: at a
scene's end `stopCineVoices()` ramped every cutscene source out in 300 ms —
including a line still being said at the scene's NATURAL end. It now takes
`keepSpeech`: `cineEnd()` passes `!c.skipped`, so a sentence finishes under
the card (whose own line waits for it, §2), while a skip still cuts
everything, because the player ended the scene and a sentence carrying on
past that is the bug the list was written for.

## 2 · The stacking

"Main character voicelines will quickly stack on top of each other." They
did, because four paths each checked only their own slot: `say()` refused
while `narSrc` was set, `speak()` while `narSrc || voiceSrc`, and the
opening line's timer checked nothing at all — so a cutscene's last line
finishing under the card, the film's last line under the fade, and the
opening line landing on the near line were all legal.

Now the rule is one rule: **nothing of his starts while ANY voice is
live.** `liveVoices` (v5.27's duck set) is the truth, and:

- `say()` waits while `liveVoices.size > 0 || voicePending` — its callers
  retry every frame, so waiting is free and the near line simply lands when
  the floor is clear;
- `speak()` (the card lines, and now the act line) polls until the floor is
  clear or its deadline passes;
- `scaredGasp()` is DROPPED rather than held — a scare three seconds late
  reads wrong;
- the opening line's timer re-arms every 250 ms while a voice is live, and
  — a second bug under the first — waits for its own DECODE instead of
  standing down: the timer used to fire once at two seconds and return if
  the buffer was not there yet, which on a slow phone dropped his opening
  line with no error. Bounded at 80 × 250 ms so a pack that never arrives
  cannot hold the near line hostage. `voicePending` is what `say()` honours,
  so the first thing heard in a chapter is the line written to be first.

Chapter 4 is where Chad heard it: the spawn stands 2.75 m from the chair,
whose near line fires at 3.2, so "Sit down. Breathe." was already running
when "I can do this." landed on top of it two seconds in. The order is now
the written one. Proven by the new `__enc.voices()` log — every voice source
with its start and end on the audio clock (a probe from the title screen
through the film, play, the chair and scene A into its card):

### 4.3 Grass

Nothing stood under the block at all; it rose out of the fog gradient with
no ground, which reads as floating the moment the eye drops below the sill.
A 256² canvas — 4,200 short strokes in four greens over a base, seeded —

### 4.4 The chair line — the fourteenth leak

"Start from the beginning" was chapter 4's `close` line, which the engine
says on coming close to the interactable — so it fired on walking up to the
chair. Chad: "that line should only be played when interacting with the
chair." A chapter may now declare `lines.act`: a line for the moment the
DECISION OPENS, said in `startDecision()` through `speak()` rather than
`say()` — it waits its turn instead of dropping when the near line is still
going, and it is not once-per-run: he says it every time he sits down to
think. `warmPlaySet()` decodes it with the near line. Chapter 1 declares
nothing here and takes exactly the path it always took.

### 4.5 More hell notes in the void-deck memory

Five notes at half size (0.15 × 0.09) was a memory of a tidier void deck
than the one he was in. Now, to chapter 1's own recipe: its full-size note
(0.30 × 0.18), **sixty** of them (36 at `LOW`) as one InstancedMesh, settled
thickest at the drum and thinning outward by the same r^1.7 chapter 1
scatters by, and its HEAP — two dozen thin boxes mounded a step to the
burner's side, where the one he took came from. Seeded LCG, so the memory is
the same memory on every replay. The v5.25 photographed note and its
brightening are unchanged.

### 4.6 The handset in his hand

It was not held at all: v4.91 parented it to `handsRoot` at a fixed offset,
near where a raised hand would be while the hand stayed on the phone base —
rendered, the receiver hung at the edge of frame on its own. Now it is a
child of the RIGHT ARM at a palm offset — chapter 1's note prop is the
precedent (`armR.add(noteProp)` at (0.012, −0.052, −0.148)) — at
(0.02, −0.03, −0.12) with rotation (1.57, 0, 0.30), which lays the receiver's
long axis (its local z, 22.6 cm) diagonally along the fingers with the
earpiece up-right. The fingers close round it (`setHandCurl` 0 → 0.85 over
0.6 s); the arm lifts to `ARM_UP` (0.22, −0.08, −0.30 / 1.22, 0.30, −0.90)
over 0.9 s and stays for the call; both come back down with the receiver at
43.3–44.7 before the cradle. `restoreWorld()`'s `layoutHands()` puts the arm
back whatever happens, and `stage.restore()` re-seats the receiver.

Chosen from EIGHT rendered candidates, not reasoned out: the first pose put
the hand in the centre of frame, and a first-person hand on a call lives at
the edge of the eye. One trap on the way, now in LEARNINGS: six of the
candidates rendered identically because `cineSeek` re-applies every passed
track at k = 1 each frame — a paused probe cannot override what a passed
tween wrote. The probe pauses at 3.6, before the pickup step.

### 5.3 Scene C — the note flies out of the window

The scene used to end on four held seconds with his back to the table. Now,
to Chad's spec: he has his back to the table, facing the window. At 16.4 the
note lifts off the wood behind him, trembles up to shoulder height, sails
past his RIGHT shoulder into frame, and goes out through the window into the
morning while the camera turns to catch it and follows it out. The fan still
stops mid-turn and the room still dims for the length of it (the v5.15
answer) — the flat answers "just paper" by sending the paper away.

The path is three legs in WORLD space written into the note's table-local
frame each frame (`flyAt(t)`: lift off the table, past the shoulder to the
glass, out and away, with a 7.3/11 Hz flutter riding on it and a tumble on
all three axes, because a sheet never flies flat). The camera aims yaw and
pitch at the note every frame from 17.4, blended in from the window heading
over one second so the turn reads as a head turn and not a cut, then steps
toward the window after it (19.6–22.6); the old kicked-horizon flinch now
lands as the paper passes his shoulder. `stage.restore()` puts the note back
at `NOTE_HOME`, so nothing here has to be undone. Measured (world position of
the note against the camera): at 18.6 it is at (0.45, 1.53, −1.17), a
shoulder's width right of the lens; at 19.8 at the glass (−0.03, 1.53,
−2.22); at 21.0 outside (−0.40, 1.62, −4.17); at 22.4 seven metres out and
rising. Screenshots at each.

`noteflight` is a new sound — one sheet lifting, flapping and a low gust,
6.5 s, ElevenLabs sound effects v2 — and the sting row that names it is the
one addition to `STING_SAMPLE`. It arrived twice: the first take was
**1.04 s** long, because the SFX node defaults to one second unless
`duration_seconds` is set through `creative_add_flow_node` +
`creative_run_flow_nodes`; and it arrived at −25.8 dBFS peak, so it is
peak-normalised on install like any other sound (−3.9). Both facts are in
LEARNINGS. The cue is proven to fire, not assumed: `cine.seek()` PAUSES the
film (the v5.25 law), so the probe seeks to 15.8, `resume()`s, and reads
`__enc.stings()` — `noteflight` is there by 17.8.

### 5.4 Scene D — the double spin

`mkTurn` walks the group to `ry1 + PI` under the catwalk take, which carries
−180° in the hips, and snaps the group to `ry1` on the cut — the two cancel
only if the hips change on the SAME frame. The cut into the next take was a
0.15 s crossfade: the group snapped first and the hips blended after, so for
0.15 s he faced the wrong way and whipped round. That is the "second spin".
Now a hard cut (`tangPlay(then, ts, 0)`), which the v5.07 law already
required (a fade of 0 is a hard cut that bakes the pose); this was the one
place not held to it. Measured on the burning's turnaround: at 30.3 the
group reads 216.9° with hips 1.4°, at 30.5 the group is 36.9° and the hips
−4.8°, and both hold at 31.5 — one change of heading, on one frame.

## 6 · The engine, exactly
