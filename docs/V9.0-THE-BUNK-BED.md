# v9.0 · CHAD'S BUNK BED, IN EVERY BED IN THE ROOM

Chad, 11 Sep 2026, with a Sketchfab model:

> Replace all bunk beds you generated, with this 3d model bunk bed.
> Credits to link.

https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/bunk-bed-c9c4c5133fe645c3a50ce3601f342d5c

Nine beds in episode 2 chapter 1 were posts, rails and slabs of `BoxGeometry`,
because there was no model to use. There is one now: a tubular army bunk with
a guard rail, a ladder and two olive mattresses with a pillow on each.

**`src/main.js` is untouched.** Episode 1 is unchanged by construction.

---

## 1 · THE ASSET

5.53 MB and 10,488 triangles in. **286 KB and 4,116 out**, through a new
`tools/prepbunk.mjs`.

The standing recipe, unchanged from v6.15: every map but base colour goes,
because the CSP-safe `rescueTextures()` only ever restores base colour and a
normal map is therefore pure download (v5.05); base sheets to JPEG; weld,
prune, dedup; `KHR_mesh_quantization` last, the one compression three.js reads
with no decoder and no blob (v5.16).

Two steps this model needed:

**The quarter turn is BAKED INTO THE VERTICES.** The file's length is on z;
every bed in e2c1 has its length on x, because `BED.len` is the x span. The
turn could not be a node rotation set at runtime, for the reason `preptree`
already writes down: a quantized attribute is an integer array and no
per-frame matrix can be applied to one. Bake first, quantize last.

**The frame and the mattresses simplify at different rates.** A simplifier
eats a round tube's silhouette, and a tubular frame IS this model, so the
frame keeps every one of its 2,524 triangles. The two mattresses carried
7,964 of the file's 10,488 for a shape a box nearly serves, and take 0.20
with nothing visible lost.

### 1.1 Two traps, now asserted rather than hoped for

Both were found by running the tool and looking at what came out, and both
would have shipped silently.

**The file instances ONE mattress mesh at two nodes** (0.373 m and 1.356 m
up). `flatten()` and `clearNodeTransform()` bake a node's transform into its
mesh — which cannot be done twice to one mesh. The first pass came out with a
single mattress and a model **2.297 m tall instead of 1.710**. Meshes
reachable from more than one node are cloned before anything is baked.

**The first pass lost the FRAME entirely.** To keep it out of a
document-wide `simplify()`, its primitives were detached and re-added
afterwards — but `simplify()` prunes, and pruning removed the emptied mesh and
its node while they were parked. The render came back with two floating
mattresses and a bounding box that looked perfectly plausible. It uses
`simplifyPrimitive()` per primitive now, and the tool **refuses to write** a
file whose frame is missing, whose base is not on y = 0, or whose length is
not on x.

### 1.2 The contract the tool prints and asserts

```
length(x) 2.004   width(z) 1.313   height(y) 1.710
mattress tops 0.592 and 1.575, bottoms 0.319 and 1.302
pillows at the −x end
```

## 2 · THE FIT IS TO THE PRIMITIVE

This is the whole reason the change is safe. Every number the room is built
on — `blockers()`, `walktest`'s route, the bed zone, where a sleeper lies,
which cells the fall-in can be reached through — comes off the **primitive
mattress**, and the primitive mattress does not move. The model is scaled onto
it:

| axis | factor | why |
|---|---|---|
| x | `BED.len / 2.004` = **0.948** | 1.90 m — barely anything |
| z | `BED.wid / 1.313` = **0.685** | 0.90 m — a real squeeze, and forced |
| y | **1.017**, frame sunk 5.2 cm | both mattress TOPS land exactly on `BED.low` and `BED.high` |

**The z squeeze is forced, not preferred.** The balcony row's beds are 1.15 m
apart (`ROW_Z_BALC`), so a bed at the model's own 1.313 m would OVERLAP its
neighbour; and scaling uniformly by width instead leaves a 1.37 m bed for a
1.72 m man to lie on. Fitting the footprint keeps the room, the collision and
the walk exactly as they are, and a tubular frame squeezed across its short
axis reads as a narrower bed rather than as a distorted one.

**The y fit is exact rather than near**, because the sheet, the folded
blanket and the sleeper's blanket all sit at `deck + a few centimetres`, and a
2 cm error is a sheet floating over a mattress. Solving both decks at once
gives the 5.2 cm sink; the feet go under the floor plane where nothing sees
them.

**The pillow end is why the bed turns.** The model's pillows are at its −x end
and `headTowardWall` is −1 on the wall row and +1 on the balcony row, so the
balcony row is turned a half circle and both rows put a pillow against their
own wall.

Measured on the shipped build: the model's world box spans **1.900 × 0.900**,
to the millimetre; the sheet sits 1 mm proud of the mattress; the folded
blanket is at the foot; the boots are beneath it.

## 3 · WHAT THE PRIMITIVES BECAME

The posts, rails, mattresses and pillows are **hidden, never removed**.
`blockers()` boxes `low.mattress` and the bed's tap test raycasts it, and
three.js does both to an invisible mesh exactly as it does to a visible one —
checked in isolation before relying on it, not assumed. They are the proxies
the room is built on.

The **wire base** is the exception: not superseded but MOVED. v7.2 added it at
`deck − 0.155` so the film's last shot and scenes B and D had something other
than a black slab to look up at. The model's mattress bottom sits at 1.272,
ABOVE that, so left where it was the mesh would hang inside the mattress. It
drops to **1.260**, and a man on the bottom bunk looks up at a spring base
with a mattress resting on it.

The bedsheet's colour came down from `0xe9e6dc` to `0xc2beb3` — v8.9's law:
the renderer is ACESFilmic at exposure 1.42, and against an olive mattress the
old value was a sheet of light.

## 4 · WHAT IT COSTS

Nine real bunks cost **11,856 triangles in the worst frame** — looking down
the whole −x bed row, 267,015 against v8.9's 255,159 — and **nothing at the
spawn**, which faces the balcony. Still under the 297,872 v8.6 left this
chapter at, because most of the nine are culled at any moment.

The model is cloned per bed rather than instanced: `clone()` shares geometry
and material, so nine beds are one upload, and each is its own object, so
v8.4's culling can drop the ones the lens is not pointed at.

## 5 · ONE HARNESS FIX, NOT A GAME CHANGE

`fixturetest`'s `objDoneBanner` passed standalone and failed under `runtests`.

Instrumented on the real build (`dbg-objbeat.mjs`, every class mutation
logged): **the banner does paint.** The sequence is

```
obox flash scan done out   "Find the marker on the floor"   ← still the OLD order
obox flash scan done in    "OBJECTIVE COMPLETE"
obox flash scan done       "OBJECTIVE COMPLETE"
obox done                  "OBJECTIVE COMPLETE"
```

v8.8 made the objective's words slide out and back in, so the box wears the
`done` class while it still shows the old order. Reading the first mutation
that carries the class is **reading the box mid-swap**. The check watches for
any matching mutation now.

Worth recording: an engine guard was written first, against the hypothesis
that a slow frame was swallowing the beat — and **reverted the moment
measurement disproved it**. `src/main.js` is byte-identical to v8.9.

## 6 · WHAT WAS RUN

Eleven harnesses: `walk`, `leak`, `chapter`, `text`, `cine`, `pile`, `hosted`,
`csp`, `census`, `menu`, `fixture` — all green. `walk` and `leak` are the two
that matter most here: the first proves the collision grid did not move, the
second that nine cloned beds give the GPU back what they took.

Probes: `dbg-glbview.mjs` (the model alone, front, end and three-quarter —
v6.17's law, a model is photographed on its own before it is wired),
`dbg-bunkmeasure.mjs` (which end the pillows are at), `dbg-v90bunk.mjs` (every
bed carries the model, every superseded primitive is hidden, the world box),
`dbg-v90props.mjs` (the surviving props against the model's own mattresses),
`dbg-v89men.mjs` (the triangle cost), `dbg-objbeat.mjs` (§5).
