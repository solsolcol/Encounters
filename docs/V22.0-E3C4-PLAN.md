## 1. The source (S1·09, docs/source/site-case-files.md 142–157)

> There was a period when I was still trying to understand the strange things
> happening around me. One day I visited an amulet shop. The shopkeeper showed
> me a Yin amulet, of a young woman who had died tragically. Out of curiosity,
> I picked it up. Almost immediately I felt an electric‑like numbness
> travelling up my arm, followed by an intense cold. Instinctively I knew my
> energy was not compatible with whatever was attached to that piece.
>
> I put it down and said what I felt was right: I will not bring you home, but
> I will make merits under your name. Later I went to a temple and made a
> contribution dedicated to her. That experience has stayed with me ever since.
>
> On another occasion I picked up a Hanuman amulet at a different shop. The
> moment I held it I felt an extremely powerful presence — in my mind it was
> almost as though something roared. Then came an overwhelming urge, as if
> something wanted to enter or take control. I released it at once and hurried
> out of the shop, afraid that people would see me going into that state in
> public.
>
> Eventually a shop owner I visited often noticed the sensitivity. He told me I
> had something unusual and began handing me pieces to hold — Lersi, Hanuman,
> all sorts — just to see what would happen. And every time I held certain
> pieces and let my mind open, something seemed to respond.
>
> **The lesson.** This is the part I most want people to hear. Never
> deliberately test spiritual items by opening yourself up to see what comes
> in. You may believe a piece is connected to a deity, a Lersi, Hanuman or some
> other force — but without proper knowledge and guidance you cannot be
> certain what you are actually experiencing. It could be psychological. It
> could be your own sensitivity reacting to the object. It could be a spirit,
> or something else entirely that you do not understand. The dangerous part is
> not the object. It is deliberately surrendering control to something you
> cannot identify. Looking back, I was fortunate that these experiences became
> part of my learning rather than something far more serious. Spiritual
> curiosity should always come with discernment, respect and proper guidance.
> You do not need to invite something in just to prove that something is
> there.

## 5. Engine work (all opt-in; episodes 1–2 never reach it; the fixture proves each)

1. **`steady` — a new event kind** (the thirty-first seam): a mark drifts
   under a tremor that grows; the player drags (finger, or the mouse while
   pressed, or the mouse under pointer lock) to keep it inside a ring; the
   score is the share of the time it stayed in. Its own looping picture under
   the title (a dot pushed out by a tremor and a fingertip pulling it back):
   a row in `EV_DEMO` and a class in shell.html (Chad's standing rule;
   chaptertest fails without them). `fixturetest`: steady input wins, no input
   loses.
2. **`kit.tremble(k, { secs })`** (the thirty-second seam): the hands shake,
   eased on wall time, added to the viewmodel's own sway; `kitReset` clears it.
   `fixturetest` checks the hands move with it and are bit-identical at 0.
3. **`timed` words**: `waitWord` / `goWord` on a timed event (the button says
   "Hold..." then "LET GO"); absent, the engine's own words. Opt-in.
4. Everything else is the existing kit: `kit.film` (the merit film, the
   run-out), `kit.hold`, `kit.captions`, `kit.presence`, `kit.daylight`,
   `kit.fade`, `kit.warm`, `kit.hands`, `kit.root`, `kit.flash`,
