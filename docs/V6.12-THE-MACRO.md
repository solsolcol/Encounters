# v6.12 — The palm turned to his face, and the macro

Chad, on v6.11, with a standing frame and the close-up attached:

> Scene 1 - Can you tell that you actually need to rotate the arms inwards
> even more? Palm should be facing his face. That standing screenshot, he
> is staring at the back of his palm, not his actual open palm.
>
> Maybe for the close up, stop showing his whole arm and body, and instead
> its just a really close close up of his palm only. I said that before.
>
> Also, why does the palm and arm angle subsequently affected the next few
> scenes? Scenes 2-4 were already perfect before, now its worse because the
> palm/arm stays fixed that way for subsequent scenes.

Three notes, three separate causes.

## 1 · The roll stopped at the sky

v6.11 picked its angle by maximising how far UP the palm pointed: -2.04 rad,
palm 0.97 up. But a palm facing the sky, seen from the eyes of the boy
standing over it, is still the back of a hand. The measure that matters is
the palm against the line from his hand to his EYES (the head bone plus
11 cm, the v5.04 offset). `dbg-palmface.mjs` scans a full turn at three
moments and reports both:

| | palm . (hand -> eyes) | palm's up |
|---|---|---|
| v6.11, -2.04 rad | 0.45 | 0.97 |
| best available, ~ -3.14 rad | 0.91 | 0.63 |
| **shipped, -3.02 rad** | **0.87** | 0.64 |

-3.02 sits a hair inside the half turn, so the sign of the roll stays
unambiguous and the palm shot's yaw stays well conditioned. The palm ends
38 degrees above horizontal, which is where his eyes are. In the wide shot
the camera now sees his knuckles — which is what a person examining
something in their palm looks like from the front.

The wide shot (1b) also runs to 11.5 rather than 11.0, so the finished pose
is seen before the cut, and the hanging-leaf track follows it.

## 2 · The close-up is a macro, on a longer lens

v6.10 and v6.11 framed the close-up by moving the camera closer: 0.30 ->
0.22 m, then 0.17 -> 0.14, then 0.13 -> 0.105. The last of those filled the
frame and destroyed the leaf. The near plane is **0.08 m**, and the leaf
sits on top of the palm, nearest the lens:

| film time | nearest leaf vertex | what renders |
|---|---|---|
| 11.6 | 0.089 m | the whole leaf |
| 12.4 | 0.082 m | the edge starting to go |
| 13.0 | 0.074 m | a sliver |
| 13.6 | 0.067 m | gone; the palm behind it remains |

So the wide lens cannot take this shot at all. `api.lens(t0, t1, from, to,
ease)` — the sixteenth engine seam — narrows the angle for one shot
instead, which is how a macro is actually taken, and it drops the
wide-angle stretch a 72 degree lens gives a hand at 10 cm. The shot is
**50 -> 44 degrees at 0.26 -> 0.22 m**: on a phone's centre crop the palm
fills the frame edge to edge with the leaf on it, and a desktop frame no
longer carries the forearm and the shirt.

Two guarantees keep the borrowed lens from escaping: `playCineFn` sets
`CAM_FOV` when a cutscene starts, and `cineEnd()` restores it when one ends
— and both exits, the natural end and `skipCine`, pass through `cineEnd`.

## 3 · The turn rode into the later scenes, and so did the lens

Chad felt this before it was measured, and it is one law twice. `cineSeek`
applies **every** track whose `t0` has passed, on **every** frame, with `k`
clamped to 1. The wrist's `palmUp(10.45, 11.25)` therefore kept writing
`k = 1` for the rest of the film, one frame after `palmOff()` reset it at
pocket two — so the bear, the five and the present all played with the
wrist rolled over. It is v5.07's held glide again.

The first fix, `if (t > t1) return`, was worse, and measurement caught it:
under a SEEK the track lands past its window having never run the ramp, so
the value never leaves its start (k = 0 at 11.4, the wrist untouched). The
shipped fix is an explicit hold — `palmUp(t0, t1, hold)` ramps to `t1`,
holds to `hold` (14.0), and writes nothing after.

Then the new `lens` track fell into exactly the same trap one shot later:
44 degrees held for the rest of the film, and every later pocket rendered
zoomed. Caught by comparing rendered frames from pockets two, three and
four against the frames from before this work began:

| frame | before the lens was handed back | after |
|---|---|---|
| 15.5 s | 14.7 dB | 37.5 dB |
| 19.0 s | 20.9 dB | 29.6 dB |
| 31.0 s | 12.2 dB | 38.4 dB |

(30-38 dB is the render's own noise between a walked render and a
directly-seeked one; 12-20 dB is a different camera.) A second `lens` track
at the cut hands `CAM_FOV` back, using the constant the scene api now
exposes.

## Verification

38 frames at ten a second over 10.3-14.0 on contact sheets; the phone crop
(420 x 760) at 11.6, 12.3, 13.0 and 13.8 for the macro, and at 15.5, 19, 31
and 52 for the later scenes; and the numeric comparison above. `cine` and
`chapter` green. No word moved; sheet v39 stands.
