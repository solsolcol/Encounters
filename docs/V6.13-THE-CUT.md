## The cut

Pocket one's wide (shot 1b) ran to 11.5 so the lift and the wrist turn were
on screen. It now ends at **9.85** — the grab frame, where `leafpick` fires
and the leaf leaves the lawn. Everything changes on that single frame:

| at 9.85 | |
|---|---|
| `leafGround.visible = false`, `leafShow('palm')` | the leaf is flat in his palm |
| `take(9.85, 14.0, 'pick', 0, 4.4)` | the take parks on its held frame |
| `palmUp(9.84, 9.85, 14.0)` | the wrist is already turned (a 10 ms ramp under the wide's last frame) |
| `lens(9.85, 14.0, 50, 44)` + `shotPalm(9.85, 14.0, 0.26, 0.22, ...)` | the macro, exactly as v6.12 framed it |

The pose either side of the cut is completely different, which is what a
cut is; the macro shows only the palm, so none of the change is on screen.
The shot is 4.15 s instead of 2.5 s, on the same start and end framing, so
the push-in is gentler and the image at any moment is the one that shipped.

v6.11's hanging-leaf track is deleted with the shot it was written for (it
kept the pinched leaf's blade on world-down through the lift). The pinch
prop stays in the stage, unshown.

## The shake

`shotPalm` computes the camera from the hand bone's world matrix every
frame. The shot is therefore perfectly steady *relative to the hand* — and
inherits everything the hand does in world space. Until this release the
pick take was still RUNNING under the macro (to 12.8 in v6.12), so the
hand's own motion, and the per-frame grounding that lifts the body to
whichever foot is lowest, went straight into the lens.

Parking the take from the cut removes the cause. Measured ten times a
second across the whole shot (`dbg-steady.mjs`):

| | |
|---|---|
| largest hand movement between samples | **0.000 mm** |
| camera step per 0.1 s | 0.14 -> 1.45 -> 0.20 mm, monotone in and out |

That curve is the eased push-in and nothing else.
