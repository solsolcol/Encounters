Chad, playing episode 2 chapter 1 on his phone: *"now episode 2 chapter 1
heats up the phone crazily when running it. are there ways to optimize
without losing quality or breaking anything?"* — and then, on the plan:

## 1 · WHAT WAS ACTUALLY HOT

Measured on the shipped v8.3 build, phone viewport, standing in the bunk,
against episode 1 chapters as the control (`dbg-cost3.mjs`, sampling inside
rAF; the first attempt read `renderer.info` *after* the frame and got
`calls: 1` for all three, because main.js renders TWICE — the world, then the
viewmodel with `autoClear` off — and three.js resets the counters at the
start of every `render()`, so what came back was the arms):

| | ch1 | ch4 | **e2c1** |
|---|---|---|---|
| draw calls / frame | 109 | 50 | 98 |
| **triangles / frame** | 251,561 | 4,463 | **661,410** |
| ms / frame | 357 | 274 | **652** |

Draw calls were *lower* than chapter 1's. It was raw triangle volume. Ranked
by what was visible (`dbg-tris.mjs`; 666,041 visible against 661,410 actually
submitted, so culling was removing essentially nothing):

| | triangles / frame | share |
|---|---|---|
| **People** — 8 recruits + 2 sergeants | **475,210** | **71%** |
| Trees — 9 instanced stands | 183,574 | 28% |
| The entire hand-built bunk: beds, lockers, table, dressing | 7,257 | 1% |

The room is free. It was the people — and the reason none of them were being
skipped is that **every skinned mesh in the chapter carried
`frustumCulled = false`** (four sites, since v7.1). All ten drew on every
frame whichever way the player faced. Measured at eight headings from his bed
(`dbg-cull.mjs`): a mean of **16%** of the character triangles were genuinely
on screen. 84% was pure waste.

## 2 · WHY THE FLAG WAS THERE, AND WHY IT IS NOT LAZINESS

three.js culls a skinned mesh by a bounding sphere. `SkinnedMesh` carries its
**own** `boundingSphere`, which `Frustum.intersectsObject` prefers over the
geometry's, and it is computed **once — from whatever pose the man happens to
be in at that first test — and cached for ever.** A push-up, a walk cycle or
an arm swing then reaches outside it and the man **blinks out** at the edge of
the screen.

Two consequences that had to be got right:

- Setting `geometry.boundingSphere` alone would have done **nothing**: the
  object's own takes precedence. The fix sets both.
- A sphere from the bind pose is not enough either. The admin tee's bind
  sphere is radius 1.008 **centred on the origin**, while the body it has to
  cover reaches 1.376 around a centre at y = 0.870.

## 3 · THE WIDEST POSE, MEASURED

`tools/clipbounds.mjs` loads each rig, plays **every clip it owns**, steps it
across the whole duration (24 poses per clip), skins the vertices at each step
and reports the sphere containing every pose plus the bind pose. Exhaustive,
because a clip that plays in one beat of one scene still has to be covered.

| rig | clips | animated sphere | bind sphere | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `admintee` | 9 | c (0.067, 0.870, 0.011) r **1.376** | r 1.008 | 1.36× |
| `fbosling` | 8 | c (0.056, 0.837, 0.212) r **1.379** | r 1.052 | 1.31× |
| `encik2` | 7 | c (0.015, 0.832, −0.037) r **1.253** | r 1.034 | 1.21× |
| `botak` | 5 | c (0.003, 0.858, −0.050) r **1.176** | r 1.026 | 1.15× |
| `ghostsoldier` | 1 | c (0.001, 0.850, −0.010) r **1.045** | r 1.053 | 0.99× |
| `sleepanim` | 3 | c (0.582, 85.062, 19.032) r **130.851** | r 1.055 | — |

**Each in its own file's units.** `sleepanim` comes through Mixamo's FBX path
at ×100, so its sphere is in centimetres — which is correct, because three.js
scales the sphere by the mesh's own `matrixWorld`. One shared number would
have been wrong by a factor of a hundred for that one rig. `sleeper` carries
no skinned mesh at all (it is a statue): a rigid mesh's own geometry sphere is
already right, so it only needed the flag removed.

`CULL_MARGIN` is **1.25**, deliberately generous. A bigger sphere culls very
slightly less often — at six metres the difference is negligible — while a
sphere a centimetre too small is a man vanishing in front of the player. It
covers the tool's vertex stride and any crossfade between two takes landing a
hair outside both.

## 4 · PROVING NOBODY VANISHES

A pixel A/B was tried first and is **useless here**: the bunk animates
(mixers, the fan, the tube's flicker), so two frames a second apart differ
with nothing changed at all. A null test — capture twice with *no* change
between — found **40 of 40** sample views unstable. The first run's list of
"differences" was entirely that noise, and reporting it would have been wrong.

The test that does work compares the two things that must agree, **on the same
frame**:

- **truth** — project a sample of the man's *skinned* vertices; if any lands
  inside the viewport in front of the lens, he is on screen and must be drawn;
- **the renderer** — three.js calls `onBeforeRender` only on objects that
  survived culling, so the set that fires *is* the set it drew.

Anyone true-visible who never fired is a man who blinked out.

| sweep | checks | genuinely on screen | vanished |
|---|---|---|---|
| play — 6 positions × 12 headings × 3 pitches | 1,296 | 152 | **0** |
| the whole opening film (ferry cabin, jetty, parade square) | 1,120 | 393 | **0** |
| the punishment beat, every man prone — the widest pose | 1,440 | 240 | **0** |
| **total** | **3,856** | **785** | **0** |

## 6 · WHAT IS STILL AVAILABLE, AND NOT DONE

These were offered and not taken up, because the free win came first and Chad
should judge whether it is enough before any quality is traded:

1. **A lighter copy of the admin tee for background recruits.** Each is 44,539
   triangles and six stand at far beds. Roughly halves what remains.
2. **Thin the tree line on phones.** 183,574 triangles — now the largest single
   share — and `plantTrees` already takes a `lowKeep`.
3. **Render at fewer pixels on the phone.** Currently `min(dpr, 1.6)`, which is
   2.6× the work of 1:1. Could be adaptive rather than fixed.
4. **Hide lights that sit at zero intensity.** A light at zero is still
   evaluated per fragment; the bunk has seven point lights and most are dark
   most of the time. Cheap, but changing the light count recompiles the shader,
   so it is only safe for lights that stay off for whole phases.

## 7 · THE LAWS THIS PAID FOR

- **A `SkinnedMesh` has its own `boundingSphere`, and three.js computes it once
  from one arbitrary pose.** Setting the geometry's does nothing. `frustumCulled
  = false` is the usual workaround and it costs every frame, for ever.
- **A bind-pose sphere is not an animated sphere** — 1.36× out on the admin
  tee, and centred in the wrong place.
- **Measure the bounds in the file's own units.** One shared radius would have
  been 100× wrong for the one rig that arrives through the FBX path.
- **A pixel A/B needs a null test first.** In an animated scene, two frames
  differ on their own; without the null test the noise reads as a finding.
