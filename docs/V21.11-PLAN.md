## The design

1. **The row stands on the altar's upper step where Guan Gong stood**, facing
   the door, filling the niche: scaled to ~2.66 m wide (×1.4 — about 0.84 m
   tall, each image 0.6–0.8 m, the size a shrine's carved images are), the
   upper step widened and deepened to carry it (its back on the niche's back
   panel), the candles moved onto the lower step's lip in front. Guan Gong, his
   plinth and his stand-in go (Chad's word); so do the four drawn stand-in
   figures on the lower step — they were placeholders for exactly what the
   row now is, and at that size they would stand in front of its bases. A
   failed download keeps the altar (a stand-in stays until the row lands, v4.7).
2. **Every triangle near, a cut far** (the shop's altar row and the Buddha's
   pattern): `tools/prepwess.mjs` (no simplification; KEEPMR; base colour
   4096, normal and metal-roughness 2048 — the shop's amulet shelf already
   carries a 4096 sheet) then `tools/lodpair.mjs` adds a permissive cut over
   the SAME vertices (one more index buffer, the same maps). The near level
   within ~3 m of the row's middle (beside the offering table the player can
   stand under a metre from the outer images); the cut beyond.
3. **The paintings hang as scrolls** where the two drawn deity paintings
   hung (Xuantian Shangdi on one side wall, the Jade Emperor on the other):
   each image whole at its own aspect, never cropped or retouched (v21.8's
   rule for the masters), on a silk mounting with a brocade border, a wooden
   rod at the top on a cord and a roller with knobs at the bottom — the way a
   Chinese hanging scroll is mounted. Loaded through `loadImageTexture`
   (CSP-safe), hosted-only like the masters' portraits.
4. **Credits**: two rows (the paintings, Wikimedia Commons, public domain,
   with the museum / Sotheby's). The deity row is Chad's own model (no row —
   the v5.23 rule) unless he says it came from somewhere to credit.
