## 1 · The flare is a smoky red trail

The climb had a white spark and nothing behind it, so what the eye got was
a dot that moved. Three things now:

- **A trail.** A pool of 40 billboards carrying one canvas-drawn puff (no
  download, CSP-safe), laid down along the arc every 2.8 % of the climb —
  about one every 45 ms at `FLARE_RISE` 1.55 s — and aged on WALL time.
  The ones nearest the head keep the flare's red (`TRAIL_HOT` 0xff5a2a) and
  the old ones grey out and spread (`TRAIL_COLD` 0x6b6f78), which is what
  smoke lit from inside looks like. NormalBlending, never additive:
  additive smoke over a night sky is a bright streak, not smoke (v8.8).
- **A red light.** `flare.color` is `FLARE_RED` through the climb and back
  to `FLARE_WHITE` on the frame it bursts.
- **A sky.** `FLARE_CLIMB` is a third-strength, red-pushed FLARE tweened in
  over the climb's own length and handed straight to FLARE at the pop — so
  the sky reddens under the rocket and opens white when it bursts. Its fog
  is the night's 0.012 exactly: a flare must never thicken the air (v12.3),
  and a climb that is not yet lighting the range must not thin it either.

**Two things were measured rather than chosen, and one was measured twice.**
The puff is sized by its DISTANCE at the moment it is dropped, exactly as
`flareBallSize` sizes the spark (v13.0: a fixed world size is not a size).
The first pass used `2.2 + 9 × k` — bigger the higher it went — and
photographed as one fat cloud at the apex over a column of specks, because a
2.2 m puff eight metres away and an 11 m puff eighty-eight metres away are
not the same picture. And the BALL is deliberately left white-hot: driving
its emissive red as well was tried and photographed, and it rendered as a
DARK DISC at the head of the trail. The plume carries the colour; the motor
is white, and 1.6× its normal size while it climbs, because against the new
smoke it otherwise reads as a lozenge rather than a head.

The tick is driven from THREE places — `flareClimb` (play and the film's
climb track), `flareFrame` (play's burn) and `setFlare` (the film's burn) —
and is idempotent within a frame, because **a film gets no `flareFrame` at
all**: `updateNotes` returns early under a cutscene (v12.1), so a trail aged
only there would hang frozen in the sky for the rest of the film.

## 2 · The cyclist's rhythm

| Chad's words | what it is |
|---|---|
| "the cycling bell ding sound that keeps playing. Always" | `bikebell` is a BED now, keyed to `cyc.a` — his alpha, not to play — so it rides him into the scenes too. It is one 3.34 s ding, so as a loop it repeats about every three seconds, which is what a bell rung by somebody riding sounds like, and it costs no new download |
| "the eerie laughing sound and the ghost cyclist fades away instead of disappearing immediately" | `ghostlaugh` on the frame the round lands, and `cycEnd(true)` — the FADE — where v12.3 called `cycEnd()` and took him off the range between two frames |
| "return 1.5 second later, nearer to the player this time, but going in the opposite direction" | 1.5 s, and the PASS index is both: `PASS_Z` brings him in and the new `passDir(pass)` turns him round |
| "Keep shooting, and it keeps coming nearer and changing direction again" | the direction is the pass's PARITY, not a stored flag, so it cannot drift out of step with the distance — and a resume, which restores `pass` and nothing else, restores the direction with it (v7.3) |
| "finally, the cyclist reappears close to the player's view this time, looking directly at the player" | the stopped pass, and it now has to be SHOWN: v12.3 rang the bell on the third shot instead, so the one appearance the escalation was building to never happened. He stands there for three and a half seconds and then it ends |
| "Every appearance and exit... in a fading manner" | `onCrossed` and `onBell` fade him in where they used to snap him on, and `onBell` does not re-place him if he is already standing there |

## 3 · Three cuts

- **The decision's countdown is gone.** It was the plan's §4.7 — the bar as
  the thing's approach — and cost 8 sanity on a timeout. A countdown on a
  menu the player is reading is pressure on the reading, not on the decision.
- **The radio hotspot is gone.** Lane five's shout and the tower's reply
  already say the target area is empty, which was the beat the radio existed
  to deliver. `n4report` and `t4roger` stay in the pack — deleting a
  recorded take to remove a prompt is a trade with no upside (v9.6).
- **The wait after "Last night. You never say anything after."** was 9.9 s
  of nothing: the line runs 3.0 to 6.13 (its own measured 3.13 s) and the
  flare went up at 16.0. It goes up at 8.0.

## 4 · "What you did"

The outcome card's conduct block was every banked note joined into ONE
run-on sentence behind a heading, "In the field", that named nothing. It is
a LIST now — a heading that says what it is, one line per thing he did, and
the stat total on its own line — built with `createElement` rather than
`innerHTML`, because a note is chapter text and the sheet can put anything
in it. The notes themselves are shorter, because they are list items now and
not clauses: "Shot the static serial clean.", "Held it in your sight. Kept
your lane.", "Fired at it."

## 5 · The four scenes

**A · 26.9 s -> 21.5.** There were five voice lines and two of them said what
another had just said. The bunkmate's echo and the encik's weapons-down are
cut; what is left is the shout he chose not to make (`r4run`), the range
being closed (`t4endex`), and his own last line. The crossing also fades out
from 58 % of its travel instead of 78 %.

**B · 22 s -> 17.6.** `n4draw` in Chad's own words as he steps off the line,
the walk 10 s -> 6.4 at the same start and end (1.56× the pace, and the
head-bob frequency with it), the cyclist fading OUT rather than switching
off, and behind him at the end: the bell, `ghostlaugh`, and `e4back` — the
encik's "GET BACK HERE! NOW!" — under the black.

**C · 21 s -> 18.4.** TWO rounds, not three, and each one is a REAL shot:
`kit.weaponShot()` plays the Shoot take, the muzzle flash, the light and the
weapon's own recoil spring. That verb is new, and it exists because **a
cutscene cannot call `weaponFire`** — that is the player's path: it spends a
round, raycasts into the live serial's bank and reports a hit to the chapter,
none of which a scene wants. Two things it deliberately does NOT do. It is
SILENT: the report stays a scheduled `sfx` cue in the scene, where the cue
log can see it and `chaptertest` checks its timing, and where a rifle whose
bytes never arrived still makes a noise — a verb that carried the sound
would be the only thing that could, and a failed download would cost the
chapter its gunshot (v4.7's rule). And it does not apply `weapon.kick`: that
writes `pitch.rotation.x`, which a cutscene owns and re-applies from its own
tracks every frame (v5.30's law), so the kick would be erased on the next
frame and fight the shot's framing while it lasted. The gun moves; the lens
is the scene's. Each round fades him out and he fades back
nearer and turned, which is play's own rhythm shown to a player who chose
not to find it out. The third press finds an empty arc, the bell rings
BEHIND him, the lens turns 180°, and `n4gasp`.

**D · 18.6 s -> 13.4.** Six metres of running, not the whole way back to the
ammo point; `n4runD` then `n4pant`, laid out against their measured lengths
so the two cannot stack; and then the engine's own FAINT, beat for beat —
the whip up, the decaying judder, the fall under gravity, the settle onto
his side with the horizon vertical, and the dark. It is a COPY of `scFaint`'s
shape rather than a call to it: the engine's faint ends a run at sanity zero,
and this is a chapter ending at a choice. Same picture, different meaning.
Everything the faint moved is put back on the scene's last step, or play
would resume with the lens on its side and the eye at 0.35 m.

**And the photograph found what the numbers could not.** At one frame a
second the whole column landed in ONE place at the head: `flareClimb`'s
catch-up loop dropped every puff at `flare.position`, and `flarePlace` had
already moved that to the CURRENT k. `trailInfo` reported 23 puffs and was
telling the truth. `flarePointAt(k)` answers where the rocket WAS, and the
column is a column at any frame rate — photographed at 0.30 and 0.62 of the
climb from the firing point, with the ground, the trees and the fog red under
it, which is the "lighting up the sky" half of the note.
