## 1 · The sofa

`sofa.glb` (Sketchfab, credited): 3.40 MB and 13,984 triangles down to
**331 KB and 6,292**, by the standing recipe — every map but base colour
dropped (the CSP-safe `rescueTextures()` only ever restores base colour,
so a normal or AO map is bytes nobody sees), the base sheet to a 1024
JPEG, weld + simplify at 0.45, then `KHR_mesh_quantization`.

It needed no fitting numbers of its own, which does not usually happen.
The file is 80 x 39 x 38 in its own units, so scaled uniformly until its
LENGTH is the primitive's 1.9 m it comes out **0.93 deep and 0.90 high**
against the boxes' 0.85 and 0.92. One scalar, read off the file, no
per-axis squashing. Measured in the running game, both chapters:

| | primitive | model |
|---|---|---|
| length (world z) | 1.90 | **1.90** |
| depth (world x) | 0.85 | **0.93** |
| top | 0.92 | **0.90** |
| floor | 0 | **0.00** |
| right edge (wall at 3.20) | 3.045 | **3.083** |

Two orientation facts, both derived rather than tried:

- Its length runs along its own **z**; the group's runs along its local
  **x**, because the group is turned a quarter about Y to face the TV. So
  the model is turned a quarter the other way inside the group.
- That same quarter turn puts its **back** where the primitive's back was.
  The model's backrest is at +x of its own centre (measured off a height
  profile: 0.88–0.90 at the +x bins, 0.46 at the -x ones), and a +90°
  turn about Y sends +x to local -z, which is exactly where `sofaBack`
  sat. Nothing further to do — it faces the TV because the group does.

The primitive stays in the group, **hidden**: `sofaBase` is what
`blockers()` boxes for the collision column, so walking into the sofa is
unchanged, and a download that never lands still leaves something to sit
on. Seven primitive meshes hidden, one model mesh added; verified.

Scene B's lying shot needed nothing. `LYING` is at y 0.72, "head on the
sofa arm"; the model's arm tops out at **0.59**, so a head resting on it
centres around 0.69 — closer to the shot's own number than the
primitive's 0.70 arm ever was.

## 2 · The living room curtains

Gone from both chapters: `curtainL`, `curtainR`, their base arrays, the
per-frame billow in `updateNotes`, `matSheet` (they were its only user),
and the whole **`billow` seam** — the snap/restore/reset field and the
stage getter/setter. Nothing else ever drove it, and a setter that moves
nothing is the exact shape of bug this repo keeps finding, so it goes
rather than lingering as a no-op.

**What that costs, and it is worth saying plainly.** Both scene Cs used
the curtains for the flat's poltergeist answer:

- **Chapter 4**, 21.4 s: a cloth lunge, then a `boom` at 22.5. The boom
  was always the punctuation under it and still lands on its own, inside
  a beat that already has TV static, a phone ringing by itself, the
  heart, the strobe and the shake.
- **Chapter 5**, 16.5 s: "THE ANSWER". The camera is on the WINDOW here —
  he has turned his back on the note and walked to it — so the curtains
  were what answered in frame. What is left in that frame is the **room
  dimming** for exactly the length of the answer, with the fan stopping
  mid-turn behind him and his own flinch. It reads, but it is quieter
  than it was, and if Chad wants that shot to have a moving thing in it
  again, it needs a new one; there is nothing cloth-like left at that
  window.

The `curtain` SOUND is now cued by nobody. Its row stays in
`STING_SAMPLE` and its file stays in the library — a few KB, and the
bias for a sound is always to keep it (a sound wrongly dropped is a cue
that plays nothing).

## 3 · The bedroom curtain, and the number behind it

Chad: "the curtain is colliding with the window." It was, and measured
from the running build rather than guessed at, in the window group's own
local z (0 is the wall, +z is into the room):

| | local z |
|---|---|
| glass | 0.000 |
| louvres, front face | 0.044 |
| **grille bars** | **0.039 – 0.061** |
| curtain cloth, back face | **0.051** ← inside the bars |

The cloth's folds reach **4.9 cm behind its own rail point**, and the rail
sat at 0.10 — so its back face lived at 0.051, a centimetre inside the
grille. Then it swings, and that is the second half of it: the swing is
`(sin·0.022 + sin·0.013) · noteStorm`, a rotation about x at the rail,
and **positive x drives the hem toward the window**. In play `noteStorm`
is 1, so the hem travelled ±5 cm and sliced the bars every few seconds.
In scene A `noteStorm` goes to **6** — 0.21 rad on a 1.42 m drop is a
**30 cm** lunge, a quarter of a metre through the grille, the glass and
the wall behind them.

So the fix is two things, not one:

1. **The rail moves to 0.21.** Measured over a full swing cycle, the
   cloth's nearest approach to the bars is now **4.9 cm in play**. (0.175
   was tried first and measured at 2 mm in a storm — arithmetically clear,
   but not a gap worth trusting on a phone.)
2. **The backward lean is clamped, and only backward.** `rotation.x` is
   capped at 0.045 rad; forward, into the room, it may lunge as far as
   the storm sends it, because that direction is free and that is the
   drama. 0.045 sits ABOVE the 0.035 the swing reaches under its own
   steam, so **in play the clamp never bites and the motion is exactly
   what it always was**. At `noteStorm` 6 it holds the cloth **2.9 cm**
   off the bars instead of 30 cm through them.

It hangs over the back edge of the desk now instead of tucking behind it,
which is what a curtain over a desk under a window does; the hem (0.82)
stays clear above the desktop (0.76), and a swing about a rail only ever
lifts a hem, never drops it.
