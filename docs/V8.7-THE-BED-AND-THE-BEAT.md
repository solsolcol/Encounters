> happens, the player must tap on his bed to officially begin and trigger the

>
> after completing the minigame, the objective hud must trigger the flash and

>

Two of these are ENGINE work and two are the chapter's, and the split matters:
"this should be a standard" is a request for the engine to own a behaviour, not
for one chapter to perform it. So the objective beats and the event briefing
are seams every chapter gets for free from here on, and the bed zone is
episode 2 chapter 1's own furniture.

Everything below is absent from chapters 1–5 by construction: episode 1
declares no objective, no timer, no hotspots and no events, so `paintObjective`
never shows a box there and `kitEvent` is never called. The base game is
byte-identical in behaviour and was checked as a control (§5).

## 1 · THE OBJECTIVE IS A BEAT, NOT A LABEL  (`src/main.js`, `shell.html`)

v8.3 already flashed the box when its words changed. What was missing is that
an order FINISHED is worth as much on screen as an order given — a player who
does the right thing should be told so.

A change of objective is now **two beats, played in order**:

| beat | look | sound | holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| `done` | jade; the box says OBJECTIVE COMPLETE | `uiconfirm` 0.55 | 1.35 s |
| `new` | sodium; the new order | `uiconfirm` 0.40 at rate 1.28 | 0.12 s |
| `timeup` | red | `uiclick` 0.50 at rate 0.78 | 0.12 s |

Each beat also buzzes the phone (45 / 25 / 60 ms).

**Why there is a queue at all.** The kit's law since v7.0 is that a verb may
only MUTATE state and the FRAME is the only thing that touches the DOM — a
chapter calls `kit.objective()` from inside `build()`, which runs before `$`,
`ui` or `state` exist. So `kitObjectiveSet` pushes beats onto `objQ` and
`paintObjective` plays them one at a time. `kitObjective` itself is still the
plain target text, which is what `worldState` saves and what `kitDebug`
reports, so nothing about the save changed.

`OBJ_DONE_HOLD` is read against **wall time**, not the frame's `dt`. That is
the same law the chapter clock learned at v7.1 and the kit's countdown at
v7.4: `dt` is clamped to 0.05 s, so on a box drawing one frame a second a
1.35 s banner would hold for half a minute.

**The escape hatch, and why it has to exist.** `kit.objective(text, { complete:
false })` skips the completion beat. A HUD that says OBJECTIVE COMPLETE when
the fall-in clock has just run out is telling the player a lie, and a lie from
the HUD is worse than no banner. Four calls in e2c1 use it:

- the whistle replacing "stand by your bed" — it INTERRUPTS, it does not complete;
- `objLate` when the 14-second fall-in expires;
- `objWarn`, which escalates the evening rather than ending it;
- `kit.objective(null)` at three in the morning, which is a scene change.

Everything else takes the default and gets the two beats.

**The timer.** Starting one queues a `new` beat (a countdown appearing IS a
change worth seeing), and a beat of the same kind already queued in the same
tick is coalesced — the objective and its timer land together as one flash, not
two. Expiry queues its own `timeup` beat BEFORE running the callback, so
whatever the chapter sets next lines up behind it.

`kitReset()` clears the queue and the `done` class (`objReset()`), so a replay
never inherits a beat aimed at the run that just ended.

## 2 · AN EVENT MAY BRIEF BEFORE IT RUNS  (`src/main.js`)

`kit.event({ ..., brief: 'text', briefButton: 'START' })`. The overlay opens on
the instructions with a START button and **nothing runs**: the clock, the
items and the beats all wait on the press. Absent by default, so every event
already shipped is untouched.

Two details that are not obvious:

- `evStart()` split into `evStart` (decides) and `evBegin` (sets the running
  panel up), and `evBegin` resets `e.t` to 0 — so the briefing cannot eat any
  of the player's time.
- `layout` is forced to `'button'` while briefing and restored on START. A
  `full`-layout event counts a tap anywhere on the overlay as a press, so
  without this a stray tap would skip the instructions the player is being
  asked to read.

The FIXTURE chapter declares it (its pipe sequence briefs), so `fixturetest`
proves the seam rather than only the chapter that asked for it. Four new
promises: `evBriefHolds`, `evBriefStopsClock`, `evBriefStarts`, `evBriefRuns`,
plus `hotspotEventBriefs`. `evBriefStopsClock` is the one that matters — the
test event's `each` is 0.2 s, so an unbriefed run would have missed both items
long before the press.

## 3 · ONE SCREEN FLASH  (`kit.flash`, `#hudflash`)

A wash of colour from the edges in, gone in half a second:
`kit.flash({ color, secs })`. Same law as everything else in the kit — the verb
sets a field, `flashRun()` in the frame touches the element.

And `pitch` now reaches a chapter through `CHCTX`, beside `camera` and `yaw`,
so a chapter can level the lens as well as turn it.

## 4 · THE CHAPTER  (`src/chapters/e2/e2c1.js`)

### 4.1 The lie-down toggle is gone

Chad was right on both halves. Tapping the bed by day toggled the LYING POSE —
something the chapter never needs the player to do, since lights out lies him
down itself — and standing back up required the bed to be on screen while the
bed was the thing under the lens. v7.4 papered over that with a special case;
this removes the thing that needed the special case.

`bedWants()` now says in ONE place what the bed can do at any moment:

| phase | what a press does |
|---|---|
| `night` / `decide` | opens the decision |
| `standby` | begins the standby bed |
| `free`, two things looked at | turns in early |
| anything else | **nothing — and the prompt is not offered** |

That last row is the fix for "it may make the player stuck": `pileInView()`
returns false when the answer is nothing, so the badge never appears and a
press falls through to the hotspots instead of dying on the mattress.

Two geometry corrections fell out of it:

- **the anchor tested is the bunk's MIDDLE, not its mattress.** v7.5's law —
  a hotspot anchor must sit near eye height or it is never offered — had never
  been applied to the bed itself. Standing in the circle beside his own bed,
  the mattress at 0.55 m is 60° under the lens, so `inView()` said no and the
  badge for the standby bed could not appear at all. `pileScreen` now projects
  `BED.low + 0.45`.
- **within 1.3 m the bed is what you are looking at**, whichever way the head
  is turned — the rule the night decision already had, now unconditional.

The words followed: `act`/`actTouch` said "Lie down", the hint for a toggle
that no longer exists. They say "E at your bed" / "Tap your bed" now.

### 4.2 The bed zone

`ZONE_R = 1.8` is **both** the radius drawn on the floor and the radius
`updateDay` tests. Before this the arrival fired at 1.8 m and the standby bed
at 2.0, with nothing on the floor to say where either of them was — what is
shown and what fires can no longer drift apart.

A group on the FLOOR beside the bed (not on the mattress — what is being asked
for is somewhere to stand): an additive disc that breathes, a bold rim, and a
ring travelling out from the centre. Every animation is driven off the world
clock rather than a per-frame increment, so it looks the same at 60 frames a
second and at 6.

It shows when the bed is a place to get to — on arrival, when the section is
sent back to it, and once lights out has been called for — and the minigame's
own phase turns it off, because the overlay owns the screen then.

### 4.3 Reaching it

`reachedBed()`: the circle blows out, `uiconfirm` at 0.62 with a low `uiclick`
a frame behind it, the kit's screen flash in jade, the phone buzzing 70 ms.
Then the HUD's own two beats, which the engine plays for any objective change.

On ARRIVAL the camera also turns to the room and the balcony — where the
whistle is about to come from — over 0.55 s rather than on one frame, because
a snap at this speed reads as a bug. The tween writes an ABSOLUTE angle, so
letting go of the mouse hands control straight back. Measured on the shipped
build: the lens lands **0.000 rad off target**.

On STANDBY it does not start the minigame. It confirms the arrival and hands
the player the press — Chad's "the player must tap on his bed to officially
begin". Walking back from the balcony used to open a timed reaction test
before anyone had looked up.

### 4.4 The briefing

The standby bed declares `brief`. Its first item used to be named and gone
inside 1.3 s while the player was still reading the panel it had arrived in.

### 4.5 The replay

`reset()` clears `stoodBy`, `zoneFlare` and the zone's visibility. The v8.1 /
v8.2 law, a third time: **a value stated by a phase must be cleared by whatever
resets that phase**, or the next run begins with the bed already "reached".

## 5 · WHAT WAS MEASURED

Two probes against the real hosted build, both capturing `pageerror` and
console first (v8.5's law).

**`dbg-v87.mjs` — the day, start to evening.** Every check a number or a class,
never a screenshot:

- the bed offers nothing on the way in, and a press there returns false;
- walking into the circle: COMPLETE banner up, screen flash fired (watched by
  a `MutationObserver` — a 0.55 s class is gone before a round trip can sample
  it), both trigger sounds decoded, camera 0.000 rad off the balcony;
- the fall-in objective arrives with NO false completion;
- standing outside the circle in `standby` does not start the minigame, and
  standing INSIDE it still does not — the tap does;
- the briefing shows, holds the clock at t = 0 across 2.5 s, and starts on the
  press;
- eight taps, the sequence resolves, the evening's objective and timer land.

**`dbg-v87b.mjs` — the paths one play-through does not walk.**

- EPISODE 1 as the control: no objective, no hotspots, the pile's badge shows,
  `interactNow()` opens the decision, the flash layer never lights. Untouched.
- the evening: no circle until the warning, the bed stays silent until two
  things have been looked at, then answers; the warning brings the circle up
  with no false COMPLETE; the tap turns in early.
- a replay: back to `arrive`, the circle back, the objective back, and walking
  in fires the arrival and its flash again.

**The suite: all 24 harnesses green**, run in foreground pairs.

One harness lesson worth keeping. The first version of the new `fixturetest`
checks SAMPLED at fixed waits (300 ms, then 1800 ms) and failed under load
while passing standalone — the banner is 1.35 s of wall time but can only be
PAINTED on a frame, and this container draws about one a second. They poll
now, and the `complete: false` check uses a `MutationObserver` rather than two
samples, because a banner that flickered past between them would go unnoticed.
**A fixed wait in a harness is a coin toss, which is worse than no check.**
