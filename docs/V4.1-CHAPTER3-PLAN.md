## THE CHAPTER, AS A PIECE OF FILM

**The thesis, in one line.** Chapter 1's terror was DISTANCE — she was over
there, and then she was closer. Chapter 2's was SMALLNESS — a room with
nowhere to go. Chapter 3's is **COMPANY**: you are standing in a crowd of
forty people, and she is sitting in row four facing the wrong way, and not
one of them turns round.

That is the whole design. Every decision below serves it.

**Where.** The open car park at the foot of James's block. A seventh-month
ritual **tentage**: red-and-white striped canvas on a scaffold frame,
fluorescent tubes wired along the poles, rows of red plastic chairs, a long
altar table at the front, giant joss sticks, a brazier of burning paper at
the edge, a Taoist priest with a hand drum, and a **tang-ki** — a spirit
medium — going into trance on a stool before the altar.

**Why there.** Chapter 1 is the void deck of this block. Chapter 2 is his
bedroom in it. Chapter 3 is the car park below it. One block, three scales,
and the tent has been up all week — which is where the note in chapter 1 came
from. It also means `hdb.glb` is reused a third time and costs nothing.

**When.** The night after chapter 2. Still the seventh month.

**Who.**

- **James** — the player, voiced (`EkK5I93UQWFDigLMpZcX`, eleven_v3).
- **The medium (tang-ki)** — at the front, on a stool, in trance. Bare
  shoulders, a yellow sash, a flag in each hand. He is the "unfamiliar
  behaviour" the chapter is about. **He is not the threat.** That is the
  teaching, and the staging must never once suggest otherwise.
- **The priest** — an older man beside the altar with a hand drum and a
  wooden fish, chanting.
- **The auntie at the paper table** — the experienced practitioner. She folds
  joss paper at the tent's edge, she has seen this a hundred times, and she is
  who choice C asks. She needs a NEW voice, distinct from chapter 2's Mother
  (Matilda, `XrExE9yKIg1WjnnlVkGX`) — record the id here when chosen.
- **Her** — the engine's ghost, because she is the game's and not any one
  chapter's. In this chapter she is **in the audience**.

**Respect.** A tang-ki ceremony is a real, living practice and real people's
faith. The chapter is not permitted to caricature it. The priest and the
medium are competent men doing a job they have done for thirty years; the
auntie is unbothered; the crowd is bored, not enraptured. The horror is
NOT the ritual. The horror is the thing sitting quietly in row four that the
ritual has nothing to do with. Choice C's whole payoff is a woman explaining,
kindly and without mysticism, that the man at the front is fine.

## THE STAGE (coordinates — the single source of truth)

The tent runs along **z**, altar at −z, entrance at +z. The player walks in
from the dark car park, exactly as chapter 1 walks in off the grass.

| thing | position |
|---|---|
| tent interior | x ∈ [−6, 6], z ∈ [−9, 9]; eave y = 3.1, ridge y = 4.1 |
| altar table | x 0, z −7.4 |
| the medium's stool | x 0, z −5.6 |
| the priest | x −1.5, z −6.6 |
| giant joss sticks | x ±1.9, z −7.0 |
| brazier (the warm light — `stage.fireLight`) | x 5.0, z −5.2 |
| paper table (the auntie) | x 4.6, z −1.0 |
| seating: 6 rows × 9 chairs | x −3.6 … 3.6 (0.9 apart), z −4.4 … 2.6 (1.4 apart) |
| `spawn` | **(0.8, 1.62, 12.4)** — out in the dark, facing the tent |
| `shrine` (HER anchor) | **(0.0, −0.9)** — the middle of the seating |
| `ghostHome` | **(2.7, 2.6)** — the back row, far side |
| `bounds` | minX −8.4, maxX 8.4, minZ −8.6, maxZ 14.0 |

**Why the shrine is the seating and not the altar.** `shrine` is the engine's
anchor for HER — the radius `appearAt` is measured from it, and it is where
`ghostPlaceBehindBurner` puts her first. Putting it on the altar would say
"the ritual is the haunting", which is the exact opposite of the chapter's
teaching, and would also make the entrance the only safe ground. Putting it in
the middle of the crowd says "the crowd is the haunting", which is true, and
it puts the relief OUTSIDE the tent — so walking out (choice D) is real relief
and walking to the altar (the interactable) is real pressure. Chapter 2 got
this wrong once and it made the family's altar the source of the haunting;
the note is in that chapter's DATA and it applies here.

**Her reach**, sized for a 12 × 18 m tent — between the deck's numbers and the
bedroom's:

```
minDist 2.4 · appearAt 9.0 · near 3.5 · far 8.0
cross [4, 7] · away [4, 8] · behind 2.2
roam { minX −5.6, maxX 5.6, minZ −5.0, maxZ 9.6 }
```

`roam.minZ = −5.0` keeps her out of the ritual space between the front row and
the altar. She belongs in the chairs.

Spawn is 13.3 m from the shrine, `appearAt` is 9.0 — so the player walks about
four metres before entering her territory, the same beat chapter 1 opens on.

## THE TWO COLLISION HEIGHTS — the seam that makes the crowd work

Two different y values, and chapter 3 lives in the gap between them:

- `collide(nx, nz)` tests the player at **y = 1.0** (`main.js:4041`).
- `lineClear(x, z)` marches HER sightline at **y = 1.4** (`main.js:778`).

So a blocker box that spans y 0 → 1.15 **stops the player and does not block
her sightline.** That is exactly what a row of chairs needs to be: you cannot
walk through the seating, and she can still be seen over it from anywhere in
the tent. Build the seating blockers by hand rather than through
`Box3.setFromObject().expandByScalar(0.22)`, which would grow them to y 1.17+
and start eating her appearances.

Blockers, then: the six seating rows (low), the altar table, the paper table,
the brazier, and the tent's four corner poles. Nothing else.

## PERFORMANCE — forty people for two draw calls

The crowd is the one genuinely new cost in this chapter, and it is cheap if it
is instanced:

- **Chairs** — one merged geometry (seat + back + four legs), one
  `InstancedMesh`, ~54 instances, 1 draw call.
- **Seated crowd** — one merged low-poly figure (torso, head, thighs), one
  `InstancedMesh`, ~31 instances, per-instance colour via `instanceColor` for
  shirt variety, 1 draw call. A slow per-instance breathing offset costs 31
  matrix writes a frame, which is nothing.
- **Standing figures at the edges** — 6, same instanced mesh, different scale.
- **The medium, the priest and the auntie** — individual meshes, because the
  scenes direct them.

Everything else is chapter-1-scale: a canvas roof (two quads), a frame of
boxes, an altar, some joss, a brazier with the existing ember/smoke idiom.

`LOW` (from ctx) halves the crowd and drops the roof's second layer.

## THE FOUR CUTSCENES

Four completely different registers on purpose: cold, violent, warm, wide.

### A · OBSERVE QUIETLY — *good* (~12 s)

The scene about **not acting**, so the camera barely moves and the world does
the work. He stays at the back. Three times the camera drifts back to the
front and then away again, and each time it comes back she is nearer:

1. row four, the chair facing the wrong way — and it is empty now
2. a shadow on the canvas roof with no body under it
3. and then she is in the aisle, three rows closer

No scream, no chase. It ends on the third look, with her four metres off in
the aisle, perfectly still, the crowd still facing front — and a slow fade
while nothing at all happens. The good answer is rewarded with **information
and no cost**, and that is scarier than a jump.

### B · JOIN THE RITUAL — *worst* (~14 s)

The loud one. The camera pushes down the aisle to the front. The drum gets
louder, the fluorescents start to strobe, the chant doubles into something
that is not language, the FOV creeps in and the roll builds, the joss smoke
thickens until the front row is a silhouette. Then everything stops — one
frame of dead silence — and she is inches from the lens. Scream. The camera
goes over backwards.

And then hands on his arms: the auntie hauling him out of it, the tent heard
from a long way off, her voice — *"Boy! Boy, come out. You cannot stand
there."* — which is the source's own consequence line, staged.

### C · QUESTION WHAT YOU SEE — *best* (~17 s, the longest)

The quiet one, and the only scene in the game with a conversation in it. He
turns **away** from the ritual — the first time in the chapter the camera
looks off the front — and walks to the paper table at the side. The light here
is warmer, the drum is behind him and small.

> **Auntie:** *"That one? He is the tang ki. The god borrows his body, that is
> all. He has done this thirty years. Watch his hands, not his face."*
>
> **James:** *"Is it real, auntie?"*
>
> **Auntie:** *"Real, not real, I don't know. I know he wakes up tomorrow and
> drives a lorry."*

Then the payoff. She looks past him at the seating, and her folding stops for
exactly one second, and she says it flat:

> **Auntie:** *"…You. Don't sit in the back row tonight."*

Which lands both halves of the teaching at once: the medium is a man doing a
job (unusual ≠ supernatural), AND there is something here, and the person with
thirty years of experience can tell. The best answer pays out in *information*
— which is precisely what the source says it does.

Close on a slow turn back to the seating: the back row, one chair, empty, and
still rocking. Chime, dread, fade.

### D · LEAVE THE GATHERING — *bad* (~13 s)

He turns and goes. The camera walks the aisle out, past the brazier, into the
car park. The tent's light falls off behind him and the drum gets small. At
about fifteen metres he stops and turns round — and it is exactly the shot the
film opened on, which is the point of it.

Forty people, all facing the front. Except one, standing at the back of the
seating, facing **out**. Facing him. At that distance she is a pale smudge.

He keeps backing away — **and she stays the same size.** (`ghostGlide` moves
her back at the same rate the camera dollies, so her projected size never
changes. Cheap, and horrible.) Fade.

**Contract** (CLAUDE.md, unchanged): ElevenLabs; James is

**The two traps that cost time last build, both from V4-CHAPTER2-PLAN:**

1. **eleven_v3 fails on long lines with fussy punctuation** — five of eleven
   failed at v4.0. Write every line as two plain short sentences with one tag
   at the front. No mid-line ellipsis, no inline `[exhale]`, no comma splice,
   no em-dash.
2. **The generator's output level is not to be trusted.** `fan` came back at
   −42 dB against a pack that sits at −20 to −25; `doorcreak` came back at −9.
   Measure every file with `volumedetect` against the existing pack and
   correct with `volume=NdB` on encode. Every time.

**Pack budget.** 72 sounds / 5.5 MB today. Nineteen more short ones is about
+1.3 MB. CLAUDE.md flags a compression pass as wanted before chapter 3 — it
is a plain download-speed improvement, not a blocker, and it is Chad's call
whether to spend the time. Keep the new loops to 4–6 s and the stings to
2–4 s so this build does not make the decision harder.

**`STING_SAMPLE` rows are engine edits** — `drum`, `ritual`, `cymbal`, `gong`,
`burn` and the twelve `v3*` lines all need a row, or the cue is SILENT with no
error. `chaptertest`'s cue check is what catches that, and it is the only

## RULES THIS BUILD MUST NOT BREAK

1. **Adding a chapter must not add a harness.** ch3 is validated by the
   existing `chaptertest` (data + cues), `fixturetest` (the engine is
   chapter-agnostic) and `leaktest` (dispose gives the GPU back). If ch3 needs
   a new harness, the design is wrong.
2. **v3.3 is the reference standard for the base game.** A ch3 change that
   reddens a base-game harness is a regression in chapters 1 and 2, not a test
   to relax.
3. **`dispose()` discipline copied exactly from ch2**, plus the two
   `InstancedMesh` geometries and their `instanceColor`, or leaktest fails.
4. **Mutate, never reassign** the chapter-derived values — `OFFER_POS` aliases
   `SHRINE`, closures captured `BOUNDS`, the ghost reads `GHOST_HOME`.
   `setChapter` does this correctly already; a chapter must not fight it.
5. **No ESM.** ch3.js is a plain script in one closure registering itself on
   `window.__CHAPTERS__`, like ch1, ch2 and the fixture. Module-level texture
   helpers get THREE through the `let _THREE = null` pattern ch2 uses.
6. **Never hand-edit a string without re-exporting the sheet.**
7. Both builds stay green, and `?ch=ch3` must boot chapter 3 directly — which
   is also how it gets tested without playing chapters 1 and 2 first.
8. **Never delete code by line-range**, and read the FULL diff before every
   commit.

## THE CONTRACT, AS OF v4.0 (re-verified, with line numbers)

What the engine actually touches on a stage handle — and nothing else:

```
stage.world  .blockers  .ready()  .pile  .noteTex  .setNoteTexture(tex)
stage.fireLight  .flying  .snap()  .restore(s)  .reset()  .dispose()
stage.updateNotes(dt,t)  .updatePile(t)  .updateFire(t)  .updateSlow(sdt,t)
```

Everything else a chapter returns is for its own scenes to borrow.
`stage.fireLight` is the light the viewmodel hands warm from at under 7 m
(`main.js:1346`) — in chapter 3 that is the **brazier**.

The cutscene language, in full (`main.js:3141` `A(c)` and `main.js:3175`
`sceneApi(c)`):

```
verbs   tr(t0,t1,fn,ease)  step(t0,fn)  sfx(at,kind,vol)  fade(t0,t1,from,to)
        camTo(t0,t1,from,to,ease)  yawTo(...)  pitchTo(...)
        bob(t0,t1,rate,amp,baseY)  ghostGlide(t0,t1,from,to)  ghostFacePlayer(t0,t1)
easing  rawK  smoothK  mixAngle  faceFrom(x,z,tx,tz)
cast    camera yaw pitch · ghost ghostLight ghostOpacity getReveal
        handsRoot armR noteProp · prayerArm() rightHand() setHandCurl setHandPrayer
        vmKey vmFire vmHemi · stage · THREE SHRINE · dirtyShadows(n)
flags   c.endFade  c.keepFade  c.keep.ghostGone  c.handsAuto  c.ghostMix
```

A track is a description of where things ARE at time t, **never a nudge** —
the engine re-derives every track from absolute values each frame, and that is
the only reason skip and seek work. A `step` fires once. A `sfx` fires once and
is never re-fired by a seek.

**Encoding, every file, no exceptions:**

```

       -af "volume=NdB" assets/audio/<name>.mp3
```

N comes from measuring, never from guessing — `ffmpeg -i <f> -af volumedetect
-f null -` against the existing pack, which sits at roughly −20 to −25 dB
mean. At v4.0 the generator handed back `fan` at −42 and `doorcreak` at −9.

## WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED IN THE AUDIO RUN (v4.1, and it corrects v4.0)

**eleven_v3 fails about one line in three, at random.** Seven of sixteen
failed with a bare "Failed to generate audio". The v4.0 note in this repo
blamed line length and fussy punctuation, and that theory did not survive
contact: *"I knew better. I went up there anyway."* failed while *"I did not
move. I just watched it come closer."* passed, same batch, same voice, same
shape — and *"Half the block is down here tonight."*, a single plain
sentence, passed. Every failed line succeeded on a straight re-run.

So: **check `has_failures`, re-run the failures, repeat. Do not rewrite.**
A rewrite that then succeeds looks like a fix and teaches the next person
the wrong lesson, which is exactly what happened at v4.0. (Five lines were
rewritten here before the pattern broke; the rewrites were kept only because
two of them read better — *"My mother told me not to come down. I came
down."* — not because they were needed.)

**The generator's output level is still not to be trusted**, and this run
was worse than the last: 36 dB of spread across seven sounds. `drum` came
back at −57 dB mean and `tentamb` at −57, both effectively silent, against a
pack that sits at −20 to −33. Re-prompting with "LOUD, close-miked, recorded
at full level" moved `drum` to −41 and `gong` to −46, which is better and
still not enough — so every file is gain-corrected from its own
`volumedetect` reading rather than from any expectation:

| kind | target | why |
|---|---|---|
| one-shots | peak −3.0 dBFS | the pack's one-shots sit at −0 to −8 |
| loops | mean −27 dB, peak capped −3 | `amb` is −27.6, `fan` −24.3, `clock` −33 |
| voice | peak −1.8 dBFS | every existing line in the pack is −1.2 to −2.6 |

`scratchpad/ch3aud/encode.sh` is that script. `gong` needed +26.5 dB and its
noise floor ended 23 dB above the pack's quietest one-shot — accepted,
because it only ever plays under a cymbal and a crowd, and noted here so
nobody is surprised by it later.

**`curl` and `ffmpeg` eat the loop's stdin.** `while read -r name url; do
curl -o "raw_$name.mp3" "$url"; done < list.txt` silently drops the FIRST
CHARACTER of every name from line two onwards — `v3aunt2` becomes `3aunt2`
and the file lands somewhere nothing will look for it. Both read stdin
unless told not to. `< /dev/null` on every command in the loop, and list the
directory afterwards.

## THE BUG THE WHOLE SUITE MISSED, AND WHAT WAS DONE ABOUT IT

`ch3.js` shipped a stale name — the returned stage still listed `roofL` and
`roofR` after the roof was rebuilt as one folded plane called `roof`. So
`build()` threw on that line, `window.__enc` was never assigned, and
`?ch=ch3` was a black screen.

**Twenty-two harnesses were green while that was true.** Every one of them:

- `chaptertest` never calls `build()` — it is plain Node, there is no WebGL
- `fixturetest` builds the FIXTURE, which is the whole point of it
- `hosted`, `title`, `final`, `cine`, `sanity`, `pile`, `step`, `inv`,
  `resume`, `state`, `restart`, `ghost`, `sound`, `perf`, `motion`,
  `census`, `text`, `csp` all play chapter 1
- `leaktest` called `rebuildStage()` with no argument, which rebuilds the
  chapter already loaded — chapter 1

Nothing in the suite had ever run chapter 2's `build()` either. The hole was
there through the whole of v4.0 and only luck kept chapter 2 out of it.

**The fix, and it adds no harness** (which is the rule): `leaktest` now
builds EVERY registered chapter once, in id order, before it starts
measuring, and asserts each one comes back with a world, blockers and a
finite `pileDist()`. It is the natural place — it already owns
`rebuildStage` — it costs a few seconds, it needs no per-chapter code, and
chapter 4 cannot ship broken the same way.

**What found it** was a one-off probe (`dbg-ch3.mjs`, gitignored) that boots
`?ch=ch3`, plays the opening film, walks the aisle, opens the decision and
runs all four scenes. Worth writing again for chapter 4: it is the only
thing that exercises a new chapter's scenes at all, and a scene that throws
on its first verb is silent everywhere else.

## AND THEN IT BECAME A MORNING

Chad, mid-build: *"the medium event should be in the morning instead of a
night scene."* He is right about the practice as well as the picture — a
seventh-month tentage ceremony is a daytime event. The tang-ki goes into
trance under a tent in the sun, with traffic going past and half the block
watching in slippers.

**It also made the chapter better, which is the part worth writing down.**
Chapters 1 and 2 hide her in the dark, because that is what dark is for.
There is nowhere to hide at ten in the morning. She is simply sitting in row
four, in daylight, in front of forty people, and not one of them turns
round — the horror has to survive being looked straight at, and it does.

**THE TENTH LEAK.** The sky is the engine's: one gradient dome, one fog, one
hemisphere, one moon, one fill, all tuned for a void deck at midnight. Fixed
exactly as the other nine were — a chapter declares `daylight`,
`applyDaylight()` MUTATES those objects in place next to
`applyGhostTerritory()`, and chapter 1's night is the default, so chapters 1
and 2 do not move. The dome's gradient is a 64-pixel canvas repainted and
marked `needsUpdate`, which is cheaper than a second texture and keeps the
material's identity.

```
daylight: { stops, bg, fog:[c,d], hemi:[sky,ground,i],
            key:[c,i,x,y,z], fill:[c,i], stars, moon }
```

**What the chapter itself had to change**, all of it lighting rather than
design:

| | night | morning |
|---|--:|--:|
| the tubes | 6.2 | 3.6 — they are still on, they are just not the key any more |
| the key spot | 17 | 9 |
| the canvas bounce | 0.32 | 0.72, sky over hot tarmac |
| the canvas | plain shadowed | slightly emissive: canvas is thin, so its underside GLOWS |
| the altar flame | 9 | 3.4 — a candle in daylight is a candle |
| the brazier | 7 | 2.8 |
| the block | ×0.34 (a silhouette) | ×0.72 (a building) |
| the ground | chapter 1's ground map, 38–72/255 | concrete, and no amount of tinting would have made the other one work |
| the haze | 0.26 | 0.34 — incense in a sunbeam is worth more than incense in the dark |
| her reveal in the film | 0.34 opacity, a 0.7 glow | 0.50 opacity, a 0.25 glow. A glow at ten in the morning reads as a lamp |

**The film's opening is inverted rather than rewritten.** The night version
faded up on a box of light in a black car park; you cannot have that shot at
ten in the morning, so the equivalent is the other way up — fade in on the
glare and the top of the block, then tilt DOWN and find the tent underneath,
already full. Same job, opposite gesture. Everything from the walk-in to the
drum stopping is unchanged: that beat is the one thing daylight cannot touch.

**Two voice lines said "tonight"** and were re-recorded: `v3near` is now
*"Half the block is down here this morning."* and `v3aunt4` *"Listen to me,
boy. Do not sit in the back row today."* The `brief` in DATA changed the same
way, so the text sheet needed a fresh export.

**The fake lit windows are gone.** Fourteen quads, wrong twice — first
hand-placed into the sky above the block's roofline, then placed off its
BOUNDING BOX, which spans x −21..27 and z −41..−3 because the export
includes its podium, so they landed on a plane in front of the tent. At ten
in the morning a lit window is not a thing anyway.
