## How it passed the review

It passed because the review could not have caught it. v6.16 was verified
with a wide shot of fourteen trees and one look up into a crown. Neither
puts a trunk in the middle of the frame, and a tree lying on its side at
twenty metres still reads as green. The check that finds it is to
**photograph each kind on its own**, and that is the check that was skipped.
It is now how a tree change gets verified, and it is what identified the
oak in one frame.

## The bug: an empty slab wins a thinness contest

`preptree.mjs` chooses which way is up by looking at the model's two ends —
one is a crown, the other a trunk. v6.15 scored that as **how thin is the
outermost twelfth**, and thinness alone is won outright by an end that is
nearly empty. Measured on the oak, over all six candidate orientations:

| orientation | vertices in the base slab | how tightly they cluster on the axis |
|---|---|---|
| `y+` — the true up | **299** | 0.16 |
| `z−` — what v6.15 chose | **13** | 0.23 |
| `x±` | 13–18 | 0.00 |

Thirteen stray vertices that happen to sit near the centre beat a real
trunk of 299. The other three files have geometry at both ends, which is
why only the oak fell over — and why the fault looked like a one-off rather
than a rule that was wrong.

## The fix

The score now asks for **both**: of the vertices in the bottom tenth, what
fraction sits within 6 % of the span from the trunk axis — a trunk
clusters, a flank of crown does not — scaled down when that slab is too
sparse to mean anything (`min(1, n / 50)`). Across all four trees the true
up wins every time:

| tree | `y+` (true) | best wrong answer |
|---|---|---|
| realistic | **0.37** | 0.17 |
| oak | **0.16** | 0.06 |
| low-poly A | **1.00** | 0.39 |
| low-poly B | **0.34** | 0.17 |

And the tool **re-scores the baked model and refuses to write it** unless
`y+` wins. This matters: the first assertion written for this — a profile
check, thin at the base and widest above — **passed the lying oak**, because
a fallen tree sliced horizontally also has thin ends and a fat middle. Only
re-scoring catches it, because it asks where the trunk *is* rather than what
the silhouette looks like.

## Density

| place | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| ch1 — the void deck, at night | 12 | **32** |
| ch1 — the East Coast memory | 9 | **21** |
| ch1 — the playground memory | 6 | **15** |
| ch3 — the car park | 14 | **34** |
| **the game** | **41** | **102** |

Counted off the live scene through the instance matrices, not off the source
lists. Every new spot stays outside the sight-lines the old ones were chosen
to protect: the corridor between the spawn and the void deck, the lens's
window to the sea and the low sun, and the playground's tracking shot.

`plantTrees` gains **`lowKeep`** — the phone's share of a stand, drawn from
the same deterministic stream, so a thinned forest is the same forest rather
than a different one. Instancing means the extra trees cost no draw calls,
but they are real triangles and a phone should not pay for a back row it can
barely see. The void deck and the car park keep 55 %, the two memories 60 %.

## Verified

- **Each kind alone**, in chapter 3: the oaks have trunks in the ground;
  every other kind unchanged.
- **All four places**, each photographed from a position that shows its
  whole stand: the car park, the void deck at night (corridor still open),
  the East Coast memory (sea and sun still visible between the banks), the
  playground memory.
- `chapter`, `leak`, `csp`, `hosted` green in 162 s. `leak` matters most:
  the stands went from 41 trees to 102 and a chapter still hands the GPU
  back everything it took.
- Deployed and byte-verified: **65/65 match, 0 mismatch**
  (deploy `6a9fc206c7c1970813c5ca3c`).

## One probe lesson, paid for four times

Framing the two memory pockets failed four times because I moved the camera
under a PAUSED film. `cineSeek` re-applies every track whose `t0` has passed
on every frame — the repo's own v5.30 law — so a camera moved by hand is put
back before the screenshot, and the one frame that came out right was the
film's own shot rather than mine. **Photograph a world from PLAY**, where
nothing owns the camera, and read the subject's position out of the scene
graph rather than recomputing it from a chapter's constants.
