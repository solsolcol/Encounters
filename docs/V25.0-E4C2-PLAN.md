## 3. The direction

### 3.1 The idea

Chapter 1 ended on *"When I got back to Singapore, I stopped feeling like myself"* and his shop at a grey dawn. This
chapter starts in that shop and ends with its colour back. The fog is the player's own sight, so the clearing is felt
as colour, focus and sound returning, not told. Between the two, the player does the lesson twice: once at the door in
the dark (check the new situation on its own evidence), once before Lersi Chin (what you saw, not what you think it
meant). The decision then asks what he does with what happened, and each answer teaches its own thing: what his words
do to someone else (A), the honest limit of a true master (B), what reaching out to the man he fears gives away (C),
and the rest that comes when there is nothing left worth proving (D).

Pong carries the chapter's title. In chapter 1 he said "Very famous. Trust me." about a man he had never met. Here he
vouches for a man he watched help his mother, and the call says the difference out loud: hearsay against a real
account from a real person, the case file's lesson as two lines of a phone call.

### 3.2 Fresh — and declared

- **Declared reuse, each with its reason:** his shop (it is his; Chad put the chapter there), seen at new hours and
  changed by the weeks; Pong (the friend who sent him, now vouching first-hand) and his voice; the charm he took in
  chapter 1 (the bag and the save carry which); the first master's voice (one voice note, ending C).
- **New:** Lersi Chin and his practice, the shop at noon in the fog and at night with the shutter half down, the
  forum, the customers' voices.
- `chaptertest`'s FRESH list (v24.0) is extended to this chapter: every asset key it loads that another chapter also
  loads must be on the declared list.

### 3.3 The look

- **The shop by day, in the fog:** noon; the cases' LEDs and the mall's light flattened by a grey that takes the colour
  out of everything; the cold's blur at the edges, the smear on every turn (his eyes lag). The one warm thing is the
  altar's red lamp, dimmed by the grey like everything else.
- **The shop at night:** after closing, the main lights off, the shutter half down with the corridor's light lying
  under its edge, the altar's lamp, the counter's LEDs low. The clearing is done here, on a mat before his altar.
- **The clearing's lift:** grey to colour over ≈ 20 s while he is still bowed: the cases' gold and the altar's red
  come back, the edges sharpen, the mall's hum loses its muffle.
- Lersi Chin's things keep the standing temple ruling where they touch it (white, yellow and gold, red only as accent;
  nothing Chinese on his side of it).

### 3.4 The sound and the score

Beds, this chapter's own copies (a sound cued by name from another chapter becomes everyone's download): the shop by
day (the mall's murmur, the LEDs' buzz, the air-con, a trolley passing), at night (the air-con, the fridge, the empty
corridor), all muffled by the fog (a low-pass on the beds, lifted in the clearing). Music: *the fog* (a slow pad with
a pulse that drags), *the visit* (plain, open), *the clearing* (under the chant, never over it), one cue per ending, and
the coda. Every candidate goes to Chad as loudness-matched previews; his ear decides.

### 3.6 The tone rules for this chapter

- Lersi Chin is sincere, competent within his own training and kind. He says what he believes ("I believe…"), never
  what is certain; he never names the first master; he asks about doctors.
- Efficacy is BELIEF throughout: no line claims the clearing cured anything; "afterwards I gradually felt like myself
  again" is his, not the world's.
- No jump scare: the shape by the door is a flower stand, and the camera never confirms anything supernatural.
- Respect: Lersi Chin's things are never set low or handled carelessly; one wai to Lersi Chin, three prostrations
  only to the Buddha.
- Pong is not a villain: he sent him to the first master on hearsay, knows it, and says sorry.
- He is never seen; the first master is never seen.

### 4.1 PLAY I — THE SHOP, IN THE FOG (`shop`)

- **P1 · The wallet.** Objective: *Find your wallet.* The till drawer, his bag under the counter, his jacket over his
  stool: each shows it isn't there, and the objective's own words blur and re-form between searches (the fog eats the
  line, never the marker). It is in the drinks fridge behind the counter, beside the bottles of water. Him: "…What is
  it doing in here?" No timer, no failure; three presses, and the marker shows where to look next.
- **P2 · The shape by the door (the trial's 5.4 as play).** Closing time: the main lights off, the shutter half down.
  As he turns from the counter, something tall by the door sways in the dark. The banner observes; it never orders. He
  can switch on the light, or stand still and watch: both are the trial's good answers, and both find the same thing —
  the Grand Opening flower stand, its flowers dried, its ribbons lifting in the air-con's draft. Him: "…The flowers. I
  never took them away." (If he walks at it in the dark, the light comes on as he reaches it. There is no wrong press.)
- **P3 · The call.** The note on the counter, CALL PONG; a press calls him.
  - Pong: "Brother! Long time no reply. You OK or not?"
  - Him: "Not really. Since I came back… I can't think. I found my wallet in the fridge."
  - Pong, a beat: "Bro… I know a famous ajahn who is good at clearing dirty things. He helped my mother before. This
    one I can vouch for. He just arrived in Singapore this week. I'm coming down with your stock tomorrow — I can bring
    him over."
  - Him: "Last time you said 'trust me' also."
  - Pong: "I know. Sorry, bro. That one I never met. This one, I was there with my mother."
  - Him: "…Bring him."
  Pong's offer is Chad's line as he wrote it, with one clause added (§2.2 #1). The fog's grey stays.

### 4.2 THE VISIT — a film (≈ 25 s)

The next evening, after closing; the shutter half down. Pong ducks under it with a carton of stock, then lifts its edge
for Lersi Chin, who steps in with a cloth bag on his shoulder. Him (VO): "Eventually I approached another Thai
practitioner I trusted, Lersi Chin, a true Lersi master." Lersi Chin looks along the cases, then up at the altar, and
wais the Buddha before anyone. Pong: "Lersi Chin, this is my friend. His shop." Lersi Chin, to him: "New shop. Tired
owner."

### 4.3 PLAY II — THE CLEARING, IN THE SHOP AT NIGHT (`clearing`)

- **P4 · The bow.** Lersi Chin spreads a mat on the shop floor before the altar and sits. Objective: *Pay respect at
  the altar.* Three prostrations to the Buddha (`kit.pray` and the bow), then a wai to Lersi Chin. Lersi Chin: "Sit,
  sit. You look like you haven't slept."
- **P5 · What he tells him — the trial's 5.2, a pick.** Lersi Chin: "Tell me what happened there." The four options,
  verbatim:
  - A **Tell Lersi Chin exactly what you saw** → Lersi Chin: "Good. You tell me what you saw, not what you think. Then
    I can look for myself."
  - B **Ask Lersi Chin what he thinks it means** → "Seeing is not understanding. Someone near a master — you
    don't know yet what he is to him."
  - C **Assume the first master is using black magic** → "You don't know that. Suspicion is not evidence. I will look
    — then we talk."
  - D **Ask Lersi Chin what you should do next** → "First, we look at you. Not at him."
  Each answer carries the trial's lesson in a caption. Awareness only (§2.2 #5).
- **P6 · The examination — watched.** He takes his wrist and asks the ordinary questions: sleep, food, the wallet,
  "You saw a doctor?" ("Yes. They said stress.") Then he sits with his eyes closed (his practice — นั่งทางใน is not
  named). His eyes open on the door, on the piece standing there facing it (Q6): "That one — from there?" He holds it
  with both hands, wais it, sets it on a clean cloth: "This one, you give to a temple. Don't keep, don't sell, don't
  throw." Then: "According to my practice, I believe something negative was placed on you." Him: "By him?" Lersi
  Chin: "I didn't say that. I said on you."
- **P7 · The clearing — a bow, then watched.** Objective: *Bow your head.* The press bows him; the clearing plays as a
  film he cannot skip the first time (`kit.film`, noSkip until sealed once): homage to his teachers; Namo tassa ×3; the
  blessing (Q5) captioned, Pali over its meaning, timed on the take's breaths; the holy water drunk from his cupped
  hands; the mayom bundle sprinkling it over his head and shoulders; the white thread tied at his wrist. The fog lifts
  under it over ≈ 20 s. Lersi Chin: "Go to a temple this week. Make merit. If the fog comes back — doctor first, then
  me."
- **P8 · The warning.** Lersi Chin, serious: "One more thing. Don't go looking for him. Don't call him. Don't tell
  people stories about him. Leave it there." Pong, at the shutter with the empty carton: "Bro, I wait outside." Lersi
  Chin begins to pack his bowl. The decision opens.

### 4.4 THE DECISION (`decide`)

*Lersi Chin has cleared him and warned him to leave it there. What he does now is his own.*

| B | **Ask Lersi Chin to be your teacher** (new) | good | *"Not every master can answer your deepest questions, and that is not a criticism of them."* (S1·12) | The honest limit of a true master: taught by Lersi Chin saying, kindly, that this is not his road. |
| C | **Contact the first master and confront him** (the trial's 5.3C) | bad | *"Physical distance does not automatically make confrontation wise."* | What reaching out gives away: taught by the voice note that answers him. |
| D | **Follow the warning and let it rest** (the trial's 5.3D; "walk away" said "let it rest") | best | *"Wisdom can be knowing when there is nothing more worth proving."* | Rest: taught by the next morning, when he can follow a customer's question again. |

Why these four: each is something a newly cleared man in his own shop might really do, none needs a journey, and no
two are the same scene with different thoughts. Deltas §2.2 #9.

An alternative Chad may swap in for A, B or C: **Ask Lersi Chin to send it back** (the clearer asked to return the harm
to whoever sent it). Lersi Chin declines kindly: "I clear. I don't send." It would teach that answering harm with harm
keeps you tied to it; its lesson would need Master Z's words, and the casebook's nearest is S1·20's *"You do not always
need to fight it, chase it away, challenge it, or prove that you are stronger than it."*

### 4.5 THE FOUR ENDINGS (each a whole story, ≈ 60–75 s)

**A · WORD GETS AROUND.** That night, alone at the counter, he opens a forum on his laptop and types it: an ajahn in
Isan does black magic; he went; stay away. Posted. Days later, in the fog again at the edges of the picture, the
thread has grown: people quoting him, someone asking "Proof?", someone whose aunt has gone to that ajahn for years,
someone asking which shop he runs. Pong calls: "Bro, why you post? His people calling me. They say I bring you there,
now you spoil his name." Him: "I know what I saw." Pong: "You saw. You don't know." A customer at the counter, heard:
"Eh, you the one who posted about the Isan ajahn?" His eyes stay on the tray. Him (VO): "I was sure enough to stay
away from him. I was never sure enough to say it about him." Lesson: *Personal caution requires less certainty than
making claims about another person.*

**B · NOT MY ROAD.** He wais. "Lersi Chin… will you teach me? I see things. I don't know why." Lersi Chin puts the
bowl down and sits back on the mat. A long look, then, kindly: "Why you see, what it is for — that question is bigger
than my practice. I clear. I cannot answer that one." A beat. "Someone can. Not me. When you find him, don't take my
word. Don't take Pong's word. Check his teacher. Check his people. Then go." Pong, at the shutter: "This time we
check first, bro." Lersi Chin wais him and goes out under the shutter; the shop in clear colour behind them. Him (VO):
"He had cleared me. He could not tell me what I was for — and he was honest enough to say so." Lesson: *Not every
master can answer your deepest questions, and that is not a criticism of them.*

**C · ONE MESSAGE.** They have gone; the shutter is down. On his phone, the ajahn's contact Pong sent him before the
trip. He types: "I saw the woman standing behind you." Deletes it. Types it again. Sent. The ticks turn blue. The
air-con, the fridge, a minute. A voice note: the first master, in Thai, soft (captioned): "You see well, nong. Come
back. I can teach you." He doesn't answer; the grey seeps back in at the edges of the picture. Him (VO): "I never went
back. I told him what I could see instead." Lesson: *Physical distance does not automatically make confrontation
wise.*

**D · ENOUGH.** "Thank you, Lersi Chin." A wai. Lersi Chin: "Go well. Make merit when you can." Pong, at the shutter:
"Bro, go eat something?" They go out under it. The next morning the shutter rises, chapter 1's dawn again, in full
colour this time: the dried flower stand gone, the charm on the counter in an envelope for the temple, his hand on his
pocket and the wallet there. A customer, heard, asks about a piece; he follows every word and answers simply. Him (VO):
"I had enough to decide. There was nothing more worth proving." Lesson: *Wisdom can be knowing when there is nothing
more worth proving.*

Every ending is directed shot by shot at M1 and measured for silence (no stretch of 1.5 s without a voice or a sound
with a reason) before it is voiced.

### 4.6 THE CODA — "LF>" (every ending, ≈ 40 s)
