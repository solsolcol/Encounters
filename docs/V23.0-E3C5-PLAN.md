## 1. The source (S1·10, docs/source/site-case-files.md 159–174)

*Classification: an experiment I regret · Period: during the search ·
Place: my own altar · Nature: deliberate trance — do not repeat.*

> **Hook.** The owner explained it as caring for homeless children — that
> offerings and merits give them something they never had. I saw it as
> compassion, and I kept several. Then one night curiosity got the better of
> me, and I did something I now tell everyone never to do.
>
> While I was still searching for answers, I began keeping Kumanthong. The
> way it was explained to me was as a form of care: think of it like looking
> after children who have nowhere to go, and when you make offerings and
> dedicate merits for them, you are giving them something they never had. I
> saw it as another way of making merit and showing compassion, so I kept
> several and made offerings to them regularly.
>
> Then one night, after everything else I had been through, I wanted to know
> something. What would happen if I deliberately opened myself to my own
> Kumanthong?
>
> I went through them one by one, letting my mind loosen each time. What
> happened frightened me. With each different Kumanthong I found myself
> behaving like a different child, with a completely different personality.
> My expressions changed. My mannerisms changed. Even the way I felt from
> the inside seemed to change. One after another after another.
>
> At that moment I was fascinated by it. Looking back today, I can see
> clearly how dangerous and how completely unnecessary that experiment was.
>
> **The lesson.** Even if you believe a Kumanthong belongs to you, protects
> you, and would never intentionally harm you — never deliberately invite it
> to enter or take control of your body. In traditional belief Kumanthong are
> regarded as Yin, while a living person carries Yang. Even with no ill
> intent on either side, repeatedly immersing yourself in something held to
> carry strong Yin energy is understood within that tradition as disturbing
> your own balance. And setting that aside entirely: deliberately
> surrendering control of your own consciousness is not something to
> experiment with casually, whatever you believe is on the other side. You do
> not need possession, trance or dramatic manifestations to prove a spiritual
> connection. Care for what you keep. Make merits if that is your practice.
> Treat the traditions with respect. But keep your boundaries and stay in
> control of yourself. Some lessons are only learned by living them — this is
> the one experiment I would tell everybody never to repeat.

These are the e3c3 and e3c4 numbers on the ±30 scale, by rank.

**Why four kinds and not two pairs** (Chad, 25 Oct: *"The four options are
not distinct enough."*). The first draft had two pairs. A and B both went
back in: one went further, the other made a recording. C and D both
stopped: one gave them back, the other kept a boundary. Now each option
acts on a DIFFERENT THING (the experience, other people, the three,
himself), and only A goes back in.
- **Each ending has its own world**, so the four do not look alike either.
  A is told in his BODY (the nights, a giggle that is not his). B is told on
  the SCREENS (the forum, the replies, a stranger's message). C is told in
  the KEEPER'S shop, and D in the ROOM AT DAWN. B is the only ending told on
  screens: A's business is shown by the cartons nobody ships, and D's dawn
  needs no laptop.
- **The two good answers differ in what he does with the three.** C sends
  them away and D keeps them. C's keeper says D's words out loud: *"You
  didn't need to send them away. You only needed to keep your own door
  shut."* That line is what places C under D.
- **B is not "stopping".** He never opens himself again in B, but he hands
  the experiment to strangers. The epilogue's last line, "This is the one
  experiment I would tell everybody never to repeat", lands on exactly
  what he did.

## 5. Engine work (all opt-in; episodes 1 and 2 never reach it; the fixture proves each)

The chapter's real engine build. Each piece gets a row in CLAUDE.md's "what
a chapter owns" table at release.

- **S37 · the `craft` event kind** — hands-on steps on a held prop, for the
  drinks and the incense. `kit.event({ kind: 'craft', steps: [{ g: 'twist',
  turns }, { g: 'slide', dir: 'down'|'side', len }, { g: 'tap', n },
  { g: 'hold', secs }, { g: 'swipe', dir }], at: () => screenPoint,
  onStep(i), onProgress(i, k), gentle: true, brief })`.
  - Gestures are read around the prop's projected point (`at`):
    - a twist is the signed angle the finger sweeps round it, either
      direction, needing at least 40 px of radius;
    - a slide is the drag projected on `dir`, as a share of `len` × the
      screen's height;
    - a swipe is a fast drag over 25 % of the width;
    - tap and hold are what they say.
  - Desktop under pointer lock: the mouse's circling for twist, its movement
    for slide and swipe, E held (twist at a steady speed, slide, hold),
    Space (tap). The pointer lock is released while it runs (v22.0 steady's
    law).
  - Each step draws its own looping hint AT the prop: a fingertip circling,
    pulling, tapping, pressing or flicking. That is the minigame rule (v18.2):
    `EV_DEMO.craft = 'twist'` for the brief, plus five hint classes in
    shell.html.
  - `gentle: true`: no grade bands, no price. The result reports `care`
    (false if any twist outran 720° a second). `kitReset` clears it; a
    scene's first frame aborts it.
  - The fixture proves: each gesture by REAL pointer input (v18.3 — a
    minigame probe drives the real input path) completes its step; a twist
    either way counts; a slide the wrong way does not; a swipe needs its
    speed; the hints are drawn.
- **S38 · the `yield` event kind** — press-and-hold to let go.
  - A ring at a world point fills while anything is held (a finger, the
    mouse, E or Space) over `fill` seconds. Released, it pauses (or drains
    at `back`). `auto: true` fills on its own after a beat.
  - `onHold(k)` every frame, so the chapter drives the loosening by k.
  - No grade, never fails. Its own picture, `press`: a fingertip pressing
    and holding as a ring fills.
  - The fixture proves the rate, the pause, auto, keys and mouse.
- **S39 · the `surface` event kind** — climb back against a pull.
  - A vertical gauge: the mark rises with a drag up (finger), the mouse up
    under lock, or rapid W / ↑ / Space; it sinks at a constant rate.
  - Pulls come every `[1.6, 2.4]` s (`onPull` lets the chapter ring the bells
    and draw the ring).
  - Under `floorK` it costs `slip` sanity, floored at `minSanity`. Above
    `goal` for 0.4 s, he is back.
  - `giveUp` after `secs` forces success at the full price, so it never
    soft-locks.
  - Graded on time (≤ 10 s full marks). It runs inside a film (v7.0: events
    run from play or from inside a film). Its own picture, `surface`.
  - The fixture proves: drag up wins, no input ends only by giveUp, slips
    cost and floor, keys under lock work, inside a film it runs and hands
    back.
- **S40 · `kit.handPose(name, k, { secs, p })` / `api.handPose`** — a
  library of first-person hand poses, for play and scenes:
  - `bottle` (left hand steadying a bottle, right hand on the cap, `p` turns
    the right wrist);
  - `straw` (a right-hand pinch over the bottle);
  - `lighter` (left hand holding the lighter up, right hand the sticks);
  - `place` (the right hand lowering a held thing ahead);
  - `reach` (the right hand forward and low);
  - `hips` (both hands on the hips, the forearms at the frame's lower
    corners);
  - `cross` (arms crossed low);
  - `peek` (both palms over the eyes, `p` the gap between the fingers);
  - `mouth` (both hands at the mouth — the shy figure's own pose, seen from
    inside).

  Each is searched like v20.1's GEST: no upper arm on screen on eight screen
  shapes, the hands never cross unless the pose crosses them, and never off

## 8. Camera and framing rules for this chapter

1. **Never a third-person shot of him** (no rigged adult him — §2.2 4).
   Every shot is his eyes, or a THING, or the keeper.
2. **The phone crop.** The lens is 72° VERTICAL and fixed, so a portrait
   phone sees a centre third of the width (docs/AUDIT-2026-09.md Part One):
   about 37° across on a 390 × 844 screen. From the mat (0.93 m away) the
   three span about 50°, so a phone sees one or two of them at a time. Every
   play beat that names a child turns to that child. Every shot that must
   hold all three pulls back to the bed (2.1 m, about 26°) or goes
   overhead. Each framing is solved numerically for both screens first
   (e3c3's `fitD` approach), then photographed.
3. **Macro** with a narrow lens (40–48°) at 0.20–0.35 m, never a wide lens
   close (the 8 cm near plane — v6.12). Every lens track states when it
   stops.
4. **Cuts across time** hide under black or a flash. A film begins on its own
   black (v4.6), except the possessions, which begin on the yield's last
   frame and must be continuous.
5. **A turn that hands over to a walk ends where the walk begins** (v21.30).
6. **In a cutaway away from his eyes, the room looks at HIM**: the figures'
   eyes catch the lamp toward the mat (v21.30's law, in a room of statues).
7. **The hands are never disembodied** (v20.1): every pose is searched.

## 11b. The shape of e3c5.js (build instructions)

One file, `src/chapters/e3/e3c5.js`: a plain script in one closure,
registering itself on `window.__CHAPTERS__` (no imports — CLAUDE.md
Architecture). Chapter 4's file is the model for every pattern below; copy
from it rather than reinventing.

1. **DATA at the top**: key, episode, id, title, brief, `words` (every word
   the HUD shows), `choices`, `core`, `prompt`, `spawn`, `bounds`, the
   daylight presets as named objects, `ambience` (beds as `[name, vol, pan,
   rate]`), `ghost: null`, `fireLight: null`, `sayPrefix: 'z5'`, `assets`,
   `items: []`, `preview`, `films: { intro, red, shy, little }`, `scenes:
   [scAgain, scTell, scReturn, scBoundary]`.
2. **Tables, not literals**: `SECS` (every line's measured length, with an
   `EST` set until CP11); `KIDS = [{ key: 'red', asset: 'kumanred', slot:
   0, colour, preset: 'RED', lens: 84, motif: 'k5red', toy: 'car',
   capTurns: 1.5, yieldSecs: 4.0 }, { key: 'shy', … }, { key: 'little', …,
   capTurns: 2.0, yieldSecs: 0.6, auto: true }]` — every per-child number
   lives there; `ROOM`, `ALTAR`, `MAT`, `DESK`, `BAG`, `WIN` (the room's
   measured places); `CLOCK` (the times of the night: 22:50, 23:05, 23:12,
   23:21, 23:33, 23:46, 02:47).
3. **build(ctx)**, in this order:
   - shared materials and textures declared FIRST (the v21.1 TDZ law);
   - the drawers, copied: chapter 4's room drawers, e3c1's `makeYantCloth`,
     the ROADEYE box art; new ones for the label, the sleeves, the window's
     four states, the calendar, the screens;
   - `buildRoom()`, `buildAltar()`, `buildProps()` (bottles, straws,
     lighter, sticks, bag, coin, phone), `buildShop()` (World S);
   - the scans: ONE parse each through the CHCTX loader, placed, with their
     doubles cloned from the same parse (`mkDouble(kid)`); the bells and
     hair nodes found by name;
   - the effects, each a small system with a `frame(t, k)` and a `reset()`:
     `smoke` (ribbons, flow field, `bend`), `embers`, `tears`, `water`,
     `hairSway`, `fizz`, `flame`, `windowState(n)`, `cityLights(k)`,
     `clock(s)`;
   - the line queue (chapter 4's: each line waits on the speech window,
     stated in the chapter's own clock, a held line holding the queue —
     the v9.5 shape; at M1, `say()` shows a placeholder subtitle when the
     sound has no row yet);
   - the phase machine: `applyPhase(p)` sets the room to exactly what that
     phase means (§10.1); `objectiveFor(phase)`; receipts in the phase
     string;
   - the hotspots: the bag, the packet, the three places, the three bowls,
     the mat's zone, and the three figures (`pressAim`) for the openings;
   - the films and the scenes in the cutscene language. Each film's
     look is a PURE FUNCTION of its own clock (chapter 4's
     `stage.filmO` / `stage.endT` frames; v6.4's "posed by time"), so a
     seek, a skip and a slow phone land on the same picture. Every lens
     and tint is a track that states when it stops (v6.12). No cue is
     written after a scene's last track (v12.3). Every cue is a literal
     `sfx(t, 'name')` so the engine can warm it (the CUE_RE law, v10.2);
   - the stage handle: `pile` (the altar), `hotspots`, `blockers()`,
     `snap()` / `restore()` / `reset()` / `dispose()`, `updateNotes(dt)`
     for the frame's systems (mixers ABOVE the `cine` return — v5.19),
     `arrivals` (World S's door), and the probes' handles
     (`stage.state()`, `stage.kids()`).
4. **reset()** clears everything §10.1 lists. **dispose()** frees every
   material and texture it made (leaktest), never the shared kit's.
