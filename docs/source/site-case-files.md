### S1·01 — The Name That Was Given

*2026-08-17 · Classification: message received*  
`Period Before my birth · Place Family home altar · Nature Trance & message received · Runtime 0:11`

**Hook.** My mother was chanting Guan Yin’s mantra at the family altar, the way she did every evening. Then the chanting stopped, and the room changed. I had not been born yet — but before that evening ended, my name had already been decided. Not by my parents.

> My mother was pregnant with me. She chanted every evening at the small altar in our home, the way she always had — Guan Yin’s mantra, nothing asked for, nothing unusual.

> That evening the chanting stopped mid‑verse. She rose and climbed onto the table. My family says she was calm. Her posture was not hers. Her voice was not hers. Nobody in that room described it as frightening; they described it as sacred, and beyond explanation.

> A message was spoken through her: the son she was carrying must be given a particular name. The cradle was still empty. I would not be born for months. That name is the one I carry today.

**The lesson.** In many traditions a name is not decoration — it is an instruction. It tells a child what they are meant to become and what they are meant to protect. Whatever you believe happened in that room, my family treated that name as a responsibility rather than a keepsake. That is the part worth keeping.

### S1·02 — The Hell Note I Should Never Have Taken

*2026-08-20 · Classification: object taken*  
`Period Primary 4 · Season Lunar Seventh Month · Nature Object taken from an offering · Runtime 0:50`

**Hook.** We were playing badminton near where the offerings were burning. In the middle of the ashes was one hell note that had not burnt at all. I was a child, and it was the only thing in that fire that had refused to burn. So I took it home and hid it.

> It was the seventh month. My friends and I were playing badminton on the open ground, near where the offerings were being burnt. In the ashes was a single hell note, perfectly unburnt. I hid it behind my headboard when I got home. I am not sure I could have told you why I wanted it.

> After that I began crying without reason. Not once — again and again. I could feel something in the room looking at me, although I never saw anything at all. My family brought me to a medium event under the void deck, which was common in those years. While I was there I felt completely normal. The moment I got home, it started again.

> My mother eventually took me to a medium. Before she explained anything, he told her I had taken something that did not belong to me. A talisman ritual was performed and something was prepared for me to drink. After that, it stopped.

**The lesson.** Not everything you find is yours to take — least of all something left at an offering. Those items were placed there with intention, for someone. You do not have to share the belief behind a practice to respect it. Respect costs you nothing; ignoring it can cost more than you expect.

### S1·03 — The Childhood Lesson I Never Forgot

*2026-08-24 · Classification: karma & reflection*  
`Period Childhood, then years later · Place Open field, then home · Nature Karma & reflection · Runtime 0:19`

**Hook.** As a boy I discovered what sunlight could do through a magnifying glass. I used it on small creatures in the grass — not out of cruelty, but because I did not yet understand what a consequence was. Many years later, something made me remember that afternoon.

> I was young enough to be fascinated and too young to be careful. A magnifying glass, a strong afternoon sun, and small creatures in the grass. I never thought of it as cruelty. I thought of it as an experiment.

> Many years later I went through a long stretch of severe itching. It lasted far longer than it should have, and there were times I scratched more than I should have. It was treated as a skin condition, and that is a reasonable explanation which I do not dismiss.

> But as I grew older and went further into practice, I kept returning to that field, that glass, that afternoon. I am not telling you the two are connected. I am telling you that I could never stop wondering.

**The lesson.** Children often act out of curiosity rather than cruelty, because they have not yet learnt what a consequence is. Growing up means learning compassion for the things that cannot object — including the smallest ones. And karma is not best understood as punishment. Karma can be a teacher. When we know better, we should do better.

### S1·04 — The 3 AM Shower

*2026-08-26 · Classification: recurring disturbance*  
`Period BMT, Hawk Company · Place Pulau Tekong bunk · Nature Recurring disturbance · Runtime 0:39`

**Hook.** My bed was at the front of the platoon bunk, right beside the toilet. For several nights running, at almost exactly three in the morning, the shower would turn itself on. One night I got up to look.

> During my BMT days I was posted to Hawk Company. My bed was at the very front of the platoon bunk, next to the toilet — the worst spot in the room, and the one nobody wanted.

> For several nights the same thing happened at almost the same time. Around three in the morning the shower would suddenly start running, and the sound would wake me. At first I ignored it. But after night upon night, curiosity got the better of me. One night I got out of bed and walked over to check. The shower was running. There was nobody there. I went straight back to my bed.

> That night something else happened. Lying under my blanket, I felt something tugging at it. I could not see anyone. I stayed where I was and tried not to react.

> After that, training became so exhausting that I simply slept through the nights. I stopped thinking about the shower. I stopped checking. I stopped paying attention to whatever might have been happening. And strangely, the disturbance eventually stopped too.

**The lesson.** Not every unexplained presence needs to be acknowledged. Rule out the ordinary causes first — faulty plumbing explains more than people expect. But if there is no danger and nothing is asking anything of you, the wiser approach is simple: don’t engage, don’t provoke, don’t feed your own fear. From a spiritual perspective, acknowledging a presence can create an awareness or a connection that was not there before. Once I stopped giving the experience my attention, it stopped giving me its attention too. Knowing how to do something does not mean we should. Understanding when not to interfere is just as important as knowing what to do.

### S1·05 — The Ghostly Cyclist

*2026-08-28 · Classification: sensation & sighting*  
`Period Infantry training · Place Jungle outfield exercise · Nature Physical sensation, then a sighting · Runtime 0:48`

**Hook.** I was at the very rear of my section that night, facing away from everyone else, looking into the jungle. Then something pressed against my leg. Not a feeling — actual pressure. I shone my torch down and there was nothing there.

> During infantry training we had an outfield exercise deep in the jungle. That night I was at the very rear of my section, facing away from the others and looking out into the dark.

> Then I felt something strange. Something was pressing against my leg. Not just a sensation — there was actual weight to it. I shone my torch straight down. Nothing. I could not see anyone, could not hear footsteps, and there was not even the sound of vegetation moving around me.

> But the pressure was still there. That alone was strange enough. I swung the torch towards it, and as the beam passed across, whatever was there was gone. The jungle was silent again.

> What I did not know until later was that something else happened that same night. Soldiers from another platoon came running out shouting. They said they had seen a figure riding a bicycle, floating across their shell scrapes in the darkness. The sight caused enough panic that they evacuated the area immediately.

**The lesson.** Sometimes you feel something before you see anything at all. But a strange pressure, a sound, a change in the air — none of that proves on its own that something spiritual is present. Always consider the ordinary explanations first. In an unfamiliar place, especially at night, stay observant without letting curiosity or fear take the wheel. And remember that your own experience and somebody else’s account are two different things. Not everything needs to be challenged or investigated. Carry on with your duties, keep your mind clear, stay grounded. Do not engage, do not provoke, and do not feed your own fear.

### S1·06 — How It All Began · Part One

*2026-08-31 · Classification: origin · part one of two*  
`Period Before the path · Place Singapore, then Thailand · Nature Origin — part one of two · Runtime 0:40`

**Hook.** Before I became Master Z, my life had nothing to do with spirituality. I was a software engineer, then a businessman selling in‑car cameras. What I was looking for was not enlightenment. It was better luck.

> I started out as a software engineer. My world was technology — computers, programming, building a career. While I was still working, I began a small side business selling in‑car cameras. What started on the side gradually grew, and eventually I left software engineering altogether to run it full time.

> And like most business owners, one thing was always on my mind. How do I make this better? More opportunities. Better luck. Protection from problems. A smoother road ahead.

> That question led me somewhere completely unlike the technical world I came from. I began engaging with Thai spirituality — amulets, blessing services. I was not thinking about becoming a master. I was a businessman looking for better luck, nothing more.

> Eventually I went further and received Sak Yant, the traditional Thai sacred tattoos that practitioners associate with blessing and protection. At the time it felt like one more step into something I had grown interested in. Software engineer. Business owner. Amulets. Blessings. Sak Yant. Looking back now I can see how quietly one step led to the next — but back then I had no idea where the road was going. And before I ever became Master Z, something else had to happen first. That is where the story really begins.

**The lesson.** Almost nobody arrives at a spiritual path by deciding to. Most people arrive the way I did — asking a practical question about their work, their luck, their protection, and following it one small step at a time. There is no shame in starting from self‑interest; that is where most of us start. What matters is what you do once the path opens. Look back honestly at your own steps and you will usually find that none of them felt like a turning point at the time.

### S1·07 — How It All Began · Part Two

*2026-09-02 · Classification: origin · part two of two*  
`Period After my first Sak Yant · Place Temples, Thailand · Nature Involuntary movement — part two of two · Runtime 0:39`

**Hook.** As my business improved I received more and more Sak Yant. Each time, things seemed to get smoother. But something else was changing too — and the first time my hands began moving on their own, I was not excited. I was frightened.

> After my first Sak Yant I kept going deeper. As my business improved and life became smoother, I received more. Each time, my luck seemed to improve with it. But at the same time I was becoming extremely sensitive in spiritual settings. Something in me was waking up.

> At first it happened when I listened to chanting. I could simply be sitting there, and my hands would begin moving on their own. The movements were not random. They were slow and graceful, almost like the traditional movements of a Thevada, a heavenly being. I was not performing them deliberately. I could not explain it, and that was exactly what frightened me.

> Then it began happening in temples. I would kneel to pray like everyone else, and sometimes before I could properly begin, my body would start moving. My upper body would rotate and sway. My hands would form gestures I did not know, instead of the ordinary way a person bows and pays respect. It felt ceremonial. It felt like something I could not simply stop.

> For someone who came from software engineering and business, this was not exciting. It was frightening. Why is my body moving like this? What if it happens in front of everyone? I worried people in the temple would stare, or think I was behaving strangely. I did not want to cause a scene. Remember, I was not a master then — I was still trying to understand it myself. I had entered this world looking for better luck and protection for my business. I never expected to end up kneeling in a temple, unable to control the way my own body was moving. And eventually I had to find out what was actually happening to me.

**The lesson.** If something like this ever happens to you, the important thing is what you do next. I did not ask for it and I did not enjoy it — I was frightened, and I was worried about what people would think. What I did was go and seek to understand it properly, from people who knew far more than I did. Trance and involuntary movement mean different things in different traditions, and the context, the lineage and the conduct of the person involved all matter. Do not imitate these states, do not provoke them, and do not chase them out of curiosity. And if something is affecting your health or your mind, see a doctor as well. Spiritual guidance sits beside proper care, never in place of it.

### S1·08 — The Journey To Seek The Truth

*2026-09-04 · Classification: trance & conflicting readings*  
`Period After the movements began · Place Amulet shop, then a medium · Nature Trance & conflicting interpretations · Runtime 0:29`

**Hook.** It was Buddha Day. I was paying respect at an amulet shop I often visit when something came into my body. I was fully conscious — I knew exactly what I was doing and what was around me. I simply could not stop.

> It was Buddha Day and I had gone to an amulet shop I frequent. As I paid respect to the main Buddha, something came into my body. I was conscious the whole time. I knew what I was doing and I knew what was happening around me, but I could not control myself. What my body performed was a traditional dance, as though dancing for the god to see.

> People rushed in to stop me and could not. In the end they gave up and let me dance for around fifteen minutes. Afterwards they were worried. Was I in trance? Was I possessed? What was it? They urged me to see a medium that night, afraid I had been taken by something unclean.

> When I approached the medium, the same presence as the dancer took over again. My body moved forward and bowed to him respectfully, as a greeting. He gave his interpretation without much hesitation: it was a ghost possessing me. And the moment he said it, the nature of it changed. Something monkey‑like came through instead. I let out a roar towards him. I could feel its anger.

> Why would it become angry only after he called it a ghost? Was he wrong? The medium performed rituals to drive them out, but the experiences did not simply stop. I left with more questions than I arrived with. Was it a ghost? A Thevada? Hanuman? Why was it angry? At that time I did not have the knowledge to know.

**The lesson.** Not every practitioner will read the same experience the same way. Every medium, master and practitioner interprets through their own knowledge, training and tradition — and being called a master does not make an interpretation infallible. I could not prove it was a ghost. I could not prove it was not. I could not even prove it was something else entirely. What I realised that night is that I could not simply accept the first explanation I was handed. If I wanted the truth, I had to go and seek it, learn it, and find it for myself. That is worth remembering whoever you consult, and about anything you are ever told with certainty.

### S1·09 — When I Started Seeking Answers

*2026-09-07 · Classification: sensitivity to objects*  
`Period During the search · Place Amulet shops, then a temple · Nature Sensitivity to objects · Runtime 0:40`

**Hook.** The shopkeeper told me it was a Yin amulet — a young woman who died tragically. Out of curiosity I picked it up. Almost immediately something like an electric numbness travelled up my arm, followed by an intense cold.

> There was a period when I was still trying to understand the strange things happening around me. One day I visited an amulet shop. The shopkeeper showed me a Yin amulet, of a young woman who had died tragically. Out of curiosity, I picked it up. Almost immediately I felt an electric‑like numbness travelling up my arm, followed by an intense cold. Instinctively I knew my energy was not compatible with whatever was attached to that piece.

> I put it down and said what I felt was right: I will not bring you home, but I will make merits under your name. Later I went to a temple and made a contribution dedicated to her. That experience has stayed with me ever since.

> On another occasion I picked up a Hanuman amulet at a different shop. The moment I held it I felt an extremely powerful presence — in my mind it was almost as though something roared. Then came an overwhelming urge, as if something wanted to enter or take control. I released it at once and hurried out of the shop, afraid that people would see me going into that state in public.

> Eventually a shop owner I visited often noticed the sensitivity. He told me I had something unusual and began handing me pieces to hold — Lersi, Hanuman, all sorts — just to see what would happen. And every time I held certain pieces and let my mind open, something seemed to respond.

**The lesson.** This is the part I most want people to hear. Never deliberately test spiritual items by opening yourself up to see what comes in. You may believe a piece is connected to a deity, a Lersi, Hanuman or some other force — but without proper knowledge and guidance you cannot be certain what you are actually experiencing. It could be psychological. It could be your own sensitivity reacting to the object. It could be a spirit, or something else entirely that you do not understand. The dangerous part is not the object. It is deliberately surrendering control to something you cannot identify. Looking back, I was fortunate that these experiences became part of my learning rather than something far more serious. Spiritual curiosity should always come with discernment, respect and proper guidance. You do not need to invite something in just to prove that something is there.

### S1·10 — I Started Keeping Kumanthong

*2026-09-09 · Classification: an experiment I regret*  
`Period During the search · Place My own altar · Nature Deliberate trance — do not repeat · Runtime 0:40`

**Hook.** The owner explained it as caring for homeless children — that offerings and merits give them something they never had. I saw it as compassion, and I kept several. Then one night curiosity got the better of me, and I did something I now tell everyone never to do.

> While I was still searching for answers, I began keeping Kumanthong. The way it was explained to me was as a form of care: think of it like looking after children who have nowhere to go, and when you make offerings and dedicate merits for them, you are giving them something they never had. I saw it as another way of making merit and showing compassion, so I kept several and made offerings to them regularly.

> Then one night, after everything else I had been through, I wanted to know something. What would happen if I deliberately opened myself to my own Kumanthong?

> I went through them one by one, letting my mind loosen each time. What happened frightened me. With each different Kumanthong I found myself behaving like a different child, with a completely different personality. My expressions changed. My mannerisms changed. Even the way I felt from the inside seemed to change. One after another after another.

> At that moment I was fascinated by it. Looking back today, I can see clearly how dangerous and how completely unnecessary that experiment was.

**The lesson.** Even if you believe a Kumanthong belongs to you, protects you, and would never intentionally harm you — never deliberately invite it to enter or take control of your body. In traditional belief Kumanthong are regarded as Yin, while a living person carries Yang. Even with no ill intent on either side, repeatedly immersing yourself in something held to carry strong Yin energy is understood within that tradition as disturbing your own balance. And setting that aside entirely: deliberately surrendering control of your own consciousness is not something to experiment with casually, whatever you believe is on the other side. You do not need possession, trance or dramatic manifestations to prove a spiritual connection. Care for what you keep. Make merits if that is your practice. Treat the traditions with respect. But keep your boundaries and stay in control of yourself. Some lessons are only learned by living them — this is the one experiment I would tell everybody never to repeat.

### S1·11 — Not Every Master Should Be Trusted

*2026-09-11 · Classification: choosing who to trust*  
`Period After I opened my amulet shop · Place Thailand, then Singapore · Nature Choosing who to trust · Runtime 0:39`

**Hook.** By then amulets had gone from curiosity to a serious interest, and I had opened a shop of my own. On a trip to Thailand I visited a master I did not know well. I did not buy much from him. When I got back to Singapore, I stopped feeling like myself.

> Not long after my earlier encounters, my interest in amulets became more than curiosity. It grew into a serious pursuit, and eventually I opened an amulet shop of my own — though I was still very much a student of this world.

> During one trip to Thailand I visited a Thai master I did not know very well. I wanted guidance about the fortune and future of my business. He had many items available, but in the end I did not take much from him.

> When I returned to Singapore my mind became extremely foggy. I could not think properly. I misplaced my wallet, and strangely I could barely remember what I had done with it. I simply felt unlike myself. Eventually I approached another Thai practitioner I trusted, Lersi Chin, a true Lersi master. He examined the situation according to his practice and told me he believed something negative had been placed on me. He performed a clearing, and afterwards I gradually felt like myself again.

> Whether you interpret that spiritually or otherwise, the lesson has stayed with me ever since. After that I became far stricter about who I go to. I research a master’s lineage thoroughly. I look for real accounts from real people. I only approach those I have checked and genuinely trust. And yet still, none of them could answer the question I actually carried — why I had these abilities at all, and what they were truly for. That question went unanswered for a long time, until the day I met someone who could finally help me understand.

**The lesson.** Do not choose a spiritual master simply because somebody calls them a master. Ask the ordinary questions you would ask about anyone you are about to trust. Who are they? What is their character? What are their intentions? Who can genuinely vouch for them? Spiritual knowledge and good character are not automatically the same thing, and the title alone tells you nothing about the second one. Take your time, check their lineage, look for real accounts from real people, and be especially careful with anyone you do not know and cannot verify. This applies whether or not you believe anything can be placed on a person — a practitioner with poor intentions can do you harm in perfectly ordinary ways too.

### S1·12 — I Finally Found My Answers

**Hook.** For years I carried the same four questions and nobody could answer them. Then at a Wai Kru ceremony in Thailand, among all the masters and practitioners present, one man in white caught my attention. Something in me knew I would have to speak to him.

> For years I had been searching for the answers to the same questions. Why was I so sensitive? Could any of it be used to help others? How could I cultivate enough to control it — and eventually use it at will? I had met many practitioners and many masters. Some gave me advice. Some gave me experiences. Nobody could really explain the truth of it to me.

> I was attending a Wai Kru ceremony in Thailand where many masters and practitioners were present. Only one person caught my attention. He was dressed in white and carried the presence of a true master. That person was Grandmaster Pho, and something inside me knew I had to speak to him one day.

> When the opportunity came I told him everything — the sensitivity, the unexplained senses, the trance experiences, the confusion. He listened. Then he said things that shocked me. He knew the answers to every question I had been carrying for years. Why I was so sensitive. What it could be used for. How to cultivate it, how to control it, and how to eventually use it at will.

> What he taught me first was control. Sensitivity alone, he explained, is not enough — without control it will overwhelm you and it will hurt you. With control you can open and close at will. Learning when to open myself and when to close is the true path of cultivation. Under his guidance I began to build discipline, awareness and inner strength, and what used to happen to me unpredictably became something I could work with deliberately. Today I can use these sensitivities to help others, which is the point of them. Not for myself. **The lesson.** Not every master can answer your deepest questions, and that is not a criticism of them. Different practitioners are at different stages of their own cultivation, and someone can be sincere, knowledgeable and genuinely helpful while still being unable to see what you need seen. That is why the search took me years. If you are carrying a question nobody around you can answer, keep looking — carefully, and with everything I said about vetting a teacher still applying. And remember the order these things go in: sensitivity needs knowledge, knowledge needs discipline, and ability needs responsibility. Skip a step and it is not a gift any more.

### S1·13 — The Woman Who Rose From Her Wheelchair

*2026-09-16 · Classification: case one · spiritual work*  
`Period Ten years of cultivation · Place With Grandmaster Pho · Nature Case one — spiritual work · Runtime 0:38`

**Hook.** She had not walked properly in years. She had seen many doctors, who could not give her a clear explanation, and many practitioners, none of whom helped. This is one of the cases from my ten years under Grandmaster Pho that I will never forget.

> Over the past ten years of cultivation under Grandmaster Pho, I have seen many cases — helping devotees, doing spiritual work, and during periods of my monkhood. This is one I will never forget.

> An elderly Indian lady was brought to see him. She had been unable to walk properly for years and used a wheelchair. She had seen many doctors and no clear explanation had been found. She had also been to many spiritual practitioners and nothing had improved. Eventually she came to Grandmaster Pho.

> Through his practice he perceived that a spirit was sitting on her back, and he believed this was connected to her years of difficulty. He also saw that she had once served as a medium of Mae Kali, and that because of past karma and matters left unresolved, the spirit had continued to oppress her. He performed a blessing to release the spirit and clear the karma.

> During the blessing her whole demeanour changed and she entered a deep trance associated with Mae Kali. As it deepened, the spirit let out a loud cry and was forced from her back. Afterwards she opened her eyes, and then, to everyone in that room, she stood up. She could walk. From that day she walked again.

**The lesson.** I want to be careful about how this one is heard. Not every unexplained physical problem is spiritual, and medical checkups matter — she had already been through the doctors before she ever came to us, and that order is the right one. If you or someone you love cannot walk properly, see a doctor first and keep seeing them. This account is what I witnessed, not a promise of what will happen to anyone else. Beyond that, the case taught me three things I still use. Look carefully at a person’s history, their previous practices and their circumstances before reaching any conclusion. Sensitivity only lets you experience something; cultivation is what teaches you how to examine it. And experience teaches you not to jump to conclusions — including the conclusion that something is spiritual at all.

**The lesson.** Three things this case taught me. Third, and this is the one I would underline: curiosity is not protection, sensitivity is not mastery, and experiencing something is not the same as understanding it. Cultivation, control, experience and discernment — that is the real path, and there is no shortcut through any of it.

If you do not understand what you are inviting into your environment, do not bring it home — curiosity may make you want to collect something, but understanding should come before possession.
