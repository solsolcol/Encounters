## Trial episode 1 · The Hell Note I Should Never Have Taken

### 1.1 · THE OFFERINGS

After badminton during the 7th Lunar Month, you notice an unburnt hell note among roadside offerings.

- **A · PICK IT UP** — d [-4, +2, -6]
  - *does:* Curiosity wins. You reach down and take the unburnt hell note.
  - *then:* You leave with the note. Nothing happens immediately, but you have interfered with something you do not understand.
  - *lesson:* Curiosity is not the same as wisdom. Do not take what may belong to an offering.
- **B · EXAMINE IT** — d [0, +8, +7]
  - *does:* You inspect the hell note carefully before deciding what to do.
  - *then:* Closer observation gives you context without immediately escalating the situation.
  - *lesson:* Observe before acting. Information can be gained without interference.
- **C · LEAVE IT** — d [+3, +2, +9]
  - *does:* You decide the offering is not yours to disturb and walk away.
  - *then:* You put real distance between yourself and the offerings. Nothing has been taken or disturbed.
  - *lesson:* Restraint can be the wiser response when you do not understand what you are dealing with.
- **D · ASK AN ADULT** — d [+2, +6, +10]
  - *does:* You ask a nearby adult about the offerings instead of touching them.
  - *then:* The adult keeps you away and warns you not to disturb what was placed there.
  - *lesson:* Seek context before acting. Asking someone more experienced can prevent an avoidable mistake.

### 1.2 · THE PRESENCE

Later at home, unexplained crying and an oppressive feeling begin. One night you sense something in the bedroom.

- **A · LOOK BEHIND THE BED** — d [-7, +9, +1]
  - *does:* You force yourself to investigate the darkness beside the bed.
  - *then:* The darkness no longer feels empty. Your awareness increases, but so does your fear.
  - *lesson:* Awareness can reveal more, but investigation without preparation has a cost.
- **B · CALL FOR MOTHER** — d [+5, +4, +9]
  - *does:* You call for your mother rather than confronting the presence alone.
  - *then:* She enters with a light. The oppressive atmosphere eases once you are no longer alone.
  - *lesson:* Seeking help is not weakness. Knowing your limits is part of wisdom.
- **C · LEAVE THE ROOM** — d [-2, +7, +6]
  - *does:* You choose distance over confrontation and leave the bedroom.
  - *then:* You reach the hallway and look back. Whatever frightened you has not simply vanished.
  - *lesson:* Creating distance can reduce exposure even when it does not solve the cause.
- **D · STAY SILENT & STILL** — d [-3, +5, +4]
  - *does:* You remain motionless in bed and refuse to react.
  - *then:* Time passes. The oppressive presence seems closer, and silence has not made the experience disappear.
  - *lesson:* Stillness may prevent escalation, but enduring fear indefinitely is not the same as solving the problem.

### 1.3 · THE GATHERING

At a traditional spiritual gathering, you encounter unfamiliar behaviour and begin trying to understand what you are witnessing.

- **A · OBSERVE QUIETLY** — d [+1, +10, +8]
  - *does:* You remain at the edge and watch carefully without interfering.
  - *then:* By observing, you notice details and patterns that others appear to miss.
  - *lesson:* Observation before interpretation strengthens awareness.
- **B · JOIN THE RITUAL** — d [-6, +3, -5]
  - *does:* You move closer and participate despite having little experience.
  - *then:* The atmosphere becomes physically overwhelming and an experienced practitioner notices your discomfort.
  - *lesson:* Participation increases exposure. Do not enter practices you do not yet understand.
- **C · QUESTION WHAT YOU SEE** — d [+2, +8, +11]
  - *does:* You ask an experienced practitioner what is actually happening.
  - *then:* The explanation helps you separate observation from assumption and belief from evidence.
  - *lesson:* Questioning respectfully is part of discernment. Unusual does not automatically mean supernatural.
- **D · LEAVE THE GATHERING** — d [+3, +4, +7]
  - *does:* You decide not to become further involved and leave.
  - *then:* You create distance, but the experience stays with you and the corridor behind feels unsettling.
  - *lesson:* Leaving can be appropriate when you lack the knowledge to engage safely.

### 1.4 · BACK HOME

Back home, you try to understand why disturbances seem to appear after particular encounters and exposures.

- **A · CONNECT THE LOCATIONS** — d [+1, +12, +10]
  - *does:* You review the sequence of events and compare where each disturbance occurred.
  - *then:* A pattern begins to emerge. The events no longer feel completely random.
  - *lesson:* Look for patterns before jumping to conclusions. Context matters.
- **B · IGNORE IT & SLEEP** — d [-7, -2, -4]
  - *does:* You convince yourself nothing is wrong and try to sleep.
  - *then:* You wake later to the same unsettling atmosphere. Ignoring a repeated pattern did not resolve it.
  - *lesson:* Dismissal is not discernment. Repeated warning signs deserve careful assessment.
- **C · PROVOKE THE PRESENCE** — d [-12, +1, -12]
  - *does:* Frustrated, you deliberately challenge whatever may be there.
  - *then:* The environment reacts violently. You immediately realise provocation added danger without giving understanding.
  - *lesson:* Never provoke what you do not understand merely to prove that it exists.
- **D · ASK FOR HELP AGAIN** — d [+6, +6, +11]
  - *does:* You stop trying to handle the disturbances alone and contact someone experienced.
  - *then:* An experienced adult arrives, keeps you back and assesses the environment before anyone engages further.
  - *lesson:* Knowing when to seek qualified guidance is a form of wisdom.

### 1.5 · THE LESSON

With the immediate fear reduced, the encounter becomes a lesson in observation, restraint and seeking proper guidance.

- **A · SEEK EXPERIENCED GUIDANCE** — d [+6, +8, +12]
  - *does:* You sit down with an experienced adult and ask what you should learn from everything that happened.
  - *then:* In daylight, the same hallway is examined calmly. Fear gives way to observation and analysis.
  - *lesson:* Do not assume. Do not provoke. Observe, understand the circumstances and seek knowledge before acting.
- **B · TRUST FEAR ALONE** — d [-6, -5, -8]
  - *does:* You let the memory of the night determine what you believe about every ambiguous shape or sound.
  - *then:* Fear begins filling in missing information before you verify what is actually there.
  - *lesson:* Fear is a signal, not proof. Verify before deciding what something means.
- **C · DISMISS EVERYTHING** — d [+2, -7, -7]
  - *does:* You decide every experience must have been imagination and refuse to examine any of it further.
  - *then:* You avoid fear, but also lose the opportunity to learn from patterns and evidence.
  - *lesson:* Blind disbelief can be as unhelpful as blind belief. Discernment requires observation.
- **D · LEARN & MOVE FORWARD** — d [+7, +10, +12]
  - *does:* You accept that you do not understand everything yet and commit to learning without recklessness.
  - *then:* The case becomes an early lesson that shapes how you approach later spiritual encounters.
  - *lesson:* Wisdom grows when experience is combined with humility, observation and proper guidance.

## Trial episode 2 · The 3 AM Shower

### 2.1 · THE SOUND

It is around 3:00 AM. You wake to the unmistakable sound of running water. The bathroom shower appears to be on, but no one should be using it.

- **A · OPEN THE BATHROOM DOOR** — d [0, +5, +2]
  - *does:* You get out of bed and go directly toward the bathroom to investigate.
  - *then:* You reach the bathroom and confirm the sound is genuinely coming from the shower area.
  - *lesson:* Investigating can provide information, but moving straight toward uncertainty is not automatically the wisest first step.
- **B · STAY STILL & LISTEN CAREFULLY** — d [+3, +6, +6]
  - *does:* You remain where you are and listen before moving.
  - *then:* By staying quiet, you confirm the sound continues and appears to come from the bathroom.
  - *lesson:* Hearing something frightening is an observation. Deciding what caused it is an interpretation.
- **C · CALL OUT TO SEE WHO IS AWAKE** — d [+2, +5, +5]
  - *does:* You call out to ask whether anyone else in the house is awake.
  - *then:* No clear answer comes back. You gain a little information without immediately entering.
  - *lesson:* Check simple human explanations before building a more complicated interpretation.
- **D · ASSUME IT'S A GHOST & HIDE** — d [-5, -3, -6]
  - *does:* Fear takes over. You decide the sound must be supernatural and hide rather than verify it.
  - *then:* The shower sound continues while your fear grows. Nothing observed proves what caused it.
  - *lesson:* Fear is a signal, not proof.

### 2.2 · THE HALLWAY

The shower sound continues. Nobody has given you a clear answer. The hallway toward the bathroom is dark.

- **A · TURN ON THE LIGHTS BEFORE APPROACHING** — d [+3, +6, +7]
  - *does:* You turn on the hallway lights before moving closer.
  - *then:* With better visibility, the route and ordinary objects become clearer.
  - *lesson:* Courage does not require deliberately removing your own safety advantages.
- **B · WALK TOWARD IT IN COMPLETE DARKNESS** — d [-4, +3, -3]
  - *does:* You move down the hallway without turning on the lights.
  - *then:* The darkness magnifies every reflection and shape while adding little reliable information.
  - *lesson:* Making observation harder is not bravery.
- **C · CHECK WHETHER ANYONE ELSE IS AWAKE** — d [+2, +7, +8]
  - *does:* You check nearby rooms and household context before approaching the bathroom.
  - *then:* You find no immediate sign that someone nearby is awake and using the shower.
  - *lesson:* Independent context can narrow possibilities without forcing a conclusion.
- **D · SHOUT, WHO'S IN THERE?** — d [-2, +2, -4]
  - *does:* You raise your voice toward the bathroom and demand an answer.
  - *then:* The house stays quiet. Calling out creates tension but little dependable information.
  - *lesson:* Provocation and investigation are not the same thing.

### 2.3 · THE BATHROOM

The shower has stopped, but the tiles, glass and floor are visibly wet.

- **A · INSPECT THE SHOWER & SURROUNDINGS** — d [+1, +8, +7]
  - *does:* You pause and inspect the wet bathroom before touching anything.
  - *then:* The wet floor, droplets and drain confirm recent water activity without changing the scene.
  - *lesson:* Observe the original state before you interfere.
- **B · TEST THE SHOWER CONTROLS** — d [+2, +5, +2]
  - *does:* You test the shower control to see whether the fixture behaves normally.
  - *then:* The shower operates normally, but you have changed the original control position.
  - *lesson:* Look for ordinary explanations—but observe before altering evidence.
- **C · SEARCH THE BATHROOM FOR SOMEONE** — d [+1, +7, +5]
  - *does:* You cautiously open the wet shower partition and check the concealed area.
  - *then:* The enclosure is empty. Eliminating one possibility does not prove another.
  - *lesson:* Eliminating one possibility does not prove the next possibility.
- **D · CALL OUT / CHALLENGE THE EMPTY ROOM** — d [-3, +1, -5]
  - *does:* You call toward the apparently empty bathroom, trying to get a response.
  - *then:* Seconds later, a toiletry bottle falls from the wet shelf. The timing is frightening, but not proof of causation.
  - *lesson:* Timing can feel like proof without actually proving causation.

### 2.4 · VERIFY THE HOUSEHOLD

Water had been running, but no cause is confirmed. You need wider context without escalation.

- **A · WAKE A FAMILY MEMBER & VERIFY** — d [+5, +8, +9]
  - *does:* You wake an adult family member and ask whether they used the shower.
  - *then:* They say they were asleep and did not use it. One explanation is removed, but the cause remains open.
  - *lesson:* Seek independent information before drawing conclusions.
- **B · CHECK FOR A PLUMBING EXPLANATION** — d [+4, +9, +8]
  - *does:* You inspect visible shower fixtures and plumbing for an ordinary explanation.
  - *then:* No obvious visible fault appears, but that does not rule out hidden or intermittent mechanical issues.
  - *lesson:* Failure to find an ordinary explanation is not proof of a supernatural explanation.
- **C · DECIDE IT MUST BE SUPERNATURAL** — d [-7, -2, -8]
  - *does:* Fear fills the gaps and you start treating the unexplained event as supernatural proof.
  - *then:* A frightening shape resolves into an ordinary hanging towel when you change angle.
  - *lesson:* Unexplained does not mean supernatural.
- **D · STOP & WAIT UNTIL DAYLIGHT** — d [+7, +3, +10]
  - *does:* You stop investigating at 3 AM and switch off the bathroom light.
  - *then:* In daylight the same bathroom looks ordinary and clear, while the original cause remains unresolved.
  - *lesson:* You do not have to solve everything immediately.

### 2.5 · WHAT CAN YOU ACTUALLY CONCLUDE?

The immediate encounter has passed. Decide what the available evidence truly allows you to conclude.

- **A · DOCUMENT THE FACTS / KEEP THE CAUSE OPEN** — d [+6, +8, +12]
  - *does:* You write down what you actually observed and separate it from what you assumed.
  - *then:* You close the notebook without forcing an explanation. Something unusual happened; the cause remains unknown.
  - *lesson:* Document before interpreting. Wisdom can include: I don't know yet.
- **B · DECLARE IT WAS DEFINITELY A GHOST** — d [-4, -3, -10]
  - *does:* You decide that because you cannot explain the shower, it must have been supernatural.
  - *then:* Later, an ordinary water droplet makes you freeze. Your prior conclusion is shaping new interpretations.
  - *lesson:* Confirmation bias can make ordinary events feel like proof.
- **C · DISMISS THE WHOLE EXPERIENCE AS IMAGINATION** — d [+3, -7, -7]
  - *does:* You decide you were tired and must have imagined everything, including physical evidence.
  - *then:* Another family member independently notices the remaining damp area.
  - *lesson:* Scepticism is not the same as dismissal.
- **D · SEEK AN EXPERIENCED PERSPECTIVE** — d [+5, +7, +11]
  - *does:* You recount the experience to a trusted older person and ask for perspective rather than confirmation.
  - *then:* The elder helps you consider multiple possibilities and accept uncertainty when evidence is insufficient.
  - *lesson:* Good guidance expands your thinking; it does not replace your judgement.

## Trial episode 3 · The Room That Didn't Want Us There

- **A · OBSERVE BEFORE REACTING** — d [+3, +9, +10]

  - *then:* The pattern becomes clearer without you provoking anything. A faint human-like shadow appears at the edge of the room and is gone when you look directly at it.
  - *lesson:* Observation should come before interpretation. Notice the evidence without immediately deciding what caused it.
- **B · SEARCH THE ROOM IMMEDIATELY** — d [-2, +7, +2]
  - *does:* You begin checking the room closely for a hidden person, object or ordinary source.
  - *then:* You find no obvious intruder. The search gives some information, but it also keeps you exposed while the atmosphere grows colder.
  - *lesson:* Checking ordinary explanations is useful, but investigation should remain proportionate and safe.
- **C · DECLARE IT SUPERNATURAL** — d [-5, -3, -7]

  - *then:* Her fear rises immediately. Every sound now feels like confirmation, even though the cause has not been established.

- **D · LEAVE THE ROOM FOR NOW** — d [+5, +5, +8]

  - *then:* The pressure eases in the corridor. Through the doorway, a vague long-haired silhouette seems to remain inside the room.
  - *lesson:* Creating distance can be a wise first response when remaining in place provides no clear benefit.

### 3.2 · THE WARNING SIGNS

The disturbance becomes harder to dismiss. Reflections, sounds and the woman's reactions suggest a repeating pattern around the room.

- **A · COMPARE THE SIGNS** — d [+2, +10, +9]
  - *does:* You compare what each person noticed and separate repeated observations from assumptions.

- **B · KEEP INVESTIGATING ALONE** — d [-5, +7, -3]

- **C · PROVOKE A RESPONSE** — d [-10, +1, -10]

- **D · MOVE TO A PUBLIC AREA** — d [+6, +6, +10]

  - *then:* The pressure lessens, but a dark figure briefly appears in a distant reflective surface as though it followed to the edge of the lobby.

### 3.3 · IT DIDN'T STAY IN THE ROOM

The same presence now appears beyond Room 512. Master Z must decide whether to observe, confront, intervene, or create distance before the situation escalates.

- **A · STOP HER AND OBSERVE BEFORE ACTING** — d [+6, +7, +10]
  - *does:* Master Z asks the woman to remain still. He does not chant, confront or assume possession. He watches her condition and studies the elevator reflection from a safe distance.
  - *then:* As the woman moves, the apparition in the elevator reflection moves with her. Just before the doors close, the ghost raises its head and looks directly toward Master Z.
  - *lesson:* Observation can reveal whether a disturbance is tied to a place or appears to be following a person.
- **B · TURN AROUND AND CONFRONT IT** — d [-6, +6, -5]
  - *does:* Master Z turns sharply toward the empty space behind the woman and challenges the presence to show itself. The corridor lights begin shutting off one after another.
  - *then:* After a blackout and a violent bang, the apparition is suddenly only a few metres behind them. Provocation has turned observation into confrontation.
  - *lesson:* Getting a response does not mean confrontation was necessary. Provocation can make an unknown situation more aggressive.
- **C · BEGIN CHANTING IMMEDIATELY** — d [-7, +4, -8]
  - *does:* Master Z positions himself between the woman and the apparition and begins protective chanting before he has fully diagnosed what is happening.
  - *then:* The apparition reacts violently and advances in impossible jumps between flickering lights. Master Z realises the intervention is escalating the manifestation.
  - *lesson:* A potentially protective method can still be the wrong method at the wrong stage. Diagnosis should come before intervention.
- **D · GET HER AWAY & SEEK EXPERIENCED GUIDANCE** — d [+5, +7, +11]
  - *does:* Master Z guides the woman toward the stairwell and tells her not to challenge or communicate with the presence. In the stairwell-door glass, the apparition is visible behind her reflection.
  - *then:* Down in the lobby, the same apparition appears behind the woman's reflection again. Master Z decides the situation requires experienced guidance rather than further experimentation.
  - *lesson:* Knowing when you have gathered enough information is part of wisdom. Create distance and seek proper guidance when the situation exceeds your understanding.

### 3.4 · THE DECISION

The disturbance has followed beyond the room and into the lobby. Master Z must decide how far he should personally intervene.

- **A · CONTACT AN EXPERIENCED MASTER FOR GUIDANCE** — d [+6, +7, +11]
  - *does:* Master Z takes the woman to a quiet part of the lobby and calls an experienced spiritual master. He reports only what has actually been observed.
  - *then:* The instruction is clear: do not communicate with it, do not challenge it, and leave the hotel. Master Z follows the guidance immediately.
  - *lesson:* Knowing your limits is part of spiritual wisdom. Seeking experienced guidance prevents confidence from becoming recklessness.
- **B · GO BACK UP & INVESTIGATE ROOM 512 AGAIN** — d [-7, +5, -9]
  - *does:* Master Z returns upstairs alone because he wants stronger evidence. Room 512 is slightly open and his mirror reflection stops walking before he does.
  - *then:* The reflection is looking at something behind the real Master Z. After a blackout, the apparition appears inches behind his reflected body and Room 512 slams shut.
  - *lesson:* Once sufficient evidence exists to make a safe decision, returning only for curiosity creates unnecessary risk.
- **C · TRY TO COMMUNICATE WITH THE PRESENCE** — d [-8, +4, -10]
  - *does:* Master Z asks what the presence wants. Knocks answer from Room 512 and dark handprints begin appearing inside the fogged mirror.
  - *then:* A voice imitates the woman from inside the empty room. Then a pale face and hands violently strike the mirror from within.
  - *lesson:* A response should never automatically be treated as truthful. Communication without understanding who or what is responding can increase danger.
- **D · ASSUME IT IS HARMLESS & IGNORE THE WARNING SIGNS** — d [-8, -7, -9]
  - *does:* Master Z decides the manifestations may simply be frightening rather than dangerous. In the lobby mirror, a third figure is suddenly seated between their reflections.
  - *then:* The apparition moves behind Master Z's reflection and grabs his reflected shoulders even though the real space behind him is empty.
  - *lesson:* There is a difference between fearlessness and carelessness. Do not ignore repeated warning signs.

### 3.5 · THE FINAL DECISION

The pattern is undeniable. Decide how Master Z should end the encounter without allowing fear, ego or curiosity to take control.

- **A · LEAVE THE HOTEL & SEEK PROPER GUIDANCE** — d [+10, +9, +13]
  - *does:* Master Z takes the woman out of the hotel without further investigation or confrontation. Through the glass entrance, the apparition remains inside and watches them leave.
  - *then:* From a safe distance, the woman no longer feels the presence behind her. One upper-floor window still contains the apparition's silhouette, but it does not follow.
  - *lesson:* Wisdom includes knowing when to stop. You do not need to defeat or fully understand everything before choosing safety.
- **B · TRY TO REMOVE THE SPIRIT YOURSELF** — d [-10, -4, -12]
  - *does:* Master Z begins protective chanting and personally confronts the disturbance near the hotel entrance.
  - *then:* A second distorted voice copies the chant. The apparition rushes forward and an unseen force throws Master Z backward.
  - *lesson:* Ability, diagnosis, method and experience must match the situation. Courage alone is not enough.
- **C · CHALLENGE THE SPIRIT TO SHOW ITSELF** — d [-12, -5, -14]
  - *does:* Master Z challenges the presence to reveal itself. The woman looks up and sees the apparition crawling upside down across the ceiling directly above them.
  - *then:* The apparition later slams both hands against the entrance glass directly in front of Master Z, cracking the surface and lunging toward him.
  - *lesson:* Courage is not provocation. Do not challenge what you do not understand merely to prove you are unafraid.
- **D · PANIC & RUN WITHOUT A PLAN** — d [-9, -6, -8]
  - *does:* Fear takes over and they run blindly, only to find themselves in an impossible corridor leading back to Room 512 with identical apparitions in multiple doorways.
  - *then:* Master Z regains control, but a second whisper comes from the wrong side. When he turns forward again, the apparition is suddenly inches from his face.
  - *lesson:* Fear itself is not failure. The danger is allowing fear to replace awareness and judgement.

  - *then:* Her reaction becomes unusually defensive and emotionally intense, giving Master Z another clue but also increasing the tension.

  - *then:* Grandmaster Pho identifies a consistent pattern linking the woman, the personal item and the unexplained presence without rushing into a ritual.
  - *lesson:* Knowing when to let someone more experienced take the lead is itself a form of wisdom.

### 4.3 · THE SOURCE
