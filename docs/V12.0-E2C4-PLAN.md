# v12.0 · EPISODE 2 · CHAPTER 4 · THE CYCLIST — the plan

*Status: PROPOSED 17 Sep 2026. Supersedes the E2C5 · THE CYCLIST section of
docs/V7.0-EPISODE2-PLAN.md (written when the cyclist was the fifth chapter
and its first half was an ambush across a gully). Nothing is built.*

## 0 · What Chad asked for, and what moved

Chad's flow for the cyclist, 9 Sep 2026, verbatim:

> The Cyclist. Second night. Ambush drill first, firing on the order, so
> the game trains you to shoot moving shapes. Then a shape crosses the
> gully and everyone shouts there is someone there, but the radio says
> nobody is out there. Shoot it and it vanishes and returns nearer, three
> times, then the bell rings. Use official Singapore NS terminology like
> 'shooters, watch your front. Ready.' Hold and it crosses slow and close
> and turns its head. The decision opens with it still coming, on a clock:
> keep your arc and report, go closer, keep firing, or run. The other
> platoon crashes out shouting your film's own line. Ending narration over
> the tonner at dawn.

And 17 Sep, on the sketch that came out of it: *"I thought it was supposed
to be a live range. Do you know how a SAF outfield live range is like?
Shooters stand in a row facing the front targets that are hundreds of
meters away. Some can be static targets and some are moving targets from
left to right, etc."*

The range was promised in the game already and never delivered: chapter
2's scene A has the encik say **"Put your focus on the live range
tomorrow."** So: **the outfield live range REPLACES the ambush drill as the
first half of the chapter, and everything after it in Chad's flow stands
as he wrote it.** The range is better for the chapter than the gully for
one reason — on a range, a shape moving left to right at two hundred
metres is exactly what he has spent the night being told to shoot. The
temptation stops being a metaphor and becomes the literal drill.

Standing rules that shape everything below: the v9.5 rule (every mechanic
is the spiritual beat wearing a uniform — the range exists to train the
reflex the ghost exploits, and no serial is in the chapter for its own
sake); the v7.0 settlement (live rounds on the night shoot, official
commands, the cyclist appears and is shot at, one soldier-ghost model
through the whole episode, **Chad supplies every 3D model**); and the
lesson-line rule in CLAUDE.md (the case file's words first; the dhamma
term, if any, is Chad's).

## 2 · The place — an outfield live range at night

What an SAF outfield live range is, so the set is the real thing:

- **The firing point**: one line, lanes numbered left to right, each firer
  on his own lane with a lane marker and a sandbag rest. Prone on the
  order (the chapter's default), kneeling or standing on later serials.
  Behind the line a **safety officer per few lanes** and the **range
  conducting officer** at a control point or a low tower with the PA.
- **The arc**: a cleared strip of lalang and scrub running out from a low
  berm at the firing point to **the target area at 100, 200 and 300 m** —
  Figure 11 man-shaped targets on posts, some **static**, some **pop-up**
  on a timer, and **movers** on a rail or trolley crossing a lane **left
  to right**. Beyond 300 m the stop butt and the **range danger area**:
  dead ground nobody enters while the range is hot.
- **The night**: flares. A parachute flare goes up, the whole arc is lit
  white for seconds, swinging shadows, then dark. Red torches only on the
  line. The **tower's voice** runs everything: *Shooters, load. Watch your
  front. Ready. At the two hundred metre target — fire. Cease fire. Unload.
  Clear weapons.* Nobody moves forward of the line while it is hot.
- **The other platoon** is the next detail — the lanes to the left, the
  same range, the same flares. (v7.0 had them in a harbour across a gully;
  on a range they are the other half of the firing line, which is where
  "crashing off the line" happens in full view.)

**Scale.** The playable ground is the firing point and the berm — about
30 m of line, 8 lanes, the tonner and the ammo point behind. The arc and
the targets are built at their real distances as GEOMETRY (a Figure 11 at
200 m is a real target, culled when it is behind the player; the rail and
the mover are real objects moving on a real track — a painted target area
would give the ghost nothing to cross), inside a night pocket the size of
chapter 3's harbour set plus the arc. The forest miniature from chapter 3's
film does not serve here; the set is the harbour's recipe (trees from the
kit, a flat ground the engine walks, blockers) with a long clear strip cut
through it. The player never walks past the berm except in scene B.

**Light.** Chapter 3's midnight daylight declaration, plus the flare: a
`kit.daylight` tween in play (the seam exists) that lifts the sky and the
key light for the flare's seconds and lets them fall, paired with a real
moving light over the arc so the target shadows swing. The flare is the
chapter's clock — everything that matters happens INSIDE a flare's light,
and the ghost is the one thing that keeps moving after it dies.

## 4 · Play

The kit carries all of this except the rifle (§12). Phases, in order,
each a resumable bookmark (§13):

1. **TO THE LINE** — objective: *Move to lane 6.* A waypoint on the lane
   marker; the bed-zone recipe from chapter 1 confirms the arrival and turns
   the lens down the arc. Red torch only (the chapter's torch declaration is
   the chapter-3 torch with the `red` colour and no white).
2. **LOAD ON THE ORDER** — the tower: *"Shooters, load."* A `sequence`
   event, three items in order on a short clock (magazine, cock, safety) —
   the standby bed's shape, four seconds, so the drill is felt rather than
   read. Late or wrong order: the safety officer's bark, awareness down.
3. **THE SERIALS** — the range proper, three serials, each inside a flare:
   - *Static, 100 m*: `timed` — fire on *"Fire!"*, not before (early:
     *"Who fired? Wait for the order!"*, a sanity chunk, the v9.3 ladder's
     BROKEN band), not after the flare dies. Hits are scored (§12).
   - *Pop-ups, 200 m*: `timed` again, three targets up for two seconds
     each, on the tower's *"Watch your front"*.
   - *Movers, 200 m*: a Figure 11 on the rail crossing left to right at
     walking pace; a `focus` event (keep the sight on it) under a `timed`
     window (fire while it is in the lane). This is the serial the whole
     chapter is built to teach: **a shape crossing left to right at two
     hundred metres is answered with the rifle.**
   Between serials: *"Cease fire. Unload. Clear weapons."*, the safety
   officer walking the line, the buddy on lane 5 muttering, the other
   detail's shots down the range. Awareness banked for clean drills, the
   v7.3 receipt rule on every award.
4. **THE STAG** — the last serial done, the line stood down but weapons
   kept loaded for the final serial, a wait in the dark: the tower's radio
   checks, the flare launcher reloading, the buddy's line (*"Eh, last night.
   You never say anything after."*). Presence begins to drain, low, keyed to
   nothing.
5. **THE CONFUSION** (Chad's beat). The tower: *"Shooters, watch your
   front. Ready."* A flare goes up for the last mover serial. The mover
   starts across. And **among the targets one more shape is crossing — from
   the left, slower than the mover, a hand-span off the ground, not on any
   rail, and giving off its own light**. Lane 5 fires. The tower:
   *"CEASE FIRE! Cease fire! Who fired? Lane five — what are you firing
   at?"* Lane 5: *"Sergeant, got someone in the target area!"* The tower on
   the PA, then the radio: *"Negative. Nobody in the target area. Range
   party confirms — nobody forward of the line. Stand fast."* Everyone on
   the line says someone is there. The tower says nobody is. **The flare
   dies and the shape is still moving in the dark.** The torch's edge finds
   the bicycle under it and a rider that does not need the flare to be seen.
   Presence spikes; a red flash; the stumble from chapter 3 as the recipe
   for the moment the eye and the radio disagree.
6. **THE PLAYED MOMENT.** The rifle is in his hands, loaded, and the range
   is hot. Two things he can do with it, and the game measures both:
   - **FIRE**: the shot, the flash lighting the arc for a frame, and the
     rider GONE — and back on the next flare, **nearer**, on a different
     lane, the chain now faintly audible. Each shot: a sanity chunk,
     presence up, closer. *"WHO FIRED? Lane six!"* After the third it is at
     the foot of the berm, twenty metres out, stopped, facing his lane, and
     **the bell rings once**. Live rounds change nothing. It does not come
     back because bullets fail; it comes back because he engaged it.
   - **HOLD**: the lowered rifle as a `hold` event, FIRE flashing on the
     HUD as the temptation, the buddy's rifle coming up beside him; it
     crosses the whole arc at walking pace, close, at the edge of the torch,
     and as it passes his lane **its head turns to him — no face — and the
     bell rings once.** Longer and nearer than the firing branch's pass.
   - **TRACK** (a `focus` event under both): keeping the torch or the sight
     on a moving thing earns awareness whether or not he fires.
   - **REPORT** (a radio hotspot on the lane): *"Tower, lane six. Contact
     in the target area. Negative enemy. Not a target. Over."*
7. **THE DECISION opens with it still coming, on a clock** — the timed
   decision seam, the bar its approach; when the bar runs out before he
   chooses, a sanity chunk lands (never wisdom) and it is at the berm.

| C | FIRE AT IT UNTIL IT STOPS COMING · *critical* | −24 | +6 | −30 | Do not engage, do not provoke, and do not feed your own fear. |
| D | GET UP AND RUN · *critical* | −21 | −15 | −24 | Your own experience and somebody else's account are two different things. |

Deltas are the v7.0 draft's, on the trial's ×3 scale capped at ±30, with
the trial's *critical* rule (wisdom ≤ −15) on C and D. B is "go closer"
made literal on a range: forward of the firing line while it is hot, which
is the one thing the safety brief said never to do.

## 6 · The four scenes

- **A · THE ARC HELD.** He keeps his lane, keys the radio, reports it in
  the form; the tower's *"Roger. Stand fast."* It reaches the right edge of
  the arc and goes into the tree line without a sound. Then, from the left:
  **the other detail crashing off the line** — rifles up, a safety officer
  screaming *"STOP! Weapons DOWN!"* — and the shout that is the film's own
  line: *"Eh, what is that? The bicycle, it's floating! Look over there!"*
  The exercise is called; cease fire, unload, clear weapons, the range
  closed. At the tonner, their story told AT him — five men who saw the
  same thing, certain — and his own kept separate from it, which is the
  lesson's last sentence in a picture.
- **B · THE TORCH, AND CLOSER.** He stands, torch on it, and goes over the
  berm — the safety officer's *"LANE SIX! GET BACK ON THE LINE!"* behind
  him, the tower's siren. The walk down the arc under the dying flare: the
  mover stopped on its rail, the empty target area, **tyre tracks in the
  lalang that stop where the ground is undisturbed**, and the torch on
  nothing. Then the chain, behind him now, between him and the line. He
  turns; it is at the berm where he was.
- **C · FIRE UNTIL IT STOPS.** Three shots in the played moment already;
  the scene gives him the rest of the magazine: flash, gone, back, nearer;
  flash, gone, back at the foot of the berm; the last shot with it close
  enough to see **there is no face**, and it does not move. The whole line
  shouting at HIM; the safety officer taking the rifle out of his hands.
  Morning: the other detail's account of a floating bicycle told to the
  officers, which now sounds like proof of what he saw and is not — it is
  somebody else's account.
- **D · RUN.** Off the line, the rifle dropped, the lalang, the tonner's
  headlights coming on — and straight into the other detail running too,
  the same direction, the same shout. The sergeant counting heads in the
  headlights and coming up one short until he arrives. Nobody looks back
  down the arc, where the flare has died.

## 7 · The ending → chapter 5

Ending narration over the tonner at dawn (Chad's words, as chapter 3's
film opened on his): the range packed, the other detail's story on every
tongue, his own kept to himself — and the sentence that loads chapter 5:
he has decided to stop looking. Chapter 5 · THE LAST NIGHT is the payoff:
*"both the toilet incident and the ghost cyclist no longer mattered once I
stopped giving attention to it."*

The core lesson, by the CLAUDE.md rule: the case file's own sentences
first —

> Your own experience and somebody else's account are two different things.
> Not everything needs to be challenged or investigated. Carry on with your
> duties, keep your mind clear, stay grounded.

— cut to card length, and **the dhamma term, if any, is Chad's to name.**
The structure of the beat, in plain words, for him to name it or say there
is none: everyone on the line sees the same thing; the radio and the range
party say there is nothing; he holds a loaded weapon in that gap; and the
morning brings five men's certain account of a thing he also saw. What he
does with the weapon is the chapter; what he does with their account is
the lesson.

## 12 · Engine seams — RIFLE MODE is the one real one

Everything in §4 except the weapon is the kit: waypoint, objective,
`sequence`, `timed`, `focus`, `hold`, presence, `hurt`, `root`,
`daylight` in play, the timed decision, phases. What does not exist:

**RIFLE MODE — the nineteenth seam.** The shape is the torch's (v11.1,
v11.6), extended:

- a chapter declares `weapon: { model, item, clips: { draw, shoot,
  reload, hide }, rounds, mags, shot, cock, empty }` — an asset key, an
  inventory id (the rifle is ISSUED as an item at the ammo point, the
  v11.6 recipe, so the bag and the hand slot already know it), the four
  clip names, a magazine size, the sounds;
- while the weapon is out the hand viewmodel gives way to the model's own
  hands (§8's "it brings its own hands"), on the v11.3 swap;
- **FIRE** (click / tap / Space) plays `shoot` from its first frame, fires
  `shot`, flashes a muzzle light for one frame, decrements the round, and
  RAYCASTS from the camera — the hit goes to the chapter as
  `stage.onShot(hit)` with the object and the distance, so a target is the
  chapter's to score and the ghost the chapter's to answer; empty plays
  `empty`; **RELOAD** (R / a HUD button) plays `reload` and costs a
  magazine; a HUD pill shows rounds and mags (engine strings, on the
  sheet);
- `kit.weaponOut(bool)` for a film or a scene, gated exactly as the torch
  is (a player's toggle needs the item; a chapter's verb does not);
- the fixture declares a weapon and `fixturetest` proves the seam: draw,
  fire, hit, reload, empty, hide.

Cost: the loader (§8's numbers are known), the swap (exists), the input,
the raycast, the HUD pill, the fixture. Its own release BEFORE the chapter
(v12.0 · rifle mode; v12.1 · the chapter), as v7.0 shipped the kit before
v7.1 shipped a chapter on it — a seam proved by the fixture first is a
seam the chapter cannot hide a bug in.

**Smaller seams**, each one line in the table: the flare as a
`kit.daylight` tween paired with a chapter-owned moving light (no seam —
the chapter drives its own light); the timed decision (exists);
`ctx.warmSounds` for the tower's lines (v8.0).

`src/main.js` gains the seam, the rows and the names and nothing else, so
episode 1 is unchanged by construction and is run as the control.

## 17 · What shipped — v12.1 (the chapter)

`src/chapters/e2/e2c4.js`, one file, plus the asset, the sounds and the
registry rows. `src/main.js` gains **rows in `STING_SAMPLE` and names in
`TEEN_TAKES` / `CAST_TAKES` and nothing else**, so episode 1 is unchanged by
construction and stands as the control.

**THE MODEL.** Chad's ghost cyclist is one baked mesh with **0 bones and 0
animations** — a soldier sitting on a bicycle, photographed before anything
was wired (the v6.17 law). That is the beat rather than a limitation: *a
rider who does not pedal is more wrong than one who does*, and it is why the
model can only ever be the cyclist and never one of the episode's other three
ghost spots, which all run `fbosling` takes. `tools/prepcyclist.mjs` takes it
from **35.0 MB to 401 KB at 29,707 triangles**, and the step that got it
there is the v8.0 attribute-seam law met a second time: with the file's own
normals, welding moved 58 of 440,292 vertices and the simplifier floored at
**52,532 triangles for every ratio asked of it**. Dropping NORMAL, welding on
position, simplifying and recomputing smooth area-weighted normals by hand
gives 297,042 welded vertices and 29,707 triangles at ratio 0.05.
Two traps paid for once each, both written into the tool:
- **the FACING is settled by the RENDER, and the measures are only its
  guard.** Two independent measures — the helmet's mean z against the mesh's
  midpoint, and which outer eighth stands taller — BOTH said the model faces
  −z, and both were wrong: an upright roadster seats its rider BEHIND the
  bike's midpoint, and the taller outer eighth is the luggage rack, not the
  handlebars. The deg-0 render, which shows his face, settled it. The tool
  asserts front = +z before it bakes the half turn that ships it facing −z.
- **the contract is asserted BEFORE `quantize()`.** Read after it, the
  wheels sit at y −27095: a quantized attribute is an integer array, and a
  metre is whatever the dequantization matrix says it is.

**THE RANGE IS COMPRESSED, and the tower still says a hundred metres.** The
camera's far plane is 160 m (v8.9), so a target at a true 300 m is simply
clipped away. The boards stand at **62, 98 and 132 m**; every order says a
hundred, two hundred, three hundred, because that is what a range officer
says. Nothing in the game measures it.

**THE SERIALS ARE PLAYED WITH THE RIFLE**, which is a deliberate departure
from §4: that section predates rifle mode, and a range fought through kit
panels would have taught a different verb from the one the ghost exploits.
The tower gives the order, `shootables()` is what a round can hit, `onShot`
scores, and firing before the order costs 6 sanity on the spot with the
safety officer's bark over it. The one kit EVENT left is the LOAD drill —
three items in order on a brief — because loading is a drill and not a shot.

**THE PLAY, phase by phase** (each a resumable bookmark; `applyPhase` covers
all seven, the v7.3 law): `line` (the ammo point, then lane six — the step is
DERIVED from the bag, not stored, so a Continue lands on the right order,
the v11.6 law), `load`, `serial1..3`, `stag`, `confuse`, `moment`, `decide`.
`moment` writes the MAGAZINE into the phase string (`moment:r14m1s2`), so a
Continue in the played moment comes back with the rounds it had.

**THE EIGHTEENTH SEAM IS NOT USED AGAIN, AND ONE OLD ONE IS**: the TRACK
hotspot is a `dwell` spot (v11.0) whose `pos` object is MUTATED every frame
onto the cyclist — the engine reads `h.pos.x` on the frame, so a moving
hotspot costs nothing and needed no engine change. The REPORT hotspot is a
plain press at his own lane, and it is the only way to bank the report before
the decision opens.

**THE FILM** is four shots, all in the range itself: the back of the parked
tonner, the ammo point, the line looking out at nothing, the first flare —
and then back to the table, where a rifle and a torch are lying, which is the
first thing play asks him to pick up (chapter 3's torch-on-the-ground ending
with the drill added). One law came out of writing it: **`updateNotes`
returns early when the state is not `play`, so `flareFrame` does not run
under a cutscene** — a film or a scene that lights a flare has to burn it
itself (`stage.setFlare`) and hand the sky back with `kit.daylight(null, …)`.

**THE SOUND**: 29 lines in six voices and 13 sounds, two takes each, picked
by measurement (`masters/v12.1/measure.json`, `make.sh` carries the reasons).
A NEW SPEAKER, the tower (George) — the range officer on the PA and the
radio, every line under the encik's BARK RULE (v8.3): the order itself, short
bursts, CAPITALS on the stress, no stage direction. **The v9.2 duration law
again**: all four bed takes came back one or two seconds long, because the
length was only ever written in English; `duration_seconds` and `loop` are
model parameters and the beds were re-generated with them. And a take is
measured, never assumed — `targethit`'s first take is SILENT at −47.1 dBFS.
Aaron peak-matched to −6.85; the cast levelled BY RMS to −16 dBFS, because
the cast bus is flat (v11.1). The chain and the rail needed a hand loop:
`proc.py` flattens the slow drift over a 2 s window and then crossfades the
tail over the head, in the source's own channel layout (stereo stays stereo).

**THE ENDING**: `n4dawn` runs UNDER the outcome card in all four scenes
rather than in front of it — the scene ends a beat after the line starts, the
card comes up, and the card's own line waits its turn (v5.30's rule that
nothing of his begins while another voice is live). It is the sentence that
loads chapter 5: *"I stopped looking after that."*
