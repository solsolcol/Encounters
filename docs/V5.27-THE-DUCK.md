# v5.27 · THE DUCK — he stops being buried

Chad, on the shipped v5.26 build: *"he is still soft, especially when there
are multiple sounds at the same time, he gets buried, this starts even in
chapter 1"*.

He is right, and v5.26 could not have fixed this. That release solved a
**level** problem — his average sat 19 dB under his own peaks — and it did:
+4.8 dB, measured. This is a **masking** problem, which is a different
thing, and the giveaway is in his own words: *when there are multiple
sounds at the same time*.

## What is actually on top of him

His post-bus average is about -16 dBFS. Measuring what lands ON his lines,
at the cue volumes the scenes actually fire them at:

| sample | effective mean | his margin |
|---|---|---|
| `firedie` | **-11.6 dBFS** | **-4.4 dB — LOUDER than he is** |
| `strings` | -17.4 | +1.4 dB |
| `gscream` | -20.1 | +4.1 dB |
| `boom` | -22.7 | +6.7 dB |
| `sobbing` | -24.6 | +8.6 dB |

Speech wants roughly 10-15 dB over whatever shares the moment with it. He
had **1.4 dB** over a dread chord, and was 4.4 dB *under* the sound of the
flame going out. And these do not arrive politely one at a time — the three
loudest firing together sum to -10.1 dBFS, which puts him **5.9 dB
underneath the mix**. That is not "soft". That is buried, exactly as
described.

Note this is worst in chapter 1, which is where he said it starts:
`firedie`, `strings` and `sobbing` are all chapter 1 cues.

## Why he could not simply come up again

`vscare1` already peaks at **-0.28 dBFS** after the v5.26 bus. There is a
quarter of a decibel of headroom left in the entire game. The voice cannot
come up, so the mix has to come down.

## What was built

**Everything that is not a voice now passes through one `bgGain` on its way
to the master, and that node dips while anyone is speaking.** This is the
move a film mixer makes under dialogue, and it is the only one available
once the voice is out of headroom.

```
his takes    -> voiceIn (x2.0) -> compressor -\
the cast     ------------------------------> voiceOut -> masterGain -> out
                                              (muted here, never ducked)
music        -> musicGain -\
loops        -> ambGain   --> bgGain -> masterGain -> out
sfx          -> packGain  -/   (x0.40 while anyone speaks)
procedural   -> sfxGain   -/
```

- **-8 dB** (`BG_DUCK = 0.40`), down over 120 ms so the first syllable is
  already clear, back up over 450 ms so a gap between two lines does not
  pump the whole world up and down.
- The trigger is a **Set of live sources**, not a counter — `stop()` fires
  `ended` too, so a skipped cutscene releases the mix. A counter that
  double-decremented would strand the game permanently quiet.
- `'ended'` is attached as a **listener**, never as `.onended`: every
  caller already sets that, and clobbering it would leak `narSrc` and wedge
  the narration floor so nothing could ever speak again.

### The v5.26 bus had to move

v5.26 deliberately hung the voice bus *before* `packGain` so mute and the
volume slider kept working untouched. That is now impossible: `packGain` is
ducked, and **a bus that ducks itself does nothing**. So voices run to
their own `voiceOut`, and `packMuteSync` gained that node — without it, a
muted game would still talk.

### The whole cast, not just him

`voiceOut` is shared by the boy, the mother, the auntie and the tang-ki,
and any of them ducks the world. Only his takes take the boost and the
compressor on the way in. Ducking for him alone would have made the other
three *newly* buried — the same bug, moved.

## The one cue volume that changed

`firedie` 0.5 -> **0.18**. This file is a measured outlier: its mean is
-5.6 dBFS, nearly **9 dB hotter than the next loudest sample in the entire
pack** (`strings`, -14.3). At the old volume the flame going out was louder
than the boy narrating over it. The new number puts it in line with
`strings`; nothing else about the cue moved, and it is one number to put
back.

This is the only change to base-game feel in the release, and it is
recorded here as a decision rather than buried in a diff.

## Where it leaves him

| masker | margin before | margin after |
|---|---|---|
| `firedie` | -4.4 dB | **+12.5 dB** |
| `strings` | +1.4 dB | **+9.4 dB** |
| `gscream` | +4.1 dB | +12.1 dB |
| `boom` | +6.7 dB | +14.7 dB |
| all three loudest at once | **-5.9 dB** | **+6.3 dB** |

## Measured in the running game, not in the source

A gain node that is wired wrong sounds exactly like one wired right — until
somebody speaks. So `__enc.duck()` exposes the node, and `dbgduck.mjs`
drives a real line in a real chapter and reads it: idle 1.0, ducked under
0.5 while he speaks, back above 0.95 after, and mute still silences the new
voice stage. Reading the source and pronouncing it correct is how the
v5.01 head bone survived three chapters.
