## 0.1 · The plan on one screen

**The story (Master Z's S1·12).** For years he has carried four questions nobody could answer. He goes to a Wai Kru in
Udon Thani with the group from the forum. It is the loudest, brightest day he has seen, with a dozen masters of every
kind, and one man in white holds his eyes. The next morning he is brought to him, tells him everything, and is
answered. What Grandmaster Pho teaches first is control. Then he tells him where the road goes, and he accepts it.

**How it plays (about 17 minutes).**
1. **Film** (47 s): the thread, his notebook with the questions, a white shirt into a bag, the festival ground.
2. **The Wai Kru** (play; festive, loud, colourful; the ground built exactly as Chad's photographs look): Ton gives
   him a bai sri. He pays
   his respects at the offering tables, bows with the whole ground, and watches the dancers honour the Thevadas. Then
   the masters receive people. He stands in the line and sees the ones ahead kneel, raise their bai sri, be empowered
   through it under a rain of petals, and go into trance. His eyes stop on the man in white. His own hands begin to
   move; that man looks straight at him, and it passes. He is next, and he steps out: not to a master he does not
   know.
3. **The check** (play): he asks the people who know the man.
4. **The next morning** (play): one bow, then one press, "Tell him everything". He is answered, in Grandmaster Pho's
   own voice.
5. **OPEN AND CLOSE** (the one new way to play): hold to open, and the world comes in; let go to close. Three breaths.
   It cannot be failed.
6. **The decision** (Chad's own four, §4.4): accept your path, challenge the path, ask why, ask to make it stop. None
   leaves the path; each is a different road to the same acceptance (§4.5).
7. **The close** (§4.6), the same day: he presents the bai sri he would not give a stranger, and is empowered through
   it. "Tomorrow we begin." Nothing is set in the future: his cultivation is what the next chapters are for.

**What it needs from Chad** (§2.3): the ranks of the four; a read of Grandmaster Pho's lines; which man in the
photographs he is; a picture or a clip of the dancers, and what the music is; voices by ear. The people and the props
are made here, end to end (§6.1, §6.6).

**How it is built** (§11): direction first (his answers, the research refuted, the voices), then the whole chapter with
a commit at every checkpoint, then one build, one deploy and one round of checks.

## 3 · Direction

### 3.1 · What the chapter is for

Chad (§0): the Wai Kru is **festive, loud and colourful**, and the chapter has to show it. So this is the loudest and
brightest place in the game: full sun on green turf, marigolds by the thousand, banners in six colours, music over
loudspeakers, a few hundred people in white, children among them, a dozen masters blessing at once, petals in
everybody's hair, people going into trance at a master's feet while their friends stand by and wait. Nothing in it is
frightening, and nobody there is frightened.

In the middle of it is a man who has spent seventeen chapters having things happen to him: the heartbeat, the shaking
hands, the cold at the edge of sight, the picture that lags his eyes. He is the only wary person on the ground. This
chapter hands him the switch. On the loudest day of his life he sees what he has feared done by others gladly, in
daylight; the next morning, in the quiet, he learns that it can be opened and closed; then he is told where that
leads, and takes it up.

No scare and no ghost (`ghost: null`). The one moment of fear is his own hands beginning to move, and it is answered
by a look.

### 3.2 · The place: one ground, built exactly as his photographs look (§1.1)

What no photograph shows (the far side of a tent, what stands behind the camera) is built plainly from what the others
show, and asked about where it matters.

The compound of a wooden hall with a red tiled roof, on the edge of Udon Thani, on the day of Grandmaster Pho's Wai
Kru. Everything is outdoors, on bright green turf, in full sun, under a blue sky.

- **North, before the hall: the offering tables.** In steps under white cloth, with a green satin skirt hung with
  swags of marigold and jasmine. At the top, the great bai sri: banana leaf in tiers, rings of marigolds, a wheel made

  of banana leaf, and towers ringed with marigolds. On the steps: yellow candles from a hand's length to a child's
  height, small candles in pink and blue along the front, fruit on rattan trays, rows of red, green and orange soda,
  sticky-rice baskets, a boiled chicken, coconuts, sweets, white chrysanthemums, roses; a sheet of names propped on
  the wheel; clay pots on the ground before it.
- **Behind the tables: the pole.** Tall, wrapped in tiers of coloured satin, strings of small flags running from its
  top to the edges of the ground; smaller poles in tiers of yellow satin to the side. Round the back and sides, a row
  of long woven banners on bamboo poles: yellow, red, white, purple, pink, green.
- **East and west: the masters' tents.** Green roofs with green-and-white swags. The chief one stands against a
  building with a red plank wall and a white-framed window, is draped in lime, gold and orange, and has a tall column
  wrapped in lime-green cloth at its corner. Under the tents: carved wooden benches, chairs and pale green sofas where
  the masters sit; low tables with water, coconuts, glasses and bowls heaped with marigold petals; a red patterned
  carpet, straw mats, a striped mat, a blue patterned mat, where people kneel; purple flowers on golden stands.

- **The middle: open turf.** Where the line forms, where the dancers dance, where a group kneels for its photograph.
- **South: where he arrives.** Rows of sandals at the turf's edge; plain tables with dozens of bai sri on golden
  bowls, Ton's group's among them; plastic chairs in blue and white; loudspeakers on poles.
- **Beyond:** sheds with red tin roofs (a string of bulbs under one), a white lattice fence, big trees, a second
  wooden house, power lines overhead. He cannot leave the ground (§12).

### 5.1 · One new seam: the event kind `breath` (the forty-fourth)

Hold to open, let go to close, in a number of breaths. Declared by the chapter:
`{ kind: 'breath', low: true, breaths: [{ enough, edge, cue }, …], onLevel(k), onBreath(report), onDone(result) }`.

- A level k from 0 to 1 that rises while anything is held and falls when it is let go, handed to the chapter every
  frame (`onLevel`). The chapter lays its own world on k: its beds' gains, the daylight's strength inside the same
  light count, the gold, and past the band the pulse, the shake and the lag.
- A band per breath (`enough` to `edge`, in seconds, both seconds wide: nothing here tests touch latency) and three
  results: closed in time, closed for him, not opened. Charges go through `evCut()` and are paid on the spot.
- The hold is timed by the events' own timestamps and counts only the time the finger was really down (v24.2). While
  it is open all input is its own; the long press is not the browser's.
- Its instructions come first with a START (v8.7). Its own moving picture, `breath` (a ring that opens under a held
  finger and closes when it lifts): a row in `EV_DEMO` and a class in shell.html, or chaptertest fails the kind.
