## 2. The episode's one axis: CONTROL

Episode 1 escalated DISTANCE → SMALLNESS → INVERSION → INTRUSION → RELEASE.
Episode 3 escalates how much of HIMSELF he is losing, and ends on the lesson
the whole run of case files is about — stay in control of yourself:

1. **ASKING** — he reaches for the unseen on purpose, for luck. Full control.
2. **HANDS** — his hands move without him. He loses his body's smallest part.
3. **VOICE** — a presence dances him and roars through him. He loses the rest.
4. **OPENING** — he learns he can open the door himself, and does, piece by piece.
5. **SURRENDER** — he opens it to his own Kumanthong, one after another. The
   lesson is the boundary.

The player gets less control of the camera and the hands as the episode goes
(the play kit's `root`, a new hand-takeover beat), so the theme is felt, not
told.

## 3. The five chapters

Every chapter keeps the house shape: one small dense place, one encounter, an
opening film, one decision of four options that are four different KINDS of
answer, a teaching from the case file's own lesson (verbatim first — the
LESSON LINE rule). Ranks below: best / good / bad / worst.

### Chapter 1 · The Luck I Went Looking For  (S1·06 — SHORT)

- **Film (~45 s):** the bridge from episode 2. ORD day, the rifle handed in
  (episode 2 chapter 5's last beat) → years compressed in shots: rows of code
  at night, the first box of dashcams, a warehouse of them, a sales chart →
  an amulet in his palm by candlelight → a flight → a Thai wat at dawn.
  Narration from S1·06's own words ("What I was looking for was not
  enlightenment. It was better luck.").
- **Play (short):** the wat's Sak Yant hall at dawn. Take the offering tray
  from the stall, kneel at the Ajarn's mat. **The tattoo is a minigame:** hold
  still under the rod's rhythm (the kit's heartbeat kind, re-skinned as the
  rod's taps — every tap a beat, a flinch is a miss). As the last line is
  struck, the yant warms gold on his back — the first stirring, nothing more.
- **Decision — what he asks for when the Ajarn asks why he came:**
  - A · "Luck for my business." (good — honest; the case file says there is no shame in starting there)
  - B · "Protection from whatever is out there." (bad — fear as the reason)
  - C · "Whatever is strongest." (worst — wanting power he does not understand)
  - D · "What does it ask of me, to carry it?" (best — asking what the path requires before taking it)
- **Teaching:** S1·06's lesson, verbatim first ("Almost nobody arrives at a
  spiritual path by deciding to...").

### Chapter 2 · The Hands That Moved  (S1·07 — the temple)

- **Film:** more Sak Yant sessions in a montage; business better, life
  smoother; the first time, at home, chanting on a speaker, his hand lifts off
  his knee by itself — he stares at it.
- **Play:** a Thai temple's hall during evening chanting — golden Buddhas,
  candles, incense, laywomen in white kneeling in rows. He takes a place and
  kneels (the kit's lying-down/pose seam, as kneeling).
- **The mechanic (Master Zavier's note):** as the chant builds, **his hands
  rise on their own** into slow, graceful gestures he does not know — the
  first-person arms moving without the player. The player's job is to keep
  them down (a stabilise event): each time it slips, the hands sweep wider,
  the body sways, and a worshipper turns to look. A faint gold Thevada forms
  behind him, mirroring the hands (seen only in a reflection on the lacquered
  floor, or the edge of the frame). Sanity drains while people stare.
- **Decision — at the peak, with heads turning:**
  - A · Clench your fists and force it to stop. (bad — suppressing what you do not understand)
  - B · Rise quietly, bow, and step outside. (good — no scene, no harm)
  - C · Let it run and see what it becomes. (worst — the lesson: do not chase or provoke these states)
  - D · Stay still, breathe, and resolve to ask someone who truly knows. (best — the case file's own answer: seek to understand it properly)
- **Teaching:** S1·07's lesson verbatim ("If something like this ever happens
  to you, the important thing is what you do next...") — including its line
  about seeing a doctor as well.

### Chapter 3 · The Dance On Buddha Day  (S1·08 — the medium)

- **Film:** Buddha Day at the amulet shop he frequents. He pays respect at the
  main Buddha — and his body DANCES. Shot from inside: the camera is his, and
  it moves without the player (the film owns it anyway; the point is the
  voice-over: "I knew what was happening around me, but I couldn't control my
  body"). Staff and customers rush in and cannot stop him. Fifteen minutes
  compressed to thirty seconds. They urge him: see a medium tonight.
- **Play:** the medium's shrine that night — a Chinese altar room, red
  lanterns, a Taoist-robed medium (this is episode 1's tang-ki model and
  animations, re-dressed — **DECIDE** whether it is literally the same man,
  which would tie the episodes together, or a stranger). He waits his turn;
  at the altar, the presence walks him forward and bows him to the medium on
  its own. The medium declares: **"It is a ghost possessing you."** — and the
  presence changes: something monkey-like surges up, **a roar comes out of
  him** (a golden Hanuman silhouette behind the camera, the screen shaking).
  The medium starts his rituals.
- **Decision — what he does with the medium's reading:**
  - A · Accept it: it is a ghost; do every ritual he says. (bad — the first explanation, taken whole)
  - B · Tell him he is wrong. (worst — replacing one certainty with another, and provoking the thing that just roared)
  - C · Thank him, leave, and put it out of mind. (good — no harm done, but the question left alone)
  - D · Thank him, hold his reading as one view, and keep seeking the truth. (best — the case file's lesson almost word for word)
- **Teaching:** S1·08's lesson verbatim ("Not every practitioner will read the
  same experience the same way...").

### Chapter 4 · The Pieces That Answered  (S1·09 — testing amulets)

- **Film:** a short one — walking into an old-style amulet shop, shelves of
  framed pieces, a wall clock, the owner who knows him.
- **Play (Master Zavier's note, the heart of the episode):** the shop, as a
  sequence of pieces to hold, each a hotspot and each an entity:
  1. **The Yin amulet** — "a young woman who died tragically." Holding it: a
     numbness up the arm (the hand shakes), breath fogging, a pale face at the
     edge of the frame. He sets it down and says the case file's line: "I will
     not bring you home, but I will make merits under your name."
  2. **The Hanuman piece** — a roar in his head, the urge to let something in
     (the screen pulses; a hold event he must RELEASE in time); he drops it and
     runs out into the street, the customers staring. He comes back later.
  3. **The owner's tray** — "You have a special sensitivity. Hold them and see
     what you feel." Lersi, Hanuman, a deity, others: each piece he holds lets
     a different presence gather behind him (silhouettes that stay, one more
     each time), and the owner leans in, fascinated.
  This is also the natural place to hand the player the **LP Tim Khun Paen**
  (saved at v14.13, not yet given — its power halves minigame damage): the
  owner gives it to him "for protection" at the end. **DECIDE.**
- **Decision — the owner holds out another piece:**
  - A · Hold it and open up — see what comes. (worst — the exact thing the lesson says never to do)
  - B · Only hold the ones that feel good. (bad — still testing, just choosier)
  - C · Put it down, thank him, and go. (good — stop, no drama)
  - D · Refuse to test, and ask him who could teach you properly. (best — discernment, respect, proper guidance: the lesson's three words)
- **Teaching:** S1·09's lesson verbatim ("Never deliberately test spiritual
  items by opening yourself up to see what comes in...").

### Chapter 5 · The Children On My Altar  (S1·10 — the Kumanthong)

- **Film:** the shop counter — "Think of it like taking care of homeless
  children." A figure handed to him; then another; then his own home altar
  with six of them, offerings laid out.
- **Play:** his flat at evening, then night. **Care first** — the chapter's
  first half is gentle and real: set out the red drinks, fruit, candles and
  incense, one at a time, for each child (a short caring sequence; the case
  file frames it as compassion and the game should too). Then night: the
  altar lit, the question in his head in his own words — "What would happen
  if I deliberately opened myself to my own Kumanthong?"
- **Decision — alone with them at night:**
  - A · Open yourself to them, one by one. (worst — what he actually did, and the one experiment he tells everyone never to repeat; its scene shows it: his face changing child to child, giggling, sulking, startled, shadow children gathering, and his own fright after)
  - B · Open to just one, to check. (bad — the same mistake, smaller)
  - C · Put them all away, afraid of them. (bad — the lesson says care for what you keep; abandoning them is not the teaching)
  - D · Make the offerings, dedicate the merit, and keep your own boundary. (best — the lesson's own list: care, merit, respect, stay in control of yourself)
- **Teaching:** S1·10's lesson verbatim ("Even if you believe a Kumanthong
  belongs to you... never deliberately invite it to enter or take control of
  your body...").
- **Episode ending:** the episode-complete card; the last line points at the
  search going on (S1·11–12: the master who should not be trusted, then
  Grandmaster Pho and the answers — a later episode).
