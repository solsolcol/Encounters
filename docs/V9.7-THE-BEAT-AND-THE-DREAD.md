## 1 · THE TENTH VOICE, LOUDER AND FURTHER AWAY

`c1ten` is Edison's own "Ten" treated (v9.5): pitched down a tenth, doubled,
rolled off above 3.4 kHz, and deliberately sat 6 dB under the rest of the cast
because it is the one voice nobody is sure they heard. Chad heard it and wanted
it clearer — but "louder" alone would have undone the point of it.

So it is louder AND further away: a four-tap echo at 90 / 210 / 380 / 620 ms
decaying 0.42 → 0.12, a second low-pass at 3 kHz over the tail, and 0.9 s of
pad so the last repeat is not clipped by the file's end.

Measured on the shipped files, against `c1nine` — the living number said
immediately before it — in both encodings:

| | mp3 | ogg |
|---|---|---|
| `c1ten` at v9.6 | −13.27 dBFS | −12.92 |
| **`c1ten` at v9.7** | **−8.07** | **−7.78** |
| `c1nine` (unchanged) | −7.23 | −6.68 |

**+5.2 dB**, which puts it 0.8–1.1 dB under the men beside it instead of six
under them. The whole count-off measures −7.1 to −7.3 mp3, so the ghost at
−8.07 is still the quietest voice in the room — deliberately. A louder target
was tried (−4.6, which lands at −5.05) and is wrong by measurement: it puts the
number nobody called 2.2 dB ABOVE every living man. What places it elsewhere is
the REVERB and the pitch, not the level — which is the point: it should be
clearly heard and clearly not one of them. 0.81 s → **2.17 s**, and `SECS.c1ten` and the registry both carry the new
length, because `runCount()` states every gap in the lines' own measured
durations.

## 2 · THE SOLDIER RUNS INTO THE WALL

The figure at the corridor's end used to be two `step()` calls 0.15 s apart —
visible for a tenth of a second, at full opacity, in a chapter where the other
two ghosts are semi-transparent.

**The model had to change first.** `ghostsoldier.glb` — the stand-in this
chapter has carried since v7.1 — ships exactly one clip, `Idle_6`. It cannot
run. `fbosling` ships `Running`, and is already the tenth man on the line and
the man in the shower, so all three ghosts are now one asset, one look
(`ghostify(r, { grey: 0.62, glow: 0x2c3a54 })`) and no extra download —
`CULL_SPHERE.fbosling` already covers every pose. `ghostsoldier` leaves the
chapter's asset list: a download nobody was going to fetch for a pose nobody
can use. When Chad's own ghost model arrives it replaces one key in three
places instead of one.

The run, measured off the corridor's own geometry rather than typed in:

| | |
|---|---|
| camera | `NEAR` = (−1.30, 1.58, 5.65), looking at `END` = (−5.55, 5.3) |
| from | x −3.60 (2.3 m down the corridor), z 5.3 |

## 3 · THE HEARTBEAT — THREE THINGS WRONG, ALL MEASURED

Chad: *"very hard to nail right, it still feels off even when my timing is
good."* It was not his timing.

### 3a · The windows were two and a half milliseconds wide

`win` and `zone` MULTIPLY inside `evGrade` — the |dt| a band allows is
`at × win × zone` SECONDS — and the chapter shipped `win: 0.15, zone: 0.55`:

| band | shipped (v9.6) | v9.7 |
|---|---|---|
| PERFECT +3 | within **2 ms** | within **45 ms** |
| GREAT +2 | 6 ms | 106 ms |
| GOOD +1 | 10 ms | 181 ms |
| SLIGHT 0 | 17 ms | 302 ms |
| MISSED −2 | 28 ms | 513 ms |
| BROKEN −4 | beyond 28 ms | beyond 513 ms |

A touchscreen's own tap latency is **50 to 100 ms**. Every band above SLIGHT
was inside the hardware's noise floor, so a player with flawless timing was
graded BROKEN on essentially every beat and charged 5 sanity for each. The
engine default and the chapter both run 0.26 / 5.8 now.

`zone` at 5.8 is far outside the trial's 0.82–0.30 difficulty scale, and that
is not an oversight: for this one kind `win` is a REAL WINDOW IN SECONDS — it
also decides how long the beat stays open (`e.t > c + o.win`) and normalises
the ring's overshoot — so it cannot be stretched to carry the grading spread,
and `zone` carries it instead.

### 3b · A beat nobody answered had become FREE

Widening the zone broke a sentinel written when it was narrow. Four call sites
passed `evScorePress(1)` to mean "the worst" — one whole window out — and `1`
only lands on BROKEN while `zone` is under 2.94. At 5.8 an unanswered beat
graded **SLIGHT: zero damage, `missCost` never charged**. It is `EV_WORST`
(`Infinity`) at all four sites now, which is BROKEN at any zone. Verified:
old sentinel at zone 5.8 → 0, new → −4; the sequence event's own two sites at
zone 1 are −4 before and after, so nothing there moved.

### 3c · The rings could not tell you you were late

*"the circles dont really align."* One ring approached the beat, then CLAMPED
at scale 1.0 for the whole late window — so a press 100 ms late saw a
perfectly aligned ring and was graded BROKEN — and snapped back out when the
beat resolved.

**Three rings in flight** (`evPulse`, `evPulse2`, `evPulse3`), each owning one
beat and nothing else: it fades in a gap before its own beat, contracts
2.2 → 1.0 so it lands ON the dashed target ring at the beat's centre, and then
keeps going, through and inside it, while the press is still allowed. Late
LOOKS late. The next beat's ring is already on its way in behind it, so there
is no jump, and an accelerating rhythm reads as rings arriving faster — which
is the thing an accelerating pattern needs the player to be able to see.

### 3d · Two more, because a rhythm game needs them

**The event clock is WALL time.** It was `e.t += dt`, and `dt` is clamped to
0.05 s, so below 20 fps every event ran in slow motion — and the phone is
slowest exactly when it is hottest. The chapter clock learned this at v7.1 and
the kit's countdown at v7.4; the event loop had not.

**The beat is HEARD.** `beattick` fires on each beat's centre with a 12 ms
buzz. A rhythm game you can only see is a reaction test; hearing the last beat
is how a player predicts the next one, which is the entire skill an
accelerating pattern asks for. It is in `EV_SOUNDS`, so the engine warms it
itself (the v8.8 lesson: the engine had no way to warm its own sounds).

The take was picked by measurement, not character. Of the two generated, `_a`
carries **three** transients (0.97 s, 1.88 s at −5 dB, 2.19 s at −15 dB) — a
real heartbeat pattern, which as a metronome would double-hit every beat. `_b`
carries exactly one, decays to −35.6 dB by 100 ms and −68.7 by 700 ms, and is
cut from 20 ms before its own peak (the v9.4 law) to 0.45 s. Peak-matched to
the HUD contract: mp3 −2.01 dBFS, ogg −1.92.

## 4 · THE DREAD, UNDER THE WHOLE CHAPTER

`e2dread` is the one bed here keyed to NOTHING — not `nightK`, not `outK`, not
a phase — because "the entire chapter" is the ask and a dread that comes and
goes is a dread the player learns to read. It runs under the opening film as
well as under play.

**The take was chosen on the masking measure, not on taste.** Of the two
generated from the same prompt:

| | <120 Hz | 120–500 Hz (his voice) | 500 Hz–3 k |
|---|---|---|---|
| the take that shipped | **80.7 %** | 13.8 % | 5.5 % |
| the take that did not | 0.4 % | **36.2 %** | 63.4 % |

The rejected one is mid-range shimmer sitting straight on top of the
narration. (Note this is the OPPOSITE verdict to v9.2's `campamb`, where
84 % under 120 Hz was disqualifying — that bed had to sound like a treeline.
Here the brief is a low sustained bass hum, so bass IS the brief. The measure
is the same; what changes is what the sound is for.)

`eleven_music_v2` has no loop flag (v9.2), so the loop is made by hand: the
source's first ~4 s is a fade-in from silence and its most stable 50 s starts
at 9 s (6.2 dB of 1-second-RMS spread), so 12.0 → 55.0 is taken and its last
3 s crossfaded over its head, giving **40.0 s**. The seam is checked at the
JOINT, not by RMS: the discontinuity between the last sample and the first is
0.005, which is **−46 dBFS**, 38 dB under the loop's own peak.

Levelled to −10.5 dBFS peak, which is where the three shipped beds sit
(e2bed −10.4, e2day −10.8, campamb −13.2), so the mix number means what it
says. **The level was priced, not picked**, against `n1A1` in the band his
voice lives in:

| source | 120–500 Hz at its mix gain |
|---|---|
| narration `n1A1` | −27.1 dBFS |
| e2bed @0.30 | −39.5 |
| e2day @0.26 | −38.9 |
| campamb @0.41 | −46.9 |
| **e2dread @0.26** | **−43.9** |

It is quieter in the speech band than either music bed, at the same nominal
gain, because most of it is under 120 Hz. Summed at the day's loudest the mix
reaches −37.2 against his −27.1: **9.9 dB of margin, 17.9 once the v5.27 duck
takes every non-voice source down 8 dB while anybody speaks.** The bed costs
1.1 dB of the margin the chapter already had.

Cost: 641 KB mp3 / 453 KB opus, in e2c1's own pack by build.py's computed
split (only this chapter names it). `beattick` is the ENGINE's, so the same
rule puts it in the shared pack — 8.4 KB / 6.2 KB — where any later chapter
with a heartbeat gets it for free.

## 5 · ONE ENCODER LAW, SHARPENED

v9.6 wrote down that mp3 and Opus move in opposite directions and each encoder
needs its own source. v9.7 adds the correction: **the delta is per-file, not a
constant.** `beattick` fed a source compensated by the v9.6 figure (+0.7 for
Opus) landed at −2.54 instead of −2.0 — this short, low-frequency file LOSES
0.8 dB through libopus where a spoken line gains 0.7. Measure the output and
re-encode; do not trust a remembered offset.
