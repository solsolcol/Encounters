# v5.17 · THE BEDROOM

Chad, with five Sketchfab models: *"Use all of these assets for Chapter 2,
the bedroom assets."* A bed, a wardrobe, a study table, a chair and a
curtain — everything in the room that was a box.

## The rule this build worked to

Chapter 2 is the SMALLNESS chapter: a room you cross in four steps, with
one gap in it you can act on. Five scenes drive cameras to fixed points in
that room, `blockers()` is computed from the primitives at build time, and
the gap's dark planes are measured off `BED`. So the models were fitted to
the room rather than the room to the models — with two deliberate
exceptions, both forced by the geometry and both written down below.

Every piece loads the way every bought model in this game loads: async,
over a primitive that stays visible until the bytes land, sized and
grounded from ITS OWN measured geometry.

## What each one cost, and what it needed

| | in | shipped | tris | the thing it needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bed | 3,244 KB | 138 KB | 4,900 | scaled by WIDTH, never length |
| wardrobe | 108 KB | 110 KB | 5,876 | a quarter turn: its doors are its own +z |
| study table | 1,140 KB | 170 KB | 7,360 | spec-gloss → metallic-roughness |
| chair | 1,376 KB | 247 KB | 6,023 | half a turn, and 18k triangles cut to 6k |
| curtain | 5,222 KB | 187 KB | 1,953 | two hundred morph targets deleted |
| | **11.7 MB** | **852 KB** | | |

Three of those needed something more than shrinking.

**The study table arrived white.** Its fourteen materials are
`KHR_materials_pbrSpecularGlossiness`, an extension three.js REMOVED from
GLTFLoader — so the loader silently ignores every colour in the file and
renders flat white, which is exactly how it first appeared in the viewer.
`metalRough()` converts the spec-gloss factors into the metallic-roughness
the loader still reads, and the desk comes back with its wood, its blue
lamp, its red notebook and its green water bottle.

**The curtain was five megabytes of 1,953 triangles.** The cloth had been
baked as **200 morph targets** with a 200×200 weight matrix — a frame of
vertex animation per target. three.js would carry all of it in a morph
texture for a background curtain seen at night. The targets and the clip
are gone; the sculpted shape stays, and the chapter moves it itself.

**The bed was scaled by its width and nothing else.** The gap between the
bed and the wall IS chapter 2 — twenty centimetres of dark that the whole
premise, the interactable and four cutscenes point at. The bed's left edge
is one wall of that slot. Scaled to `BED.w`, the model's edge lands at
x −1.695, which is exactly where the primitive's was; scaled by length
instead it would have sat five centimetres short and left the dark plane
hanging in air. Its length follows from that scale (2.12 m), so `BED.len`
became the model's figure and the gap planes were rebuilt to it.

## The two things that moved, and why

**The lying camera came down 12 cm.** `PILLOW.y` was 0.72, eight
centimetres above the primitive bed's pillow at 0.64. Chad's bed is a low
one: measured, its mattress tops out at 0.42 and its pillow at 0.52. Left
at 0.72 the opening film and scene D would have played from a head
floating a hand's width over the bed. It is 0.60 now — the same eight
centimetres above the pillow that was always intended.

**The desk moved left and back, and got bigger.** His study table at a
real desk height of 0.74 is 1.40 × 0.91, where the primitive was
1.06 × 0.52. Scaled down to the old footprint it would have stood 0.56
high — a desk at a child's knee — so the footprint became his, and `DESK`
moved to x 0.40, z −1.705 to take it: against the far wall, clear of the
wardrobe's corner at x 1.29, still under the window. Because the primitive
is built from the same constants, `blockers()` fences the real desk and
the hell note lies on the real wood.

The note moved with it, from the middle of the desk to its front edge: the
model brings its own lamp, notebook, pen and bottle, and the middle is
where they are. It is the chapter's premise object and it must be seen.

## The two beats that had to keep working

**Scene D's mattress dips beside his head.** The scene drives
`stage.mattress` and `stage.blanket` down 3.5 cm; the model is one mesh
and cannot dip a corner of itself. So the primitive mattress and blanket
stay in the group as INVISIBLE PROXIES — the scene still drives them,
untouched — and `updateNotes` lays their movement onto the model as a sink
of the whole bed. On legs nobody is looking at, in a shot pointed at the
ceiling.

**The curtain still moves when nothing does.** The primitive rippled in
its vertices every frame. The model cannot: it ships quantized, so its
positions are normalised integers and no per-frame float can be written
into them. It hangs from a RAIL POINT instead — a group whose origin is
the rod — and swings from it on the chapter's own clock, scaled by the
same `noteStorm` the vertex ripple used.

## The harness this build had to fix first

`leaktest` failed once in four runs, and it was not this build's doing: the
same four runs on the v5.16 build flipped the same way. The world's steady
state is fifteen geometries above what two frames after a rebuild have
uploaded, and the harness settled only the BASELINE before comparing —
read the baseline early and the slope is a fictitious +1.88, read the end
early and it is a harmless −1.88. It was a coin toss that had been landing
green. The final count is now settled exactly as the baseline is, which is
what the harness's own comment always said it wanted. A real per-cycle
leak still shows: it grows with every one of the eight cycles.

## Cost in engine seams

Zero. `ch2.js`, five asset rows in `build.py`, five embed tokens in
`main.js`, fifteen credit strings, and the leak harness's second settle.
