## 1. The measure (tools/probes/frameprof.mjs, v26.5's build, a phone's frame, four places in play)

Chrome's own profiler sampled the page's main thread every 0.2 ms for ten seconds at each place. This box draws in
software, so its frame rate (0.4 a second) is the graphics chip's and says nothing of a phone's; what it measures is the
game's own code, which a phone runs too, on its CPU, every frame.

| before the brown hermit | 118 | 1,433,219 | ~20 ms | ~7 | ~1 | 0.3 | 1.1 |

(Few frames a place, so read these as where the time goes, not as exact figures. The heap grew 1.4 MB in ten seconds where
play begins: memory made and dropped every frame, which the collector takes back in pauses.)

What the time is: the engine already composes a matrix only when what it is made of changed (v15), so the matrices
still computed are the moving ones, and the moving ones are nearly all PEOPLE'S BONES: about 2,900 of them (the cast's
and the crowd's drivers', about 65 a body) turned by their takes, laid on by the poses (arms, hands, legs, heads), and
multiplied down every skeleton, every frame, whether the person is on screen or not.

## 2. What can be done without one pixel changing, in the order of what it saves

1. **Animate only who can be seen** (the largest). A person, or a crowd's driver none of whose members is in the lens's
   view this frame (by the sphere that already culls them, which covers every pose of every take), skips the take's
   advance, every pose laid on after it and the bones' matrices; their clock still runs, and the frame they come back
   into view their take is set to where that clock says before they are drawn, so the picture is the one it would have
   been. Saves most of the animation and matrix time wherever half the people are behind the lens.
2. **Nothing made new each frame.** Find what the frame functions and the poses allocate (the heap's own sampler) and
   reuse it: the same arithmetic on the same numbers, no pauses for the collector.
3. **The order triangles are drawn in, for the graphics chip's cache.** Each body and thing is rewritten at prep with its
   triangles reordered for the vertex cache and its points for fetching (meshoptimizer's own reordering): the same
   triangles and points, so the same picture, and each point is transformed fewer times. Measured offline by the cache's
   miss ratio before and after; on a phone the skinned bodies are where the triangles are.
4. **Keep one copy of each picture, not two.** Once a sheet is on the graphics chip, the decoded copy the page keeps in
   its own memory is freed (on a phone the two share the same memory); kept only where a lost context must be redrawn.

Each is built behind an OPT switch, proved by the same frames drawn with it on and off, compared pixel for pixel (play at
each place, a frame of each film), every matrix of every visible body compared bit for bit, and the profile taken again.

## 3. What is not on the list, and why

Fewer triangles, smaller or compressed sheets, mipmaps where there are none, a lower pixel ratio, a cheaper material,
shadows or lights changed, a shorter switch distance for the full bodies: each changes the picture.
Not drawing what cannot be seen (a sheet at zero opacity, a thing behind a wall) changes no pixel, but it is hiding
things, which his words rule out: left out unless he says otherwise.
