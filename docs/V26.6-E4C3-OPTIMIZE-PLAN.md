# v26.6 · Episode 4 chapter 3: faster with nothing on screen changed (the plan)

## 0. His words, verbatim (13 Nov 2026, after v26.4)

"Think about how to optimize this chapter with absolutely no compression or reduction in detail or visual tricks or lod,
or hiding things or risking a degraded presentation at the slightest, think about how to optimize on a low level engine
optimization or bare metal optimization to improve performance and efficiency in this chapter without touching anything
visual."

The rules this stands on (CLAUDE.md): optimise only in ways that cost no visual quality at all (v15.0); an optimisation is
proven, not argued: behind an OPT switch, the same frame compared pixel for pixel and every matrix bit for bit, plus its
invariants checked (v15.0); moving work to another core does not cool a phone, deleting the work does (v15.0); measure
first (v8.4).

## 1. The measure (tools/probes/frameprof.mjs, v26.5's build, a phone's frame, four places in play)

Chrome's own profiler sampled the page's main thread every 0.2 ms for ten seconds at each place. This box draws in
software, so its frame rate (0.4 a second) is the graphics chip's and says nothing of a phone's; what it measures is the
game's own code, which a phone runs too, on its CPU, every frame.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| where play begins | 273 | 1,612,099 | ~40 ms | ~6 | ~5 | 3.3 | 3.1 |
| before the brown hermit | 118 | 1,433,219 | ~20 ms | ~7 | ~1 | 0.3 | 1.1 |
| in the crowd | 213 | 1,431,134 | ~25 ms | ~6 | ~1.5 | 0.5 | 1.3 |
| at the offering tables | 178 | 1,834,925 | ~25 ms | ~7 | ~0.5 | 1.2 | 0.6 |

(Few frames a place, so read these as where the time goes, not as exact figures. The heap grew 1.4 MB in ten seconds where
play begins: memory made and dropped every frame, which the collector takes back in pauses.)

What the time is: the engine already composes a matrix only when what it is made of changed (v15), so the matrices
still computed are the moving ones, and the moving ones are nearly all PEOPLE'S BONES: about 2,900 of them (the cast's
and the crowd's drivers', about 65 a body) turned by their takes, laid on by the poses (arms, hands, legs, heads), and
multiplied down every skeleton, every frame, whether the person is on screen or not.

## 2. What can be done without one pixel changing (REVISED 13 Nov, his second message: the list below replaces the first)

His words: "Like i said before, i dont want the slightest risk that the player ever sees the trickery or compromise made,
even by the smallest accident or edge case bug, that is why i asked not to resort to these cheap tricks that can affect
visually. I want true deep technical optimization, think true engine level, multithreading, bare metal optimization,
removing overhead etc, those sort of infra optimizations that has nothing to do with visual tricks".

So the test for every item: the game computes EXACTLY what it computes now (every bone's matrix the same bits, every
frame the same pixels), only with less overhead, on more cores, or in faster code. Nothing is skipped, culled,
reordered, merged or approximated. "Animate only who can be seen" is withdrawn (it skips work by a guess about what is
seen; no headroom makes a guess risk-free).

1. **No memory made and thrown away each frame.** The frame's own temporaries (vectors, quaternions, arrays, closures)
   made once and reused: the same arithmetic in the same order on the same numbers, and no collector pauses (the heap
   grew 1.4 MB in ten seconds where play begins). Found with the heap's sampling profiler, removed one by one.
2. **The people's bone arithmetic done faster.** The takes' sampling, the poses laid on after them and the matrices
   down every skeleton are the largest cost left: the same operations in the same order, written without the generic
   machinery's overhead (property lookups, closures, interpolant objects), and where it proves bit-identical, in
   WebAssembly with SIMD. Proved by every bone of every body compared bit for bit against the current code, every frame
   of a long walk and of every film.
3. **Loading on other cores.** Decoding the models and their pictures in workers (threads) instead of the main thread:
   the same bytes in, the same geometry and pixels out, a shorter wait behind the curtain and nothing parsed on the
   thread that draws.
4. **One copy of each picture in memory, not two.** Once a sheet is on the graphics chip, the page's own decoded copy is
   released (on a phone both share one memory); the context-loss path decodes again from the asset bytes, proved by a
   forced context loss and a pixel compare after it.
5. **Drawing on its own thread (OffscreenCanvas), considered last.** The whole renderer moved to a worker, the page's
   thread left for input, sound and the HUD. The same three.js code draws the same frames; but it reaches every chapter
   and the engine's input and HUD paths, so it is put to Chad as its own decision with its cost, after 1 to 4.

Each is built behind an OPT switch, proved by the same frames with it on and off (play at the four places, a frame of
each film, a walk), every matrix bit for bit, the profile taken again, and episode 1 run as the control.

## 3. What is not on the list, and why

Fewer triangles, smaller or compressed sheets, mipmaps where there are none, a lower pixel ratio, a cheaper material,
shadows or lights changed, a shorter switch distance for the full bodies: each changes the picture.
Not drawing what cannot be seen, animating only who is on screen, merging or instancing meshes, reordering draws: each
can differ by an edge case or a rounding, so none is done (his words, §2).

## 4. Log

- 13 Nov: measured (§1); the plan put to Chad.
- 13 Nov: his answer, asked which to build after the seated masters' hands are fixed: "All four, in order (Recommended)".
- 13 Nov, his condition on the first: "However, the animate only on screen has potential to cause many issues, therefore
  it needs to have a generous headroom to prevent users from ever seeing paused animations by mistake". So it is built
  with headroom on every side, and none of it is ever relied on alone: everyone within 8 m of the lens animates always,
  whatever way he faces (a turn on a phone is a flick); beyond that, anyone inside a cone half again as wide as the
  view on each side animates; anyone who leaves that keeps animating two seconds more; and a person who comes back
  into it has the exact pose of this moment set from his clock before the frame he is drawn in, so even a mistake in
  the margins shows the right pose, never a paused one. Films are untouched (a film poses everyone from its own clock).
  Proved by a turn on the spot at a phone's fastest flick and a walk across the ground, every frame compared with the
  switch off, pixel for pixel.
