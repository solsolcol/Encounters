## 1. Why only the fourth card looked transparent — it was never the card

Root-caused before touching any CSS. The card and its backdrop are the
same for all four choices (`rgba(4,5,8,.86)` + blur). What differs is
`c.endFade`: scenes A, B and C end with `endFade = 1`, so `#cineFade`
— a solid black full-screen layer — is left at opacity 1 UNDER the card.
Scene D ends with `endFade = 0`, so the world shows through and the card
reads as glass.

The black is not decoration: `cineEnd()` calls `restoreWorld()`, which
snaps the camera and the ghost back to where the player actually stands.
Without cover, the player watches that snap. So the fix is not "remove
the fade" — it is **hold the black through the restore, then dissolve it
once the card is up**. A CSS transition on `#cineFade`, started when the
result card appears.

## 2–4. The viewmodel is a hand and nothing else

The pack ships a right hand from the wrist out — no forearm, no body. So:

- **Forearm**: a tapered mesh from the wrist back toward the camera, plus
  a darker sleeve cuff, added INSIDE `rightOriented` so `buildPrayerArm()`
  clones and mirrors it for free.
- **Leg**: the kick scene has no leg at all. A shin + shoe built in the
  viewmodel scene, swinging through frame on the kick beat only.
- **Prayer hands**: currently two splayed hands, palms to camera, apart —
  the pack's rest pose is "flat and splayed", and uncurling it gives
  splayed. Añjali needs palms flat TOGETHER, fingers extended and
  ADDUCTED, held vertical. Needs a third finger pose (straight + closed)
  and orientations built from a basis rather than hand-tuned Eulers.

Hand local axes, set by the orientation step: fingers −Z, palm −Y,
index→pinky +X. Prayer, right hand: fingers → +Y (up), palm → −X (toward
the other hand). Verified by rendering, not by arithmetic.

### As built

Two engine changes were needed before a scene could say any of this.

- **The vocabulary was the bottleneck, not the samples.** A scene could
  only ask for eight sting kinds and four of them were the same thump.
  `STING_SAMPLE` now carries the whole pack a cutscene can reach, and the
  hard-coded `kick || scream || chant` guard — the list every new sound
  would have had to be added to, and none of them would have been —
  became `STING_SYNTH`, the set of kinds the procedural fallback can
  actually fake. Anything else is sample-only and stays silent rather than
  falling into a switch with no matching case.
- **`sfx(at, kind, vol)`.** The same dread bed plays loud under scene A's
  reveal and at 0.28 behind scene C's turned back. Without a volume scale
  that is two kinds and two samples for one sound.
- **Scene sounds end with the scene.** A nine second chant or a four
  second wail outlives a skipped cutscene and carries on over the teaching
  card. Every sample a scene starts is now remembered and ramped out over
  300 ms in `cineEnd()`.

Scene D grew from 8.6 s to 10.4 s so his chant finishes rather than being
cut off by the card, with the eyes settling back to level so the held beat
is still moving while it waits.

Two things check this rather than trust it:

- `chaptertest` now reads every `sfx(t, 'kind')` out of every chapter file
  and fails if the kind is not in `STING_SAMPLE` or its sample has no file
  in `assets/audio/`. A mistyped cue is silent with no error, which is the
  one bug in this area you cannot see in a screenshot — and it costs no new
  harness, so the "a chapter must not add a harness" rule still holds.
- A one-off probe played all four scenes with the sound on and recorded
  every AudioBuffer that started, identified by buffer identity (several
  samples decode to identical lengths, so matching on duration is
  ambiguous). All 32 cues fired with a decoded sample; nothing was silent.

## 7. The title video

Source is 1920×1080, 26.8 s, 8.3 Mbps, **27.9 MB with an audio track** —
unusable as shipped. Re-encoded: no audio (the game owns the soundtrack),
downscaled, heavily compressed, because it sits behind a darkening scrim
and never needs to be sharp. Target well under 2 MB.

Rules it must obey:
- it is decoration, so it can NEVER block or delay the title. Poster
  colour behind it, `preload="none"`-ish behaviour, and the title screen
  is fully usable whether or not the video ever arrives;
- muted + `playsinline` + `loop`, or iOS refuses to autoplay it;
- `prefers-reduced-motion` gets the still, not the loop;
- it is a chapter-independent title asset, so it belongs to the ENGINE's
  shared assets, not to ch1.
