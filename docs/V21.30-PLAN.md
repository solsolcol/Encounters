## 1 · What shipped, and why it was wrong

**The medium was made the villain of ending B.** "Tell him he is wrong" was the worst option, and I made it the worst
by turning the shrine hostile: the medium's "[coldly] What did you say?" (`md3B1`), the roar a second time, the
stools going over, the assistant's "[shouting] Go! Get out! Don't come back here!" (`as3B`) and the shutter brought
down on him. The case file never shows the medium as anything but a man reading through his own training, and Master
Z's lesson is about exactly that — "every medium, master and practitioner interprets through their own knowledge,
training and tradition". I gave the practitioner the anger the case file gives to the PRESENCE, for drama. It taught
the opposite of the lesson.

Ending A leaned the same way more quietly: the assistant's "Bring the things on this list" read as a shop selling a
cure, and the scene let the rite look like the thing that failed, rather than his own surrender.

**C and D were the same scene twice.** Both options began "Thank him", both scenes were a wai, a short line from the
medium and a walk out to the kerb; they differed only in what he was thinking, which a camera cannot show.

**The endings were checked as pictures, never read as stories.** Every review since v21.0 asked whether a frame was
framed, whether a body went through a chair, whether two voices overlapped — never "what happens, what does it cost
him, what does he understand, and is every second carrying it". Measured from the shipped timelines:

| ending | length | longest stretch with nothing said |
|---|---|---|
| A | 45.8 s | four silences of 2.7–4.2 s (around the rite and the cup) |
| B | 34.8 s | 5.7 s (the walk out and the shutter) |
| C | 36.6 s | **11.3 s** (the whole walk from the table to the kerb, footsteps only) |

## 2 · The two rules this writes down (into CLAUDE.md, as Chad's)

1. **NO PRACTICE AND NO PRACTITIONER IS EVER THE VILLAIN.** Every tradition, and everyone who practises one, is shown
   sincere, competent within their own training, kind to the people who come to them, and gracious when someone
   disagrees. A choice's consequence comes from the PLAYER's own act and attitude — never from a practitioner turning
   hostile, mocking, greedy or cruel. "A reading can be wrong" is said as "each reads through their own training",
   never as "this one is a fraud".
2. **AN OUTCOME SCENE IS A WHOLE STORY.** What he does, how it is answered, what it costs or gives him, and what he
   understands — told the whole way through. No stretch passes in silence: a walk is spoken over, a pause is held by a
   sound with a reason. Read every ending as a story before it is built, and measure its silences after.

## 3 · The four options (proposed)

Four different KINDS of answer to a reading, each with its own place, its own people and its own lesson — the shape
of e3c1 (four different questions) and e3c2 (four different things done with the hands).

| | option (the words on the card) | verdict | the kind of answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Ask him to drive it out, and do everything he says. | bad | hand the question over |
| B | Tell him he's wrong: it is not a ghost. | worst | certainty against certainty |
| C | Thank him, then get a second opinion from the shop owner. | good | collect another answer |
| D | Ask him why it grew angry — then seek the truth for yourself. | best | ask, and keep seeking |

The deltas stay at each verdict's existing numbers (bad −9/+6/−12, worst −18/−9/−27 critical, good +9/+12/+15, best
+15/+24/+30). The core lesson stays as it is (Chad's pick, v21.8). The prompt stays: "The medium has given his
reading, and something roared back at him through you. What do you do with what he said?"

| | line under the outcome card (his voice) | teaching on the card |
|---|---|---|
| A | I handed it all to him... and stopped asking. | A sincere reading is still one person's reading, through his own training. Take the first explanation whole, and the search ends before it begins. |
| B | I told him he was wrong. I couldn't say what was right. | Rejecting what you cannot disprove is the same certainty, turned around. Respect the one who tries to help you, even when you see it differently. |
| C | I asked for a second opinion, and got a second certainty. | A second view is wiser than swallowing the first. But collecting answers is not the same as understanding. |
| D | I asked him honestly, and went to find the truth myself. | Respect the one who reads for you, and hold what he says as one view. The truth is still yours to seek, learn and find. |

**The medium in all four**: calm, sincere, kind. In A he takes care of a frightened young man; in B he takes no
offence and still offers protection; in C he sends him off with a talisman and an open door; in D he answers honestly
and humbly. His assistant is kind in A and polite in B. Nobody raises a voice at the young man and nobody shows him
the door.

## 4 · The four scenes, line by line

Times are planned from each line's expected length (the cast reads about 2.5 words a second, Brian about 2.3) and
will be re-timed against the measured takes. Every scene begins where the decision leaves him: at the medium's table,
the medium standing with the sword and the bell, the rites' drum going under it. `ZAV` is Brian (jamesAdult), `MED`
the medium (Roger), `ASST` the assistant, `OWNER` the shop owner. Every scene ends on the existing bridge to chapter 4
over the black: **"And some of the pieces I found... answered back."** (`z3next`).

### A · "The answer I was handed" (bad) — about 67 s
1. He wais. **ZAV** [pleading]: "Sifu... whatever it is, please... drive it out. I'll do anything you say."
2. The drum softens. **MED** [calmly, reassuring]: "Don't be afraid. Kneel down. Close your eyes. I will settle it
   for you." — he kneels before the table.
3. His eyes close. In the dark the bell, the sword's sweep, the medium's whispered tongue (`md3chant`, as in play),
   the talisman burning. Over its last breath, his own thought, **ZAV** [quietly]: "I didn't ask what it was. I didn't
   want to know. I only wanted someone to take it away."
4. His eyes open on the cup at his lips. **MED** [gently]: "Drink. Slowly." — he drinks.
5. He stands. The assistant puts a folded yellow talisman in his hand. **ASST** [kindly]: "Sifu says come back seven
   nights, same time. And this one — paste it above your bed, okay?"
6. A dip to black, and three short shots of the nights that follow — the shutter half down under the lantern, the
   joss sticks taking light in the urn, the bell in the medium's hand — under **ZAV** [weary]: "Seven nights, I went
   back. I drank what he gave me. I pasted the talisman over my bed. He did everything he knew to help me... and I
   believed every word, because believing was easier than asking."
7. The seventh night, at the door. **MED** [warmly]: "Should be alright now. Go home. Rest well."
8. The kerb under the lantern: his right hand begins to lift on its own (the shipped beat). **ZAV** [shaken]: "But it
   didn't stop. Not really. And I had nothing left to ask with... only an answer I had never once questioned."
9. Up to the lanterns and the night. **ZAV** [reflective]: "I had handed my question to someone else... and stopped
   looking, just when I should have started." — then `z3next` over the black.

### B · "Certainty against certainty" (worst) — about 54 s
1. **ZAV** [firm, dismissive]: "No. You're wrong. It's not a ghost — I would know."
2. The drum stops. The medium lowers the sword and the bell and looks at him — not angry; considering.
   **MED** [calmly, unoffended]: "Maybe. I can only tell you what I see."
3. He takes a folded talisman from the table and holds it out. **MED**: "Whatever it is, it is strong. Take this... for
   your protection."
4. **ZAV** [curtly]: "I don't need it." — he turns away.
5. **MED** [gently, to his back]: "Okay. If you change your mind, my door is always open."
6. Out through the room; at the door the assistant steps aside for him. **ASST** [kindly]: "Walk safe, ah." Over the
   walk, **ZAV** [defiant]: "I walked out sure of myself. A ghost? I knew what I felt..."
7. At the kerb his right hand lifts on its own, and something stirs in his chest; he grips his wrist; across the road
   two people at the coffee shop look over. **ZAV** [shaken]: "...but I didn't. I couldn't prove it wasn't a ghost. I
   couldn't prove anything at all."
8. He looks back at the lit doorway: inside, the medium is already with someone else, and the assistant calls the next
   one in (`as3next`, "Next! You, come."). **ZAV** [ashamed]: "He had only been trying to help me. I threw away the one
   answer I had... and had nothing to put in its place."
9. **ZAV** [reflective]: "It took me a long time to swallow my pride... and go looking for answers properly." — then
   `z3next`.

### C · "A second opinion" (good) — about 65 s
1. He wais. **ZAV** [respectfully]: "Thank you, Sifu. I'll think carefully about what you said."
2. **MED** [nodding, kind]: "Mm. Keep this talisman with you. If it comes again, come back and see me."
3. Out through the room and the door to the kerb, a bus going by (the shipped beat), over **ZAV** [uncertain]: "A
   ghost... He sounded so sure. But I'd felt it dance for the Buddha... I needed someone else to tell me what it was."
4. A dip to black. **ZAV** [quietly]: "The next morning, I went back to the shop."
5. **The amulet shop at noon** (the chapter's own first world, put back in its daylight under the black): the radio's
   music, the owner at his counter looking up. **OWNER** [warm, concerned]: "Ah, you came back! You okay or not? So...
   what did the sifu say?"
6. **ZAV** [hesitant]: "He said... it could be a ghost."
7. **OWNER** [a gentle laugh, shaking his head]: "Ghost? Hm... I don't think so lah. Yesterday you danced for the
   Buddha — the whole shop saw. A ghost won't pay respect like that. I think something good is looking after you.
   Maybe a Thevada."
8. He looks to the altar, the Buddha, the old masters on the wall. **ZAV** [thoughtful]: "One said a ghost. One said
   a Thevada. Both kind... both so sure. They couldn't both be right... and they could both be wrong."
9. **ZAV** [reflective]: "Asking around gave me more answers... but none of them were mine yet." — then `z3next`.

### D · "The honest answer" (best) — about 69 s
1. He wais. **ZAV** [respectfully]: "Sifu... may I ask you something? When you said 'ghost'... it became angry. Why
   would it do that?" (the case file's own question: "Why would it become angry only after he called it a ghost?")
2. The medium sets the sword down on the table and looks at him a long moment. **MED** [thoughtfully]: "Hm. I saw that
   also."
3. **MED** [honestly, humbly]: "I read it the way my teachers taught me... through my god, in my tradition. Another
   master — the Thai side — may read it another way."
4. He holds out a folded talisman with a small smile. **MED**: "Keep this anyway. Cannot hurt."
5. **ZAV**: "Thank you, Sifu." (`z3thank`) — a wai.
6. Out through the room, over **ZAV** [quietly]: "He had given me his answer honestly... and told me himself it might
   not be the only one."
7. Under the lantern his hand holds up the Luang Phu Tim in its light (the shipped beat). **ZAV** [thoughtful]: "A
   ghost? A Thevada? Hanuman? I couldn't prove it was a ghost. I couldn't prove it wasn't. I couldn't even prove it was
   something else entirely."
8. To the kerb, and up at the stars (the shipped beat). **ZAV** [resolved, quietly]: "What I realised that night was
   simple. I couldn't just accept the first explanation I was handed. If I wanted the truth... I had to go and seek
   it, learn it, and find it for myself."
9. **ZAV** [reflective] (`z3close`, as shipped): "After that night, I started visiting other shops... other masters...
   looking for answers." — then `z3next`.

## 5 · An alternative for C, if the shop owner is not wanted

**C · "Tell him honestly what you felt" (good).** He tells the medium it danced for the Buddha and only grew angry at
the word "ghost". The medium listens and takes it seriously ("Danced for the Buddha... Hm. Then maybe it is not what I
said."), and gives him a talisman. Outside, he realises he has told the truth about his experience and then gone home
to wait for someone else to tell him what it means. Lesson: honesty about your own experience helps the person helping
you — but the answer still has to be sought, not waited for. (Closer to D in kind, which is why it is the second
choice, not the first.)
