## The ten that matter most

### 1 · The fight's own count leaves nothing to fight (BLOCKER · CERTAIN, the plan's arithmetic)

**What breaks.** Stage 5, the only stage "where the fight can be lost" and the only one that is not taught or
scripted, holds two presences, both already worn out by the lesson before it. The climax can be skipped by one hold.

**The plan's words.** §5.4: three kinds, three of each. §5.5: stage 1 ends at "8 left", stage 2 at "6 left", stage 3
at "5 left"; stage 4, "the last five rise together ... the light ones are worn out, the heavy ones only thrown back",
ends when "the first wave has gone out"; stage 5, "60 to 90 s | everything; nothing new", ends at "3 left"; stage 6,
"the last three draw together into one tall leaning shape". And: "Stages 1 to 4 are the teaching and are also four of
the nine ... the count of stage 5 is where the fight can be lost." §5.3, BAG: "he draws every worn-out one in front of
him out of the air and into the bag". §5.10: BAG 1.0 s, 60°, reach 5 m, in a play area of 3.9 x 6.0 m.

**Why.** Five are left when stage 5 opens and it ends at three: it bags exactly two. The stage-4 wave has just left
the "light ones" worn out for 12 s, so stage 5 is one or two one-second holds. If stage 2's two are of the kind that
comes (its words: "two come for Zavier"), the three that remain are the three heavy ones: the kind §5.4 says teaches
"hold, switch, fire: the pair as a pair" is never played, and ph5f10 ("The heavy ones do not tire. Hold them in the
WARD first.") teaches a rule nobody needs. The mercy ("after four and a half minutes of stage 5") can never be
reached. Worse, the bag takes "every worn-out one in front of him": if the wave wears out everything (as §5.3 and
§6.1 say it does) one hold can take all five, and the count goes 5 to 0 with no surge, no TOGETHER and no Naga; and
on §5.5's reading, if stage 2's two were heavy ones, four light ones lie worn out and one hold can go 5 to 1, past
the "3 left" the surge waits on. The chapter's remembered picture hangs on a number the bag can jump. Added up:
120 s taught (25 + 35 + 30 + 30), about 60 s scripted (20 + 25 + 15), seconds of play.

**The fix.** Take the taught presences out of the nine (or teach on three and leave six, the heavy ones among them,
for stage 5), and rule that a hold of the bag never takes the count under three before the surge.

### 2 · The rules contradict each other, and one held button wins (BLOCKER · CERTAIN on the text, SUSPECTED on tuning)

thrown back two paces". §5.10: FIRE 0.7 s, 9 m/s, reach 8 m; a presence's pace 0.9 (heavy 0.5) m/s; Zavier's walk 1.1.
§5.6: "Pho arrives with one free fireball at whatever is nearest Zavier; Zavier arrives with one second inside a ward".
§5.7: Zavier, left alone, never does "raise a new ward", against the fourth rung's q5h1, "My hands rose without me."
§14.1: a player who "presses nothing" is one of six who "must finish", against §5.5 ("the court waits for the first

**Why.** (a) In 0.7 s a presence walks 0.63 m and a hit throws it back about 1.5 m: one held button keeps any of them

both kits. (f) Whether the wave wears out a heavy one is said three ways. (g) A player who presses nothing can
neither finish (§5.5, §5.7, §6.1) nor be left waiting (§14.1). His own words on chapter 4's fight, two days ago
(R44): "any player that keeps spamming the water at the bowl area can indefinitely prevent any green spirits from even
reaching her. Its way too easy". The plan cites R16 to R22 and not R44, and then adds a mercy, a helper who acts for
him, a half-pace last third, gifts on the switch and slower presences after a loss.

**The fix.** Write §5 once as one table with (a) to (g) settled (fire with a short reach and no push on a heavy one,
the gift on a wait of its own, one answer for the wave, one for the player who never presses), run the six simulated
players on it before anything else, and put "how hard" to him with R44 quoted beside it.
