## Design decisions, with reasons

1. **The chapter file becomes an IIFE.** `build(ctx)` fills closure
   variables; `scenes` read them. Cutscenes and the world they animate
   are the same chapter, so they share a closure instead of a 20-field
   prop bag passed back and forth. Still a plain script — no ESM, which
   file:// and the CSP build both need.
2. **`A(c)` (the cutscene authoring helpers) stays in the ENGINE** and is
   handed to scenes via ctx. It is the cutscene *language*, not chapter
   content; every chapter writes scenes with the same verbs.
3. **The ghost stays engine.** She is the game's, not chapter 1's — which
   is why `ghost` also moves out of ch1's `assets:` list (step C).
4. **`dispose()` must genuinely free.** Geometries, materials, textures,
   render targets. Proven by a harness that builds/disposes 50× and
   watches `renderer.info`, not by reading the code.
5. **No page reload on chapter advance.** `dispose()` → `build()`. A
   reload re-pays GLB parse, shader compile and audio decode.
6. **Persistence does NOT change boot.** Restore-on-boot is a
   player-visible change to a frozen baseline, and with one chapter it is
   meaningless. The mechanism ships; auto-resume is Chad's call later.

## Where this plan turned out to be wrong

**D2 said: re-point ~13 engine harnesses at the fixture chapter, so most
tests never load a real one.** That was wrong, and it only became obvious
once the rest existed:

- It solves a problem that does not exist. The engine harnesses run once
  whatever the chapter count — they do not grow with the game. What grows
  is per-chapter checking, and that is now one data-driven file
  (`chaptertest.mjs`, plain Node, well under a second).
- It would have REMOVED coverage. CLAUDE.md says the harnesses are what
  *enforce* the v3.3 reference standard. Pointed at a primitive room they
  would enforce nothing about the game anyone actually plays.

So the rule stands, restated honestly: **adding a chapter must not add a
harness.** The engine harnesses stay on the real chapter 1.

The fixture chapter was still built, for what it is genuinely good for:
being a second chapter. With only ch1 registered, "the engine is
chapter-agnostic" was an untestable claim — there was nothing to be
agnostic about. `fixturetest.mjs` now boots `?ch=chtest` and plays it end
to end (spawn → reach → decide → cutscene → outcome card → swap back to
ch1 in place), and `statetest` dropped the Proxy it needed to fake a
second chapter.

**B2b assumed a cutscene was chapter content with a few engine touches.**
Measured, the four scenes reference 28 engine names and about six chapter
props. A cutscene is stage direction; only the stage belongs to the
chapter. So the seam had to be a cutscene LANGUAGE (`sceneApi`), not a
prop bag — see LEARNINGS.

## One failure found, and it was the test

The full suite came back red on `ghosttest`'s `deepDeckAllVisible` — the
v3.3 line-of-sight fix. It was not a regression. The section teleports the
player to the burner and counts the next spawns, but she is often still
visible from the previous section, staged for a player standing somewhere
else; that appearance got scored as a deep-deck spawn. Two runs in five.
Proven by reproducing the exact sequence and printing her state on
arrival, not by re-running until it went green. The section now retreats
first and asserts she is gone (`deepStartsClean`). Three clean runs after.

## Not done, deliberately

**E2 — deleting the embedded build (`hellnote.html`).** Kept. It costs
~5.5 s of build time, it is the anywhere/offline fallback, and it is the
only surface `csptest` can test the strict no-blob/no-data CSP on — the
policy that shaped the hand-parsed GLB and audio loaders. Chad's standing
rule is "don't lose anything", and deleting a working fallback to save
5 seconds is the wrong trade. Revisit only if it starts costing real
download or maintenance weight.
