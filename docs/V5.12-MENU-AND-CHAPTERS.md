# v5.12 — THE PAUSE MENU AND THE CHAPTER SELECTOR

Chad's ask, verbatim:

> Add a chapter selector on the main title screen. User who finished
> previous chapters can go back to those chapters, and it will play from
> the starting intro Cinematic cutscene again, then the title appearance,
> then the playable scene. In the in-game HUD, add a new menu icon with
> the settings gear icon. User can use that to go back/return to main
> title screen, or select chapter as well, or continue back to the
> playable scene. position the menu icon below the mute button, but above
> the inventory icon. Meaning the inventory icon is moved down to give
> space to this new menu icon. Menu icon should be activated by pressing
> 'M' or tapping on it.

This file is the build's memory: what "reached" means and where it is
kept, how a replay re-enters the world, what leaving to the title does
and undoes, and why the selector is a button on the title rather than a
list.

## 1. What is open, and where that is kept

A chapter is OPEN in the selector once the player has REACHED it. The
furthest chapter reached is recorded on its own key,
`mz.encounters.progress` (`{ reached: 'chN' }`), separate from the run's
save at `mz.encounters.checkpoint`. That separation is the point:

- starting a new game wipes the run, not the chapters it earned;
- replaying chapter 1 does not lock chapter 4 again;
- a save already sitting in chapter 3 (from before this build) counts:
  at boot the save's chapter is folded in with `markReached`.

`markReached()` is called at exactly two moments: when play begins in a
chapter (the chapter card's callback in `enterWorld`, so a chapter you
are standing in is open), and when a chapter is SEALED with a next one
(`finish()`, so finishing chapter 2 opens chapter 3 at once, without
waiting for its card). It only ever moves forward. `unlockedKeys()` is
every playable chapter with an id up to the furthest reached, and
chapter 1 always.

## 2. The selector

One panel, `#chapters`, reached from two places: the **Chapters** button
on the title screen (under New game, in the same outlined style) and
**Select a chapter** in the pause menu. It lists every playable chapter
in registry order — `chapterOrder()` filtered below id 90, so the fixture
never appears — with its card label and its name; a chapter not yet
reached shows "Not yet reached" in place of its name, is dimmed and is
disabled. The chapter being played is outlined in jade.

**Why a button and not a list on the title.** Five rows under the brief,
the big button, New game and the controls help would push the bottom of
the title off a phone screen, and Chad plays from his phone. A button
costs one tap and the panel scrolls if it ever has to.

**Picking a chapter asks first when there is something to lose** — a run
in progress in play, or a save on the title — with the chapter named in
the question; on a fresh profile the title's selector just goes. Yes
runs `startChapter(key)`:

```
closeChapters(); menuClose(false); invClose() if open;
setChapter(key);            // rebuilds the world, mutating in place
restart();                  // everything a fresh run has to put back
enterWorld(place-at-spawn, { intro: true });
```

That is the same sequence the sealed card's Continue uses to advance,
so a replay arrives the way every chapter arrives: its opening film,
its title card, then the night. `restart()` writes a save at the
chapter's start, and the card's callback writes another when play
begins; Continue on the title then resumes THIS chapter, and the
progress key keeps the later ones open.

## 3. The pause menu

`#menuBtn` is a gear in the round-button column: mute at the top, the
gear 56 px under it, the inventory 56 px under that (`.invBtn` moved
from +56 to +112). It shows only in play (`body.inplay`), hides in a
scene (`body.cine`), and on a laptop wears the **M** keycap the way the
inventory wears **I**. M or a tap opens `#menu`; while it is up the
round buttons step aside (`body.menuopen .soundBtn{display:none}`, the
same trick the inventory uses). Escape, Return to the game, or a tap on
the backdrop closes it and hands the mouse back to looking.

**Freezing.** One state, `menu`, set the way the inventory sets
`inventory`: every gate in the engine that asks `state === 'play'` —
movement, edge turn, drain, autosave, the voice — is closed by it. Held
movement keys are cleared on opening so a W pressed at the moment of
opening does not walk under the panel. `menutest` measures it: nine
hundred milliseconds of W with the menu up moves the player by nothing.

**Back to the title screen** is the one path that leaves play without a
card, so it has to undo by hand what `enterWorld()` did:

1. `saveCheckpoint()` FIRST, with the position — Continue brings the
   player straight back to this spot (`menutest`: `continueLandsThere`).
2. panels closed, `state = 'title'`, pointer lock released.
3. HUD, hint, prompt, interact and decision layers hidden;
   `body.inplay` removed so the round buttons go.
4. the bed, the chapter's loops and any cutscene voices stopped, the
   narration line cut, the explore music ramped to zero (`musicRamp`;
   `enterWorld()` ramps it back to `musicVolNow()`, which is a no-op on
   every other entry because the gain is already there).
5. her re-armed hidden at home, keys and velocity cleared.
6. the title layer shown, its backdrop video played again, `paintTitle()`
   so the big button reads Continue and the note names the chapter.

## 4. Tests

`menutest.mjs` (new, listed in `runtests.mjs` as `menu`) drives all of
it: the title's selector on a fresh profile, the column of buttons, M
and Escape, the frozen walk, the gear and Return, back to the title with
the save and Continue landing on the same spot, then reaching chapter 2,
the ask, the film (`state === 'cine'`, skipped through `cine.skip()`) and
play in chapter 2. One trap it hit, already known from `invtest`: after
the menu closes the game takes the mouse back for looking, and a
Playwright click on the gear is then intercepted by the canvas — release
the pointer lock before clicking a round button.

`texttest` keeps every new word on the sheet; sheet **v18**. `invtest`,
`resumetest`, `csptest`, `leaktest` green on the build. Engine seams:
none — the menu is a fourth screen state beside the inventory's, built
from the pieces `enterWorld`, `restart` and `setChapter` already were.
