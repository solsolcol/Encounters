## 1 · THE BEDDING CHAD'S MODEL ALREADY HAS

v9.0 hid the frame, the mattresses and the pillows when the bunk model landed,
and left the *bedding* standing. Photographed from his own eye at the foot of
bed one — which is where the player stands for the whole standby-bed beat —
that is three things drawn twice:

| leftover | where it sat | against the model |
|---|---|---|
| bedsheet | y 0.550–0.562 | 1.2 cm proud of a mattress that has its own cover |
| folded blanket | y 0.550–0.670, at the aisle end | a green box on the guard rail, nearest the lens |
| wire base | y 1.260, a 1.84 × 0.84 plane | 1.5 cm under a mattress whose frame carries its own |

All three join `b.supersede` (16 meshes → 21). What stays is what the model
does NOT carry: the **boots** under the bed, and the blanket pulled over a
**sleeper**, which is a man's blanket rather than the bed's and is only ever
visible at lights out. Measured on the shipped build afterwards, the only
thing still drawn in a bed group besides the model is the two boots.

Two consequences handled with it:

* `setNightRoom` wrote `b.low.fold.visible = true` on most beds every time the
  lights changed, which would have **resurrected a superseded mesh**. It is
  now guarded on `!b.model`, so a bed carrying the model never has its
  primitives switched back on. (The same class of bug as v8.1/v8.2: a value
  one part of the code retires, another part keeps writing.)
* v9.0's line that *moved* the wire base to 1.260 is gone with it. If the
  download never lands, `supersede` never runs and the primitive bed stands
  untouched, base and all.

## 2 · THE DOORWAY YOU CANNOT SEE FROM INSIDE THE BLOCK

v7.5 measured the toilet block and found every cell of it reachable; v8.9
opened the leaf permanently so nothing had to be interacted with. Chad still
could not get out. The reason was never collision — it was one mesh:

```js
const back = new THREE.Mesh(new THREE.PlaneGeometry(bw, R.h), matTile);
```

5.5 m wide, 3.0 m tall, hung across the wall the block shares with the bunk —
**including the doorway**. From inside, the way out was a flat sheet of tile.
And because that plane is not a blocker, the exit that worked was the one you
could not see: worse than a wall, not better.

It is three pieces now — left of the door, right of it, and the lintel over
it — so the lit bunk shows through the hole, with a painted jamb round the
opening so it still reads as a doorway when the bunk beyond it is dark.

## 3 · THE DOOR SWUNG INTO THE BED

The comment above the swing contract said *positive = into the block*. It was
wrong, and it had been wrong since v7.1. A positive rotation about y takes the
leaf's +x arm toward **−z**, which is the bunk. Measured on the shipped v9.0
build, the open leaf ran from its hinge at (−3.750, 4.080) to (−3.686, 3.182);
bed one's frame spans x −5.55…−3.65 and z 2.55…3.45, so the last 27 cm of the
door stood inside the bed.

`DOOR_AJAR` and `DOOR_OPEN` are negative now, which swings it the other way —
into the block, where the nearest thing is a cubicle partition 2.4 m further
on. Both are symbolic at every call site (the film's `step(0)`, scene A's
`tr`), so the film's almost-shut door and the scene's swing are one motion in
one direction rather than a door that opens through its own frame. Scene A's
camera runs down x = −3.3 and the leaf sits at x ≈ −3.72: it passes 0.42 m
clear of it, and the open leaf is in frame as he walks in, which is what a
door that has just been pushed open should do.

## 4 · THE CORRIDOR THAT WENT NOWHERE

The −z wall had a doorway in it, a dark BackSide box 2.4 m deep beyond it, a
rubber doormat under that, and a hotspot whose whole behaviour was:

```js
onInteract() { return false; }
```

A thing to walk up to, with a prompt, that does nothing — which is exactly
what makes it read as pointless rather than as atmosphere. All of it is gone.
The wall runs the full width now, so `blockers()` boxes one wall instead of
three (verified: one blocker spans the whole −z end) and the 8 cm floor gap
the doormat was covering cannot exist. The word `hotOut` leaves the chapter's
`words`.

Sealing it left 12 m² of blank paint in the middle of the shot the film ends
on, so the wall is dressed the way a bunk's is: a rail of five hooks with two
towels on it, and a first-aid box beside them. All primitives, no download,
all flat against the wall — the blockers box the wall, so nothing there is
walked into. The extinguisher, the bin and the notice board stay where they
were.

## 5 · EIGHT MEN SLEEPING THE WRONG WAY

Both halves of Chad's note were real, and both were measured on the shipped
v9.0 build before anything moved.

**The statue** (`sleeper.glb`) lies along its own z, and its head is at that
model's **−z** end. Sliced into ten bins along that axis:

| bin (−z → +z) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 5 | 6 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| width (m) | 0.795 | 0.812 | 0.418 | 0.461 | 0.411 | 0.238 | 0.245 |
| top y (m) | 0.32 | 0.29 | 0.10 | 0.126 | 0.152 | −0.115 | −0.073 |

The −z end is 0.80 m across and the highest thing in the file — shoulders,
with the arms up behind the head. The +z end is 0.24 m across and the lowest —
feet. v7.1 turned it by `+π/2 (+π when head < 0)`, which puts −z at +x on the
balcony row and at −x on the wall row: the opposite of the pillow in **both**.
The turn is `−b.head · π/2` now, which is the one angle the measurement gives.

**The rig** (`sleepanim.glb`) was worse and nobody had looked. Measured, its
world box was **0.58 m on x by 1.60 m on z** — it lay *across* the bed,
sticking a third of a metre into the aisle at one end and into the next bed at
the other, with its head bone over the middle of the mattress.
v7.1's `if (size.x > size.z) m.rotation.y += Math.PI / 2` is the test inverted
(a body must be turned when it is long on Z, not on X) and it was applied
*after* the centring, which the turn then invalidated.

The facing is no longer guessed. The rig is turned by its own **head bone** —
long axis onto the bed's length, head toward the pillow — and only then
scaled, centred and grounded, working in the bed group's own frame throughout,
because that is the frame the pillow is always at +x in (that is what
`g.rotation.y` is for).

Two things the rewrite got right that the obvious version does not:

* **Every copy is made before any of them is touched.** `cloneSkinned` copies
  transforms as well as bones, so cloning inside the loop takes each copy from
  the previous one *after* it has been scaled, turned and grounded.
* **The scale and the turn are measured once**, on the first copy at its rest
  pose, and given to all four. Measuring each man on his own take would scale
  them differently — a curled-up cough take is a shorter box, which asks for a
  bigger man. The v5.05 law: when new things are placed among existing things,
  the measure that matters is the one the existing things used.

**The mix** is four and four, dealt so no two rigs share a wall: the wall row
runs statue, rig, statue, rig and the balcony row statue, rig, rig, statue.
Index 3 is the bed beside his — Chad's "must be the animated one" — and it is
also scene C's neighbour, the man who rolls over when the player whispers,
which is the one sleeper in the chapter a cutscene points the camera at. The
four rigs come from ONE parse and three `cloneSkinned` copies (shared
geometry, one upload), each with its own mixer, its own take out of the file's
three (`Sleep_Normally`, `Cough_While_Sleeping`,
`Groan_Holding_Stomach_in_Sleep`) and its own rate, started at a different
point of its own breath — so four breathing men are four different men.

Measured on the shipped build afterwards, at lights out, all eight:

* head at the pillow end: **8 of 8**
* lying along the bed's length, not across it: **8 of 8**
* inside the mattress's own z: **8 of 8**
* back on the mattress top (y 0.565–0.570 against a 0.549 mattress): **8 of 8**
